AWS Cloud Credit for Research → Rolling 90–120 day review

Amazon Web ServicesCompute grantRemote

AWS promotional credit for researchers at accredited institutions. Graduate, post-graduate and PhD students are explicitly eligible, capped at $5,000; faculty and staff awards are uncapped. Reviewed on a rolling basis with no fixed deadline, but AWS states typical review cycles run 90 to 120 days and that expedited reviews are not possible. Credit covers EC2 on-demand and spot instances.

Why youThe largest student-eligible compute grant here, and five times what Google gives a PhD student. Adapting MedSAM2 for text prompts and benchmarking SegVol and SAT on ReXGroundingCT is exactly the 3D workload that eats GPU hours. Apply early — on a four-month review cycle, a request made now lands around January.

Plan the ask around the review lag. This is not the one to reach for when you need compute next week; it is the one to file now so it is there for the spring. Note also that AWS cannot process applications from the greater China region — irrelevant to you, but it is the only geographic bar they name.

Google Cloud Research Credits → Rolling 4–6 week decision

Google CloudCompute grantRemote

Cloud credit for academic research, usable across compute, storage and the AI/ML services — which is to say GPU and TPU time for training and inference. The landing page headlines $5,000, but the award is tiered: that figure is the faculty, postdoc and non-profit-lab award, and PhD students apply for up to $1,000, renewable once a year. Decisions come in four to six weeks. Credits must be activated within 60 days and expire 365 days after issue.

Why youSmall on its own, but the fastest turnaround of the three credit programmes and the only one that renews annually, so it is worth starting the clock. The more interesting route is the other tier: Rajpurkar can apply as faculty for the full $5,000 against a lab project, which is a five-minute ask with a much better return than your own $1,000. TPU access is the genuinely differentiated part if you ever want to pretrain a Harmony-style backbone off the lab cluster.

Two chores before applying. Google wants a billing account created first and a cost estimate built in its pricing calculator; the application is rejected without them. Master’s students are ineligible, PhD students are not, and the US is on the approved-country list.

ACCESS Allocations — Explore / Discover → Rolling Reviewed continually

NSF ACCESSCompute allocationUS institutions, remote

ACCESS is the NSF-funded successor to XSEDE and allocates time on national supercomputing and GPU systems at no cost. The allocations policy states that "All U.S.-based graduate students, including NSF Graduate Student Fellows, are eligible to be PIs on Explore or Discover ACCESS allocations; their graduate advisor must be included on the request as a co-PI." Submission is rolling: "ACCESS accepts requests for these opportunities at any time, and the requests are reviewed and processed continually", typically within about two weeks. Explore is described as suited to resource evaluation, graduate student projects, benchmarking and code development.

NewHe can be the PI himself rather than needing a faculty member to lead it, with Rajpurkar simply added as co-PI, which is unusual among compute programmes and makes it low-friction for a first-year. Self-supervised pretraining and 3D chest CT segmentation are GPU-heavy in a way that API credit programmes like the Anthropic and Google Cloud entries do not cover. Eligibility turns on being at a US-based academic institution, not on citizenship, so his F-1 status and Saudi/Canadian citizenship are not obstacles.

Graduate students can lead only the two smaller tiers, Explore and Discover; the larger Accelerate and Maximize allocations need a faculty PI. An advisor letter on institutional letterhead is required as part of the request.

NAIRR Pilot — Resource Allocation Request → Rolling — 15th monthly next cutoff 15 Sep 2026

NSF-led NAIRR PilotCompute allocationUS institutions

Free allocations of national AI computing resources — GPU time on federal systems plus donated access to industry models and datasets — awarded through a standing call. The call has been open since 6 May 2024 and runs until the pilot ends or all resources are committed, on an ongoing monthly cycle: requests submitted by the 15th of a month are decided by the end of the following month. There are two tracks, a roughly three-month Start-Up Project for demonstrating feasibility and a roughly twelve-month Research Project.

Why youEligibility turns on affiliation with a US academic institution rather than citizenship, so the F-1 visa is irrelevant here, and graduate students may apply directly provided they attach a support letter from a faculty advisor — one line of email to Rajpurkar. Self-supervised pretraining on 3D chest CT and report-generation experiments are exactly the GPU-hungry work these allocations exist for, and the three-month Start-Up track is a low-cost way to test whether the larger request is worth making. It also complements the Anthropic credits already on the list, which cover API usage rather than training compute.

Needs Rajpurkar’s signature, not his time. Graduate students can be the requester with a support letter from a faculty advisor, and eligibility keys off being at a US institution rather than on nationality, so Harvard covers you. Submit from your HMS address — requests from personal email are discarded unread. Requests in by the 15th are decided by the end of the following month.
