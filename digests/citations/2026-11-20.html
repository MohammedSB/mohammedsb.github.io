ORGANIC3D: Grounding 3D CT Vision Language Models in Organ Level Image Text Coupling

Ze-Long Liu, Hamza Ahmed, Wynn Wingyi Lee, Mickael Torjman et al. (10 authors) · 2026

What they say: ORGANIC3D uses ReXGroundingCT as one of its evaluation datasets. The authors describe it as radiologist-annotated chest lesion masks for 464 CT-RATE test scans and use it for lesion-level tests. They also treat it as an example of conventional spatial grounding, meaning report sentences are matched to segmented findings to localize them directly. They contrast this with their own controlled-edit approach, which removes or alters the image evidence for a finding and checks whether the model's prediction changes.

  • …other public datasets, including RAD-ChestCT [16] (3,630 chest volumes), and two datasets with radiologist-annotated lesion masks for lesion-level tests, ReXGroundingCT [2] (chest lesions in 464 CT-RATE test scans) and AbdomenAtlas 3.0 [4] (liver, pancreas and kidney tumors in 926 test scans).
  • Alignment is usually inferred from classification accuracy, sentence grounding to radiologist-segmented findings in ReXGroundingCT [2], or attention maps [29, 55].
  • Unlike conventional spatial grounding, which directly localizes findings [2], controlled edits test whether its prediction changes when the image evidence for the finding is removed or altered.

What they say: MedSAM2 is treated as a stateless external refiner in a cascade. It gets a bounding box for each active slice but has no empty-slice screening, no global-to-local VLM context and no closed-loop error verification, so it often overwrites masks that were already accurate. X-Strata: Separating Aneurysms and Overlapping Vessels into Layers from 2D X-ray Angiography

What they say: No sentences citing MedSAM2 were available, so this is an inference from the abstract, not something the paper says. MedSAM2 is probably cited as a promptable segmentation foundation model for 3D medical images and videos, possibly used as a baseline. Such a model would only outline the visible aneurysm frame by frame in a cine angiogram. Citing: State-of-the-Art Text-Prompted Medical Segmentation Models Struggle to Ground Chest CT Findings

Grounding Radiology Reports in 3D Chest CT: Results of the ReXGrounding Challenge

Mohammed Baharoon, Mahmoud Hussain Alabbad, Sung Eun Kim, Xiao-Man Zhang et al. (62 authors) · 2026

What they say: Baharoon et al. (2025) is cited as evidence that general text-promptable medical segmentation models fail out of the box at grounding the descriptive findings in chest CT radiology reports. These models are trained mostly on short category-level prompts, such as names of anatomical structures and lesion classes. Report sentences instead identify the target by size, laterality, lobe or segment, and morphology, and the models could not handle them.

  • Current text-promptable segmentation models, trained mostly on short category-level prompts, fail on the descriptive findings written in radiology reports (Zhao et al., 2025c,a,b; Du et al., 2024; Subramanian et al., 2026; Baharoon et al., 2025).
