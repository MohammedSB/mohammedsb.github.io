1. A 3D foundation model unifying CT interpretation and segmentation

Yulu Xie, Zilong Li, Shen Xu, Yifan Bai et al. (17 authors)  ·  2611.06471

Medical ImagingFoundation ModelsCT Segmentation

TL;DR: A unified 3D CT foundation model (Holistic) jointly performs radiology report-style interpretation and 3D anatomical segmentation, enabling spatially grounded clinical findings.

Problem: CT interpretation and 3D segmentation have been treated as separate tasks: textual findings from report generation are spatially unverifiable, and segmentation models lack clinical semantic understanding. This separation ignores the natural synergy between understanding what a finding is and localizing where it appears in 3D anatomy.

Method: The authors present Holistic, a 3D foundation model trained to unify CT interpretation (generating structured clinical findings) and 3D segmentation within a single architecture. The model jointly conditions text generation on spatial features and spatial segmentation on textual semantic cues, enabling bi-directional grounding between language descriptions and volumetric anatomy.

Results: Holistic achieves competitive or superior performance on both CT report generation and 3D segmentation benchmarks compared to task-specific models, demonstrating that joint training produces synergistic benefits rather than trade-offs.

Why it matters: Directly addresses the reader's core interests in vision-language foundation models for radiology, multimodal medical imaging, and radiology report generation, extending CLIP-style cross-modal alignment into 3D volumetric CT analysis.

2. ME^2FDiff: Multiscale Expert Enhancing Feature Diffusion in Residual Network for Brain Tumor Segmentation

Afrida Rahman, Bijoy Kundu  ·  2611.05766

Medical ImagingBrain Tumor SegmentationDiffusion Models

TL;DR: ME²FDiff introduces a multiscale expert-enhanced feature diffusion model for post-treatment glioma segmentation from MRI, addressing the challenge of therapy-induced structural changes that confound standard segmenters.

Problem: Automated post-treatment glioma segmentation from MRI is difficult because surgery and chemoradiotherapy create resection cavities, edema, and fibrosis that alter imaging appearance and mimic tumor recurrence, causing standard deep learning segmenters to fail or produce overconfident incorrect predictions.

Method: The proposed ME²FDiff framework embeds a residual network backbone with multiscale expert modules that capture feature representations at multiple spatial scales, combined with a feature diffusion mechanism that iteratively refines segmentation boundaries within a probabilistic generation framework. The diffusion process learns to denoise ambiguous boundary regions specific to post-treatment anatomy.

Results: ME²FDiff achieves state-of-the-art segmentation accuracy on post-treatment glioma MRI benchmarks, with particular gains on the challenging resection cavity and infiltrative edema sub-regions that prior architectures frequently misclassify.

Why it matters: Relevant to the reader's interests in self-supervised and multimodal learning for medical imaging, diffusion-based generative approaches in radiology/pathology, and evaluation of segmentation models on clinically challenging cases.

3. 3D Banff Glomerulitis Scoring for Kidney Transplant Pathology: Feasibility Study for Volumetric Quantification

Yanfan Zhu, Junchao Zhu, Ruining Deng, Shilin Zhao et al. (15 authors)  ·  2611.05925

HistopathologyMedical Imaging3D Reconstruction

TL;DR: A feasibility study demonstrates volumetric 3D Banff glomerulitis scoring from serial kidney-transplant biopsy sections, revealing that 2D single-section assessment is unstable when inflammation varies across slices.

Problem: Kidney transplant biopsy scoring for glomerulitis (Banff g-score) is performed on a single 2D whole-slide section using a 'most-severe section' rule, despite glomerular inflammation being inherently 3D. This 2D approach introduces instability when inflammation varies across tissue depth, potentially leading to inaccurate grading.

Method: The authors construct a volumetric quantification pipeline that co-registers serial whole-slide sections from kidney biopsies into a 3D atlas, segments individual glomeruli, and computes inflammation metrics volumetrically across sections. The pipeline includes automated glomerulus detection, multi-section alignment, and 3D lesion distribution analysis to enable cross-slice Banff scoring.

Results: 3D volumetric assessment reveals significant inter-slice variation in glomerulitis that the standard single-section rule misses, confirming that 2D scoring is unstable. The feasibility study establishes that full 3D Banff scoring is technically achievable with modern serial-section imaging pipelines.

Why it matters: Relevant to the reader's interests in computational pathology, medical image segmentation, and quantitative evaluation frameworks in clinical medical imaging contexts.

1. Where to Connect? Boosting MLLMs via Dynamic Gated Pathways across ALL ViT and LLM Layers

Yingying Yan, Jiaqi Tang, Wei Wei, Qianzhou Wang et al. (10 authors)  ·  2611.06844

Vision-Language ModelsMultimodal Large Language ModelsFeature Fusion

TL;DR: This work proposes dynamic gated cross-layer pathways that selectively route visual features from all ViT layers into all LLM layers, outperforming fixed single-layer injection in multimodal LLMs.

Problem: Current multimodal LLMs inject a fixed single-layer visual representation from the vision encoder into the LLM, ignoring that different LLM layers have different semantic needs and that hierarchical ViT layers encode complementary spatial and semantic information. This rigid coupling limits visual grounding and understanding quality.

Method: The authors introduce dynamic gated pathways that connect every ViT layer to every LLM layer through learned gating functions. At each LLM layer, a lightweight gate dynamically selects which ViT layer's features are most relevant and in what proportion, allowing the model to adaptively draw low-level spatial detail from early ViT layers and high-level semantics from later ones.

Results: The dynamic gated pathway architecture achieves consistent improvements over strong MLLM baselines on a broad suite of visual understanding benchmarks, with particularly large gains on tasks requiring fine-grained spatial reasoning and detailed visual description.

Why it matters: Directly relevant to the reader's interests in CLIP-style vision-language foundation models, multimodal learning, and architectural innovations for connecting vision encoders to language models—applicable to radiology report generation and medical VLMs.

2. Prism: Bidirectional Self-Teaching for Unified Multimodal Models

Peng Zhang, Qian Chen, Xianyin Zhang, Yimin Tian et al. (9 authors)  ·  2611.06343

Vision-Language ModelsMultimodal LearningSelf-Supervised Learning

TL;DR: Prism derives bidirectional self-supervised training signals for unified multimodal models by having the model generate interleaved image-text rollouts and using understanding to supervise generation and vice versa.

Problem: Unified multimodal models that jointly handle visual understanding and generation require expensive ground-truth supervision for both modalities, and existing post-training methods rely on either fixed teacher models or labeled trajectories, limiting scalability and cross-modal synergy.

Method: Prism constructs bidirectional supervision entirely from self-generated interleaved rollouts: the model generates sequences of alternating image and text outputs, then uses its own understanding branch to score generated images (improving generation) and uses generated images as synthetic visual context to improve understanding. No external ground-truth labels or fixed teacher models are required during this self-teaching phase.

Results: Prism achieves competitive or superior performance on both visual generation and understanding benchmarks compared to methods using external supervision, and demonstrates that the bidirectional self-teaching loop improves both tasks simultaneously more than either one-directional method.

Why it matters: Highly relevant to the reader's interests in self-supervised and multimodal learning, vision-language foundation models, and techniques that could enable label-efficient training for medical vision-language models.

3. On the Design of Hyper-Connections: Simplification, Stability, and Scalability

Xiangdong Zhang, Xiaohan Qin, Sunan Zou, Tuo Dai et al. (11 authors)  ·  2611.05984

Foundation ModelsLLM ArchitectureEfficient Training

TL;DR: A systematic study of Hyper-Connections (HC) architecture identifies which components are essential, simplifies the design to reduce training overhead, and shows improved stability and scalability in large language models.

Problem: Hyper-Connections (HC) architectures, increasingly used in frontier language models, introduce non-negligible training overhead and their components remain poorly understood, making it difficult to identify simpler or more efficient alternatives without sacrificing the benefits they provide.

Method: The authors conduct controlled ablations of HC components—including read/write gating, mixing operations, and depth constraints—across multiple model scales. They identify that learned read/write gating without mixing and unconstrained mixing parameters are the key effective ingredients, leading to a simplified HC design that eliminates unnecessary complexity and reduces training cost.

Results: The simplified HC design matches or exceeds the performance of full HC architectures on standard language modeling benchmarks while reducing training overhead by a meaningful margin, and shows improved training stability compared to standard Transformer baselines at scale.

Why it matters: Relevant to the reader's interests in foundation model architectures and training techniques that underpin large vision-language models like CLIP-style systems used in medical imaging.
