## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [G-CARL: Grounded Checklist-Aligned Reward Learning for Patient-Oriented Medical Report Interpretation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20331)
**Authors:** Shiao Xie, Siyu Chen, Jianwei Lv, Bo Yuan et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.20331`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Vision-Language Models, Reward Modeling

**TL;DR:** G-CARL introduces a patient-oriented medical report interpretation task plus a grounded, checklist-aligned reward learning scheme that makes report explanations both clinically faithful and patient-appropriate.

**Problem:** Patients increasingly want plain-language interpretations of their imaging/lab reports, which demands evidence-grounded factuality and audience-appropriate communication at the same time. Existing medical vision-language tasks (VQA, report generation) evaluate neither requirement jointly, so there is no training signal or benchmark for this dual objective.

**Method:** The authors define Patient-oriented Medical Report Interpretation as a new multimodal task and build a paired benchmark for it. Their method decomposes quality into explicit clinical checklists whose items must be grounded in report/image evidence, then learns a reward model aligned to those checklist items rather than to holistic preference scores. This checklist-grounded reward is used to align a medical VLM so that factual grounding and patient-facing communication are optimized as separable, verifiable components.

**Results:** Against instruction-tuned and preference-optimized medical VLM baselines, the checklist-aligned reward yields reported gains in evidence grounding and factual correctness while improving patient-oriented communication quality, with the checklist decomposition also serving as a more interpretable evaluation signal than single-score judges.

**Why it matters:** This sits directly at the intersection of radiology report generation and evaluation-metric design: checklist-decomposed, evidence-grounded rewards are exactly the kind of fine-grained factuality signal the report-generation metrics literature has been converging on, and the reward model here doubles as a metric. The patient-facing framing is also a natural bridge to AI-for-medical-education use cases.


### 2. [MUST-PET: MUltimodal Self-supervised learning across Tracers for whole-body PET/CT-based lesion segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19666)
**Authors:** Bashirul Azam Biswas, Amartya Bhattacharya, Biratal Raj Wagle, Matthew E. Maeder et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.19666`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Multimodal Medical Imaging, Lesion Segmentation

**TL;DR:** MUST-PET pretrains a multimodal self-supervised model across PET tracers and CT to improve whole-body pan-cancer lesion segmentation under scarce annotations and domain shift.

**Problem:** Whole-body PET/CT lesion segmentation is limited by expensive voxel-level annotations and by domain shifts across tracers, scanners, and cancer types. Self-supervised pretraining is an obvious remedy but has been barely explored in the pan-cancer, multi-tracer PET/CT setting.

**Method:** The authors build a multimodal SSL framework that pretrains jointly on paired PET and CT volumes, treating the two modalities as complementary views so that the encoder learns anatomy from CT and tracer-uptake semantics from PET. Pretraining spans multiple tracers rather than a single-tracer corpus, which forces representations that are not tied to one radiotracer's uptake statistics. The pretrained encoder is then fine-tuned for whole-body lesion segmentation with limited labeled data.

**Results:** Cross-tracer and low-label fine-tuning show improved segmentation over training from scratch and over single-modality/single-tracer SSL baselines, with the largest gains reported in the label-scarce and out-of-tracer regimes.

**Why it matters:** This is a clean instance of the reader's core thesis area — multimodal SSL for medical imaging — with the modality pairing (functional + anatomical) playing the role that image-text pairing plays in CLIP-style pretraining. The multi-tracer generalization protocol is a useful template for arguing that a medical SSL encoder learned something transferable rather than dataset-specific.


### 3. [Towards Clinically Faithful Medical Image Captioning via Enhanced Vision-Language Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19825)
**Authors:** Yunseo Lee, Hyun Jun Kim, Heeseung Shin, Changwon Lim  
**arXiv:** `2608.19825`  
**Tags:** Medical Image Captioning, Vision-Language Alignment, Clinical Factuality

**TL;DR:** A vision-language alignment recipe tailored to grayscale medical images and specialized clinical phrasing that improves the clinical faithfulness of generated medical captions.

**Problem:** Medical image captioning must handle grayscale modalities, subtle anatomical cues, highly specialized phrasing, and uneven data quality — conditions under which general-domain captioning models produce fluent but clinically unfaithful text. Standard contrastive/alignment pipelines transferred from natural images do not preserve the fine-grained findings that determine clinical correctness.

**Method:** The paper strengthens the vision-language alignment stage rather than only scaling the decoder: image features are aligned to clinical text at a granularity that preserves subtle findings, with training adjustments that account for grayscale, low-contrast inputs and for noisy or heterogeneous report text. The aligned encoder then conditions caption generation, so the decoder is constrained by clinically discriminative visual evidence instead of language priors.

**Results:** Reported improvements cover both standard captioning overlap metrics and clinical-faithfulness measures, with the alignment component ablated as the main driver of the factuality gains.

**Why it matters:** Directly on-topic for radiology report generation and for the question of what alignment objective actually buys clinical faithfulness — the same tension the reader's evaluation-metric work targets. Worth reading alongside G-CARL [5] as the alignment-side counterpart to a reward-side fix.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Orthogonal JEPA: Factorized Predictive States for Latent World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20065)
**Authors:** Taoyong Cui, Pheng Ann Heng, Wanli Ouyang  
**arXiv:** `2608.20065`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, JEPA, World Models

**TL;DR:** Orthogonal JEPA factorizes a joint-embedding predictive architecture's target space into orthogonal subspaces so different predictable factors are represented separately instead of collapsing into one target embedding.

**Problem:** JEPAs learn latent states by predicting in representation space, but standard formulations route all predictable content through a single target embedding. That entangles distinct factors of the underlying system and limits how usable the latent state is for planning and reasoning.

**Method:** The authors impose an orthogonal factorization on the predictive target, splitting it into multiple subspaces that are each predicted separately, so that independent sources of predictability are assigned to different components of the latent state. Training keeps the JEPA recipe — predict representations of masked/future views rather than reconstructing pixels — while the orthogonality constraint prevents the factors from re-collapsing into a shared direction. The result is a latent world model whose state is structured rather than monolithic.

**Results:** The factorized variant reports better predictive-state quality and downstream world-model performance than standard JEPA baselines, with the learned subspaces separating distinct dynamics factors.

**Why it matters:** JEPA-style latent prediction is the main non-contrastive competitor to CLIP/MAE-style pretraining, and factorized targets are a plausible answer to the representation-collapse problems that hurt medical SSL encoders. The idea of dedicating subspaces to distinct predictable factors maps naturally onto multimodal medical data where anatomy, pathology, and acquisition effects are all separately predictable.


### 2. [Projector Is All You Train](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19726)
**Authors:** Nyx Iskandar, Saathvik Selvan, Slater Victoroff  
**arXiv:** `2608.19726`  
**Tags:** Multimodal LLMs, Modality Adaptation, Parameter-Efficient Training

**TL;DR:** Training only the projector — freezing the LLM backbone entirely — is enough to adapt an MLLM to a new modality, demonstrated on 3D multimodal LLMs.

**Problem:** Standard MLLM training tunes both the language backbone and the projector connecting it to a modality encoder, which is expensive and risks degrading the backbone's language ability. Whether backbone fine-tuning is actually necessary for adding a modality has not been isolated.

**Method:** The authors hold the LLM backbone and modality encoder frozen and train only the intervening projector, then compare against full and partial backbone fine-tuning on 3D MLLM tasks. The experiments treat the projector as the sole adaptation surface, testing how much cross-modal capability can be expressed purely as a learned mapping into the frozen language embedding space. Ablations vary projector capacity and training data scale to locate where, if anywhere, backbone updates become necessary.

**Results:** Projector-only training reaches strong multimodal performance competitive with backbone-tuned counterparts at a fraction of the trainable parameters, indicating the backbone contributes little modality-specific learning in this regime.

**Why it matters:** Highly actionable for medical VLMs: if a frozen general LLM plus a trained projector suffices, adapting to CXR, CT, or pathology becomes a small-data, low-compute problem and avoids catastrophic forgetting of clinical language knowledge. It also sharpens the question of where medical knowledge actually lives in a med-MLLM — encoder, projector, or backbone.


### 3. [Ask Self, Ask Others: Relation Is All You Need](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20172)
**Authors:** Yuting Ge, Pengju Yang, Mingkai Nie  
**arXiv:** `2608.20172`  
**Tags:** Attention Alternatives, Sequence Architectures, Efficient Inference

**TL;DR:** "Relation" replaces attention's direct score-to-flow normalization with explicit Self and Exchange relations, yielding a family of variants (Full, Flash, Linear, Hybrid) and a KV-style cache.

**Problem:** Attention derives normalized information flow directly from pairwise scores, conflating how evidence is gathered with how it is routed. That coupling constrains both the inductive bias and the efficiency options available to a token mixer.

**Method:** The paper proposes organizing pairwise evidence first into explicit Self and Exchange relations, and only then deriving information flow from that structure. This two-step formulation admits several concrete instantiations — Full Relation, a fused FlashRelation kernel, Linear Relation for subquadratic cost, and Hybrid Relation mixing the two — plus a Relation Cache that plays the role of the KV cache at inference. The primitive is drop-in at the token-mixing layer, so existing transformer stacks can be converted.

**Results:** The authors report competitive or improved quality against attention baselines across their evaluated settings, with the linear and hybrid variants providing the efficiency trade-offs the decomposition is meant to enable.

**Why it matters:** Attention-replacement proposals rarely come with a full efficiency family plus a cache story, which makes this one worth tracking for long-sequence vision work — 3D volumes, whole-slide images, and long video are exactly where quadratic mixing hurts most. Treat the headline claims as unverified until independent reproduction, as with most "X is all you need" primitives.
