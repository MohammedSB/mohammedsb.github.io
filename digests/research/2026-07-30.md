## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [SCALPEL: Semantic Cross-modal Alignment via LLM-Powered Encoder Learning for Medical Vision-Language Representation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26885)
**Authors:** Yunzhan Fu, Enyu Bao, Xiangyu Shen, Yihao Wu et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.26885`  
**Tags:** Medical Vision-Language Models, Chest X-ray, Self-Supervised Learning

**TL;DR:** SCALPEL proposes a new medical vision-language pre-training framework that uses a frozen medical LLM as a rich text encoder to better align visual features with lengthy, terminology-dense clinical reports.

**Problem:** Existing medical vision-language pre-training (VLP) frameworks are constrained by limited context windows and shallow representational capacities of lightweight text encoders when processing long, terminology-dense clinical radiology reports. Simply swapping in large medical LLMs introduces gradient conflicts and training instability due to architectural mismatches.

**Method:** SCALPEL introduces a Semantic Cross-modal Alignment via LLM-Powered Encoder Learning approach that freezes a medical LLM as a high-capacity text encoder and adds lightweight learnable adapters to bridge it with the visual encoder. A cross-modal alignment objective aligns anatomy-level visual tokens with LLM-generated dense text representations, preserving the LLM's rich semantic capacity while enabling stable joint training.

**Results:** SCALPEL achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple downstream medical imaging benchmarks including zero-shot and fine-tuned classification and retrieval tasks on chest X-ray and other radiology datasets, outperforming prior medical VLP baselines that rely on smaller text encoders.

**Why it matters:** Directly addresses the reader's interests in CLIP-style vision-language foundation models for medical imaging and chest X-ray analysis; the LLM-powered text encoding strategy is highly relevant to improving radiology report generation and multimodal medical representation learning.


### 2. [HERMES: A Hybrid Ensemble for Head-and-Neck Tumor Segmentation, TN Staging, and Recurrence-Free Survival on PET/CT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26498)
**Authors:** Kai Wang, Meixu Chen, Elie Nasr, Ryan Lanning et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.26498`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Tumor Segmentation, PET/CT

**TL;DR:** HERMES is a hybrid ensemble system for head-and-neck tumor segmentation, T/N staging, and recurrence-free survival prediction from paired FDG-PET/CT scans and EHR data in the HECKTOR 2026 challenge.

**Problem:** Head-and-neck cancer management requires simultaneously solving multiple clinical tasks—primary tumor and lymph node segmentation, radiological staging, and survival prediction—from heterogeneous PET/CT and clinical data, yet most methods address these tasks independently without exploiting their inter-relationships.

**Method:** HERMES combines a hybrid ensemble of specialized segmentation networks (trained on GTVp and GTVn targets with PET/CT fusion) with staging and survival prediction modules that consume both imaging-derived features and structured EHR variables. The ensemble aggregates diverse model predictions through weighted fusion, and survival prediction employs Cox-based models over radiomics and deep features extracted from the segmentation outputs.

**Results:** The system achieves competitive performance across all three HECKTOR 2026 subtasks within a single containerized pipeline, demonstrating that joint modeling of segmentation, staging, and survival is feasible and benefits from shared representations.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to the reader's interest in medical imaging AI and multimodal learning for clinical decision support; the integration of imaging with EHR data for survival prediction illustrates multi-task clinical AI pipelines that complement work on radiology foundation models.


### 3. [BATS: Resource-Efficient Volumetric Segmentation with Boundary-Aware Mixed-Resolution Tokens](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26829)
**Authors:** David Hagerman, Roman Naeem, Fredrik Kahl  
**arXiv:** `2607.26829`  
**Tags:** Medical Image Segmentation, 3D Volumetric Imaging, Efficient Architectures

**TL;DR:** BATS is a boundary-aware mixed-resolution token selection architecture for 3D medical image segmentation that concentrates fine-resolution computation near predicted class boundaries, reducing memory and compute while maintaining accuracy.

**Problem:** High-performing volumetric medical image segmentation models maintain dense multi-scale feature maps throughout the entire volume, leading to prohibitive activation memory and inference cost that limits clinical deployment on standard hardware.

**Method:** BATS uses a dense boundary predictor to identify voxels near class boundaries that require fine-resolution processing, then selectively routes only those tokens through high-resolution feature extraction while using coarse representations elsewhere. This boundary-aware token selection is integrated into a 3D transformer-style backbone that dynamically adjusts resolution allocation per image.

**Results:** BATS achieves segmentation accuracy comparable to dense multi-scale baselines while substantially reducing activation memory and inference time on standard volumetric medical imaging benchmarks, enabling deployment on more constrained hardware.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to the reader's interest in medical imaging and computer vision; efficient 3D segmentation architectures are critical for scaling radiology AI to clinical practice, and the boundary-aware token selection idea connects to referring segmentation and spatial attention mechanisms in vision-language models.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Flow Map Learning via Nongradient Vector Flow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26398)
**Authors:** Mark Goldstein, Anshuk Uppal, Raghav Singhal, Aahlad Puli et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.26398`  
**Tags:** Diffusion Models, Flow Matching, Generative Models

**TL;DR:** Flow Map Learning via Nongradient Vector Flow introduces a new consistency-model-style approach that learns ODE flow maps using nongradient vector fields, enabling efficient few-step sampling without requiring model inverses or auxiliary networks.

**Problem:** Consistency models enable direct flow map learning for fast sampling from diffusion/flow models, but existing approaches require computationally expensive model inverses or auxiliary networks, and standard regression losses can be unstable when the target flow map is discontinuous or poorly conditioned.

**Method:** The authors propose learning flow maps through nongradient vector flow fields, reformulating the consistency training objective so that the learned map satisfies the ODE boundary conditions without needing explicit model inversion. The method introduces a novel training loss derived from the nongradient structure of the velocity field, enabling stable end-to-end training with simple regression objectives.

**Results:** The approach achieves competitive sample quality to existing consistency model baselines while removing the need for model inverses, reducing training complexity, and enabling flexible interpolation between one-step and multi-step inference regimes.

**Why it matters:** Highly relevant to the reader's work on generative models and diffusion-based approaches; improvements to flow map learning and consistency models directly impact image generation quality and sampling efficiency, which are foundational to vision-language and medical image synthesis applications.


### 2. [Amortized Moment Matching for Visual Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26860)
**Authors:** Wenze Liu, Xintao Wang, Pengfei Wan, Xiangyu Yue  
**arXiv:** `2607.26860`  
**Tags:** Diffusion Models, Generative Models, Distribution Matching

**TL;DR:** Amortized Moment Matching proposes using neural networks to learn data moments as distributional training signals for diffusion denoisers, establishing a general polynomial projection framework that explicitly identifies data moments up to order n+1.

**Problem:** Diffusion model training typically optimizes per-sample score matching objectives that do not directly enforce distributional properties of the generated outputs, leading to potential mismatches between the learned distribution and true data moments, particularly in the low-data or few-step sampling regime.

**Method:** The authors cast diffusion denoisers through polynomial projections and show that an nth-degree projection explicitly identifies data moments up to order n+1. They derive an amortized moment matching objective from the tractable affine case and instantiate it as a practical training signal that can be combined with standard denoising objectives, using neural networks to amortize the computation of empirical moments across the training set.

**Results:** Amortized moment matching improves sample quality and distributional fidelity of diffusion models on standard image generation benchmarks, and the polynomial projection framework provides a unified theoretical account of how denoiser parameterization relates to moment recovery.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to the reader's interest in self-supervised and generative modeling; distributional training signals for diffusion models have direct implications for medical image synthesis and augmentation, as well as improving the quality of vision-language generative pipelines.


### 3. [Do Unified Multimodal Models Think in One Space? A Lens Through Cross-Branch Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26411)
**Authors:** Yu Wang, Sharon Li  
**arXiv:** `2607.26411`  
**Tags:** Multimodal Large Language Models, Vision-Language Models, Representation Learning

**TL;DR:** This paper investigates whether the understanding and generation branches of unified multimodal models share a transferable semantic space by using cross-branch activation steering to measure inter-branch representational alignment.

**Problem:** Unified multimodal models (UMMs) aim to integrate visual understanding and generation in a single architecture, but it remains unclear whether these two branches share a common, transferable semantic representation space, or whether they operate in fundamentally disjoint feature spaces despite sharing parameters.

**Method:** The authors introduce a cross-branch steering protocol that extracts activation directions from one branch (e.g., understanding) and injects them into the other (e.g., generation) at matched layers, measuring the downstream behavioral effect on generation quality and understanding accuracy. This causal intervention provides a lens on the degree of representational sharing without requiring external probes or retraining.

**Results:** Experiments reveal that cross-branch steering produces meaningful semantic effects, suggesting partial but not complete representational overlap; the understanding branch tends to encode more transferable semantic directions, while generation-side directions are more entangled with decoding-specific computation.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to the reader's interests in vision-language foundation models like CLIP and multimodal architectures; understanding when and how visual understanding and generation share representations informs the design of unified medical VLP models and radiology report generation systems.
