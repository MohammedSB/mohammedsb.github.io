1. Diagnosis by Composition: Detecting and Localizing Unseen Diseases by Composing Learned Radiological Findings

Kaihua Hou, Wei Liu, Yi Huang, Hao Zhang et al. (13 authors)  ·  2611.05231

Radiology Report GenerationMedical ImagingChest X-ray

TL;DR: A framework for detecting and localizing unseen diseases in radiology by composing learned radiological findings rather than predicting disease labels directly.

Problem: Deep learning models for radiological diagnosis are trained to predict disease labels directly from images, limiting them to diseases seen during training and making decisions difficult to interpret in radiologists' terms. Radiology practice, however, proceeds from visual findings to disease through explicit, interpretable guidelines.

Method: The authors propose a 'diagnosis by composition' approach that first trains a model to recognize low-level radiological findings (e.g., consolidation, effusion), then composes these findings into disease diagnoses using published clinical guidelines. This allows the model to generalize to unseen diseases by combining familiar findings in new ways, mirroring radiologist reasoning.

Results: The composition approach enables detection and localization of diseases not seen during training while producing interpretable, finding-grounded explanations that align with radiological terminology and clinical guidelines.

Why it matters: Directly addresses open-vocabulary disease detection and interpretability in chest X-ray AI, with a compositional vision-language approach that aligns closely with how radiologists report findings — highly relevant to radiology report generation and clinical AI research.

2. GroundSeek-CXR: Retrospective Evaluation-Guided Active Observation Framework for Multi-Label Chest Radiography Diagnosis

Yihang Fu, Xinyu Liu, Qing Xu, Xiaofeng Yang et al. (5 authors)  ·  2611.04286

Chest X-rayMedical ImagingVision-Language Models

TL;DR: An active observation framework for multi-label chest radiography diagnosis that uses retrospective evaluation to guide where the model looks, improving both localization and diagnosis.

Problem: Multi-label chest radiograph diagnosis requires identifying subtle lesions and co-occurring abnormalities across regions, yet existing vision-language models that actively observe local regions are supervised by each observation's immediate effect on diagnosis, failing to capture how each region contributes cumulatively to the final prediction.

Method: GroundSeek-CXR introduces a retrospective evaluation-guided active observation framework that scores each observed region based on its contribution to the final diagnosis rather than its immediate effect, training the model to plan a sequence of observations that collectively support accurate multi-label prediction. The framework integrates region-level visual grounding with language-based diagnosis generation.

Results: The proposed method improves multi-label chest radiograph diagnosis performance and region localization accuracy compared to prior observation-based methods, with better calibration of observation sequences toward diagnostically informative regions.

Why it matters: Combines active visual grounding with multi-label CXR diagnosis — directly relevant to the reader's interests in referring segmentation, radiology report generation, and vision-language models applied to chest X-ray analysis.

3. Improving PI-RADS Prediction and Prostate Cancer Detection from Multiparametric MRI with Anatomical Guidance

Seungbin Park, Thibault Heintz, Rachit Saluja, Francesca Khani et al. (8 authors)  ·  2611.04155

Medical ImagingProstate MRISemantic Segmentation

TL;DR: Adding prostate zonal anatomy masks as guidance improves PI-RADS score prediction and clinically significant prostate cancer detection from multiparametric MRI across multiple architectures.

Problem: Accurate prediction of PI-RADS scores from multiparametric MRI is more challenging than simply detecting clinically significant prostate cancer, partly because PI-RADS scoring explicitly depends on lesion location within prostate zones (peripheral zone vs. transition zone), information that standard detection models do not explicitly encode.

Method: The authors incorporate prostate zonal segmentation masks (peripheral zone, transition zone, whole gland) as anatomical guidance into multiple deep learning architectures trained on multiparametric MRI. They conduct a controlled comparison across architectures to isolate the contribution of anatomical context to PI-RADS prediction and cancer detection.

Results: Incorporating anatomical zonal masks consistently improves PI-RADS prediction accuracy and clinically significant prostate cancer detection across the evaluated architectures, confirming that explicit spatial anatomical guidance provides meaningful benefit beyond image features alone.

Why it matters: Demonstrates how anatomical segmentation masks can be used as structured guidance in medical image analysis — relevant to the reader's interests in referring segmentation, multimodal learning for medical imaging, and structured spatial reasoning in radiology AI.

1. Bridging CLIP with DINO: Cross-Modal Information Maximization for Online Test-Time Adaptation

Taehoon Kim, Wonjun Hwang, Hyunsouk Cho  ·  2611.04489

Vision-Language ModelsTest-Time AdaptationSelf-Supervised Learning

TL;DR: A cross-modal information maximization approach bridges CLIP's visual encoder and DINO's dense features for online test-time adaptation under distribution shift without requiring labeled data.

Problem: CLIP-style vision-language models suffer from 'modality bias' under test-time corruptions: distorted visual features map to incorrect textual anchors with high confidence, and standard entropy minimization reinforces these overconfident errors, creating a confirmation-bias loop that collapses predictions.

Method: The proposed method bridges CLIP with DINO by using DINO's self-supervised dense visual representations as an auxiliary target during test-time adaptation. Cross-modal information maximization aligns corrupted CLIP visual features toward DINO's more robust spatial representations while preventing trivial solutions, enabling adaptation without any labeled test data.

Results: The approach outperforms standard entropy minimization and other TTA baselines on multiple vision benchmarks under various corruption types, demonstrating that cross-modal grounding stabilizes the adaptation loop and prevents confidence collapse.

Why it matters: Directly relevant to CLIP-style foundation model adaptation and self-supervised learning — addresses a key failure mode of VLMs under distribution shift with a method transferable to medical imaging domains where labeled test data is unavailable.

2. Perturb, Repair, Verify: Self-Play Vision-Language Verifiers for Compositional Understanding

Zijun Huang, Yingjun Du, Cees G. M. Snoek  ·  2611.05158

Vision-Language ModelsSelf-Supervised LearningCompositional Reasoning

TL;DR: A self-play training framework teaches vision-language verifiers to judge image-text alignment without external supervision by iteratively perturbing and repairing image-caption pairs.

Problem: Training vision-language verifiers that judge whether an image faithfully matches a textual description typically requires supervision from stronger external models or curated hard-negative data, making the approach teacher-bounded, expensive to construct, and difficult to adapt as model failures evolve.

Method: PROVE (Perturb-and-Repair Optimization for VErification) introduces a self-play loop in which the model generates hard negatives by perturbing images or captions and then attempts to repair them. The verifier is trained against these self-generated failures, iteratively improving without any external teacher or static negative corpus.

Results: PROVE achieves competitive or superior performance to teacher-supervised baselines on compositional visual understanding benchmarks, while remaining adaptive to the model's own evolving failure modes throughout training.

Why it matters: Directly relevant to vision-language model training, compositionality evaluation, and self-supervised approaches — the self-play paradigm has potential application to radiology report evaluation metrics and medical VLM verification.

3. OCR in the Foundation Model Era: From Specialist Models to Unified Models

Zhiheng Li, Zongyang Ma, Jiaxian Chen, Cheng Cui et al. (10 authors)  ·  2611.04874

Foundation ModelsOCRVision-Language Models

TL;DR: A comprehensive survey of OCR in the foundation model era, reviewing the transition from specialist models to unified vision-language systems capable of faithful document transcription.

Problem: Optical character recognition has evolved from narrow specialist models to broad foundation model deployments, but the field lacks a unified review that traces this evolution and characterizes what faithful transcription requires — complete reconstruction of text, notation, and document structure — in modern unified models.

Method: The survey systematically reviews OCR development through the lens of foundation models, categorizing approaches by their transcription scope (character, word, document, structured notation), their architectural evolution from CNN-based recognizers to vision-language models, and their evaluation paradigms. It defines OCR as faithful transcription within a specified visual scope.

Results: The review identifies key capability gaps in modern unified models for tasks like mathematical notation, complex layouts, and multi-page documents, and outlines research directions for grounding-faithful transcription in large VLMs.

Why it matters: Highly relevant to the reader's interests in vision-language foundation models and document understanding — OCR capabilities are foundational to radiology report generation pipelines and multimodal medical record analysis.
