### 3. [Raysemble: Metric 3D Bone Reconstruction from a Single X-ray](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16869)
**Authors:** Xiaoyang Li, Xinghao Yan, Yuan Chai  
**arXiv:** `2611.16869`  
**Tags:** X-ray, 3D Reconstruction, Musculoskeletal Imaging

**TL;DR:** Raysemble reconstructs eight lumbar and pelvic bones in metric 3D from a single X-ray by separating shape prediction from placement.

**Problem:** Recovering metrically accurate 3D bone anatomy from one 2D radiograph is ill-posed, because shape, depth, and scale are entangled in a single projection.

**Method:** Each 3D query samples regional and global image features at its projection, and a shared decoder predicts normalized bone shapes. Predicted depth and camera geometry then set each bone's 3D center and scale, and the shapes are resampled onto a common millimeter grid.

**Results:** Evaluation uses 23 held-out patients; the truncated abstract does not give the numerical outcomes.

**Why it matters:** It shows how projection-aware feature sampling can extract 3D structure from plain radiographs. That is useful background for X-ray representation learning beyond 2D tasks.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17334)
**Authors:** Ellis Brown, Chris Hoang, Shengyi Qian, Shang-Wen Li et al. (12 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.17334`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Vision Encoders, Multimodal LLMs

**TL;DR:** This paper separates the design choices bundled into a multimodal LLM's vision encoder: the pretrained visual prior, dedicated vision capacity, and pre-fusion placement of that capacity.

**Problem:** It is still debated how multimodal language models should encode images. Most use a pretrained vision encoder, while some recent recipes train the encoder from scratch with the language model or remove it entirely.

**Method:** The authors treat a vision encoder as a bundle of three separable choices: a visual prior from upstream pretraining, dedicated vision capacity, and placing that capacity before fusion. The framing implies controlled comparisons that vary each factor independently; the truncated abstract does not detail the experiments.

**Results:** The available abstract excerpt does not state the findings.

**Why it matters:** It bears directly on whether CLIP-style pretrained encoders are still needed in VLMs. That question shapes how you would build medical vision-language models and report generators.


### 2. [ElasticDino: Dense Semantic Features Using Elastic Deformations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16984)
**Authors:** Ulysse Mizrahi, Sean Zaretzky, Dov Danon, Amit H. Bermano  
**arXiv:** `2611.16984`  
**Tags:** Dense Visual Features, Vision Transformers, Feature Upsampling

**TL;DR:** ElasticDino is a lightweight adapter that produces high-resolution dense semantic feature maps from ViT backbones using elastic deformations.

**Problem:** ViTs underpin dense prediction tasks such as segmentation and depth estimation, but the quadratic cost of self-attention severely limits feature resolution and induces spatial artifacts.

**Method:** A lightweight adapter on top of a DINO-style ViT outputs high-resolution feature maps. Per the title, elastic deformations are the core mechanism; the truncated abstract does not explain how they are used.

**Results:** The available abstract excerpt does not report quantitative results.

**Why it matters:** High-resolution dense features from frozen self-supervised backbones could help medical segmentation and referring segmentation, where fine anatomical detail matters.


### 3. [Asymmetric Multimodal Learning with Decomposed JEPAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16639)
**Authors:** Yusuf Shihata  
**arXiv:** `2611.16639`  
**Tags:** JEPA, Multimodal Representation Learning, Visual Question Answering

**TL;DR:** This paper argues that cross-modal alignment as the sole objective is lossy when modalities are informationally asymmetric, and proposes decomposed JEPAs instead.

**Problem:** Alignment is the standard objective for multimodal representations, but it loses information when neither modality can express what the other carries. In VQA, for example, a question is a selective relational operator over a dense visual field.

**Method:** The authors analyze why alignment alone fails under informational asymmetry. Per the title, they propose an asymmetric multimodal learning scheme built on decomposed joint-embedding predictive architectures; the truncated abstract gives no further detail.

**Results:** The available abstract excerpt does not report quantitative results.

**Why it matters:** Image-report pairs are highly asymmetric, since a report describes only part of the image. This critique of CLIP-style alignment and its JEPA alternative applies directly to medical vision-language pretraining.
