1. A Unified Framework for Comprehensive Cardiac CT Segmentation and Phenotyping: Human-in-the-Loop Data Annotation, Vision Foundation Model Development, Multicenter Evaluation and Clinical Validation

Pooya Mohammadi Kazaj, Leo Fridolin Weber, Wen Xie, Seyed Amir Ahmad Safavi-Naini et al. (20 authors)  ·  2607.11287

Medical ImagingVision Foundation ModelsCardiac CT SegmentationSelf-Supervised Learning

TL;DR: A self-supervised vision foundation model with a human-in-the-loop annotation pipeline enables comprehensive, scalable cardiac CT segmentation and phenotyping validated across multiple centers.

Problem: Comprehensive quantification of cardiac structures from CT is limited not by data but by the scalability of measurements, making routine clinical use impractical. Existing pipelines cannot cheaply produce the many structural measurements clinicians need.

Method: The authors combine a human-in-the-loop annotation pipeline, a cardiac-CT-specific augmentation technique, and a self-supervised vision foundation model to segment cardiac structures and derive phenotypes. The framework is developed, then evaluated on multicenter data with explicit clinical validation.

Results: The unified framework delivers comprehensive segmentation and phenotyping across multiple centers and demonstrates clinical validity, moving routine cardiac CT quantification toward practicality.

Why it matters: Directly aligns with the reader's interest in self-supervised learning and foundation models for medical imaging, offering a template for scalable, clinically validated 3D imaging models.

2. Longitudinal Multi-View Breast Cancer Risk Prediction

Solveig Thrun, Zijun Sun, Suaiba A. Salahuddin, Kristoffer Wickstrøm et al. (8 authors)  ·  2607.11343

Breast Cancer Risk PredictionMammographyMulti-View LearningLongitudinal Modeling

TL;DR: A longitudinal multi-view deep learning model jointly aligns temporal and cross-view mammographic information to improve breast cancer risk prediction.

Problem: Accurate risk prediction from screening mammography is critical for personalized screening, but existing methods either align temporally using only a single view or use multiple views without explicit longitudinal alignment.

Method: The authors propose a model that performs explicit temporal alignment across longitudinal exams while simultaneously integrating multiple mammographic views. This joint multi-view, multi-timepoint alignment captures both spatial and temporal signals of developing risk.

Results: The approach improves risk prediction over methods that handle either views or longitudinal alignment in isolation, supporting more personalized screening intervals and earlier detection.

Why it matters: Combines multimodal/multi-view learning with medical imaging for a clinically impactful screening task, matching the reader's imaging and multimodal interests.

3. LaGuadia: Language-Guided Adaptive Distillation from Pathology Foundation Models

Gangsu Kim, Won-Ki Jeong  ·  2607.11257

HistopathologyPathology Foundation ModelsKnowledge DistillationVision-Language Models

TL;DR: LaGuadia uses language-guided, tissue-adaptive weighting to distill multiple heavy pathology foundation models into an efficient student for whole-slide image representation.

Problem: Pathology foundation models give powerful whole-slide representations but at massive computational cost, and existing multi-teacher distillation uses uniform weighting that ignores tissue heterogeneity.

Method: LaGuadia is a language-guided adaptive distillation framework that adaptively weights multiple teacher foundation models according to tissue context rather than using uniform weights. Language guidance informs which teachers to trust for different tissue regions during distillation.

Results: The method produces efficient student models that better account for tissue heterogeneity than uniform multi-teacher distillation, improving WSI representation quality at reduced cost.

Why it matters: Ties together foundation models, vision-language guidance, and efficient distillation in medical imaging — squarely within the reader's core research themes.

1. SynCLIP: Synonym-Coherent Language-Image Pretraining for Robust Open-Vocabulary Dense Perception

Mingjie Xie, Guangjun He, Dongli Xu, Youtian Lin et al. (8 authors)  ·  2607.11008

Vision-Language ModelsCLIPOpen-Vocabulary SegmentationContrastive Pretraining

TL;DR: SynCLIP enforces synonym-coherent language-image pretraining so that semantically equivalent phrases produce consistent spatial attention for robust open-vocabulary dense perception.

Problem: CLIP-based open-vocabulary dense perception suffers from synonym-induced grounding inconsistency, where semantically equivalent text expressions yield different spatial attention maps, undermining robustness.

Method: SynCLIP introduces a synonym-coherent language-image pretraining objective that encourages consistent grounding across synonymous expressions. This aligns spatial attention patterns for equivalent phrases, improving dense localization of unseen categories.

Results: The approach yields more robust and consistent open-vocabulary dense perception than prior CLIP-based methods, reducing sensitivity to phrasing.

Why it matters: Directly advances CLIP-style vision-language foundation models and open-vocabulary segmentation, central to the reader's work on CLIP/SLIP/MaskCLIP-style models.

2. FoundationGeo: Learning Spatial Pixel-Wise Fields for Monocular Metric Geometry

Muxin Liu, Xiaoyang Lyu, Tianhe Ren, Peng Dai et al. (10 authors)  ·  2607.11588

Monocular GeometryVision Foundation ModelsMetric Depth EstimationDINOv3

TL;DR: FoundationGeo bridges relative and metric geometry prediction via spatial calibration and a two-stage design, producing sharp, metric, cross-domain monocular geometry.

Problem: Monocular metric geometry estimation must reconcile relative (affine-invariant) and absolute metric prediction, which existing methods handle poorly, sacrificing either sharpness or metric accuracy.

Method: FoundationGeo is a two-stage framework: Stage 1 learns an affine-invariant geometry model initialized from DINOv3 on a curated 10.2M-sample multi-domain corpus with local-detail supervision; a second stage adds spatial calibration to recover metric scale with principled data design.

Results: It yields sharp boundaries, strong cross-domain generalization, and metric-scale predictions, bridging relative and metric geometry in a unified model.

Why it matters: A large-scale self-supervised/foundation-model approach to a core CV problem, relevant to the reader's interest in foundation models and representation learning.

3. Domain-Aware Scaling Laws Uncover Data Synergy

Kimia Hamidieh, Lester Mackey, David Alvarez-Melis  ·  2607.11052

Scaling LawsData MixturesTraining TechniquesLanguage Models

TL;DR: Domain-aware scaling laws quantify cross-domain data synergy and interference, explaining when mixing datasets (e.g., code helping math) helps or hurts.

Problem: ML progress is usually attributed to scaling model and data size, yet data composition matters just as much and cross-domain interactions (synergy or interference) are poorly characterized.

Method: The authors develop domain-aware scaling laws that model how combining data from different domains interacts, capturing nontrivial synergy and interference effects analytically. The framework predicts the effect of specific mixtures on downstream performance.

Results: The laws uncover measurable data synergy (e.g., code improving mathematical reasoning) and interference, giving a principled account of how mixture composition shapes model performance.

Why it matters: Foundational insight for training multimodal and foundation models where data-mixture decisions are decisive, relevant to the reader's model-building work.
