## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [BioLoom: Scaling Biomedical Vision-Language Models by Learning from the Structure of Scientific Articles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33151)
**Authors:** Yuwei Yang, Xiaoxiao Sun, Alejandro Lozano, Yuan-Sen Ting et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.33151`  
**Tags:** Biomedical Vision-Language Models, Contrastive Pretraining, Scientific Literature Mining

**TL;DR:** BioLoom improves biomedical CLIP-style pretraining by using how scientific articles are structured as an extra source of supervision, instead of relying only on more image-text pairs.

**Problem:** Biomedical vision-language models have mostly improved by scaling up image-text datasets. Most pipelines treat figure-caption pairs as isolated samples and ignore the structure of the articles they come from.

**Method:** The authors propose that the way papers are organized can serve as a training signal. This includes how figures relate to captions, to sub-panels, to in-text references and to the surrounding sections. BioLoom builds this structure into contrastive image-text pretraining so the model learns richer representations from existing literature data.

**Results:** The abstract is cut off before the numbers. The paper presents structure-aware pretraining as a way to scale biomedical VLMs that adds to, rather than replaces, data scaling.

**Why it matters:** This is directly about CLIP-style biomedical foundation models. The idea of supervision from document structure could carry over to radiology, where reports have sections, findings link to impressions and images belong to studies.


### 2. [Reliable and grounded reporting of any computed tomography scan with FORA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32823)
**Authors:** Sezgin Er, Utku Turkbey, Alperen Gur, Omer Faruk Durugol et al. (24 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.32823`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, CT Foundation Models, Grounded Localization

**TL;DR:** FORA is a general-purpose CT model that recognizes and spatially localizes 1,028 hierarchically organized radiological concepts to produce reliable, grounded reports for any CT scan.

**Problem:** Most CT AI systems are limited to particular body regions, exam types or a predefined list of abnormalities. They cannot give a comprehensive interpretation that is grounded in specific locations on the scan.

**Method:** FORA detects and localizes a large hierarchical vocabulary of radiological concepts across CT exams. It then uses these grounded concepts as the basis for full report generation, so each statement in the report can be traced back to image regions.

**Results:** The abstract is cut off before quantitative results. It describes broad coverage: 1,028 concepts across arbitrary CT scans, with outputs that are both reliable and spatially grounded.

**Why it matters:** This brings together radiology report generation and grounding/referring segmentation. Concept-level grounding also offers a natural basis for evaluating factual accuracy in generated reports.


### 3. [JECT: Addressing the Context Shortcut in Joint-Embedding Predictive Learning for Chest CT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31849)
**Authors:** Hanbin Ko, Hong-Jun Choi, Jihun Kim, Young-Gon Kim et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.31849`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, JEPA, Chest CT

**TL;DR:** JECT identifies a 'context shortcut' in joint-embedding predictive architectures for chest CT, where small findings that can't be inferred from surrounding anatomy get predicted as normal, and addresses it.

**Problem:** JEPA-style models learn by predicting the features of masked regions from the visible context. In chest CT, the findings that decide a diagnosis, such as small nodules, often can't be predicted from surrounding anatomy. The loss therefore stops rewarding the encoder for representing them.

**Method:** The authors formalize this failure mode as the context shortcut: when a finding is hidden, the best prediction is normal anatomy. They then change the JEPA training objective for chest CT so the encoder is pushed to represent local pathology rather than only anatomical regularities.

**Results:** The abstract is cut off before results. The paper reports that addressing the shortcut gives representations that better capture diagnostically relevant findings.

**Why it matters:** This shows a basic mismatch between masked-prediction SSL and medical imaging, where abnormalities are sparse and unpredictable by nature. It is important for anyone designing SSL pretraining for CT or chest X-ray.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Text Is Just Another Shot in Vision-Language Models: Simple Probes on Frozen Encoders Do It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32672)
**Authors:** Reda Bensaid, Ismail Ben Ayed, François Leduc-Primeau, Vincent Gripon  
**arXiv:** `2611.32672`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Few-Shot Adaptation, CLIP

**TL;DR:** Simple probes on frozen VLM encoders, which treat a class's text embedding as roughly equal to a few labeled image shots, match increasingly elaborate CLIP few-shot adaptation methods.

**Problem:** Few-shot CLIP adaptation methods share one assumption: the zero-shot text classifier is the anchor, and labeled images only correct it. This has produced more and more complex methods.

**Method:** The authors give a Bayesian interpretation in which a class name is worth exactly kappa image shots. Text becomes just another sample, not a privileged prior. They argue that the anchor principle reflects properties of CLIP's visual features rather than of the task itself, and they propose simple linear probes on frozen encoders.

**Results:** Simple probes are reported to perform on par with elaborate few-shot adaptation methods across vision-language encoders. Full numbers are cut off in the abstract.

**Why it matters:** This is a strong baseline and a useful conceptual reframing for CLIP adaptation. It applies directly to few-shot transfer of medical CLIP models, where labeled images are scarce.


### 2. [Beyond Image-Text Pairs: Aligning Semantic Changes for Fine-Grained Visual Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32817)
**Authors:** Yujie Sun, Zeqing Wang, Shuang Liang, Zhenlin Zhao et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.32817`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Alignment, Fine-Grained Understanding, Contrastive Post-Training

**TL;DR:** A post-training method teaches VLMs to match semantic changes across modalities, using controlled visual and textual variations, to improve fine-grained understanding.

**Problem:** Standard image-text alignment trains on static pairs. It gives little supervision on how a specific change in an image corresponds to a specific change in its text, which limits fine-grained visual understanding.

**Method:** The authors build controlled variations of images and captions and train the model to align the difference between two images with the difference between their captions. This goes beyond aligning each image with its caption on its own.

**Results:** The abstract reports improved fine-grained visual understanding over standard pair-based alignment. Exact numbers are cut off.

**Why it matters:** Fine-grained, change-aware alignment matters for medical VLMs: subtle findings, laterality, severity changes and comparisons with prior studies are all hard for pair-level CLIP training.


### 3. [Subliminal Learning Crosses Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31870)
**Authors:** Saraswathy Amjith, Kimia Hamidieh, Antonio Torralba, Yoon Kim  
**arXiv:** `2611.31870`  
**Tags:** Subliminal Learning, Multimodal Distillation, AI Safety

**TL;DR:** Subliminal learning, where hidden traits pass between models through training data that looks meaningless, also works across modalities, including from text into vision models.

**Problem:** Earlier work showed that a language model's traits, such as a preference for cats, can be distilled into another model through training data that seems unrelated, like number sequences. Whether this also crosses modality boundaries was unknown.

**Method:** The authors set up teacher-student distillation across modalities. A topic or preference is induced in one model, and the student is trained on data that looks unrelated in a different modality. They then measure whether the trait is transferred.

**Results:** Text-based subliminal learning is shown to transfer cross-modally: preferences such as particular animals carry over through data that appears semantically empty.

**Why it matters:** This matters for anyone distilling or synthetically training multimodal foundation models, medical ones included. Unwanted biases could spread through training data that looks harmless.
