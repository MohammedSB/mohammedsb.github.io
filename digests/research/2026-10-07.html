1. Knee3DVLM: Dual-Sequence Full-Volume Vision-Language Modeling for Comprehensive Knee MRI Assessment

Maryam Baizhigitova, Andrew Seohwan Yu, Po-Hao Chen, Naveen Subhas et al. (10 authors)  ·  2610.08482

Vision-Language Models3D Medical ImagingKnee MRI

TL;DR: Knee3DVLM is a vision-language model that reads two whole knee MRI volumes together, DESS and fluid-sensitive TSE, to predict 57 diagnostic findings tied to specific anatomy.

Problem: Vision-language models for 3D medical imaging are rarely applied to knee MRI. Radiologists read several complementary MRI sequences together, and existing models do not handle that multi-sequence reading well.

Method: The model encodes full-volume DESS and fluid-sensitive TSE MRI separately for each sequence and fuses them in a language-model framework. It predicts 57 binary diagnostic targets, each tied to a specific anatomical structure. The targets are derived from clinical radiology reports, so it supervises a 3D VLM with labels mined from reports.

Results: The abstract excerpt is cut off before the results. It frames this as broad, anatomically resolved knee assessment, which suggests better performance than single-sequence or 2D baselines, but no numbers are given.

Why it matters: It moves report-supervised vision-language modeling from 2D chest X-rays to multi-sequence 3D MRI. Its report-derived, anatomy-resolved labels are a useful template for grounded radiology report generation and for evaluating it.

2. Incidental information contaminates patient notes and disrupts clinical reasoning in large language models

Krithik Vishwanath, Brandon Ye, Anton Alyakin, John E. Markert et al. (7 authors)  ·  2610.08585

Clinical Note GenerationLLM EvaluationAmbient Documentation

TL;DR: Frontier LLMs used for ambient scribing copy small talk and other incidental content into clinical notes, and that content also degrades their downstream clinical reasoning.

Problem: LLMs are increasingly used both to draft notes from patient–clinician conversations and to support clinical reasoning. Both uses can be hurt by information that is incidental to the medical encounter.

Method: The authors use 576 patient–clinician dialogues to measure how often frontier models insert non-clinical small-talk exchanges into the notes they generate. They then test how this contamination affects downstream clinical reasoning tasks.

Results: Frontier models put small talk into 35% of generated notes. The abstract also reports measurable disruption of clinical reasoning when incidental information is present.

Why it matters: This is a failure mode in generated clinical documentation that standard metrics would miss. It is directly relevant to designing evaluation metrics for generated reports, since content fidelity and relevance need to be scored separately from fluency.

3. MedCORE: Criteria-Grounded Clinical Reasoning for Interpretable Medical Image Diagnosis

Asim Khan, Samee Ullah Khan, Dwarikanath Mahapatra  ·  2610.08528

Interpretable DiagnosisClinical ReasoningMedical Image Classification

TL;DR: MedCORE makes medical image models reason through the morphological and textural criteria that clinicians check, instead of mapping image features straight to a disease label.

Problem: Most deep diagnostic models skip the structured criteria-based reasoning clinicians use. That makes their decisions opaque and potentially unsafe to deploy.

Method: MedCORE (Medical Criteria-Oriented REasoning) explicitly checks clinically defined morphological and textural criteria as an intermediate step. It then grounds the final diagnosis in those criteria, giving an interpretable chain from evidence to diagnosis.

Results: The abstract excerpt is truncated. It frames MedCORE as more transparent while staying competitive on diagnostic performance, but gives no numbers.

Why it matters: Criteria-grounded reasoning connects interpretable classification with structured report generation. It also suggests ways to check whether generated findings are supported by visual evidence.

1. Unlocking Fine-Grained Perception in CLIP via Structurally-Aware Latent Masked Modeling

Juntong Li, Lingwei Dang, Haomin Wu, Ziyan Qiu et al. (6 authors)  ·  2610.07689

CLIPMasked Image ModelingDense Prediction

TL;DR: The paper adds structurally-aware latent masked modeling to CLIP so it gains fine-grained, dense perception without losing its global image-text alignment.

Problem: CLIP aligns global semantics well but lacks fine-grained spatial perception. That hurts dense prediction tasks and limits the visual side of multimodal LLMs. Earlier fixes that distill geometric priors from vision-centric models have structural shortcomings.

Method: The authors train CLIP with a masked-modeling objective in latent space that is aware of image structure. The visual encoder learns to reconstruct structured latent targets for masked regions, which improves local features while preserving language alignment.

Results: Per the abstract, it improves fine-grained and dense prediction tasks and the visual ability of downstream MLLMs compared with CLIP baselines and prior geometric-prior methods. The truncated abstract gives no numbers.

Why it matters: This continues the MaskCLIP/SLIP line of combining masked modeling with contrastive pretraining. It applies directly to medical CLIP models used for referring segmentation and region-grounded report generation.

2. GeoPID: Decomposing and Steering Visual Information in Vision-Language Models

Seulgi Kim, Zhixiong Zhang, Xinwei Zhang, Jie Ling et al. (5 authors)  ·  2610.08401

Vision-Language ModelsInterpretabilityModality Bias

TL;DR: GeoPID is a training-free method that splits the information inside a VLM into redundant, modality-unique and synergistic parts, then steers the model toward using visual information more.

Problem: VLMs tend to under-use visual information and over-rely on textual context, which leads to hallucination and weak visual grounding.

Method: GeoPID applies a geometric form of partial information decomposition to internal representations. It splits multimodal information into Redundant, Modality-Unique and Synergistic components and uses that decomposition to amplify visual contributions at inference time, with no training.

Results: According to the abstract, steering with GeoPID improves visual reliance and task performance across VLM benchmarks. The excerpt gives no numbers.

Why it matters: Medical VLMs that over-rely on text are a known source of hallucinated findings in generated reports. A training-free diagnostic and steering tool could be applied to radiology VLMs.

3. Foveated Compression: Selective High-Resolution Preservation for Token-Efficient VLMs

Donghyun Han, Jangho Park, Yuseok Bae  ·  2610.07729

Vision-Language ModelsVisual Token CompressionEfficient Inference

TL;DR: Foveated Compression encodes an image once at full resolution, then keeps native-resolution tokens only in selected regions and compresses the rest, to stay within a fixed visual-token budget.

Problem: Visual tokens dominate VLM inference cost, and simple downsampling is a surprisingly strong compression baseline. The open question is where to keep visual detail under a fixed token budget.

Method: The image is encoded once at full resolution and represented as a mixture of native and compressed tokens. High-resolution tokens are kept selectively in important regions, like foveated vision.

Results: The abstract reports better accuracy than downsampling and token-pruning baselines at matched token budgets. The truncated excerpt gives no numbers.

Why it matters: High-resolution medical images such as chest X-rays and pathology slides need small findings kept intact under compute limits. Foveated token allocation is a practical design option for medical MLLMs.
