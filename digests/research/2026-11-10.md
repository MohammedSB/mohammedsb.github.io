## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Evaluating Cross-Signal Coherence in Mammography Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12574)
**Authors:** Rebeca Nam, Chase M. Fensore, Christian A. Fauria-Robinson, Judy Gichoya et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.12574`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Mammography, Model Evaluation

**TL;DR:** Evaluates whether medical VLMs' answers, stated confidence, and written justifications actually agree with each other on mammography tasks, rather than scoring accuracy alone.

**Problem:** As VLMs interpret medical images directly, their outputs include a label, a confidence, and a free-text explanation, yet the field evaluates them almost exclusively by accuracy, leaving the internal consistency of these three signals unexamined.

**Method:** The authors run four VLMs on breast density and BI-RADS categorization across two mammography datasets, eliciting answer, confidence, and explanation for each case. They then define and measure cross-signal coherence, checking whether confidence tracks correctness and whether the stated justification supports the chosen label.

**Results:** The study reports systematic incoherence between the three output signals across models, indicating that accurate answers can be paired with unreliable confidence or explanations that do not support them.

**Why it matters:** This is directly in your lane on evaluation of generative radiology outputs: it argues for metrics that go beyond label accuracy to the faithfulness of explanations and confidence, which also applies to report generation.


### 2. [Answer or Defer? Testing VLMs' Stated Confidence on Brain MRI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11833)
**Authors:** Amir Sabbaghziarani, Parsa Ghazvinian, Sepehr Salem, Sergey Plis  
**arXiv:** `2611.11833`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Brain MRI, Selective Prediction

**TL;DR:** Tests whether thresholding VLMs' stated confidence on brain MRI can safely decide which cases to accept and which to defer to a clinician.

**Problem:** Deploying VLMs for medical image interpretation requires a policy for accepting answers versus referring images, but it is unknown whether the models' own stated confidence is reliable enough to drive that decision, or how it depends on case population.

**Method:** Eight open-weight VLMs, including two reasoning models, assess brain MRI cases and report a confidence score. The authors sweep confidence thresholds to measure retained coverage against error rate among accepted answers, and compare behavior across different case populations.

**Results:** The work characterizes the coverage-versus-error trade-off achievable through stated confidence and shows that its usefulness is population dependent, cautioning against naive confidence thresholds for clinical deferral.

**Why it matters:** Pairs with the mammography coherence paper as a concrete study of VLM calibration in radiology; useful if you build or evaluate deferral mechanisms for generative radiology systems.


### 3. [Weight-Space Programming for Medical Imaging: Do We Still Need Training Data?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12066)
**Authors:** Maximilian Lindholz, Richard Ruppel, Annette Milnik, Sebastian Halskov et al. (14 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.12066`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, LLM Weight Generation, Data-Free Learning

**TL;DR:** Asks whether an LLM can directly write the weights of a medical image classifier from medical knowledge alone, with no training images.

**Problem:** Medical image classifiers need labelled images that are costly or unavailable; the paper probes whether an LLM's encoded medical knowledge can substitute for training data by programming classifier parameters directly.

**Method:** In weight-space programming, an LLM writes or edits the weights and biases of a neural network without gradient optimization, guided only by its knowledge of the task. The authors evaluate the resulting classifiers on medical imaging tasks and compare against conventionally trained baselines.

**Results:** The abstract frames this as an investigation of feasibility; the key outcome is a characterization of when LLM-written weights yield usable medical classifiers and where they fall short of data-driven training.

**Why it matters:** A provocative data-free angle on medical imaging that touches your interests in foundation models and label-scarce learning, worth reading for the methodology even if performance is limited.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [dNaViT: Discrete Codes are General-Purpose Visual Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12426)
**Authors:** Xiaotong Li, Zhengrong Yue, Xu Huang, Jialv Zou et al. (12 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.12426`  
**Tags:** Visual Tokenization, Discrete Representation Learning, Foundation Models

**TL;DR:** dNaViT shows that a finite discrete code vocabulary can serve as a general-purpose visual representation, unifying vision with the next-token paradigm of language models.

**Problem:** Discrete autoregressive modeling underpins language models, but it is unclear how far a finite discrete vocabulary can go in capturing the richness of visual content well enough to serve downstream vision tasks.

**Method:** The authors build a discrete native-resolution vision transformer that maps images into discrete codes and study the representational capacity of those codes across tasks. They analyze what information the vocabulary retains and how discrete codes compare to continuous features.

**Results:** Discrete codes are reported to function as competitive general-purpose visual representations, supporting the case for a unified discrete framework across vision and language.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to vision-language foundation models for radiology, where a shared discrete token space could simplify joint image-report modeling and generation.


### 2. [Training Vision Transformers to Focus: Emergence of Spatial Attention Head Specialization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11890)
**Authors:** Yuji Nozawa, Yu-Chieh Lin, Youyang Ng  
**arXiv:** `2611.11890`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Vision Transformers, Attention Specialization

**TL;DR:** Introduces two visual focusing losses that make self-supervised ViTs like DINO develop spatially specialized attention heads and less ambiguous representations.

**Problem:** Self-supervised ViTs such as DINO learn strong features without labels, but they lack a focusing mechanism during training, so their perceptual representations are often ambiguous or overly similar across heads.

**Method:** The authors add two focusing loss functions to self-supervised training that encourage attention heads to concentrate on distinct spatial regions. They study the emergence of spatial attention head specialization and its effect on downstream representation quality.

**Results:** The focusing losses are reported to yield heads with emergent spatial specialization and improved perceptual representations relative to the unmodified DINO objective.

**Why it matters:** Directly applicable to your self-supervised medical imaging work; sharper attention specialization could benefit localization-heavy tasks like referring segmentation and chest X-ray finding localization.


### 3. [Beyond Modality Preference: Controlling Multimodal Arbitration under Modality Conflict in MLLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12883)
**Authors:** Zhaoying Pan, Shenyu Lu, Jun Hui Wong, Junyi Chai et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.12883`  
**Tags:** Multimodal LLMs, Mechanistic Interpretability, Modality Conflict

**TL;DR:** Identifies a small set of 'arbitration heads' in MLLMs that causally control which modality wins under conflicting inputs, enabling inference-time control.

**Problem:** MLLMs become unreliable when their input modalities disagree, often favoring a modality that is not the most trustworthy for the task, and there has been no mechanism to steer that arbitration at inference time.

**Method:** The authors locate attention heads whose activity causally influences whether the model trusts the visual or textual input under conflict. They then intervene on these heads at inference to control modality arbitration without retraining.

**Results:** Targeted intervention on arbitration heads is reported to shift the model toward the more reliable modality under conflict, improving robustness.

**Why it matters:** Modality conflict is central in radiology VLMs, where a clinical prompt may contradict the image; controllable arbitration is a practical lever for safer report generation.
