1. An end-to-end-trained vision-language model for native-language prostate pathology report generation

Christian Grashei, Fabian Gülhan, Maximilian Legnar, Fabian Stögbauer et al. (7 authors)  ·  2608.23143

Computational PathologyReport GenerationVision-Language Models

TL;DR: An end-to-end-trained slide-level vision-language model that generates structured prostate biopsy pathology reports directly in a non-English (German) clinical language.

Problem: Structured per-core reporting of prostate biopsies is a major workload for pathologists, and existing AI tools frame the task as classification, leaving report assembly to humans. Most slide-level vision-language models also depend on English-centric text encoders that transfer poorly to other clinical languages.

Method: The authors build a slide-level framework that couples a whole-slide image encoder with a language decoder and trains the whole pipeline end-to-end for report generation rather than stitching together frozen classification heads. Crucially, the language side is native to the target clinical language instead of relying on English-pretrained encoders plus translation, so the generated text matches the reporting conventions pathologists actually use.

Results: The model produces coherent, structured per-core prostate pathology reports in the native clinical language, outperforming English-centric slide-level VLM baselines transferred to that language.

Why it matters: This is directly the radiology-report-generation problem transposed to pathology: end-to-end multimodal training, slide/image-to-structured-text generation, and the non-English clinical language issue that most report-generation evaluation work ignores.

2. A Source-Grounded Framework for Constructing and Evaluating Progressive Multimodal Diagnostic Dialogues from Clinical Case Reports

Yufan Wang, Rui Yang, Yi Liu, Yi Lin et al. (5 authors)  ·  2608.22713

Multimodal Medical BenchmarksDiagnostic DialogueClinical Evaluation

TL;DR: A source-grounded framework that converts published clinical case reports into progressive multimodal diagnostic dialogues, separating evidence selection from evidence interpretation in evaluation.

Problem: Real diagnosis unfolds sequentially — history, exam, labs, images, confirmatory tests — but most multimodal medical benchmarks give models fixed inputs and score only the endpoint answer. Fully interactive agent benchmarks, meanwhile, conflate whether an agent chose the right evidence with whether it read that evidence correctly.

Method: The authors build a pipeline that mines clinical case reports and restructures them into staged, source-grounded diagnostic dialogues in which each turn releases a new slice of evidence (including medical images) traceable back to the originating text. Because every revealed item is anchored to the source case, the framework can score evidence-acquisition decisions and evidence-interpretation accuracy as separate axes rather than as one blended success metric.

Results: The resulting benchmark exposes model failures that endpoint-only evaluation hides, showing that strong final-diagnosis accuracy can coexist with poor evidence-seeking behavior.

Why it matters: For anyone building evaluation metrics for generative clinical outputs, the decoupling of "did it gather the right evidence" from "did it interpret it correctly" is a transferable design principle — and the source-grounding trick makes the labels auditable.

3. AnaDiffusion: Anatomically CompositionalLatent Diffusion for Controllable 3D Brain MRI Generation

Huiwen Han, Lulin Liu, Bangya Liu, Yuanhao Cai et al. (11 authors)  ·  2608.23014

Diffusion ModelsBrain MRIControllable Medical Image Synthesis

TL;DR: AnaDiffusion is an anatomically compositional latent diffusion model that generates 3D brain MRI region-by-region for local, anatomy-aware controllability.

Problem: Existing 3D brain MRI generative models synthesize whole volumes monolithically, so they neither respect regional anatomical structure nor allow a user to control one anatomical region independently of the rest.

Method: AnaDiffusion decomposes the volume into anatomical components and runs latent diffusion in a compositional fashion, conditioning generation on per-region anatomical structure rather than a single global latent. This lets the model be steered at the level of individual anatomical regions while keeping the assembled volume globally coherent, supporting controllable counterfactual-style anatomical edits in 3D.

Results: The compositional formulation improves anatomical fidelity and gives substantially finer local controllability than monolithic 3D latent diffusion baselines.

Why it matters: Controllable, anatomy-grounded 3D medical image synthesis is a practical source of augmentation and counterfactual data for self-supervised and multimodal medical imaging pipelines, and the compositional conditioning idea generalizes to CT and CXR.

1. EchoWM: Open and Enterable Omnimodal World Models

Songchun Zhang, Yaowei Li, Junhao Zhuang, Weiyang Jin et al. (22 authors)  ·  2608.23189

World ModelsOmnimodal GenerationVideo Generation

TL;DR: EchoWM is an open omnimodal world model that responds to continuous camera navigation while jointly generating 720p video, environmental sound, music, and speech.

Problem: World models are mostly evaluated as video predictors, and interactive "enterable" media requires jointly generating a coherent visual stream and its full audio counterpart under continuous user control — a combination existing systems don't deliver.

Method: EchoWM organizes interaction around camera intent: in first-person scenes the camera specifies observer motion, while in third-person scenes camera–character dynamics are learned directly from data without hand-built view-specific controllers. On top of this control interface, the model jointly generates 720p video plus environmental sound, music, and speech, so the audio and visual channels are produced by one omnimodal system rather than stitched post hoc.

Results: The system supports real-time-style navigation with jointly generated 720p audio-visual output, and unifies first- and third-person control under a single learned camera-intent formulation.

Why it matters: A strong reference point for multimodal generative architectures where several output modalities must stay mutually consistent under a shared conditioning signal — the same structural problem as image-plus-text clinical generation.

2. Sigmoid Attention as a Better Substrate for Learned KV Cache Eviction

Isaac, Li  ·  2608.23296

Attention MechanismsKV Cache CompressionEfficient Inference

TL;DR: A controlled study showing that sigmoid attention, rather than softmax, is a better substrate for learned KV-cache eviction because it avoids the soft-to-hard training/inference mismatch.

Problem: Learned KV-cache eviction trains differentiable gates that merely attenuate token contributions, but inference only saves memory when KV entries are physically deleted. This soft-to-hard gap means training-time behavior systematically misrepresents deployment behavior.

Method: The authors run a controlled 2×2×2 comparison on GPT-2-scale Transformers trained on OpenWebText, varying the attention substrate (softmax vs. sigmoid) alongside the gating and eviction choices. The key insight is that softmax's normalization redistributes mass when an entry is removed, so attenuating a token is not equivalent to deleting it; sigmoid attention has no cross-token normalization, so soft gating and hard eviction coincide much more closely.

Results: Sigmoid attention shrinks the soft-to-hard degradation compared to softmax under matched gating and memory budgets, making learned eviction policies transfer far more faithfully from training to inference.

Why it matters: A clean example of diagnosing a train/test mismatch at the level of architectural primitives — and directly useful if you run long-context multimodal models where KV memory is the binding constraint.

3. A Physical Response-and-Memory Model for Muon Optimization

Yinze Hu, Hongjun Xiang, Xingao Gong, Hongyu Yu  ·  2608.22994

OptimizationLLM TrainingMuon Optimizer

TL;DR: A physical response-and-memory model that explains why Muon's momentum semi-orthogonalization works and derives principled improvements to the update rule.

Problem: Optimizers from SGD and AdamW to Muon have largely been shaped by engineering intuition and then selected on benchmarks, leaving no principled account of why a given gradient-to-update transformation reaches lower loss for the same compute.

Method: The authors recast the optimizer as a physical system with a response function and a memory kernel, then show that Muon's semi-orthogonalization of the momentum matrix is a particular choice within this family. Framing the update rule this way makes the design space explicit and lets them derive modifications to the response and memory terms rather than tuning them by trial and error.

Results: The framework reproduces Muon's behavior as a special case and yields derived variants that improve on it under matched compute budgets for LLM pretraining.

Why it matters: Muon-family optimizers are becoming the default for large-scale pretraining, and a theory that predicts which update rules help is more durable than another benchmark table when you are choosing a training recipe.
