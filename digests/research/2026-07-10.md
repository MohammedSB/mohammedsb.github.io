## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [CT-CLIP Representations for Multimodal Lung Cancer Survival Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08503)
**Authors:** Sofie Allgöwer, Mikael Johansson, Andreas Hallqvist, Jonas Andersson et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.08503`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Foundation Models, Chest/Chest-CT Imaging, Survival Prediction

**TL;DR:** Representations from the CT-CLIP chest-CT foundation model are repurposed for multimodal lung cancer survival prediction in data-scarce clinical cohorts.

**Problem:** Deep-learning survival modeling in lung cancer is limited by the scarcity of curated imaging cohorts with reliable outcome labels. The authors ask whether a domain-specific vision-language foundation model can supply useful features under these constraints.

**Method:** They extract features from CT-CLIP (a CLIP-style chest-CT foundation model) and use them, alone and fused with clinical/other modalities, for prognosis prediction. The study evaluates transferability of frozen foundation-model embeddings to survival modeling in small, real clinical settings.

**Results:** CT-CLIP-derived representations enable competitive multimodal survival prediction despite limited training data, showing the value of domain-specific foundation features for prognosis.

**Why it matters:** Directly at the intersection of CLIP-style vision-language foundation models and chest imaging — exactly the reader's core interests — and demonstrates transfer of SSL/foundation features to a clinical downstream task.


### 2. [Attribute Retrieving for Open-Vocabulary Endoscopic Compositional Referring Segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08397)
**Authors:** Shun Liu, Nan Xi, Yang Liu, Tianyu Luan et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.08397`  
**Tags:** Referring Segmentation, Vision-Language Models, Endoscopy

**TL;DR:** An attribute-retrieval framework extends open-vocabulary compositional referring image segmentation to endoscopic imagery.

**Problem:** Referring image segmentation (segment regions specified by language) is hard to extend to endoscopy due to scarce high-quality annotations and complex, domain-specific image-text relationships. Existing VLM-based RIS methods transfer poorly to this domain.

**Method:** The authors propose an attribute-retrieving approach for open-vocabulary compositional referring segmentation, leveraging vision-language models to bridge the domain gap. Compositional attributes are retrieved to ground language expressions to endoscopic regions with limited supervision.

**Results:** The method improves open-vocabulary referring segmentation performance on endoscopic imagery over prior VLM/RIS baselines.

**Why it matters:** Referring segmentation and vision-language models are named interests, applied here to a medical imaging modality — a tight match to the reader's research.


### 3. [ProsMAE: Multi-Source MAE Pretraining for ISUP Grade Classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08162)
**Authors:** Anna Jung, Kyeonghun Kim, Youngung Han, Eunseob Choi et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.08162`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Histopathology, Masked Autoencoders

**TL;DR:** ProsMAE is a multi-source masked-autoencoder pretraining framework for prostate whole-slide-image representation learning and ISUP grade classification.

**Problem:** Gigapixel WSIs suffer from stain variation, scanner differences, artifacts, and limited expert annotation, making robust computational-pathology model training difficult. Self-supervised pretraining is needed to learn transferable representations.

**Method:** ProsMAE trains a masked autoencoder on tiles drawn from multiple prostate-cancer data sources to build robust histopathology representations. The learned encoder is then applied to ISUP grade classification.

**Results:** Multi-source MAE pretraining yields improved, more robust representations for ISUP grading versus baselines under stain/scanner variability.

**Why it matters:** Combines self-supervised learning (MAE) with medical imaging (pathology) — both central interests — and shows an SSL recipe robust to domain shift in a clinical setting.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Hidden Decoding at Scale: Latent Computation Scaling for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08186)
**Authors:** Aiwei Liu, Cheng Shi, Chuhan Wu, Ci Lei et al. (48 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.08186`  
**Tags:** LLM Efficiency, Test-Time Compute, Looped Transformers

**TL;DR:** Latent computation scaling improves a fixed LLM backbone by allocating more per-token computation in latent space rather than enlarging or retraining the Transformer.

**Problem:** LLM gains have mainly come from enlarging the Transformer backbone, which requires costly re-pretraining. The authors ask whether an existing backbone can keep improving by spending more compute per token instead.

**Method:** They study depth-recurrent (looped) Transformers and propose a hidden-decoding-at-scale scheme that adds latent computation to each token while keeping the backbone frozen. This decouples capability gains from backbone size and pretraining cost.

**Results:** The approach improves model quality by scaling latent per-token computation without retraining the backbone, offering an alternative axis to parameter scaling.

**Why it matters:** A general, method-level contribution on how to scale LLM capability efficiently — broadly impactful for anyone deploying or extending large models.


### 2. [When Structured Sparse Autoencoders Learn Consistent Concepts Across Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08605)
**Authors:** Weiduo Liao, Yunqiao Yang, Ying Wei  
**arXiv:** `2607.08605`  
**Tags:** Mechanistic Interpretability, Sparse Autoencoders, Vision-Language Models

**TL;DR:** Structured sparse autoencoders learn modality-consistent concepts across the vision and language modalities of VLMs, fixing the fragmented coverage of vanilla SAEs.

**Problem:** Vanilla SAEs used for interpretability of VLMs fail to learn modality-consistent concepts, producing fragmented, disjoint visual coverage for a given concept. This limits their usefulness for understanding multimodal models.

**Method:** The authors introduce structural constraints into SAEs so that latent concepts remain consistent across image and text modalities. They analyze when such structured SAEs recover coherent cross-modal concepts.

**Results:** Structured SAEs yield more consistent, less fragmented concept representations across modalities than vanilla SAEs, improving mechanistic interpretability of VLMs.

**Why it matters:** Interpretability of vision-language models connects to the reader's VLM focus and offers a general tool for understanding multimodal foundation models.


### 3. [An exact information theory of generalization phase transitions in Bayesian diffusion models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08041)
**Authors:** Henry Hunt, Mason Kamb, Surya Ganguli  
**arXiv:** `2607.08041`  
**Tags:** Diffusion Models, Generalization Theory, Information Theory

**TL;DR:** An analytically tractable Bayesian information-restricted diffusion model explains how diffusion models generalize rather than memorize via information-theoretic phase transitions.

**Problem:** Why diffusion models learn complex high-dimensional distributions from finite data instead of memorizing remains a fundamental open question. A tractable theory of their generalization is lacking.

**Method:** The authors introduce BIRD (Bayesian information restricted diffusion) models, where each pixel observes restricted information about noisy data, yielding exact analysis. They derive an information theory characterizing generalization phase transitions.

**Results:** The framework produces an exact account of memorization-to-generalization transitions in diffusion models as a function of information and data.

**Why it matters:** A principled theoretical advance on why generative diffusion models generalize — foundational for the many diffusion-based methods used across vision and medical imaging.
