1. What Do Medical Vision-Language Models Learn in Radiology? Transfer, Alignment, and Source-Proxy Leakage Under Distribution Shift

Ayoub Louaye Bouaziz, Lokmane Chebouba, Yassine Himeur  ·  2608.25251

Medical Vision-Language ModelsChest X-rayDistribution Shift

TL;DR: An audit of medical vision-language models on chest X-rays showing that apparent in-domain reliability hides representation-level failures and 'source-proxy leakage' when acquisition domain or evaluation protocol changes.

Problem: Medical VLMs trained on paired image-report data can look reliable in-domain yet degrade sharply when the acquisition site, paired supervision, or evaluation protocol changes. Standard benchmarks do not separate genuine clinical-concept transfer from dataset-identifying shortcuts.

Method: Using NIH ChestXray14 and CheXpert, the authors first isolate source-only cross-dataset transfer to measure how much performance survives a site change, then probe image-text alignment quality at the representation level. They frame the residual failure mode as source-proxy leakage: features that encode dataset/acquisition provenance rather than pathology, and diagnose it as a representation-level blind spot rather than a formal epistemic-uncertainty estimate.

Results: The analysis reports substantial cross-dataset degradation and evidence that alignment metrics and in-domain scores overstate transferable radiological understanding; exact numbers are not in the truncated abstract.

Why it matters: This sits directly on your CLIP-style medical VLM and chest X-ray axis, and gives a concrete diagnostic protocol for the failure mode most likely to undermine CXR foundation-model claims — including for report-generation pipelines built on such encoders.

2. Auditable CT Phenotyping Through Report-derived Radiological Observations

Riga Wu, Walter Witschey, Yicheng Li, Felix Barajas Ordonez et al. (12 authors)  ·  2608.25948

CT ImagingRadiology Report SupervisionInterpretability

TL;DR: ACT predicts 221 EHR phenotypes from CT by routing predictions through report-derived radiological observations, making it auditable whether the model reads disease findings or shortcuts.

Problem: Medical image foundation models can predict clinical phenotypes from CT with strong metrics, but high AUC leaves open whether the model uses disease-specific imaging findings or spurious correlates of the diagnosis. Without an interpretable intermediate, phenotype prediction cannot be clinically audited.

Method: The authors build Auditable CT phenotyping (ACT) on top of radiological observations extracted from free-text CT reports, using them as an explicit intermediate representation between image features and phenotype labels. ACT is trained on 38,317 studies and evaluated across 221 electronic-health-record phenotypes, allowing each phenotype prediction to be traced to the specific report-derived findings that support it.

Results: ACT supports phenotype prediction while exposing which radiological observations drive each decision, enabling shortcut detection that raw end-to-end phenotype models cannot provide; full quantitative comparisons are beyond the truncated abstract.

Why it matters: Report-derived observation supervision is exactly the bridge between radiology text and image models that your report-generation and evaluation work depends on, and the auditing framing is a template for showing a CXR/CT model reads findings rather than dataset artifacts.

3. SeVeR: Selective Visual Exposure and Retrieval for 3D Medical Image Question Answering

Yaojun Hu, Danyang Tu, Yang Liu, Jiajin Zhang et al. (11 authors)  ·  2608.25630

Medical Visual Question AnsweringBreast MRIMultimodal Reasoning

TL;DR: SeVeR tackles token redundancy in multi-sequence 3D medical VQA with selective visual exposure and retrieval, and introduces BreMRIs-VQA, a 1.19M-QA clinically curated breast MRI benchmark.

Problem: Volumetric medical VQA forces a decoder to reason over long, highly redundant 3D token sequences, and multi-sequence MRI compounds this: complementary modalities add diagnostic signal but also repeatedly re-expose the same anatomy. Existing benchmarks do not isolate reasoning under this redundancy.

Method: The authors first curate BreMRIs-VQA, a clinically grounded breast MRI benchmark with roughly 1.19M question-answer pairs spanning multiple MRI sequences. They then propose SeVeR, which selectively exposes a subset of visual tokens to the decoder and retrieves additional volumetric evidence on demand, so that complementary cross-sequence cues are preserved while repeated anatomical regions are suppressed.

Results: SeVeR is reported to improve reasoning over multi-sequence volumetric inputs relative to feeding full 3D token sequences, with the new benchmark surfacing redundancy-driven failure modes; specific gains are cut off in the truncated abstract.

Why it matters: 3D multi-sequence token budgeting is the central practical obstacle to scaling multimodal medical LLMs beyond 2D chest X-rays, and a million-scale MRI VQA benchmark is a directly usable resource for medical vision-language evaluation.

1. MLLMCLIP: Feature-Level Distillation of MLLM for Robust Vision-Language Representations

Jongsuk Kim, Qiyu Wu, Zhuoyuan Mao, Hiromi Wakaki et al. (6 authors)  ·  2608.25575

Vision-Language ModelsKnowledge DistillationCompositionality

TL;DR: MLLMCLIP distills multimodal LLM features directly into CLIP to fix attribute-object and relational compositionality without any synthetic hard-negative generation pipeline.

Problem: CLIP-style models are excellent zero-shot recognizers but weak at compositional structure such as attribute-object binding and spatial/relational descriptions. The dominant fix — generating synthetic hard negatives with cascaded LLMs and text-to-image models — adds heavy pipeline cost and brittleness.

Method: Instead of data augmentation, MLLMCLIP performs feature-level distillation: the richer joint representations of a pretrained multimodal LLM act as the teacher signal for the CLIP encoders, transferring compositional structure into the contrastive embedding space. This keeps the CLIP dual-encoder architecture and its efficient retrieval properties while removing the synthetic-negative generation stage.

Results: The distilled model reports improved compositional and relational benchmark performance with more robust vision-language representations than hard-negative-augmented baselines, at lower pipeline overhead.

Why it matters: This is a cheap, architecture-preserving recipe for improving CLIP-style encoders — precisely the kind of change that transfers to medical CLIP variants, where synthetic image-text hard negatives are hard to generate safely but strong MLLM teachers exist.

2. V-Rubrics: Visual Faithfulness via Rubric-Based Reinforcement Learning

Shulin Tian, Minglun Li, Yuhao Dong, Hao Ding et al. (9 authors)  ·  2608.25580

Reinforcement Learning From RubricsVisual GroundingMultimodal Evaluation

TL;DR: V-Rubrics reframes visual hallucination as a credit-assignment failure and uses rubric-based rewards to supervise which specific visual claims in an answer are grounded.

Problem: Vision-language models produce fluent answers where a single unsupported object, chart value, or intermediate inference silently breaks faithfulness. Scalar outcome rewards used in multimodal post-training say whether an answer is acceptable but never localize which visual assertion was wrong.

Method: The authors decompose a response into checkable visual claims and score each against a rubric derived from the image evidence, converting a single scalar reward into dense, claim-level reward signal. This rubric reward is then used for reinforcement learning post-training so gradient credit flows to the specific ungrounded assertions rather than the whole trajectory.

Results: Rubric-based RL is reported to improve visual faithfulness over outcome-reward post-training, with gains attributed to finer credit assignment across visual claims.

Why it matters: Claim-level faithfulness rewards are the closest general-domain analogue to clinically factual radiology report evaluation — this is a directly portable framework for both training and scoring generated reports on finding-level correctness.

3. Visual General Intelligence: A White Paper

Hirokatsu Kataoka, Yoshihiro Fukuhara, Yonglong Tian, Shangzhe Wu et al. (21 authors)  ·  2608.25924

Visual Foundation ModelsSelf-Supervised LearningPosition Paper

TL;DR: A white paper arguing that vision, not language, can be the substrate of general intelligence, and laying out what a GPT-scale visual pretraining paradigm would require.

Problem: Language modeling produced a clear recipe — one autoregressive objective plus web-scale data plus scale — that transfers to unseen tasks, but vision has no equivalent unified pretraining objective. It remains unresolved whether intelligence grounded in visual experience is achievable and what would constitute its GPT moment.

Method: The authors reconsider intelligence from a vision-centric standpoint, tracing the language field's trajectory from Transformers through the GPT series and asking which components have credible visual counterparts. They survey candidate objectives, data regimes, and evaluation criteria for visual pretraining and outline research directions toward visual general intelligence.

Results: The paper is a position/roadmap contribution rather than an empirical one, offering an agenda for scalable visual pretraining and transfer rather than new benchmark numbers.

Why it matters: Written by authors central to large-scale visual pretraining (including SSL/CLIP-adjacent work), this is a useful framing document for arguing where self-supervised medical imaging pretraining should aim beyond copying text-model recipes.
