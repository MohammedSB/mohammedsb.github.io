## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [The Diagnosis a Reporter Leaves Unspoken: Surfacing Frozen Tumor Features for Brain-Tumor MRI Reporting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02411)
**Authors:** Khawaja Murad ul Hassan, Ruqiyya Adil, Adil Qayyum, Rida Hassan et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.02411`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Brain MRI, Linear Probing

**TL;DR:** A chain-of-thought brain-MRI report generator fluently mislabels most non-glioma tumors as "glioma", yet a linear probe on its own frozen features recovers the correct diagnosis — the knowledge is present but never verbalized.

**Problem:** Report generators are typically judged by fluency and n-gram overlap, which can hide the fact that a model is diagnostically silent: it produces plausible prose while failing to name the actual tumor class. It is unclear whether such failures reflect missing representations or a decoding/verbalization gap.

**Method:** The authors build a multi-chain chain-of-thought reporter on a medical Mistral-7B backbone with a segmentation-aware visual front end and evaluate it on held-out brain-tumor cohorts. They then attach a supervised linear probe to the model's frozen intermediate (segmentation/backbone) features to test whether tumor-type information is linearly decodable even when the generated report omits or misstates it.

**Results:** The generator reaches diagnosis recall of only 0.44 for meningioma and 0.07 for metastasis, defaulting to "glioma", while the linear probe on the same frozen features recovers tumor identity far more accurately, demonstrating a representation-to-report gap rather than a perception failure.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct, well-designed argument that standard report-generation metrics miss diagnostic content — exactly the concern in evaluation metrics for generative radiology reports — and it offers a cheap probing protocol you could port to chest X-ray reporting to separate 'the model doesn't see it' from 'the model won't say it'.


### 2. [Characterizing Text Branch Sensitivity in Medical Vision-Language Segmentation via Evidence Decoupling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02663)
**Authors:** Ziquan Liu, Zhewei Zhu, Xuyang Shi  
**arXiv:** `2609.02663`  
**Tags:** Medical Vision-Language Segmentation, Referring Segmentation, Multimodal Fusion

**TL;DR:** A systematic audit of text-guided medical segmentation shows that in several common fusion designs the language branch contributes far less to pixel predictions than assumed, and proposes evidence decoupling to quantify it.

**Problem:** Pretrained VLMs are increasingly used to inject clinical text into medical segmentation, but nobody has measured how much the text branch actually drives pixel-level output versus acting as a weak prior the vision branch could supply on its own.

**Method:** The authors analyze several standard fusion strategies (early/late, cross-attention, prompt-conditioned) in medical vision-language segmentation and introduce an evidence-decoupling procedure that separates the contribution of visual and textual evidence to each pixel decision. Text-branch sensitivity is then characterized by perturbing, ablating, or swapping the clinical text and measuring the induced change in segmentation evidence rather than only in final Dice.

**Results:** Across fusion designs the text branch shows markedly uneven and often small influence on pixel-level predictions, revealing that reported gains from clinical text can be partly attributable to architecture or regularization rather than genuine language grounding.

**Why it matters:** If you work on referring segmentation in medical images, this gives you a diagnostic tool and a sobering baseline check: it tells you whether your language conditioning is real grounding or decoration, which is the same failure mode that plagues CLIP-style medical adaptations.


### 3. [Synergistic Information Disentanglement for Omni-modal Slide Representation Learning in Computational Pathology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02118)
**Authors:** Mingxin Liu, Chengfei Cai, Anwen Lu, Pengbo Xu et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.02118`  
**Tags:** Computational Pathology, Omni-Modal Self-Supervised Learning, Whole Slide Images

**TL;DR:** An omni-modal self-supervised framework for whole-slide representation learning that disentangles modality-unique from shared information, avoiding the modality collapse caused by naive contrastive alignment of histology, genomics, and reports.

**Problem:** Omni-modal SSL for pathology typically forces histology, genomics, and clinical text into one latent space via contrastive alignment, which collapses modality-specific signal and discards the synergistic diagnostic cues that only appear when modalities disagree or complement each other.

**Method:** The paper proposes a synergistic information disentanglement objective that factorizes each modality's representation into shared, unique, and synergistic components rather than pulling all embeddings toward a single alignment target. Slide-level representations are then learned so that modality-unique diagnostic evidence is preserved and explicitly combined, and the pretrained encoder is transferred to downstream WSI tasks.

**Results:** The disentangled omni-modal representations transfer better than contrastive-alignment baselines on downstream whole-slide prediction tasks, with the gains attributed to retained modality-unique and synergistic information.

**Why it matters:** This is the cleanest statement this week of a problem that also afflicts CXR-report and image-genomics pretraining — CLIP-style alignment is lossy when modalities are complementary rather than redundant — and the disentanglement recipe is directly portable to radiology multimodal pretraining.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Language Models Can Control Their Own Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02737)
**Authors:** Namgyu Ho, Huzama Ahmad, Woosung Koh, Se-Young Yun et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.02737`  
**Tags:** Efficient Attention, Long-Context LLMs, Sparse Attention

**TL;DR:** Instead of using lightweight external proxies to guess which KV-cache tokens matter, the model itself is trained to select the context it attends to, cutting long-context decoding cost without the proxy's retrieval errors.

**Problem:** Language models attend to a small fraction of context but still read the entire KV cache each step, so 1M-token conversations force global attention layers to scan everything per generated token. Existing sparse-attention fixes pre-select tokens with cheap proxy scorers whose relevance estimates are misaligned with what the model actually needs.

**Method:** The paper gives the model direct control over its own attention: rather than an external retriever, the LM emits selection signals that determine which cached tokens enter the attention computation, so selection is trained end-to-end with the generation objective. This removes the proxy/model mismatch and lets selection adapt per query and per layer.

**Results:** The approach preserves long-context quality while reading only a small fraction of the KV cache, outperforming proxy-based pre-selection baselines at comparable or lower compute.

**Why it matters:** Learned, end-to-end context selection is the same structural idea you need for long-token medical inputs — full 3D volumes, multi-image studies, or whole-slide token sequences — where a fixed heuristic for 'which patches matter' is exactly what fails.


### 2. [SolarWM: Open Data and Scalable Training for Long-Horizon Video World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02886)
**Authors:** Junchao Huang, Guian Fang, Shengju Qian, Xianghao Kong et al. (18 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.02886`  
**Tags:** Video World Models, Foundation Models, Open Data

**TL;DR:** SolarWM is a fully open pipeline — data curation through long-horizon inference — for training interactive video world models across heterogeneous video sources and backbones.

**Problem:** Building interactive video world models requires mixing datasets that differ in temporal scale, camera geometry, visual quality, motion statistics, and captioning style, while the generators themselves use incompatible representations; naive pooling degrades training and no fully open recipe exists.

**Method:** The authors define a unified data-preparation and conditioning scheme that normalizes heterogeneous video sources into a common interface for multiple video backbones, then train with a curriculum that supports long-horizon rollout rather than short-clip generation. Everything from the data pipeline to the inference procedure for extended interactive generation is released.

**Results:** The open stack yields interactive long-horizon video world models trained across heterogeneous sources, with the mixing and conditioning choices ablated to show where naive data pooling breaks down.

**Why it matters:** An open, documented recipe for harmonizing wildly heterogeneous video corpora is a useful template for anyone assembling multi-site, multi-protocol medical video or volumetric datasets, where the same scale/geometry/annotation-style mismatches dominate.


### 3. [Balancing Frequencies and Pixels in Flow Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02748)
**Authors:** Lucas Degeorge, Paul Couairon, Arijit Ghosh, Alexei A. Efros et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.02748`  
**Tags:** Flow Matching, Generative Models, Frequency-Aware Training

**TL;DR:** Because natural images follow a 1/f² spectrum, pixel-space flow-matching losses are dominated by low frequencies; reweighting the objective across frequency bands accelerates learning of textures and edges.

**Problem:** Pixel-space reconstruction objectives treat all spatial errors uniformly, so the bulk of the signal energy — concentrated in low spatial frequencies — dominates the gradient and delays learning of the sparse high-frequency structure that determines perceptual quality.

**Method:** The paper analyzes the spectral distribution of the flow-matching training signal and introduces a balancing scheme that equalizes the contribution of frequency bands to the loss, rather than reweighting only in pixel space. The reweighting is applied within the standard flow-matching training loop so it composes with existing architectures and samplers.

**Results:** Frequency-balanced training improves fine-detail fidelity and convergence speed over uniform pixel-space flow-matching objectives at matched compute.

**Why it matters:** Medical images are even more low-frequency-dominated than natural images, and the diagnostically decisive content (lesion margins, micro-calcifications, texture) lives in exactly the high-frequency bands this loss reweighting targets — an easy, architecture-agnostic change for medical generative and reconstruction models.
