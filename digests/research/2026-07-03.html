1. Spatio-Temporal and Clinical Conditioning for Fine-Grained Radiology Report Retrieval

P. Sloan, E. Simpson, M. Mirmehdi  ·  2607.02024

Radiology Report GenerationVision-Language ModelsMedical Image Retrieval

TL;DR: A retrieval-based radiology reporting method that conditions on spatio-temporal and clinical context for anatomically grounded, longitudinally-aware report generation.

Problem: Rising imaging demand and radiologist shortages strain reporting, yet existing retrieval-based report generation methods are rigid, lack anatomical grounding, and ignore longitudinal disease progression.

Method: The approach adds explicit anatomical/spatial grounding and conditions retrieval on both the imaging findings and clinical context, plus prior studies to capture longitudinal change, enabling fine-grained retrieval of report content matched to specific regions.

Results: The method yields more anatomically faithful and clinically conditioned report retrieval than rigid baselines (per the paper's reported gains on fine-grained retrieval).

Why it matters: Directly targets radiology report generation and evaluation—core interests—by improving anatomical grounding and longitudinal reasoning, which are exactly the weak points current report-generation systems face.

2. Towards Real-World Ultrasound Understanding: Large Vision-Language Models from Multi-Image Examinations with Long-Form Reports

Bingcong Yan, Chunlei Li, Jingliang Hu, Yilei Shi et al. (6 authors)  ·  2607.01908

UltrasoundVision-Language ModelsRadiology Report Generation

TL;DR: Scaling data from multi-image ultrasound examinations with long-form reports, rather than fancy architectures, unlocks real-world ultrasound understanding in LVLMs.

Problem: LVLMs perform well on many medical imaging tasks but struggle with ultrasound due to its complexity, multi-image exams, and variability that single-image, short-caption pipelines fail to capture.

Method: The authors revisit what is truly needed for ultrasound understanding, showing that scaling to realistic multi-image examinations paired with long-form clinical reports—instead of complex architectures or elaborate training tricks—is the key driver, and build LVLMs trained on this data.

Results: Data scale and realistic multi-image/long-report supervision substantially improve real-world ultrasound understanding over prior approaches.

Why it matters: Combines multimodal vision-language foundation models with radiology report generation on a challenging modality, reinforcing the reader's interest in data-centric VLM training for clinical reporting.

3. Assessing VLM Reliability for Medical Image Quality Evaluation Under Corruption and Bias

Sofiane Ouaari, Kevin Vorwalder, Nico Pfeifer  ·  2607.01973

Vision-Language ModelsMedical Image Quality AssessmentRobustness Evaluation

TL;DR: A study assessing how reliably VLMs perform medical image quality assessment under corruption and bias, exposing failure modes relevant to clinical safety.

Problem: VLMs are increasingly used for medical image quality assessment (MIQA) to support diagnostic accuracy, but their reliability under image corruption and dataset bias is poorly understood.

Method: The work evaluates VLM behavior on MIQA tasks by systematically applying corruptions and probing for bias, measuring whether quality judgments remain stable and trustworthy for clinical decision-making.

Results: VLMs show reliability gaps under corruption and bias, indicating they cannot yet be trusted for automated MIQA without safeguards.

Why it matters: Speaks directly to evaluation of vision-language models in medical imaging—an area central to the reader's focus on robust metrics and VLM foundation models for radiology.

1. Object-centric LeJEPA

Jakob Geusen, Ender Konukoglu  ·  2607.02404

Self-Supervised LearningObject-Centric LearningRepresentation Learning

TL;DR: Object-centric LeJEPA aligns self-supervised representations at the object level rather than whole-scene, improving data efficiency of learned image encoders.

Problem: Image-level self-supervised methods like LeJEPA need large datasets; aligning representations at the object level promises data efficiency but doing so fully self-supervised requires jointly partitioning and representing scenes.

Method: The authors extend LeJEPA to operate at the level of objects, jointly discovering scene partitions and object representations without supervision, so alignment is computed per-object instead of over entire scenes.

Results: Object-level alignment improves data efficiency of the learned features relative to whole-scene self-supervision.

Why it matters: Self-supervised representation learning is a core interest; the object-centric, data-efficient framing is directly transferable to medical imaging where labeled data is scarce.

2. Towards Robustness against Typographic Attack with Training-free Concept Localization

Bohan Liu, Wenqian Ye, Guangzhi Xiong, Zhenghao He et al. (6 authors)  ·  2607.02494

Vision-Language ModelsCLIPAdversarial Robustness

TL;DR: A training-free concept localization method makes CLIP encoders robust to typographic attacks where in-image text confounds visual semantics.

Problem: CLIP encoders—the backbone of most LVLMs—are biased by irrelevant text appearing within images, shifting representations toward lexical meaning rather than true visual content.

Method: The paper introduces a training-free approach that localizes the text-driven concept within the image representation and suppresses its confounding influence, restoring visually-grounded features without retraining.

Results: The method improves robustness of CLIP-based encoders against typographic attacks while requiring no additional training.

Why it matters: CLIP-style foundation models and their failure modes are central to the reader's interests; understanding and fixing text-confound biases matters for any downstream medical VLM built on CLIP.

3. A Hippocampus for Linear Attention: An Exact Memory for What the Recurrent State Forgets

Wanyun Cui  ·  2607.02303

Linear AttentionSequence ModelsMemory Architectures

TL;DR: HOLA gives linear-attention/state-space models a hippocampal exact-memory complement to recover facts that the compressed recurrent state forgets.

Problem: Linear-attention and state-space models compress the prefix into a fixed-size state, giving O(1) memory but lossy recall—earlier key-value associations get overwritten and needle retrieval degrades.

Method: Inspired by Complementary Learning Systems, HOLA (Hippocampal Linear Attention) pairs the fast lossy recurrent state with an exact hippocampal memory that stores associations the recurrent state cannot retain, enabling accurate recall without abandoning linear-time efficiency.

Results: The hybrid recovers needle-recall performance that pure linear attention loses while preserving efficiency benefits.

Why it matters: A general architecture advance addressing the long-context memory bottleneck in efficient sequence models—broadly impactful for scaling any transformer-alternative backbone.
