## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Multimodal Error Detection in Chest X-ray Reports: A Large Language Model Reading Image Evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26492)
**Authors:** Yanda Cheng, Bo Wen, Yuhao Lu  
**arXiv:** `2611.26492`  
**Tags:** Chest X-ray, Radiology Report Error Detection, Multimodal LLMs

**TL;DR:** Rad-Linter flags likely errors in chest X-ray reports by checking the report text against pathology scores from an image classifier, using a small LoRA-fine-tuned language model.

**Problem:** Radiology reports can contain findings that contradict the image, and text-only checks cannot catch these. Reviewers need an automated tool that compares a report against what the image actually shows.

**Method:** A pretrained chest X-ray classifier produces scores for nine pathologies. A 1.5B-parameter language model, fine-tuned with LoRA on 1,816 examples, judges whether the report agrees with those scores and flags mismatches for human review. It is compared with a keyword-and-negation rule baseline that receives the same reports and scores.

**Results:** The learned checker is benchmarked against the rule-based baseline on the same image evidence. The abstract excerpt is cut off before any quantitative results.

**Why it matters:** This sits where your report-generation and report-evaluation work meet: it is a reference-free, image-grounded way to check report consistency. It could serve as a metric or a filter for generated CXR reports.


### 2. [VoxForge: A Unified Foundation Model for Dense 3D Features Across MRI, CT and PET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25309)
**Authors:** Rushil Anirudh, Jayaraman J. Thiagarajan  
**arXiv:** `2611.25309`  
**Tags:** Medical Foundation Models, 3D Medical Imaging, Self-Supervised Learning

**TL;DR:** VoxForge is a single 3D foundation model that produces dense, frozen voxel-level features for MRI, CT and PET, aiming to match specialist models on segmentation, classification and registration.

**Problem:** Current 3D medical foundation models either need separate backbones for localized tasks (segmentation) and global tasks (classification), or cover only one modality. There is also concern that training across modalities weakens each one.

**Method:** One 3D backbone is trained across MRI, CT and PET to produce dense features that are reused frozen. Localized, global and registration tasks run on these shared features through lightweight heads.

**Results:** The paper reports that the frozen unified features are competitive with modality-specific specialists across segmentation, classification and registration. The excerpt gives no exact numbers.

**Why it matters:** Shared dense features across modalities are a strong base for referring segmentation and grounded report generation beyond 2D chest X-rays. This is a likely backbone candidate for 3D vision-language work.


### 3. [Harnessing Medical Visual-Language Models with Agentic Evidence Management](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25840)
**Authors:** Qiming Huang, Hanze Dong, Jipeng Zhang, Mike Zheng Shou et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.25840`  
**Tags:** Medical Vision-Language Models, Agentic AI, Medical Image Segmentation

**TL;DR:** A benchmark and framework that separate how well a medical VLM agent uses segmentation evidence from how good the segmentation mask is.

**Problem:** Medical VLM agents increasingly call segmentation tools, but prior work treats a good mask as the end goal. Whether the agent actually reasons correctly from that mask has gone unmeasured.

**Method:** The authors build a benchmark covering multiple medical image types. It controls mask quality so that evidence use can be measured separately from segmentation accuracy. They then propose agentic evidence management so the VLM organizes and uses tool outputs more effectively in its reasoning.

**Results:** Current agents often fail to exploit good masks. The proposed evidence-management approach improves downstream answers across image types.

**Why it matters:** This applies directly to segmentation-grounded medical VLMs and to judging whether grounding evidence actually reaches the final output. That concern carries over to grounded report generation.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [SAM 3 Sees More Than It Detects: Promptable Concept Maps Beyond Categories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25747)
**Authors:** Yu-Huan Wu, Yun Liu, Liangli Zhen  
**arXiv:** `2611.25747`  
**Tags:** Promptable Segmentation, Foundation Models, Dense Prediction

**TL;DR:** SAM 3's promptable concept segmentation is extended from noun instances to class-agnostic dense maps such as saliency, camouflage, shadows, transparency and structure.

**Problem:** Promptable segmenters answer "what is this thing". Many dense tasks instead ask class-agnostic questions: what stands out, what is hidden, or how light is blocked or reflected. These are usually handled by separate specialist models.

**Method:** The authors define promptable concept maps (PCM), which reuse SAM 3's concept-prompting interface to produce dense maps for these non-noun concepts. This shows that SAM 3's features already encode more than its detection outputs reveal.

**Results:** A single promptable model covers a wide range of dense prediction tasks. The finding is that SAM 3 represents far more than it detects.

**Why it matters:** Concept-prompted dense prediction is closely related to referring segmentation. The idea of exposing hidden capacity in a frozen segmenter could carry over to medical concepts such as opacities or effusions.


### 2. [DiLE: Discrimination-Aware Latent-Space Learning for Universal Multimodal Embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25419)
**Authors:** Ben Kang, Junda Wang, Yuxuan Yuan, Hang Li et al. (12 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.25419`  
**Tags:** Multimodal Embeddings, Contrastive Learning, Vision-Language Models

**TL;DR:** DiLE trains MLLM-based universal multimodal embedding models in latent space so that embeddings keep the details needed to tell relevant items apart from confusable ones.

**Problem:** MLLMs improve how embedding models understand their inputs. That does not guarantee the final embedding preserves the fine-grained evidence that separates a match from a hard negative.

**Method:** DiLE adds latent-space learning objectives that explicitly preserve discriminative evidence. This goes beyond standard contrastive alignment, targeting confusable alternatives.

**Results:** The paper reports improved retrieval on universal multimodal embedding benchmarks over prior MLLM-based embedders, especially on hard negatives.

**Why it matters:** This is a successor to the CLIP-style line for retrieval. Fine-grained discrimination matters a great deal in medical image-text retrieval, where near-duplicate reports differ in one finding.


### 3. [RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26627)
**Authors:** Tuna Han Salih Meral, Kaan Oktay, Alec Helbling, Atakan Kara et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.26627`  
**Tags:** Diffusion Transformers, Reinforcement Learning, Activation Steering

**TL;DR:** Much of what RL post-training adds to diffusion transformers can be reproduced by simple activation steering, not complex prompt-dependent transformations.

**Problem:** RL post-training improves text rendering and composition in diffusion transformers. It is commonly assumed this requires learning complex, input-dependent changes to the weights.

**Method:** The authors isolate how much of RL's gain can be explained by simple, largely input-independent shifts in the model's activations. They compare RL-tuned models with base models plus steering vectors.

**Results:** Activation steering recovers a large share of the RL improvements in visual quality, text rendering and compositional accuracy.

**Why it matters:** This is a cheap, interpretable alternative to RL fine-tuning for generative models. It is relevant if you consider preference or RL tuning for medical image or report generators.
