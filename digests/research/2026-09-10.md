## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Multi-Pass, Multi-View Blended Learning for High-Fidelity Volumetric CT Synthesis from Chest X-Rays](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09920)
**Authors:** Ozer Can Devecioglu, Serkan Kiranyaz, Rashid Mazhar, Tahir Hamid et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.09920`  
**Tags:** Chest X-ray, CT Synthesis, Cross-Modal Generation

**TL;DR:** A multi-pass, multi-view blended learning scheme reconstructs volumetric CT from a single chest radiograph while addressing the domain gap between synthetic DRRs and real CXRs.

**Problem:** Reconstructing a 3D CT volume from one 2D chest X-ray is severely ill-posed, and paired CXR-CT data is scarce. Prior methods train on digitally reconstructed radiographs (DRRs) projected from CT, but the DRR-to-real-CXR domain gap limits generalization to clinical radiographs.

**Method:** The authors propose a multi-pass, multi-view blended learning strategy for CXR-to-CT synthesis. Training blends information across multiple views and refinement passes so the model learns volumetric structure that transfers from DRR-based supervision toward real radiograph inputs. The abstract frames the contribution as narrowing the DRR-real gap rather than only improving reconstruction fidelity on synthetic projections.

**Results:** The paper reports high-fidelity volumetric CT synthesis from chest X-rays; the truncated abstract does not give specific numbers, so quantitative gains over prior DRR-trained baselines should be checked in the full text.

**Why it matters:** Directly in your chest X-ray and generative radiology wheelhouse: single-view CT synthesis is a strong test of what 2D radiograph models actually encode about anatomy, and the DRR-real domain gap problem recurs in any CXR pretraining pipeline using synthetic projections.


### 2. [Learning to Adapt and Calibrate: Score Distribution Alignment for Few-Shot Uncertainty Prediction in Medical VLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10333)
**Authors:** Xuan Cuong Ngo, Ngan Le  
**arXiv:** `2609.10333`  
**Tags:** Medical Vision-Language Models, Conformal Prediction, Uncertainty Estimation

**TL;DR:** Score distribution alignment lets conformal prediction give reliable uncertainty for medical VLMs even with very few calibration samples.

**Problem:** Conformal prediction offers distribution-free coverage guarantees for medical VLMs, but it assumes exchangeability between calibration and test data and needs a large calibration set. In medical settings with few labeled samples and distribution shift, both assumptions break, leading to unreliable coverage.

**Method:** The authors propose a learn-to-adapt-and-calibrate approach that aligns the distribution of nonconformity scores between the few-shot calibration set and the target test distribution. This alignment restores the conditions conformal prediction needs, allowing calibrated prediction sets from a small number of shots. The method operates on top of existing medical VLMs without retraining the backbone.

**Results:** The abstract reports improved coverage reliability and efficiency of prediction sets in few-shot medical VLM settings compared to standard conformal prediction; exact figures are not in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** Uncertainty quantification for medical VLMs is a prerequisite for clinical deployment and connects to how you evaluate generative radiology outputs; few-shot calibration is realistic for the small labeled cohorts typical in radiology.


### 3. [BrainTaskonomy: Learning How to Pretrain and What to Transfer in fMRI Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10518)
**Authors:** Junfeng Xia, Wenhao Ye, Junxiang Zhang, Jiayu Zuo et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.10518`  
**Tags:** fMRI Foundation Models, Transfer Learning, Pretraining Curriculum

**TL;DR:** BrainTaskonomy uses measured learning relations to decide how to weight pretraining domains and which pretrained knowledge to transfer in fMRI foundation models.

**Problem:** fMRI foundation models pretrain on flat mixtures of heterogeneous brain states, cohorts, and scanners, then adapt to each downstream task independently. Neither stage exploits measurable relationships between data domains and tasks.

**Method:** During pretraining, a lightweight Brain-DiT proxy estimates domain difficulty and inter-domain relations to organize the pretraining mixture. During transfer, the framework uses measured task relations (a taskonomy) to decide what to transfer to each downstream task. Crucially, the backbone is left unmodified; the contribution is in curriculum and transfer organization.

**Results:** The abstract indicates that organizing both pretraining and transfer via learned relations improves downstream performance over flat-mixture pretraining and independent adaptation; specific numbers are not visible in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** The data-mixture and transfer-selection ideas apply directly to self-supervised medical imaging pretraining across heterogeneous sources, a central issue when building CXR or multimodal foundation models from mixed institutional datasets.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [UOT-Gap: A Variational Principle for the Modality Gap in Vision-Language Models via Unbalanced Optimal Transport](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10224)
**Authors:** Zonglin Yang, Huilan Ma, Xudan Zheng, Yuejun Xie  
**arXiv:** `2609.10224`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Modality Gap, Optimal Transport

**TL;DR:** UOT-Gap is a training-free variational diagnostic that decomposes the CLIP modality gap into distributional and pairwise components via unbalanced optimal transport.

**Problem:** CLIP-style models embed images and text in a shared space, yet the two modalities remain separated. Existing explanations cite initialization, contrastive dynamics, and information imbalance, but how the distributional gap versus pairwise misalignment actually affects retrieval is unresolved.

**Method:** The authors cast the modality gap as an unbalanced optimal transport problem, yielding a variational principle that separates distribution-level offset from instance-level pairwise misalignment. The diagnostic requires no training and can be applied to any pretrained vision-language encoder. It quantifies how each component contributes to retrieval behavior.

**Results:** The paper provides a principled decomposition and analysis of the gap's effect on retrieval across VLMs; concrete retrieval numbers are not in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** You work with CLIP, SLIP, and MaskCLIP variants; a rigorous tool for diagnosing the modality gap is useful for understanding why medical image-text alignment underperforms and where to intervene.


### 2. [Isotropic Embedding Perturbations for Robust Vision Language Encoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10292)
**Authors:** Hyesong Choi, Daeun Kim, Song Park, Taekyung Kim et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.10292`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Pretraining, Data Augmentation, Robust Representation Learning

**TL;DR:** Isotropic perturbations in embedding space provide a regularizer for vision-language encoders that avoids the cross-modal damage of aggressive pixel-level augmentation.

**Problem:** Combining standard augmentations like RandAug, CutMix, Mixup, and DropPath has saturated because their effects overlap, and aggressive pixel manipulations can disrupt the delicate image-text alignment that vision-language encoders rely on.

**Method:** Instead of manipulating pixels, the authors inject isotropic perturbations directly into the embedding space of the encoders. This regularizes the representation uniformly across directions, providing robustness without breaking cross-modal correspondence. The technique is orthogonal to and combinable with existing augmentation pipelines.

**Results:** The abstract reports improved robustness and performance for vision-language encoders over saturated augmentation stacks; quantitative details are truncated.

**Why it matters:** A simple, augmentation-agnostic regularizer for CLIP-style training is directly applicable to medical image-text pretraining, where pixel augmentations that are safe for natural images can destroy diagnostically relevant detail.


### 3. [Putting Captions to the Test: Evaluating Video Caption Quality through Multiple-Choice Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09973)
**Authors:** Zizhen Wang, Bo Feng, Zhengfeng Lai, Shiyu Li et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.09973`  
**Tags:** Caption Evaluation, Video-Language Models, Evaluation Metrics

**TL;DR:** Video caption quality is evaluated by whether the caption alone suffices to answer multiple-choice questions about the video, sidestepping reference-matching metrics.

**Problem:** Reference-based captioning metrics penalize valid captions for lexical mismatch or shifts in visual focus, a consequence of the one-to-many nature of description. This makes it hard to judge whether video LLM captions actually capture the content.

**Method:** The authors put captions to the test: a generated caption is fed to a reader model that must answer multiple-choice questions about the source video using only the caption. Caption quality is measured by downstream QA accuracy rather than n-gram or embedding overlap with references. This turns evaluation into a functional test of information content.

**Results:** The abstract argues the MCQ-based protocol better reflects caption utility than reference matching; specific correlations with human judgment are not in the truncated text.

**Why it matters:** This is essentially the same problem you face in evaluating generated radiology reports, where BLEU-style metrics fail; a QA-based functional evaluation is a template worth adapting to report generation.
