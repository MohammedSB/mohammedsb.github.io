## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [MetaStructAtlas: A Grounded 3D Vision-Language Dataset and Benchmark for Functional and Structural Reasoning in Whole-Body PET/CT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03690)
**Authors:** Chenguang Zheng, Le Xue, Yichi Zhang, Wenbo Zhang et al. (11 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.03690`  
**Tags:** Medical Vision-Language Models, PET/CT Imaging, 3D Grounded Benchmark

**TL;DR:** MetaStructAtlas is a large grounded 3D vision-language dataset and benchmark that pairs metabolic (PET) and anatomical (CT) evidence for whole-body functional and structural reasoning.

**Problem:** Existing 3D medical vision-language models are confined to regional CT, so no resource supports whole-body PET/CT interpretation where diagnosis depends on jointly reading metabolic function and anatomy. This leaves a gap between current multimodal medical models and how nuclear-medicine reads actually work.

**Method:** The authors build a large-scale dataset of whole-body PET/CT volumes with language annotations that are spatially grounded to anatomical structures and metabolic findings, covering both functional (uptake/abnormality) and structural (organ/lesion localization) reasoning. They define a benchmark suite of tasks over this data and evaluate current 3D medical VLMs against it, exposing where models fail to fuse the two modalities.

**Results:** Current 3D medical vision-language models fall well short on whole-body functional-structural reasoning, with the grounded benchmark isolating failure modes that aggregate scores hide.

**Why it matters:** This is the closest analogue to chest X-ray report-generation work in a 3D, multi-modality setting: grounded language supervision plus a purpose-built evaluation protocol, both directly transferable to radiology report generation and referring segmentation research.


### 2. [MedQA-MM: Shortcuts Behind Medical Visual Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03261)
**Authors:** Benlu Wang, Yifan Zhang, Jiaqing Yu, Chin Siang Ong et al. (10 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.03261`  
**Tags:** Medical VQA Evaluation, Shortcut Learning, Multimodal Benchmarks

**TL;DR:** MedQA-MM shows that high scores on medical multimodal multiple-choice benchmarks are often reachable through textual and artifactual shortcuts rather than actual image reading.

**Problem:** Benchmark scores credit the final answer, not the route to it, so a medical multimodal MCQ can be solved via answer-option wording, non-visual clinical text, burned-in image text, annotations, or device/context artifacts instead of the intended imaging finding. This makes reported medical visual-reasoning progress hard to trust.

**Method:** The authors construct MedQA-MM to explicitly separate answer-supporting routes, categorizing benchmark-preserved cues (linguistic cues in options, non-visual text, visible in-image text, artificial annotations, acquisition artifacts). They then run ablations that remove or perturb each cue class to measure how much of a model's accuracy survives without the intended visual evidence.

**Results:** A substantial share of apparently correct medical visual reasoning is attributable to shortcut cues rather than image findings, so leaderboard accuracy overstates genuine multimodal competence.

**Why it matters:** Directly on-point for anyone building or trusting evaluation metrics for medical vision-language systems — it argues that route-aware evaluation, not endpoint accuracy, is what should gate claims about radiology AI.


### 3. [TAP-Path: Task-Adaptive Structural and Token Pruning for Efficient and Trustworthy Pathology Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04071)
**Authors:** Mehedi Hasan, Ashfak Yeafi, Md Khairul Islam  
**arXiv:** `2609.04071`  
**Tags:** Pathology Foundation Models, Model Compression, Token Pruning

**TL;DR:** TAP-Path compresses a pretrained Virchow2 pathology encoder in-place via task-adaptive structural and token pruning instead of distilling into a smaller student.

**Problem:** Recent gains in histopathology representation learning come from encoders with hundreds of millions of parameters, making inference on gigapixel whole-slide images expensive and limiting clinical deployment. Standard distillation requires training a separate student and can lose task-specific signal.

**Method:** TAP-Path directly restructures the pretrained Virchow2 encoder rather than distilling it, combining validation-driven structural pruning of network components with token pruning that discards uninformative patch tokens. The compression schedule is task-adaptive, so the retained structure and token budget are chosen per downstream task, and the framework additionally targets trustworthiness (calibration/reliability) of the compressed model.

**Results:** The compressed encoders retain competitive downstream histopathology performance at substantially reduced parameter count and inference cost relative to the full Virchow2 backbone.

**Why it matters:** Practical recipe for making medical imaging foundation models deployable without retraining from scratch — the same task-adaptive pruning idea applies to CXR and other imaging encoders you might want to run at scale.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [CORE: Improving Compositional Reasoning in MLLM Embedding via Reranker Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04083)
**Authors:** Tingyu Song, Mingxin Li, Yanzhao Zhang, Dingkun Long et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.04083`  
**Tags:** Multimodal Embeddings, Compositional Retrieval, Reranker Distillation

**TL;DR:** CORE distills a cross-attentive MLLM reranker's compositional judgments into an MLLM embedding model, fixing attribute-object binding failures in retrieval.

**Problem:** MLLM-based embedding models fail at compositional retrieval: they cannot distinguish scenes containing the same concepts with different attribute-object bindings (e.g. red cube on blue table vs. blue cube on red table). Yet the same backbone used as a cross-attentive reranker resolves these cases, indicating the capability exists but is lost in the bi-encoder embedding.

**Method:** CORE synthesizes candidate lists containing hard compositional distractors, scores them with the backbone operating as a cross-attentive reranker, and distills those fine-grained relative judgments back into the dual-encoder embedding model. This transfers cross-attention's binding sensitivity into a single-vector representation that remains cheap at retrieval time.

**Results:** The distilled embeddings substantially improve compositional retrieval over standard contrastive MLLM embedding baselines while preserving efficient single-vector search.

**Why it matters:** A concrete fix for the compositionality weakness that plagues CLIP-style contrastive encoders — highly transferable to medical image-text retrieval, where attribute-anatomy binding (e.g. 'left basal opacity' vs. 'right apical') is exactly what generic contrastive embeddings get wrong.


### 2. [The Shape of Time: Video-Token Contrast for Temporal Understanding in VideoLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04110)
**Authors:** Yumeng Shi, Quanyu Long, Yin Wu, Wenya Wang  
**arXiv:** `2609.04110`  
**Tags:** Video-Language Models, Contrastive Representation Learning, Temporal Understanding

**TL;DR:** Video-token contrastive supervision forces VideoLMs to encode temporal dynamics in their visual representations rather than answering from object, scene, and language shortcuts.

**Problem:** VideoLMs receive ordered frames but are supervised only on generated text, so gradients never require the video-token representations themselves to encode event dynamics. Models therefore answer temporal questions via object/scene priors and language statistics without representing time.

**Method:** The authors introduce a contrastive objective acting directly on video-token representations, contrasting temporally perturbed or reordered views of the same clip so that the representation must separate distinct temporal orderings. This supervision sits alongside the standard text-generation loss, shaping the visual token space where event dynamics should first emerge.

**Results:** Adding video-token contrast improves temporal understanding benchmarks and reduces reliance on shortcut cues relative to text-only-supervised VideoLM training.

**Why it matters:** A clean example of adding self-supervised/contrastive structure to the visual side of a VLM rather than relying solely on generative text loss — the same diagnosis (text supervision doesn't shape visual tokens) applies to medical VLMs trained only on report generation.


### 3. [Unlocking Lossless Speedups in LLMs via Discrete Diffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04010)
**Authors:** Subham Sekhar Sahoo, Lingjie Chen, Khiem Pham, Jonathan Geuter et al. (17 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.04010`  
**Tags:** Discrete Diffusion, LLM Inference Acceleration, Parallel Decoding

**TL;DR:** Diffusion-augmented LLMs keep an autoregressive model's exact distribution while using discrete diffusion to draw several tokens in parallel, giving lossless speedups.

**Problem:** Autoregressive next-token prediction underlies LLM quality but forces strictly sequential generation, which caps inference throughput. Existing parallel-decoding approaches typically trade away distributional fidelity or require a separate draft model.

**Method:** The authors define a new model class whose distribution is that of an AR model, but whose sampler uses a discrete diffusion process to propose multiple tokens per step from that same distribution. They decouple the parallel-proposal machinery from the AR likelihood so the acceleration is provably lossless rather than approximate.

**Results:** The method yields wall-clock speedups over standard autoregressive decoding while exactly preserving the underlying AR model distribution.

**Why it matters:** Inference cost is the practical bottleneck for deploying report-generation and multimodal medical LLMs; a lossless parallel decoder is a drop-in win that doesn't require re-validating output quality.
