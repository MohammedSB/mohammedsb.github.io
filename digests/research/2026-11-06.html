1. Multimodal Hierarchical Mixture-of-Experts for 3D Medical Vision-Language Modeling

Gizem Mert, Szymon Płotka, Pedro R. A. S. Bassi, Wenxuan Li et al. (8 authors)  ·  2611.08185

Medical ImagingVision-Language Models3D Medical Segmentation

TL;DR: MedHiVE introduces a hierarchical vision-language Soft Mixture-of-Experts model for 3D medical scan analysis that explicitly focuses on clinically relevant anatomical regions.

Problem: Most medical vision-language models ignore the inherent hierarchical structure of 3D scans and reports, failing to focus on diagnostically important anatomical regions across multiple levels of organization.

Method: MedHiVE combines hierarchical vision-language Soft Mixture-of-Experts (SMoE) with contrastive learning, routing visual tokens to specialized experts based on anatomical context and aggregating features across multiple scales of the volume.

Results: MedHiVE achieves strong performance on 3D medical vision-language tasks, outperforming baselines that do not exploit hierarchical anatomical structure.

Why it matters: Directly addresses the challenge of applying vision-language foundation models to 3D medical volumes (CT/MRI), highly relevant to the reader's interest in multimodal learning for radiology and medical imaging.

2. TACT-KV: Tri-Axis Cosine Transform for Compressing Volumetric KV Caches in Medical VLMs

Chongyu Qu, Ritchie Zhao, Yufan He, Dong Yang et al. (15 authors)  ·  2611.07593

Medical ImagingVision-Language ModelsKV Cache Compression

TL;DR: TACT-KV proposes a tri-axis cosine transform to compress the massive KV caches generated when vision-language models process 3D medical volumes like CT and MRI scans.

Problem: Applying VLMs to 3D medical scans generates tens of thousands of vision tokens, causing the KV cache to scale linearly with both batch size and context length, creating a severe memory bottleneck that limits practical deployment.

Method: TACT-KV applies separable 3D discrete cosine transforms along all three spatial axes of the volumetric KV cache, exploiting the structured spatial redundancy unique to medical volumes and compressing the cache while preserving diagnostically relevant frequency components.

Results: TACT-KV achieves substantial KV cache compression on 3D medical VLM workloads with minimal degradation in downstream task performance compared to uncompressed baselines.

Why it matters: Directly solves a critical engineering barrier for deploying medical VLMs on CT/MRI volumes, relevant to the reader's interest in foundation models for radiology and efficient multimodal inference.

3. CMDiff: Left Atrial Scar Segmentation from 3D LGE MRI Using Conditional Mask Diffusion Model: A Feasibility Study

Jing Zhang, Bastien Bergere, Jonas Leite, Emilie Bollache et al. (7 authors)  ·  2611.07814

Medical ImagingCardiac MRIDiffusion Models

TL;DR: CMDiff is a conditional mask diffusion model for automatic left atrial scar segmentation from 3D LGE-MRI, addressing extreme class imbalance and scarce labels.

Problem: Automatic scar segmentation from 3D LGE cardiac MRI is hindered by low contrast at the thin atrial wall, scarce labeled data, and extreme class imbalance between scar and non-scar voxels.

Method: A conditional diffusion model iteratively generates segmentation masks conditioned on the 3D LGE-MRI volume, leveraging the generative process to handle sparse label distributions without requiring large annotated datasets.

Results: CMDiff demonstrates feasibility for 3D LA scar segmentation, showing competitive performance versus conventional approaches on a challenging dataset with limited annotations.

Why it matters: Combines diffusion models with 3D medical image segmentation in a clinically important cardiac MRI application, relevant to the reader's interests in self-supervised/generative approaches for medical imaging.

1. Muir: Linear Attention with Sensitivity-Allocated Gating and Matched-Filter Readout

Tushaar Gangavarapu, Gil Tabak, Clemens JS Schaefer  ·  2611.08376

Linear AttentionSequence ModelingFoundation Models

TL;DR: Muir improves gated delta-rule linear attention by introducing sensitivity-allocated gating and a matched-filter readout, addressing poor state utilization in existing models.

Problem: Gated delta-rule linear attention models like GDN-2 suffer from state updates that are far less informative than their capacity allows, limiting their ability to encode and retrieve information from long contexts.

Method: Muir introduces two innovations: sensitivity-allocated gating that scales state updates by how much each key activates the current state, and a matched-filter readout that projects queries through the state using a learned filter, improving information retrieval without increasing state size.

Results: Muir outperforms GDN-2 and other linear attention variants on standard language modeling benchmarks while maintaining linear-time training and constant-memory inference.

Why it matters: Advances efficient sequence modeling architectures directly applicable to long-context medical report generation and processing of lengthy clinical documents.

2. Beyond Quadratic Attention: Separable Gaussian Operators for ODE Vision Transformers

Carlos Boned Riera, David Romero Sanchez, Oriol Ramos Terrades  ·  2611.08236

Vision TransformersEfficient ArchitecturesSelf-Supervised Learning

TL;DR: SSoG replaces quadratic self-attention in Vision Transformers with a separable sum-of-Gaussians spatial operator, reducing per-layer complexity from O(N²) to subquadratic while maintaining content-dependent, translation-equivariant representations.

Problem: Standard self-attention in Vision Transformers has quadratic complexity in token count, severely limiting scalability to high-resolution medical images and large input grids.

Method: SSoG parameterizes attention as a learned mixture of axis-factorized Gaussian kernels, computed separately along each spatial axis and then combined, yielding a content-aware spatial operator with subquadratic cost that is differentiable and naturally equivariant to translations.

Results: SSoG matches or exceeds standard ViT performance on image classification benchmarks while significantly reducing computational cost at higher resolutions.

Why it matters: Efficient ViT alternatives are crucial for high-resolution medical imaging; this approach is directly applicable to radiology image analysis and could integrate with CLIP-style vision encoders.

3. Multimodal Information Interactions Bottleneck for Multimodal Self-Supervised Learning

Liangjian Wen, Jiangtao Zheng, Jiang Duan, Qun Dai et al. (11 authors)  ·  2611.07718

Multimodal Self-Supervised LearningInformation BottleneckContrastive Learning

TL;DR: InfMasking is a unified multimodal self-supervised learning framework that explicitly captures modality-specific and synergistic information beyond the shared semantics targeted by contrastive methods.

Problem: Existing multimodal contrastive SSL methods assume strong cross-modal redundancy and optimize only for shared semantics, systematically ignoring modality-specific features and cross-modal synergies that are critical for downstream tasks.

Method: InfMasking applies a variational information bottleneck with strategic masking across modalities, decomposing multimodal information into shared, unique, and synergistic components and training dedicated encoder pathways for each, without requiring paired data at inference.

Results: InfMasking outperforms contrastive SSL baselines across multiple multimodal benchmarks spanning image-text, image-audio, and tabular-image combinations, demonstrating better utilization of synergistic information.

Why it matters: Directly relevant to the reader's interests in self-supervised and multimodal learning; the framework could advance CLIP-style medical VLMs by better exploiting complementary information between radiology images and reports.
