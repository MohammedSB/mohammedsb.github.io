## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Diagnostic Loss: Asymmetric Supervision for Radiology Report Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30980)
**Authors:** Ignacy Kolton, Wenxuan Li, Zongwei Zhou, Jarosław B. Ćwikła et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.30980`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Medical Vision-Language Models, Training Objectives

**TL;DR:** Diagnostic Loss replaces uniform token-level likelihood with asymmetric supervision that rewards stating supported diagnoses and penalizes unsupported ones in generated radiology reports.

**Problem:** Medical vision-language models for report generation are trained with maximum-likelihood objectives that weight every token equally, so fluent narrative is prioritized over the few diagnostically critical terms, and models may omit true findings or hallucinate absent ones.

**Method:** The authors introduce an asymmetric training loss that treats diagnostic terms differently from ordinary tokens, upweighting correct assertion of supported diagnoses and explicitly penalizing unsupported ones. It is designed as a drop-in modification to standard MedVLM fine-tuning rather than a new architecture.

**Results:** The abstract reports that the loss improves diagnostically meaningful accuracy of generated reports over token-level MLE baselines; specific numbers are not visible in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** This directly targets the gap between fluency metrics and clinical correctness that your evaluation-metrics work addresses, and comes from Zongwei Zhou's group, which is worth tracking for report generation.


### 2. [ICGF-CXRMate: Image-Conditioned Clinical Context Compression and Gated Fusion for Chest X-ray Report Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30928)
**Authors:** Vu Minh Tran, Thuc Huynh, Son Thanh Luu, Doanh C. Bui  
**arXiv:** `2610.30928`  
**Tags:** Chest X-ray, Radiology Report Generation, Multimodal Fusion

**TL;DR:** ICGF-CXRMate compresses heterogeneous clinical context into a fixed set of image-conditioned queries and gates its fusion with visual tokens for chest X-ray report generation.

**Problem:** Adding patient data such as indications, history, and prior reports helps CXR report generation, but naive concatenation produces long variable-length prompts and can inject redundant or image-irrelevant context into the decoder.

**Method:** Learnable queries are first conditioned on the image and then attend over the clinical context to produce a compact fixed-length summary. A gated fusion module then controls how much of this compressed context is mixed with the visual tokens before decoding, so irrelevant context can be suppressed.

**Results:** Built on the CXRMate framework, the method is reported to improve report generation quality over direct concatenation of clinical context while shortening decoder prompts; exact metrics are not in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** It is a concrete design for using the non-image context that real radiology workflows provide, directly relevant to your CXR report generation experiments.


### 3. [nnInteractive Agent: General-Purpose AI Agents as 3D Image Annotators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31080)
**Authors:** Fabian Isensee, Constantin Ulrich Harsy, Maximilian Rokuss, Benjamin Hamm et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.31080`  
**Tags:** Medical Image Segmentation, Interactive Segmentation, LLM Agents

**TL;DR:** General-purpose AI agents can drive nnInteractive, placing prompts, inspecting results, and correcting masks to annotate 3D medical images without a human in the loop.

**Problem:** Interactive 3D segmentation models turn a few points or scribbles into volumetric masks, but a human still has to locate each structure, place prompts, check outputs, and fix errors, which remains the annotation bottleneck.

**Method:** The authors give a general-purpose agent access to nnInteractive as a tool, plus image viewing and code execution, and let it autonomously find structures, issue spatial prompts, evaluate the resulting masks, and iterate with corrections. No task-specific training of the agent is required.

**Results:** The agents are shown to produce usable 3D annotations across structures without human prompting; quantitative comparisons to human annotators are not visible in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** From the nnU-Net group, this points to agentic annotation as a route to scaling segmentation labels for self-supervised and foundation-model pretraining in medical imaging.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [A Fix for Softmax and Cross-Entropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31642)
**Authors:** Jonathan T. Barron, Dan Gnanapragasam, Michael Tschannen  
**arXiv:** `2610.31642`  
**Tags:** Numerical Stability, Training Objectives, Knowledge Distillation

**TL;DR:** Barron and colleagues identify two numerical bugs in standard softmax and cross-entropy implementations that corrupt gradients, and propose a fix.

**Problem:** Softmax Collapse rounds the target logit's gradient to zero when it dominates the others so gradients no longer sum to zero, and Softmax Cancellation yields spurious non-zero gradients when student logits nearly match teacher logits, as in distillation.

**Method:** The authors analyze the floating-point behavior of softmax, log-softmax, and cross-entropy, characterize when each failure occurs, and derive reformulated implementations that avoid the catastrophic rounding while remaining drop-in replacements.

**Results:** The fixed implementations restore correct gradient behavior in the regimes where standard code fails; downstream training effects are described in the paper but not in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** Cross-entropy and distillation losses underlie CLIP-style and report generation training, so this is a cheap correctness check for any pipeline you run.


### 2. [Learning from Next-Frame Prediction: Towards Unified and Scalable Visual Representation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30724)
**Authors:** Yifeng Yao, Jinghan Li, Zhihao Zhang, Yajiao Xiong et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.30724`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Video Representation Learning, World Models

**TL;DR:** A unified visual pretraining objective that learns by predicting the next frame from past frames, aiming to match joint-embedding methods while scaling like autoregressive ones.

**Problem:** Masked video pretraining reconstructs missing content from surrounding context rather than predicting the future, while token-by-token autoregressive methods have lagged joint-embedding approaches in representation quality.

**Method:** The paper formulates visual representation learning as next-frame prediction from past frames, framing future observations as a natural supervisory signal for world modeling. The approach is designed to unify image and video pretraining under a single scalable objective.

**Results:** The authors report competitive or improved representation quality relative to masked and joint-embedding baselines with favorable scaling; specific benchmarks are not in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** A new general SSL recipe that could transfer to temporal medical data such as ultrasound or fluoroscopy, and worth comparing to MAE and DINO-style pretraining you may use.


### 3. [WeVisOne-VL: Unified Visual Perception from Objects to Pixels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31329)
**Authors:** Tianming Liang, Junyi Pan, Yuxi Li, Canmiao Fu et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.31329`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Referring Segmentation, Unified Visual Perception

**TL;DR:** WeVisOne-VL is a VLM that unifies detection, referring localization, counting, OCR, segmentation, and matting through structured next-token prediction.

**Problem:** Existing vision-language models remain unreliable for fine-grained perception, and object-level and pixel-level tasks are usually handled by separate heads or models.

**Method:** The model expresses all perception outputs, from boxes to masks, as structured token sequences predicted by a single decoder, so one VLM handles object-level and pixel-level tasks. Training covers detection, referring localization, counting, OCR, segmentation, and matting jointly.

**Results:** The technical report claims strong results across these perception tasks within a single model; detailed numbers are not visible in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** A unified referring-segmentation-capable VLM is directly relevant to your referring segmentation work and to grounded report generation.
