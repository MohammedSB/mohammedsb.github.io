TL;DR: ARIVE is an explainable radiology report generation framework that interleaves anatomical inspection with diagnostic reasoning, so each reported finding can be traced to visual evidence.

Problem: Automated report generators can draft clinically plausible reports, but the pathway from image evidence to reported findings is opaque. This makes reports hard to review or revise.

Method: ARIVE (Anatomy-guided Reasoning with Interleaved Visual Evidence) alternates between inspecting anatomical regions and reasoning diagnostically while it generates the report. The truncated abstract does not give architecture or training details.

Results: The truncated abstract reports no quantitative results; check the full paper for datasets and metrics.

Why it matters: This is directly on your core topic of radiology report generation. Its evidence-linked reasoning also bears on how generated reports should be evaluated and audited beyond text-overlap metrics.

2. Counterfactual Analysis of Extent Leakage from Annotation-Derived Prompts in Vision-Language Medical Image Segmentation

Niran Nataraj, Ankita Das, C. Krishna Mohan, Kenji Kawashima  ·  2611.13970

Text-Guided SegmentationBenchmark LeakageVision-Language Models

TL;DR: A counterfactual audit shows that captions used in text-guided medical segmentation benchmarks leak lesion extent, because they were derived from the ground-truth masks that later score the prediction.

Problem: Text-guided segmentation models gain considerable accuracy from captions on public benchmarks. On colonoscopy and breast ultrasound, the size, count and location words were computed from the evaluation masks, and the chest X-ray captions were written by benchmark annotators.

Method: The authors run counterfactual tests on the prompts to measure how much of the benefit comes from annotation-derived extent information. The truncated abstract does not say which models were tested or how the prompts were altered.

Results: The truncated abstract cuts off before the findings; it establishes the leakage pathway but not the size of the effect.

Why it matters: This matters for referring and text-prompted medical segmentation, including chest X-ray benchmarks: reported gains from language may be inflated. It is worth reading before you design or trust evaluations in this area.

TL;DR: P2Echo is a single text-prompted model that segments diverse cardiac structures across multiple standard echocardiographic views.

Problem: Multi-view echocardiography segmentation is hard because anatomy and appearance vary substantially across acoustic windows. This usually pushes practitioners toward view-specific or structure-specific models.

Method: P2Echo is a unified framework in which one model is controlled by text prompts to select the target cardiac structure in any standard view. The truncated abstract does not describe the architecture.

Results: The truncated abstract reports no quantitative results.

Why it matters: It applies referring, language-conditioned segmentation to a clinical modality with one model, which is close to your interest in vision-language medical segmentation.

Method: As the title indicates, the method mines global-to-zoom perception gaps to find such questions automatically. The truncated abstract does not describe the pipeline.

3. Where Do Vision-Language Models Get Left and Right? Position-Code Counterfactuals in Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL and InternVL3

Enhao Cui, Junzhi Yang, Yaoshun Lin, Yuhan Feng et al. (5 authors)  ·  2611.14060

Vision-Language ModelsMechanistic InterpretabilitySpatial Reasoning

TL;DR: Position-code counterfactuals in Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL and InternVL3 locate where left-right information comes from, showing the vision transformer's position code is both necessary and sufficient to reproduce direction.

Problem: A VLM could take left-right direction from image content within patches, from the ViT position code, or from token order in the language model. Ablations show the ViT code is necessary but do not test sufficiency.

Method: The authors mirror only the position code, leaving image content unchanged, in Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-4B and InternVL3-2B. They then observe whether the model's left-right behaviour follows.

Results: Mirroring the ViT width positions reproduces the mirrored behaviour in all three models; the truncated abstract cuts off before further detail.

Why it matters: Laterality errors are a known failure mode in radiology report generation and grounding. Knowing where VLMs encode left and right informs both diagnosis of those errors and architecture choices.
