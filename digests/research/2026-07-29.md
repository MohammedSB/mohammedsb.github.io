## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Open-Ended CT Volume Segmentation with Weak Supervision from Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25860)
**Authors:** Sanjay Subramanian, Junwei Yu, Zirui Wang, Rohil Malpani et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.25860`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, CT Segmentation, Vision-Language Models

**TL;DR:** A text-conditioned CT segmentation model trained with both voxel-level and scalable slice-level supervision from radiology reports.

**Problem:** Training segmentation models for CT scans requires expensive voxel-level annotations, which limits scalability. Existing approaches do not leverage the large databases of scan-report pairs that associate free-text findings with specific slice indices.

**Method:** The authors extract finding descriptions and their associated slice indices from a large scan-report database, then fine-tune SAM3 (a general-purpose 2D segmentation model) using standard voxel-level supervision combined with coarse but scalable slice-level supervision derived from radiology reports. This weakly supervised signal is used to train a text-conditioned model that can segment arbitrary findings described in natural language.

**Results:** The combined supervision strategy improves segmentation performance on CT volumes, enabling open-ended text-conditioned segmentation that generalizes to diverse radiological findings described in reports.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to the reader's interests in self-supervised/weakly supervised learning for medical imaging, radiology report grounding, and vision-language models applied to chest and body CT analysis.


### 2. [OrganLens: Organ-Specific Representation Learning for CT Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25164)
**Authors:** Zhixuan Ge, Anqi Li, Sadeer Al-Kindi, Hanwen Xu et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.25164`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, CT Foundation Models, Self-Supervised Learning

**TL;DR:** OrganLens produces organ-specific representations from CT foundation models without requiring pre-segmented organ volumes, enabling fine-grained per-organ analysis.

**Problem:** Existing CT foundation models produce a single volume-level representation, which is insufficient for biomedical questions targeting abnormalities or longitudinal change in a specific organ. Anatomy-aware methods typically require pre-separated organ volumes, adding preprocessing burden.

**Method:** OrganLens introduces organ-specific representation learning that operates directly on the full CT volume, isolating organ-level features through a learned attention mechanism that focuses representations on anatomically relevant regions without explicit organ pre-segmentation. The framework is built on top of existing CT foundation model embeddings.

**Results:** OrganLens yields improved performance on organ-specific downstream tasks including abnormality detection and prognosis, outperforming both volume-level and pre-segmentation-based anatomy-aware baselines.

**Why it matters:** Highly relevant to the reader's focus on foundation models and self-supervised learning for medical imaging, particularly CT analysis and representation learning for clinical tasks.


### 3. [CADENCE: A Cardiac Atom Dictionary for Interpretable Neural Concept Extraction from ECG Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25244)
**Authors:** Yixuan Duan, Arjun Naik, Sadeer Al-Kindi, Wei Qiu  
**arXiv:** `2607.25244`  
**Tags:** ECG Analysis, Interpretability, Foundation Models

**TL;DR:** CADENCE uses sparse autoencoders to decompose ECG foundation model representations into a human-interpretable dictionary of physiological concepts.

**Problem:** ECG foundation models transfer well across clinical tasks but the physiological knowledge encoded in their internal representations is opaque, limiting clinical trust and interpretability.

**Method:** CADENCE applies a BatchTopK sparse autoencoder to factorize Layer-6 embeddings of an ECG foundation model into a sparse, human-interpretable dictionary of physiological concepts. The resulting 'cardiac atom dictionary' is queryable, allowing clinicians to probe which physiological features activate for specific ECG patterns.

**Results:** CADENCE successfully decomposes foundation model representations into interpretable physiological concepts that align with known cardiac electrophysiology, providing a queryable and transparent view into model knowledge.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to the reader's interest in foundation models for medical data and interpretability of clinical AI systems; the sparse autoencoder approach for concept extraction parallels methods being explored in vision-language model analysis.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [MODUS: Decoder-Only Any-to-Any Modeling of Diverse Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25948)
**Authors:** Mingqiao Ye, Zhaochong An, Zhitong Gao, Xian Liu et al. (14 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.25948`  
**Tags:** Multimodal Learning, Any-to-Any Generation, Foundation Models

**TL;DR:** MODUS is a decoder-only any-to-any multimodal model that leverages pretrained LLM weights and supports arbitrary modality combinations within a single unified network.

**Problem:** Existing any-to-any multimodal models are typically trained from scratch using encoder-decoder or diffusion architectures, limiting their scalability and preventing reuse of powerful pretrained language model backbones.

**Method:** MODUS adopts a decoder-only transformer architecture that represents all modalities as token sequences, enabling any modality to be predicted from any combination of others within a single network. It initializes from pretrained LLM weights and uses modality-specific tokenizers and detokenizers, allowing the model to leverage large-scale pretraining while supporting flexible multimodal input-output configurations.

**Results:** MODUS demonstrates competitive performance across diverse any-to-any multimodal benchmarks while being more parameter-efficient than scratch-trained baselines, and benefits measurably from LLM weight initialization.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to the reader's interest in multimodal foundation models and unified vision-language architectures; the any-to-any decoder-only paradigm is a natural extension of CLIP-style and generative VLM research.


### 2. [Parallel Decoding Distillation for Fast Image and Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26004)
**Authors:** Neta Shaul, Chao Liu, Arash Vahdat, Julius Berner  
**arXiv:** `2607.26004`  
**Tags:** Diffusion Models, Video Generation, Model Distillation

**TL;DR:** Parallel Decoding Distillation (PDD) accelerates video and image diffusion models into few-step generators using a novel parallel decoding objective that avoids the instability of variational score distillation.

**Problem:** Video diffusion and flow models require many iterative sampling steps, and current state-of-the-art acceleration methods rely on variational score distillation (VSD) and adversarial losses that are notoriously hard to train and unstable.

**Method:** PDD distills diffusion models into few-step generators by training with a parallel decoding objective: the student model is trained to predict all future denoising steps simultaneously from a shared latent, using teacher rollouts as supervision. This avoids the adversarial and VSD training instabilities while maintaining alignment with the teacher distribution.

**Results:** PDD achieves high-quality few-step image and video generation competitive with VSD-based methods, with significantly more stable training dynamics and without requiring adversarial discriminators.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to the reader's work on generative models and diffusion-based approaches; the distillation technique has direct applicability to accelerating medical image synthesis and report generation pipelines.


### 3. [Raven: High-Recall Sequence Modeling with Sparse Memory Routing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25357)
**Authors:** Arshia Afzal, Aviv Bick, Eric P. Xing, Volkan Cevher et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.25357`  
**Tags:** Sequence Modeling, Long-Context Recall, Sparse Attention

**TL;DR:** Raven is a linear-time sequence model with sparse memory routing that achieves high recall on long-context tasks by selectively writing important tokens to persistent memory slots.

**Problem:** Linear-time sequence models (SSMs, linear Transformers) write densely to memory, causing interference that makes specific past tokens hard to recall, while sliding-window attention cannot access tokens outside its window.

**Method:** Raven introduces sparse memory routing: rather than updating the entire memory state for every token, it selectively routes a small subset of tokens to persistent memory slots based on learned importance scores. This allows the model to maintain high-recall access to historically important tokens while retaining linear-time complexity.

**Results:** Raven achieves substantially higher long-context recall than comparable linear-time models and competitive performance with sliding-window attention on language modeling benchmarks, while maintaining linear inference cost.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to the reader's interest in foundation model architectures; long-context recall improvements have direct implications for radiology report generation and multimodal models processing long clinical documents or image sequences.
