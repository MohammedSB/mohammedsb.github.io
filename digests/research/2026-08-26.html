1. Graph-Supervised Hierarchical Clinical Alignment for Radiology Report Generation with Large Language Models

Yingshu Li, Yunyi Liu, Zhanyu Wang, Zailong Chen et al. (7 authors)  ·  2608.24121

Radiology Report GenerationVision-Language ModelsChest X-ray

TL;DR: A graph-supervised hierarchical alignment framework injects disease-grounded, finding-level supervision into LLM-based radiology report generation instead of relying only on report-level loss.

Problem: LLM-based radiology report generation produces fluent text but remains clinically unfaithful because supervision is applied at the whole-report level. This creates a granularity mismatch: reports are composed of disease-grounded findings, yet training signals never localize to individual findings.

Method: The authors build a hierarchical clinical alignment scheme in which a graph representation of clinical entities and their relations supervises intermediate visual-textual representations. Alignment is enforced at multiple granularities (finding/entity level up to report level), so the LLM decoder is conditioned on evidence that is explicitly tied to disease concepts rather than to a single global caption embedding.

Results: The approach improves clinical-efficacy metrics over report-level-supervised LLM baselines while maintaining language-generation quality, indicating that finer-grained supervision translates into more faithful findings.

Why it matters: This sits squarely in your core area: it targets the exact failure mode (fluency without clinical faithfulness) that motivates better RRG evaluation metrics, and the graph-supervision idea is directly transferable to your own report-generation and metric work.

2. MC-CXR: A Multi-Context Chest X-ray Benchmark for Context-Induced Disruption in Vision-Language Models

Junhyeok Lee, Songsoo Kim, Kyu Sung Choi  ·  2608.24118

Chest X-rayVision-Language ModelsClinical Benchmarking

TL;DR: MC-CXR is a chest X-ray benchmark that measures whether VLMs keep a correct image-only answer when plausible but conflicting textual context (prior reports, preliminary notes) is supplied.

Problem: Clinical VLM pipelines rarely see an image in isolation — retrieved reports, preliminary notes, and priors are concatenated into the prompt. Existing CXR benchmarks only score isolated accuracy and cannot detect when added context flips a previously correct visual decision.

Method: The authors construct a multi-context CXR evaluation in which each case is paired with several context conditions, including contexts that plausibly contradict the image. They then measure context-induced disruption: the rate at which a model that answered correctly image-only is pushed to an incorrect answer once conflicting text is present, separating this from baseline accuracy.

Results: Evaluated VLMs show substantial context-induced disruption, with correct image-grounded decisions frequently overridden by misleading textual context — a failure invisible to standard isolated-accuracy benchmarks.

Why it matters: Directly relevant to both your CXR work and your interest in evaluation: it provides a ready-made protocol for stress-testing report-generation and VQA systems that condition on priors or retrieved reports, which is exactly the deployment setting for longitudinal RRG.

3. ReGround-Surg: Reliability-Guided Anchor Grounding for Referring Surgical Video Segmentation

Jiaxin Wen, Ming Yin, Lu Liu, Zeyu Fu  ·  2608.24671

Referring SegmentationSurgical Video AnalysisSAM2 Adaptation

TL;DR: ReGround-Surg improves referring surgical video segmentation by making anchor-frame grounding reliability-aware rather than trusting a single selected frame before mask propagation.

Problem: Two-stage SAM2-based referring video segmentation first grounds the language expression in one anchor frame and then propagates the mask. Performance is therefore hostage to that single grounding decision: a poor or ambiguous anchor frame corrupts the entire tracked sequence.

Method: The method introduces a reliability estimate over candidate anchor frames and uses it to guide grounding, selecting and weighting anchors whose language-to-region alignment is trustworthy. The reliability-guided anchor is then handed to SAM2-style propagation, so mask tracking is initialized from evidence the model is confident about rather than from a heuristically chosen frame.

Results: The framework outperforms prior two-stage referring surgical video segmentation systems such as ReSurgSAM2, with the gains concentrated in cases where naive anchor selection fails.

Why it matters: Referring segmentation is one of your stated CV interests, and this is a clean medical instantiation of it; the reliability-gated grounding idea generalizes naturally to referring segmentation in radiology volumes and other medical video.

1. LAION-BVD: A 10-Million-Hour Open Video Dataset for Multimodal Pre-training

Andreas Hochlehnert, Marianna Nezhurina, Mehdi Cherti, Andrej Radonjic et al. (12 authors)  ·  2608.24845

Video DatasetsMultimodal PretrainingOpen Foundation Models

TL;DR: LAION-BVD releases 10 million hours of open video (80M videos from 1.3B crawled URLs) with scene-level clips for video-audio-image multimodal pretraining.

Problem: Open multimodal pretraining at video scale is bottlenecked by data: existing public video corpora are far smaller than what image-text pretraining enjoys, and most large video collections are proprietary or license-restricted.

Method: The authors mine 1.3B platform-specific video URLs from CommonCrawl, download 80M videos totaling 10M hours, and apply content-aware scene detection to cut them into coherent clips. The release is designed for joint video, audio, and image pretraining, with metadata and processing pipelines provided so the corpus can be regenerated and filtered by downstream users.

Results: The result is, by a wide margin, one of the largest openly available video corpora for multimodal pretraining, enabling CLIP-style and video-language training at a scale previously restricted to industrial labs.

Why it matters: If you train or fine-tune CLIP-style vision-language backbones, this is a substantial new pretraining substrate; large open video-audio corpora are also the most likely source of general-purpose priors that later transfer into medical video and multi-frame imaging.

2. Effective Learning Rate Governs Loss Dynamics in Language Model Pretraining

Zihan Liu, Ruiheng Zheng, Shaobo Zhang, Changxin Tian et al. (7 authors)  ·  2608.24814

LLM PretrainingOptimization DynamicsLearning Rate Schedules

TL;DR: Loss trajectories in language model pretraining collapse onto a single curve when the effective learning rate (LR divided by parameter norm) is matched, regardless of the individual LR and norm values.

Problem: Learning rate and weight decay/parameter norm are usually tuned as separate knobs, and their interaction with loss dynamics is treated empirically. It has been unclear which quantity actually governs training progress.

Method: The authors define the effective learning rate (ELR) as the ratio of LR to parameter norm and run controlled pretraining sweeps in which ELR is held fixed while LR and norm vary substantially. They measure trajectory collapse across optimizers, architectures, datasets, and model scales to test whether ELR — not LR — is the controlling variable.

Results: Runs with matched ELR show near-identical loss curves throughout training despite very different raw LRs and parameter norms, with low mean collapse error across all tested settings.

Why it matters: A practical and generalizable result for anyone pretraining or continually pretraining vision or vision-language encoders: it reduces a two-dimensional hyperparameter search to one dimension and explains why LR transfer across scales often fails.

3. PhysMLLMs: Spatial Priors for Unified Referring Segmentation and Grounded Reasoning of Images and Videos

Siyao Yan, Bo Han, Jisheng Dang, Bimei Wang et al. (10 authors)  ·  2608.24574

Referring SegmentationMultimodal LLMsSpatio-Temporal Grounding

TL;DR: PhysMLLMs injects explicit spatial priors into video multimodal LLMs to unify referring segmentation and grounded reasoning across images and video with fewer jitter, drift, and identity-switch failures.

Problem: Video MLLMs that support language-guided segmentation suffer spatio-temporal inconsistency — mask jitter, target drift, and identity switches — especially under partial occlusion or with visually similar distractors. A likely cause is that training provides no explicit spatial prior to stabilize predictions over time.

Method: The authors add spatial priors into the MLLM's training and representation so that grounding decisions carry explicit positional structure rather than being inferred purely from language-conditioned attention. The same model is trained to handle referring segmentation and grounded reasoning jointly over both images and videos, so spatial consistency is enforced across modalities and time.

Results: The unified model reduces temporal inconsistency artifacts and improves referring segmentation and grounded reasoning relative to MLLM baselines lacking explicit spatial priors.

Why it matters: This is the general-domain counterpart of the medical referring-segmentation problem you work on, and its spatial-prior mechanism is a plausible drop-in for stabilizing language-guided segmentation of anatomy across slices or frames.
