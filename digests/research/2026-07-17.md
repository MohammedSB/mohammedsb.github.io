## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Multi-LLM Collaborative MRI Report Generation for Visual Instruction Tuning in Brain Oncology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14581)
**Authors:** Sinyoung Ra, Jonghun Kim, Hyunjin Park  
**arXiv:** `2607.14581`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Vision-Language Models, Brain MRI

**TL;DR:** A multi-LLM collaborative pipeline synthesizes paired 3D MRI-report data and visual instruction-tuning sets to enable report generation for brain oncology.

**Problem:** Medical VLMs are largely confined to 2D imaging (e.g., chest X-rays) because paired 3D imaging-text corpora barely exist, blocking report generation for volumetric modalities like brain MRI.

**Method:** The authors use multiple collaborating LLMs to generate and refine MRI reports, then build a visual instruction-tuning dataset that couples 3D brain oncology imaging with the generated text to train a report-generating VLM. This bypasses the scarcity of native paired 3D imaging-text supervision.

**Results:** The approach produces a usable 3D imaging-text instruction dataset and a VLM capable of generating brain oncology MRI reports where none was previously trainable.

**Why it matters:** Directly targets radiology report generation and vision-language foundation models for a 3D modality — a core interest and a rare extension beyond 2D chest X-ray work.


### 2. [Multimodal Semantic-Aware Contrastive Learning For False Negative Mitigation in 3D Medical Imaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14995)
**Authors:** Sara Ketabi, Matthias W. Wagner, Cynthia Hawkins, Uri Tabori et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.14995`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Multimodal Contrastive Learning, 3D Medical Imaging

**TL;DR:** A semantic-aware contrastive learning framework mitigates false negatives when aligning modalities in 3D medical imaging.

**Problem:** Standard multimodal contrastive learning treats all non-matched samples as negatives, but in healthcare many 'negatives' are semantically similar patients/scans, creating false negatives that corrupt the learned alignment.

**Method:** The method injects semantic awareness into the contrastive objective so that semantically related cross-modal pairs are not wrongly pushed apart, correcting the instance-based positive/negative assumption for 3D medical data. This yields cleaner cross-modal representations for downstream tasks.

**Results:** The semantic-aware formulation improves representation quality and downstream performance over conventional instance-based contrastive baselines on 3D medical imaging.

**Why it matters:** Squarely in self-supervised and multimodal representation learning for medical imaging — the reader's primary methodological interest.


### 3. [Pretraining Multiple Instance Learning Networks with Multi-Teacher Distillation from Pathology Slide Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14703)
**Authors:** Mingxi Fu, Jiawen Li, Renao Yan, Jiali Hu et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.14703`  
**Tags:** Histopathology, Foundation Models, Multiple Instance Learning

**TL;DR:** Multi-teacher distillation from pathology slide foundation models pretrains MIL aggregators so they no longer train from scratch per task.

**Problem:** Whole-slide image analysis relies on MIL aggregators that are trained from scratch for every downstream task using scarce slide-level labels, forcing them to learn aggregation and discrimination simultaneously and limiting robustness.

**Method:** The authors pretrain MIL networks via multi-teacher knowledge distillation from several pathology slide foundation models, transferring rich slide-level representations before fine-tuning. This decouples representation learning from the limited-label downstream objective.

**Results:** Pretrained MIL aggregators outperform train-from-scratch baselines and generalize better across computational pathology downstream tasks with limited labels.

**Why it matters:** Combines foundation models, distillation, and label-efficient learning for medical imaging (pathology), matching the reader's foundation-model and self-supervised interests.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [SceneBind: Binding What and Where Across Vision, Audio and Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15265)
**Authors:** Mingfei Chen, Zijun Cui, Ruoke Zhang, Hyeonggon Ryu et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.15265`  
**Tags:** Multimodal Foundation Models, Representation Learning, 3D Scene Understanding

**TL;DR:** SceneBind is an omni-modal encoder that binds semantics ('what') and 3D spatial location ('where') across vision, audio, and language.

**Problem:** Existing omni-modal encoders capture instance-level semantics well but lack explicit spatial structure, so they know what is present in a scene but not where it is in 3D.

**Method:** SceneBind represents each scene as a semantic-spatial entity, jointly encoding what and where across vision, audio, and language into a shared space. This augments instance-level binding with explicit 3D spatial grounding.

**Results:** The model achieves joint semantic and spatial understanding across modalities that instance-level omni-modal encoders cannot, improving spatially grounded multimodal tasks.

**Why it matters:** A CLIP-style multimodal binding foundation model with spatial grounding — directly relevant to the reader's interest in vision-language foundation models and representation learning.


### 2. [VideoChat3: Fully Open Video MLLM for Efficient and Generalist Video Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14935)
**Authors:** Xinhao Li, Yuhan Zhu, Xiangyu Zeng, Yuhao Dong et al. (27 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.14935`  
**Tags:** Video Understanding, Multimodal LLMs, Open-Source Foundation Models

**TL;DR:** VideoChat3 is a fully open, efficient generalist video MLLM that generalizes across diverse video types.

**Problem:** Open-source video MLLMs are narrow (effective only in specific domains) and computationally heavy, limiting real-world generalist video understanding.

**Method:** VideoChat3 is built as a fully open video MLLM with an efficiency-oriented design and training recipe spanning motion, long-video, and streaming interaction. It targets broad generalization while reducing computational demands.

**Results:** The model delivers generalist video understanding across heterogeneous video types with improved efficiency relative to existing open-source models.

**Why it matters:** A fully open multimodal foundation model for video — useful reference architecture for anyone building vision-language systems, including medical video applications.


### 3. [Beyond Single Expert: Harmonizing Diverse Visual Priors in MLLMs for Spatial Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15054)
**Authors:** Xiao Lin, Xiaohu Huang, Kai Han  
**arXiv:** `2607.15054`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Spatial Understanding, Visual Priors

**TL;DR:** A method harmonizes complementary spatial priors from multiple pretrained foundation models to improve spatial reasoning in MLLMs.

**Problem:** MLLMs gain spatial awareness by injecting priors from a single foundation model, but different foundation models provide complementary spatial cues that a single-expert approach fails to exploit.

**Method:** The authors integrate diverse visual priors from multiple foundation models and design a mechanism to harmonize their complementary (and sometimes conflicting) spatial signals within an MLLM. This yields richer, more reliable spatial representations.

**Results:** Combining and harmonizing multiple expert priors improves MLLM spatial understanding over single-expert baselines.

**Why it matters:** Advances how visual foundation-model priors are fused into MLLMs — methodologically relevant to the reader's vision-language and representation-learning focus.
