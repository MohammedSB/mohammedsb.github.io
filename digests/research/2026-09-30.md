## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Multi-Site Real-World Performance of Commercial AI for Pulmonary and Incidental Pulmonary Embolism Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37750)
**Authors:** Aawez Mansuri, Mohammadreza Chavoshi, Theodorus Dapamede, Wasif Bala et al. (16 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.37750`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Radiology AI, Clinical Validation

**TL;DR:** Multi-site retrospective evaluation of two FDA-cleared AI algorithms for pulmonary embolism detection on CTPA and incidental PE on chest CT across 30,678+ scans.

**Problem:** Pulmonary embolism is a leading cause of cardiovascular mortality, but the real-world performance of FDA-cleared AI detection models remains incompletely characterized at scale and across diverse clinical sites.

**Method:** The authors retrospectively evaluated two Aidoc Medical BriefCase AI algorithms—one for dedicated CTPA PE triage (n=30,678) and one for incidental PE detection on non-CTPA chest CT—across multiple sites, comparing AI outputs to radiologist reads and clinical ground truth.

**Results:** The study provides site-stratified performance characterization of both models, revealing variability in sensitivity and specificity across real-world deployment conditions not captured by regulatory trial data.

**Why it matters:** This large-scale real-world validation is directly relevant to understanding how radiology AI models generalize beyond controlled trial settings, informing deployment decisions and highlighting evaluation gaps for clinical AI.


### 2. [Multi-task learning for the automatic grading of enlarged perivascular space burden using MRI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37387)
**Authors:** Jesse Phitidis, William N. Whiteley, Joanna M. Wardlaw, Miguel O. Bernabeu et al. (14 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.37387`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Brain MRI, Multi-task Learning

**TL;DR:** Multi-task deep learning framework for automated grading of enlarged perivascular space burden from brain MRI, leveraging shared representations across related neuroimaging tasks.

**Problem:** Enlarged perivascular spaces (PVS) in brain MRI are a marker of poor brain health, but manual radiological grading is tedious and variable; automated grading could accelerate large-scale studies.

**Method:** The authors develop a multi-task learning model that jointly learns to segment and grade PVS burden following clinical grading scales, using shared encoder representations trained on MRI data with radiologist-derived labels.

**Results:** The multi-task approach improves grading performance over single-task baselines, demonstrating that joint learning of related tasks benefits the automated PVS burden assessment pipeline.

**Why it matters:** Automated brain MRI analysis for small vessel disease markers is a key clinical AI application; this work advances grading automation relevant to neuroimaging foundation model development and clinical decision support.


### 3. [PCaPaint: Prostate Cancer Inpainting by Mitigating Shortcut Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37350)
**Authors:** Levente Lippenszky, Hongxu Yang, Marcell Dömötör, Krisztian Koos et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.37350`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Diffusion Models, Prostate Cancer MRI

**TL;DR:** PCaPaint uses latent diffusion model inpainting to generate synthetic prostate cancer tumors in MRI while mitigating shortcut learning that causes the model to trivially reproduce training artifacts.

**Problem:** AI systems for prostate cancer detection are limited by scarce labeled data; synthetic tumor inpainting via latent diffusion models (LDMs) is promising but prone to shortcut learning, where the model copies apparent patterns rather than generating realistic tumors.

**Method:** PCaPaint identifies and mitigates shortcut learning in LDM-based tumor inpainting for multi-sequence prostate MRI by analyzing how the model exploits spurious correlations and introducing targeted regularization to encourage realistic synthesis.

**Results:** The approach produces more realistic and diverse synthetic prostate cancer lesions, reducing shortcut artifacts while maintaining large-volume synthesis capability suitable for data augmentation.

**Why it matters:** Synthetic data generation for rare pathology in MRI is a critical bottleneck for medical AI; this work directly addresses the reliability of diffusion-based augmentation in a challenging multi-sequence setting.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [TomoTransformer: Towards a Foundation Model for CT Reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37605)
**Authors:** AmirEhsan Khorashadizadeh, Benjamín Béjar  
**arXiv:** `2609.37605`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, CT Reconstruction, Foundation Models

**TL;DR:** TomoTransformer is a transformer-based foundation model for CT reconstruction that generalizes across varying projection counts, angles, and detector resolutions without retraining.

**Problem:** Supervised deep learning for sparse-view CT reconstruction is brittle under distribution shift; conventional models must be retrained whenever acquisition parameters change, limiting real-world deployment.

**Method:** TomoTransformer trains a transformer architecture that takes sinograms or FBP images as input and maps them to clean reconstructions in a parameter-conditioned manner, enabling zero-shot generalization across different acquisition geometries by encoding scan parameters as conditioning signals.

**Results:** The model achieves competitive reconstruction quality across diverse projection settings without task-specific retraining, demonstrating strong generalization as a foundation model for tomographic reconstruction.

**Why it matters:** A general-purpose CT reconstruction foundation model is of direct interest for radiology AI; the approach mirrors vision foundation model design principles applied to the sinogram-to-image domain.


### 2. [SAM Meets VLM: Parameter-Decoupled Full-Parameter Training for Unified Medical Reasoning and Segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37283)
**Authors:** Xuyang Cao, Enyou Liu, Jun Zhao, Zhuoyun Liu et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.37283`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Medical Image Segmentation, Multimodal LLMs

**TL;DR:** SAM Meets VLM introduces parameter-decoupled full-parameter training to unify a vision-language model with SAM-style segmentation for joint medical reasoning and localization.

**Problem:** Medical multimodal LLMs are expected to both answer clinical questions and localize visual evidence, but full-parameter training of a unified VLM+SAM architecture is difficult because image-level reasoning and pixel-level segmentation objectives interfere with each other.

**Method:** The authors decouple parameter updates for the VLM reasoning pathway and the SAM segmentation pathway during training, using a special <SEG> token to bridge language outputs to segmentation masks while maintaining gradient isolation between the two components.

**Results:** The decoupled training strategy enables stable full-parameter optimization, achieving strong performance on both medical VQA benchmarks and segmentation tasks simultaneously.

**Why it matters:** Unifying radiology report reasoning with pixel-level grounding is a central challenge for medical vision-language foundation models; this approach directly addresses the training instability that limits such systems.


### 3. [Med-RADIO: Reducing All Medical Domains Into One via Multi-Teacher Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37682)
**Authors:** Chu Zhang, Haoyu Jiang, Hongyuan Zhang, Hongbin Liu et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.37682`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Foundation Models, Knowledge Distillation

**TL;DR:** Med-RADIO uses multi-teacher distillation to compress specialized single-modality medical foundation models into a single generalist model that handles heterogeneous imaging modalities.

**Problem:** Medical foundation models either specialize in one imaging modality (losing generality) or attempt generalist training (suffering from modality heterogeneity); no principled method exists to combine the strengths of specialist models.

**Method:** Med-RADIO distills knowledge from multiple modality-specific teacher models into a single student using multi-teacher distillation, aligning representations across CT, MRI, X-ray, and other modalities through shared projection heads and cross-modal contrastive objectives.

**Results:** The distilled generalist model matches or exceeds individual specialist models on their respective modalities, demonstrating that multi-teacher distillation is an effective path to broad medical image understanding.

**Why it matters:** Building a single foundation model that handles the full spectrum of medical imaging modalities is a central goal for clinical AI; this multi-teacher approach offers a scalable recipe directly applicable to multimodal medical representation learning.
