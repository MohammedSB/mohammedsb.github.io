## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Harrison.Rad 1.5 Technical Report: A radiology foundation model that can draft reports from images, priors and clinical context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05880)
**Authors:** Suneeta Mall, Vladimir Nekrasov, Ashnil Kumar, Sajith Karunasena et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.05880`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Foundation Models, Vision-Language Models

**TL;DR:** Harrison.Rad 1.5 is a radiology foundation model that drafts structured reports directly from images, prior studies, and clinical context.

**Problem:** Imaging demand is outpacing the radiology workforce, creating reporting backlogs that recruitment alone cannot fix. The most direct lever is reducing the time radiologists spend interpreting images and drafting findings.

**Method:** The authors present a radiology foundation model that ingests images together with clinical history and prior studies to produce structured findings, mimicking the full reporting workflow rather than image-only captioning. It integrates multimodal priors and clinical context as conditioning inputs to draft reports.

**Results:** The technical report demonstrates the model can draft clinically usable structured reports by combining image interpretation with historical and contextual signals.

**Why it matters:** This is squarely in the reader's core interest of radiology report generation and vision-language foundation models, and integrating priors/clinical context is a frontier problem in the field.


### 2. [MAC-XA: Multi-view Anatomy-Correspondence Fusion for Coronary Stenosis Reporting from X-ray Angiography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06268)
**Authors:** Chen Jia, Baochang Zhang, Fatia Kusuma Dewi, Amir Yousefi et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.06268`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Multi-View Fusion, Coronary Angiography

**TL;DR:** MAC-XA generates coronary stenosis reports from multi-view X-ray angiography by explicitly modeling cross-projection anatomical correspondence.

**Problem:** Coronary X-ray angiography report generation is largely unexplored, and single-view modeling is incomplete because 3D vascular topology causes projection-dependent branch overlap and foreshortening. This makes lesion localization and stenosis grading unstable.

**Method:** MAC-XA is a multi-view anatomy-correspondence fusion framework that aligns branches across projections to resolve overlap and foreshortening. It fuses complementary view information to stabilize lesion localization and stenosis grading before generating the report.

**Results:** The approach establishes multi-view reasoning as a route to more reliable automated stenosis reporting from angiography.

**Why it matters:** A concrete radiology report generation task with a multi-view geometric fusion twist, directly relevant to the reader's report-generation and medical imaging focus.


### 3. [TMF-RSE: Tri-Modal Fusion with Regional Semantics and Evidential Uncertainty for Lung Severity Scoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06356)
**Authors:** Fadi Abdeladhim Zidi, Salah Eddine Bekhouche, Abdellah Zakaria Sellam, Gaby Maroun et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.06356`  
**Tags:** Chest X-ray, Multimodal Fusion, Uncertainty Estimation

**TL;DR:** TMF-RSE fuses appearance, segmentation-mask structure, and regional semantics with evidential uncertainty to score lung disease severity from chest imaging.

**Problem:** Accurate lung disease severity quantification from chest imaging is critical for triage and resource allocation but is hard to make both accurate and trustworthy. Existing methods often ignore structural and regional-semantic cues and provide no calibrated uncertainty.

**Method:** TMF-RSE is a tri-modal deep learning framework combining 2D chest appearance features, structural features from lung segmentation masks, and regional semantic features. It adds evidential uncertainty estimation to produce calibrated severity scores.

**Results:** The fusion of appearance, structure, and regional semantics with evidential modeling improves lung severity scoring while supplying uncertainty estimates.

**Why it matters:** Combines chest imaging, segmentation-driven structure, and uncertainty—hitting the reader's chest X-ray analysis and evaluation/reliability interests.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Segmentation before Answering: Pixel Grounding for MLLM Visual Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05798)
**Authors:** Yake Wei, Yuan Wang, Fengyun Rao, Jing Lyu et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.05798`  
**Tags:** Referring Segmentation, Multimodal Large Language Models, Visual Reasoning

**TL;DR:** SegAnswer performs pixel-level grounding (segmentation) before answering to make MLLM visual reasoning more reliable when zooming into regions of interest.

**Problem:** MLLMs that 'think with images' rely on zooming into regions (usually via bounding boxes) but hallucinate these regions prolifically, degrading reasoning. Bounding boxes are too coarse for precise grounding.

**Method:** The paper proposes segmentation before answering, replacing coarse bounding-box zooming with pixel-level grounding of regions of interest during the reasoning process. The MLLM segments the relevant region and then conditions its answer on that finer localization.

**Results:** Grounding at the pixel level before answering improves the fidelity of interleaved visual-language reasoning over box-based zooming.

**Why it matters:** Directly connects referring segmentation with vision-language reasoning—both central to the reader's CV and VLM interests—and the method transfers naturally to medical grounding tasks.


### 2. [Vision as Unified Multimodal Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06560)
**Authors:** Xiaoyang Han, Jianhua Li, Kewang Deng, Zukai Chen et al. (17 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.06560`  
**Tags:** Unified Multimodal Models, Foundation Models, Vision Generation

**TL;DR:** SenseNova-Vision reformulates computer vision as unified multimodal generation, expressing heterogeneous visual tasks in a single model's native text and image spaces without task-specific heads.

**Problem:** Vision systems typically require task-specific architectures for each task. A unified formulation that handles diverse visual tasks through natural-language instructions would greatly simplify deployment.

**Method:** SenseNova-Vision casts vision as unified multimodal generation, using natural-language instructions plus optional visual prompts to specify tasks, target regions/views, and decoding conventions. Tasks are expressed in the model's native text and image generation spaces rather than through specialized architectures.

**Results:** A single instruction-driven generative backbone handles heterogeneous visual tasks without per-task architectures.

**Why it matters:** Represents the CLIP-successor trajectory toward unified vision-language foundation models the reader tracks, with clear implications for generalist medical imaging models.


### 3. [Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion: A Tri-Mode Language Model Unifying Autoregressive, Diffusion, and Self-Speculation Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05722)
**Authors:** Yonggan Fu, Lexington Whalen, Abhinav Garg, Chengyue Wu et al. (26 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.05722`  
**Tags:** Language Model Architectures, Diffusion Language Models, Efficient Inference

**TL;DR:** Nemotron-Labs-Diffusion unifies autoregressive, diffusion, and self-speculation decoding in one language model trained with a joint AR-diffusion objective.

**Problem:** AR and diffusion decoding each have distinct throughput/quality tradeoffs across deployment settings, and models are usually locked into one paradigm. A single model that can switch modes would sustain high throughput across concurrency levels.

**Method:** The authors train a tri-mode LM with a joint AR-diffusion objective so it can switch among autoregressive, diffusion, and self-speculation decoding at inference. They show the two objectives are complementary during training and enable mode switching for throughput.

**Results:** The unified model sustains high throughput across deployment settings and concurrency levels by selecting the decoding mode that fits the workload.

**Why it matters:** A notable training/architecture advance in efficient generative modeling and diffusion-LM unification, relevant to the reader's interest in new methods and training techniques.
