1. ALTER: Modeling Longitudinal Changes via Regional Differencing for 3D CT Report Generation

Dongchen Li, Jitao Liang, Wei Li  ·  2608.05615

Radiology Report Generation3D CT ImagingLongitudinal Modeling

TL;DR: ALTER generates 3D CT radiology reports by explicitly differencing anatomical regions against a patient's prior scan rather than describing only the current study.

Problem: Automatic report generation from 3D CT remains hard, and existing region-aware methods describe only the current examination. Real clinical CT reporting is often a follow-up task where the clinically salient content is what changed relative to the patient's prior scan.

Method: The method models longitudinal change through regional differencing: anatomical regions are aligned across the current and prior 3D CT volumes, and region-level difference representations are computed and fed to the report decoder. This makes patient-specific temporal evidence, rather than a single-timepoint appearance, the conditioning signal for text generation.

Results: The authors report improved accuracy and completeness of generated 3D CT reports over current-exam-only region-modeling baselines, with particular gains on change-describing content.

Why it matters: This sits directly on the reader's radiology-report-generation axis and extends it to the 3D/longitudinal setting that most CXR-centric work ignores; the regional-differencing formulation is also a concrete idea for change-aware report metrics.

2. Curia-MAE: Multi-Modal Multi-Anatomy MAE Pre-Training for 3D Medical Image Segmentation

Théo Danielou, Antoine Saporta, Léo Alberge, Corentin Dancette  ·  2608.05844

Self-Supervised LearningMedical Image SegmentationFoundation Models

TL;DR: Curia-MAE is a multi-modal, multi-anatomy masked-autoencoder pretraining recipe for 3D medical images aimed at closing the gap between frozen radiology foundation encoders and nnU-Net on dense segmentation.

Problem: Radiology foundation models are usually evaluated on classification-style transfer, while dense 3D segmentation is underrepresented. With the encoder frozen, existing pretrained models still lose to nnU-Net trained from scratch, which undercuts the foundation-model premise for segmentation.

Method: The authors pretrain a 3D MAE across multiple imaging modalities and multiple anatomical regions, so the encoder sees heterogeneous volumetric anatomy rather than one modality/organ family. Downstream 3D segmentation is then performed by training only lightweight decoder layers on top of the frozen encoder, isolating representation quality from decoder capacity.

Results: The paper reports that this multi-modal multi-anatomy MAE pretraining substantially narrows — and on their benchmarks closes — the frozen-encoder gap to nnU-Net for 3D segmentation.

Why it matters: It is a direct test of whether self-supervised pretraining actually produces reusable dense representations in medical imaging, which bears on any plan to build downstream heads (including referring/promptable segmentation) on a frozen medical encoder.

3. DistMedVL: Distributional Vision-Language Alignment for Uncertainty-Aware Medical Image Segmentation

Jiaxuan Li, Qing Xu, Xiangjian He, Yue Li et al. (7 authors)  ·  2608.05683

Vision-Language ModelsMedical Image SegmentationUncertainty Estimation

TL;DR: DistMedVL replaces deterministic image-text matching in medical vision-language segmentation with a distributional alignment that models uncertainty in both modalities.

Problem: Text-guided medical segmentation methods align a single visual embedding to a single text embedding, which ignores aleatoric uncertainty from ambiguous lesion/organ boundaries and epistemic uncertainty from limited, noisy clinical annotations and reports.

Method: Instead of point embeddings, each modality is represented as a distribution, and cross-modal alignment is defined between these distributions so that ambiguous regions and vague report phrasing widen rather than corrupt the match. The resulting uncertainty estimates are propagated into the segmentation decoder to modulate predictions where evidence is weak.

Results: The authors report improved segmentation accuracy alongside better-calibrated uncertainty relative to deterministic vision-language segmentation baselines under clinically realistic ambiguity.

Why it matters: This is the closest thing on today's list to medical referring segmentation, and the distributional-alignment idea is a plausible upgrade path for CLIP-style medical encoders where report text is inherently underspecified.

1. SCI-CLIP: Segment-Centric Inference with Reference Memory for Training-Free Open-Vocabulary Segmentation

Mohamad Zamini, Diksha Shukla  ·  2608.05627

Open-Vocabulary SegmentationCLIPTraining-Free Inference

TL;DR: SCI-CLIP makes training-free open-vocabulary segmentation work at the segment level instead of the patch level, adding a reference memory to correct retrieval errors.

Problem: Frozen CLIP-style features are produced per patch, but dense prediction needs a unit that governs feature interaction, spatial support, context recovery, and correction together. Patch-level inference is the missing abstraction that limits training-free open-vocabulary segmentation quality.

Method: The framework reorganizes inference around segments: features are aggregated and interact within segment-level units that define spatial support and recover context lost by patch-wise scoring. A reference memory of exemplar representations is then used to correct retrieval-based class assignment for segments whose direct text similarity is unreliable.

Results: SCI-CLIP reports improved training-free open-vocabulary segmentation over patch-level attribution baselines across standard benchmarks, without any segmentation training.

Why it matters: Directly on the reader's CLIP/SLIP/MaskCLIP line: it is a recipe for extracting dense, language-conditioned masks from a frozen VLM, which transfers cleanly to medical open-vocabulary and referring segmentation where dense labels are scarce.

2. The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use: A Causal Audit of Thinking with Images

Zhiheng Wang, Bo Peng, Lai Wei, Chaochao Lu  ·  2608.06270

Multimodal LLMsVisual ReasoningCausal Analysis

TL;DR: A causal audit finds that 'thinking with images' crop-and-zoom operations often do not actually supply the visual evidence models are credited with using.

Problem: Multimodal LLMs equipped with active visual operations frequently gain little or nothing over direct inference while burning far more tokens, sometimes cropping irrelevant regions and failing questions direct inference answers correctly. It is unclear whether the returned crops causally drive the answer.

Method: The authors design an intervention-based audit that manipulates the returned visual evidence — substituting, ablating, or corrupting the crops — and measures whether the model's answer actually depends on that content. This separates genuine visual grounding from the confounded case where the model would have answered the same way regardless of what the tool returned.

Results: The audit finds that a substantial share of apparent tool-use gains is not causally attributable to the retrieved visual evidence, exposing the paradigm's headline benefits as partly illusory.

Why it matters: A useful methodological template for anyone evaluating whether a medical VLM's stated visual evidence (attended region, cited finding) actually drives its output — the same confound that makes radiology report evaluation hard.

3. TruthLens: Object Hallucination Detection via Self-Evaluating Truthfulness Scores in LVLMs

Yanqi Wu, Runhe Lai, Xinhua Lu, Qichao Chen et al. (8 authors)  ·  2608.05616

Hallucination DetectionLarge Vision-Language ModelsModel Interpretability

TL;DR: TruthLens detects object hallucinations in LVLMs by reading a truthfulness signal from hidden states, where real and hallucinated object tokens remain separable, instead of from the LM head.

Problem: Object hallucination still blocks trustworthy LVLM deployment. The authors observe that real and hallucinated object tokens are clearly separable in intermediate hidden representations, but that separability is largely destroyed by the time signals reach the language-modeling head.

Method: TruthLens computes self-evaluating truthfulness scores from the internal representations of candidate object tokens rather than from output logits, preserving the discriminative structure the LM head washes out. The scores are produced by the model itself, so detection requires no external verifier or reference annotation.

Results: The method reports stronger hallucination detection than logit- and confidence-based baselines while remaining reference-free and cheap to run at decode time.

Why it matters: Reference-free hallucination detection from internal states is exactly the primitive needed for factuality metrics on generated radiology reports, where hallucinated findings are the dominant failure and reference reports are expensive.
