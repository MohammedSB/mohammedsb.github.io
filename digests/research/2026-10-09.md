## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Efficient Multi-Granularity Knowledge Transfer for Radiology Report Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11303)
**Authors:** Xubin Zhong, Zheyu Zhang, Wenjian Qin, Ning Wen  
**arXiv:** `2610.11303`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Chest X-ray, Knowledge Transfer

**TL;DR:** This paper improves radiology report generation from X-rays by transferring medical knowledge at several levels of granularity.

**Problem:** Report generation requires both spotting diseases and describing them in professional clinical language. Existing methods often underuse medical knowledge when describing findings, which produces generic or clinically imprecise reports.

**Method:** The authors propose an efficient multi-granularity knowledge transfer framework. It injects medical knowledge at different levels, from coarse disease-level concepts to finer descriptive detail, into the image-to-report pipeline. The goal is to improve disease identification and the quality of the descriptions without heavy compute overhead.

**Results:** The abstract is truncated, so specific numbers are not available. The framework is presented as improving the clinical accuracy and descriptiveness of generated X-ray reports over existing methods.

**Why it matters:** This is core work on your topic of radiology report generation from chest X-rays. It is worth checking which clinical-efficacy and report-quality metrics they use, given your interest in evaluating generated reports.


### 2. [Beyond Report Imitation: Clinically Aware Multi-Image Ultrasound Report Generation from Visible Evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11610)
**Authors:** Yuchen Yang, Xin Wang, Lufan Wang, Yinghong Pan et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.11610`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Ultrasound, Clinical Alignment

**TL;DR:** This paper generates multi-image ultrasound reports that contain only findings verifiable from the available key frames, instead of copying full archived reports.

**Problem:** Archived ultrasound reports describe the whole dynamic exam, but models only see a few saved key frames. Training a model to imitate the raw report therefore teaches it to state findings the images cannot support, a mismatch between the model's clinical behavior and its visual evidence.

**Method:** The authors reframe report generation around visible evidence. Report content is filtered or re-aligned so that supervision matches what the input images actually support. Evidence is aggregated across multiple views, and training is made clinically aware so the model's statements stay grounded in the visual input.

**Results:** The abstract is truncated. The approach is reported to produce more clinically faithful, image-grounded reports than training on raw reports.

**Why it matters:** The paper directly addresses hallucination and faithfulness in report generation, a central concern for report-generation metrics. The 'verifiable from the image' idea could carry over to chest X-ray report evaluation and to building training targets.


### 3. [PathLang: A Language-Centered Benchmark for Vision-Language Models in Computational Pathology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11329)
**Authors:** Fanqi Cheng, Kuo Gong, Shangke Liu, Beidi Zhao et al. (18 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.11329`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Histopathology, Benchmark

**TL;DR:** PathLang is a benchmark that tests how robust pathology vision-language models are to realistic variation in clinical language.

**Problem:** Pathology VLM benchmarks mostly use fixed, canonical prompts or perturb generic templates. As a result, they treat language as fixed even though clinicians phrase queries in many different ways.

**Method:** PathLang makes language a controlled evaluation axis. It systematically varies prompts with clinically realistic paraphrases, terminology, and phrasing while keeping the images fixed, then measures how CLIP-style and generative pathology VLMs respond.

**Results:** The abstract is truncated. The benchmark appears to show that pathology VLMs with strong visual perception remain fragile when the wording of prompts changes.

**Why it matters:** This is a useful evaluation lens for CLIP-style medical foundation models. The same prompt-robustness analysis could be applied to chest X-ray VLMs and to zero-shot classification with radiology text encoders.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Rethinking Contrastive Loss in CLIP Post-training: A Complementary Framework with Frozen Text Encoder](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11374)
**Authors:** Zidan Wang, Yaqian Li, Xiaokai Zhang, Kaiwen Long et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.11374`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, CLIP, Contrastive Learning

**TL;DR:** This paper argues that the contrastive loss remains useful for CLIP post-training if the text encoder is frozen, and adds a complementary framework around it.

**Problem:** Recent work claims the standard contrastive loss is unsuitable for post-training CLIP because small batches cause catastrophic forgetting. That claim has pushed methods toward distillation-only objectives.

**Method:** The authors re-examine the contrastive objective and identify a frozen text encoder as the key to stable post-training. They pair the contrastive loss with complementary objectives so that the vision encoder improves while keeping its alignment with the original text embedding space.

**Results:** The abstract is truncated. The method is reported to refine CLIP without catastrophic forgetting and to benefit downstream use, including as a VLM vision encoder.

**Why it matters:** This is directly relevant to CLIP-style medical foundation models. Freezing the text encoder during domain post-training is a practical recipe for adapting CLIP to radiology with small batches.


### 2. [ContourVLA: A Closed-Loop Perception-Action Contour Policy for Generalized Referring Expression Segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12107)
**Authors:** Ruicheng Zhang, Kaiwen Shen, Jiaqi Hou, Shuhan Yang et al. (9 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.12107`  
**Tags:** Referring Segmentation, Vision-Language Models, Closed-Loop Perception

**TL;DR:** ContourVLA recasts generalized referring expression segmentation as a closed-loop policy that predicts object contours step by step.

**Problem:** Generalized referring expression segmentation (GRES) must use high-level semantics to identify a variable number of referents and fine visual evidence to trace their boundaries precisely. Cascaded vision-language pipelines with static feature interfaces and single-pass mask prediction struggle to do both.

**Method:** Borrowing the vision-language-action paradigm, ContourVLA treats segmentation as a perception-action loop. The model iteratively predicts and refines object contours, looking back at visual evidence at each step instead of decoding a mask in one pass.

**Results:** The abstract is truncated. The method is reported to improve multi-target and boundary-precise segmentation on GRES benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** This is directly related to your referring segmentation interest. Iterative, contour-based closed-loop prediction could transfer to language-guided segmentation of medical images, such as lesions described in reports.


### 3. [Mid-Training Language Models on Raw Video](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11019)
**Authors:** Jaedong Hwang, Xiaoqian Shen, Ernie Chang, Changsheng Zhao et al. (12 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.11019`  
**Tags:** Multimodal LLMs, Video Pretraining, Self-Supervised Learning

**TL;DR:** This paper shows that raw, uncaptioned web video can be used as mid-training data for a pretrained language model by predicting the next visual token.

**Problem:** Multimodal LLMs learn mostly from paired image-text data or annotated video. The large supply of unlabeled web video is rarely used to further train an existing language model.

**Method:** Video frames are encoded into continuous visual tokens. The pretrained LM is mid-trained to predict the next visual token, with no captions and no text loss, which is a self-supervised objective applied directly inside the LLM.

**Results:** The abstract is truncated. The authors report that this caption-free mid-training produces useful visual and world knowledge in the language model.

**Why it matters:** Self-supervised multimodal pretraining without paired text is relevant where paired reports are scarce, for example unreported imaging studies. It suggests a way to bring visual knowledge into LLMs from unpaired data.
