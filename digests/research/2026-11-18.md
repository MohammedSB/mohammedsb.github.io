## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Visual Harness Scales General VLMs to Medical Images](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22815)
**Authors:** Shaowen Wan, Hanqi Jiang, Zihao Wu, Junhao Chen et al. (9 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.22815`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Medical Imaging, Agentic Tool Use

**TL;DR:** A visual harness gives general-purpose VLMs interactive visual tools and a structured way to manage diagnostic evidence, which substantially improves their performance across diverse medical imaging tasks without medical fine-tuning.

**Problem:** General VLMs usually trail specialist medical models on medical images, and the field's default fix is costly domain fine-tuning. It is not clear how much of that gap comes from how the model inspects the image rather than from what it knows.

**Method:** The authors build a medical visual harness. It wraps a general VLM with interactive visual tools, such as region inspection, and with systematic tracking of diagnostic evidence. The model explores the image step by step and gathers evidence before it reaches a decision, rather than answering in a single pass.

**Results:** According to the abstract, the harness substantially improves general VLMs across diverse medical imaging tasks. The abstract was truncated, so exact benchmarks and margins were not available.

**Why it matters:** This applies directly to chest X-ray and radiology VLM work. It suggests that inference-time scaffolding, rather than retraining, may close much of the gap between generalist and medical models. It could also serve as a baseline or an add-on for grounded report generation.


### 2. [PeriaTrace: Tooth-Level Grounding Diagnosis and Patient-Facing Explanations from Periapical Radiographs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23012)
**Authors:** Zhenhuan Zhou, Yuchen Zhang, Jingbo Zhu, Shiqing Ma et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.23012`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Visual Grounding, Dental Radiography

**TL;DR:** PeriaTrace is a three-stage framework for periapical radiographs: it diagnoses each tooth, generates a report grounded in image evidence, and produces explanations written for patients.

**Problem:** Reading periapical radiographs and writing reports takes clinicians a lot of time, and technical language limits what patients understand. Existing automated methods also lack tooth-level links between reported findings and the image regions that support them.

**Method:** The pipeline has three stages. It first diagnoses each tooth. It then writes a visually grounded report in which each finding is tied to a localized tooth and its image evidence. Finally, it rewrites the findings as plain-language explanations for patients.

**Results:** The abstract was truncated before quantitative results. The key contribution is an end-to-end grounded pipeline that runs from diagnosis to report to patient explanation.

**Why it matters:** This is a close template for grounded radiology report generation, where each finding is traceable to image regions. The patient-facing stage also connects to AI for medical communication and education. The same design could carry over to chest X-ray reporting.


### 3. [Efficient Self-Supervised Adaptation of 3D Abdominal Vision-Language Model for Institution-Specific HCC Classification via Full Fine-Tuning and PEFT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22504)
**Authors:** Febryan Putra Kartika, Cheng-Yu Ma, Ying-Jia Lin, Chi-Tung Cheng et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.22504`  
**Tags:** Medical Vision-Language Models, Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning, 3D CT

**TL;DR:** A pretrained 3D abdominal CT vision-language model is adapted to a new institution for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) classification, comparing self-supervised full fine-tuning with parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) on paired CT scans and radiology reports.

**Problem:** Medical VLMs learn image–text relationships, but how to adapt them to institution-specific clinical tasks, especially under shifts in acquisition protocols, has received little study.

**Method:** The authors take a pretrained 3D medical VLM and fine-tune it on paired abdominal CT scans and radiology reports from a different institution. They use self-supervised image–report adaptation and compare full fine-tuning against PEFT strategies before evaluating HCC classification.

**Results:** The abstract (truncated) frames the study as a comparison of adaptation efficiency and accuracy under cross-institution acquisition shift. Specific metrics were not visible.

**Why it matters:** This is a practical study of using radiology reports as free supervision to adapt CLIP-style 3D medical VLMs to local data. Its findings on full fine-tuning versus PEFT apply to deploying chest X-ray or CT foundation models across hospitals.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Scaling Laws for Vision-Language Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22351)
**Authors:** Seng Pei Liew, Teppei Suzuki, Seiya Tokui  
**arXiv:** `2611.22351`  
**Tags:** Scaling Laws, Vision-Language Models, Multimodal Pretraining

**TL;DR:** The paper derives scaling laws comparing late fusion and early fusion when building vision-language models.

**Problem:** Most VLMs use late fusion: an off-the-shelf language model is further pretrained on image–text data. Early fusion, which mixes modalities from the start of pretraining, is gaining interest, but the basic compute and data trade-offs between the two approaches have not been characterized.

**Method:** The authors run controlled pretraining experiments that vary the fusion strategy, model size, and data mixture. They fit scaling laws describing how performance changes with compute under each fusion regime.

**Results:** The abstract was truncated. The contribution is a set of quantitative scaling relationships that indicate when early fusion beats late fusion and how to allocate compute between modalities.

**Why it matters:** Anyone building or pretraining multimodal medical foundation models has to choose a fusion strategy and an image–text data mix. These scaling laws give principled guidance where practitioners currently rely on guesswork.


### 2. [Learning Grounded Reasoning from Human Gaze](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23209)
**Authors:** Gabriel Sarch, Declan Campbell, Nicholas Budny, Kia Ghods et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.23209`  
**Tags:** Visual Grounding, Human Gaze, VLM Reasoning

**TL;DR:** The authors study how to use human gaze data to teach VLMs grounded visual reasoning while filtering out gaze noise and redundancy that transfers poorly to models.

**Problem:** Human gaze shows how people systematically search an image for task-relevant evidence, which makes it a promising supervision signal for grounded reasoning in VLMs. Raw gaze, however, also contains noise and redundant fixations that may not help a model.

**Method:** The paper examines which aspects of human gaze data transfer usefully to VLMs and builds a training approach that turns gaze into structured evidence-seeking supervision for visually grounded reasoning. Exact details were truncated in the abstract.

**Results:** The abstract was truncated. It indicates that gaze-guided training improves grounded visual reasoning once noise in the gaze data is handled.

**Why it matters:** This is highly relevant to radiology, where eye-tracking datasets such as REFLACX and EGD-CXR exist. The method could inform gaze-supervised chest X-ray VLMs and grounded report generation, and it connects to medical education research on how experts search images.


### 3. [When AUC Meets Generative Reconstruction: A Unified Principle for Balanced Visual Representation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22094)
**Authors:** Boyu Han, Qianqian Xu, Shilong Bao, Zhiyong Yang et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2611.22094`  
**Tags:** Visual Representation Learning, Contrastive Learning, Generative Reconstruction

**TL;DR:** The paper proposes a unified AUC-based principle that balances contrastive discrimination with generative reconstruction when training visual encoders for multimodal LLMs.

**Problem:** Visual encoders for multimodal LLMs need both fine-grained perceptual detail and discriminative, semantically separated features. Contrastive learning suppresses variation within a class, while reconstruction keeps detail but weakens discrimination, so the two objectives pull against each other.

**Method:** The authors reformulate the objective from an AUC-maximization perspective and combine it with generative reconstruction under one principle. The goal is representations that separate classes well while still keeping fine-grained visual detail.

**Results:** The abstract was truncated. The method is claimed to produce more balanced visual representations for multimodal LLMs than purely contrastive or purely generative encoders.

**Why it matters:** This speaks directly to CLIP-style versus masked-reconstruction pretraining, a central design choice for medical image encoders, where subtle findings such as small chest X-ray opacities need both fine detail and semantic discrimination.
