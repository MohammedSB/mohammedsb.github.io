## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [PerFact: Perception-Derived Fact Prompting for 3D Brain MRI Report Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17926)
**Authors:** Jianyu Sun, Zhenxuan Zhang, Guang Yang, Peter J. Lally  
**arXiv:** `2608.17926`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Generation, Brain MRI, Vision-Language Models

**TL;DR:** PerFact shows that for 3D multi-sequence brain MRI report generation, injecting perception-derived clinical facts into the prompt matters far more than scaling the vision backbone or medical pretraining.

**Problem:** Radiology report generation has been developed almost exclusively on 2D chest radiographs, where progress is usually chased through bigger backbones or domain pretraining. On volumetric, multi-sequence, multi-disease brain MRI, off-the-shelf medical and radiology VLMs transfer poorly and the usual scaling levers stop paying off.

**Method:** The authors reframe the task around perception rather than model capacity: dedicated perception modules extract structured, image-grounded facts from the 3D multi-sequence volumes, and these facts are supplied as explicit prompts that condition report generation. This decouples 'what is visible in the volume' from 'how it is phrased', so the language model composes reports from verified perceptual evidence instead of inferring findings directly from weak volumetric features.

**Results:** Fact prompting yields substantially better reports on volumetric brain MRI than zero-shot medical/radiology VLMs and than simply enlarging or medically pretraining the backbone, with gains concentrated in clinical/finding-level correctness rather than surface fluency.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct challenge to the chest-X-ray-centric orthodoxy of report generation and to the assumption that better backbones drive better reports — highly pertinent if you work on report generation architectures and on metrics that reward clinical fact coverage over n-gram overlap.


### 2. [BrainNorm: A Foundation Model that knows Normal via Semantic Atlas Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17521)
**Authors:** Madhumitha Venkatesh, Shanawaj S Madarkar, Konda Reddy Mopuri  
**arXiv:** `2608.17521`  
**Tags:** Medical Foundation Models, Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining, Structural MRI

**TL;DR:** BrainNorm is a normative brain MRI foundation model trained with CLIP-style language-image contrastive learning on ~66,000 healthy T1-weighted scans, representing each brain as a set of atlas-parcel embeddings in a Semantic Atlas Latent space.

**Problem:** Most medical foundation models are trained to discriminate disease, which conflates pathology with normal anatomical and age-related variation and gives no calibrated notion of what 'normal' looks like for a given subject. Normative modeling exists in neuroimaging but has not been combined with large-scale vision-language pretraining.

**Method:** The model applies language-image contrastive pretraining exclusively on healthy cohorts spanning a wide age range, learning a Semantic Atlas Latent (SAL) space in which each scan decomposes into parcel-level embeddings aligned to an anatomical atlas. Because the latent space is parcel-structured and healthy-only, it yields parcel-specific healthy aging trajectories that act as reference templates; deviation from those trajectories provides a localized, interpretable abnormality signal at inference time.

**Results:** Trained and evaluated on roughly 66,000 T1w structural MRI scans, BrainNorm learns parcel-wise healthy aging templates and supports normative deviation-based assessment, positioning it as a general-purpose brain MRI encoder rather than a single-disease classifier.

**Why it matters:** It is a clean instance of CLIP-style multimodal pretraining repurposed for medical imaging with an anatomically structured latent space — directly on-topic for self-supervised/multimodal medical representation learning, and the 'know normal first' framing transfers naturally to chest imaging.


### 3. [Counterfactual Anatomy-guided Spatial-Temporal Decoding for Annotation-Free Hallucination Mitigation in Medical VLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17427)
**Authors:** Yifan Lu, Adinath Dukre, Abhijit Das, Ziyun Zou et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.17427`  
**Tags:** Medical Vision-Language Models, Hallucination Mitigation, Contrastive Decoding

**TL;DR:** An annotation-free decoding method uses counterfactual anatomical perturbations plus spatial-temporal aggregation to suppress ungrounded statements from medical VLMs at inference time.

**Problem:** Medical VLMs score well on visual question answering yet still produce clinically unsupported claims that are not grounded in the image. Existing decoding-time mitigations are either anatomy-agnostic or depend on ground-truth annotations/region labels that are unavailable in deployment.

**Method:** The method constructs counterfactual versions of the visual input by perturbing or ablating anatomy-relevant evidence, then contrasts the model's token distributions between original and counterfactual views so that tokens surviving only under the intact anatomy are up-weighted. A spatial-temporal decoding scheme aggregates this signal across regions and generation steps, requiring no additional annotation, retraining, or external grounding supervision.

**Results:** The approach reduces hallucinated clinical statements relative to standard and contrastive-decoding baselines on medical VQA/generation settings while preserving answer utility, and does so without any labeled grounding data.

**Why it matters:** Faithfulness-at-decoding is the practical counterpart to report-generation metrics: if hallucinations can be suppressed without annotations, it changes both how generative radiology systems are built and what evaluation must measure.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [MoE-ViE: Mixture of Experts Vision Encoder for Efficient Image and Video Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17402)
**Authors:** Bonan Zhang, Shiyu Dong, Quan Hung Tran, Katharina Gschwind et al. (12 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.17402`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Mixture-of-Experts, Efficient Vision Encoders

**TL;DR:** MoE-ViE systematically explores the Mixture-of-Experts design space for CLIP-style vision encoders, scaling encoder capacity for image and video understanding without paying dense compute cost.

**Problem:** Vision encoders are the capacity bottleneck of vision-language models, but dense scaling raises FLOPs and inference latency. MoE has unlocked cheap scaling in LLMs, yet its design space for CLIP-style contrastive vision encoders is largely unexplored at competitive scale.

**Method:** The paper builds a sparse Mixture-of-Experts vision encoder and ablates the key design axes — where experts are inserted, routing granularity (token vs. patch-group), expert count and top-k, and how routing interacts with the contrastive objective — for both image and video inputs. The resulting encoder plugs into standard VLM pipelines as a drop-in replacement for a dense ViT tower, so added capacity is activated conditionally per token rather than for every input.

**Results:** The MoE encoder matches or exceeds strong dense baselines on image and video understanding benchmarks at markedly lower activated compute and latency, with the reported ablations identifying which routing/placement choices actually carry the gains.

**Why it matters:** If you build or fine-tune CLIP-style encoders, this is the design-space map for making the vision tower bigger without making it slower — directly applicable to scaling medical image-text encoders where compute, not data, is often the binding constraint.


### 2. [Recirculation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17981)
**Authors:** Michael C. Mozer, Shoaib Ahmed Siddiqui, Danny Sawyer, Sunny Sanyal et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.17981`  
**Tags:** Transformer Architectures, Inference-Time Compute, LLM Reasoning

**TL;DR:** Recirculation adds an inference-time recirculation of hidden states through off-the-shelf foundation models, cutting perplexity and lifting generation and reasoning accuracy with essentially no extra latency during generation.

**Problem:** Feedforward transformers update each token's state in a single forward pass, so a state is committed before the model has seen how it will be used — a structural limit on how much computation any one token can receive. Retraining a model to fix this is prohibitively expensive.

**Method:** The authors introduce an architectural enhancement applied at inference to already-trained models: hidden states are recirculated through the network so representations are refined with additional passes rather than fixed by a single sweep. The cost is paid in the prefill phase, which must be processed serially, while autoregressive generation proceeds at effectively unchanged latency.

**Results:** Across generation and reasoning tasks, recirculation markedly reduces perplexity and improves accuracy for off-the-shelf foundation models, with no fine-tuning and no meaningful generation-time slowdown.

**Why it matters:** A training-free, model-agnostic accuracy gain is unusually cheap to adopt, and the framing — extra computation per token at inference — is the same lever behind current reasoning-model gains, applicable to any frozen VLM or LLM in your stack.


### 3. [Abra: Scaling Diffusion Image Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17286)
**Authors:** Kyle Chickering, Wei-An Lin, Swayam Bhanded, Dan Saunders et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.17286`  
**Tags:** Diffusion Models, Scaling Laws, Text-to-Image Generation

**TL;DR:** Abra delivers a systematic compute-optimal scaling law study for text-to-image diffusion, training a controlled family of flow-matching transformers from 10^19 to 10^22 FLOPs.

**Problem:** Compute-optimal scaling laws guide frontier language model training but remain largely uncharted for visual generation, leaving practitioners guessing at the right model-size/data/step allocation for diffusion training.

**Method:** The authors train Abra, a deliberately controlled family of flow-matching transformers, across three orders of magnitude of compute, holding architecture and data pipeline fixed so that model size, token/step budget, and compute can be varied cleanly. From these runs they fit scaling relations and derive compute-optimal allocation recommendations for text-to-image diffusion at budgets well beyond those probed by prior studies.

**Results:** The study yields fitted scaling laws and compute-optimal frontier allocations for diffusion image training, reaching substantially larger compute budgets than earlier work and identifying where prior recommendations mis-allocate parameters versus training steps.

**Why it matters:** Generative image models are increasingly used for medical data synthesis and augmentation, and this gives a principled budget-allocation recipe instead of folklore — useful whenever you must decide how big a generator to train under a fixed GPU budget.
