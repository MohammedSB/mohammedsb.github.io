## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [A report-grounded vision-language foundation model for colonoscopy from 280000 routine reports](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28466)
**Authors:** Jia Yu, Yan Zhu, Yili He, Zilong Wang et al. (15 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.28466`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Colonoscopy, Medical Imaging

**TL;DR:** EndoCLIP is a colonoscopy vision-language foundation model trained on 280,000 routine clinical reports using weak image-report supervision.

**Problem:** Vision-language models remain underutilized in colonoscopy despite the wealth of expert descriptions in routine endoscopy reports. These reports summarize entire procedures rather than captioning individual frames, leaving clinical findings only weakly linked to corresponding images.

**Method:** The authors develop EndoCLIP by leveraging 280,000 routine colonoscopy reports as weak supervision. They design a report-grounded contrastive learning framework that aligns video frames with the clinical text descriptions from paired procedure reports, bridging the gap between procedure-level reports and frame-level visual content.

**Results:** EndoCLIP demonstrates strong performance on downstream colonoscopy tasks including lesion classification and retrieval, outperforming general-purpose CLIP models and competing biomedical VLMs on colonoscopy-specific benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to the reader's interests in CLIP-style vision-language foundation models applied to medical imaging; demonstrates how report-grounded pretraining can be extended beyond radiology chest X-rays to endoscopy with weakly paired data.


### 2. [LoMeVQA: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Longitudinal Medical VQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27806)
**Authors:** Zhilin Wu, Zhangkai Ni, Chengmei Yang, Longzhen Yang et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.27806`  
**Tags:** Longitudinal Medical Imaging, Medical VQA, Chest X-ray

**TL;DR:** LoMeVQA introduces the first comprehensive benchmark for longitudinal medical visual question answering, targeting temporal reasoning across sequential patient imaging studies.

**Problem:** In clinical practice, patients undergo multiple imaging examinations over time, yet multimodal large language models are not systematically evaluated on longitudinal reasoning tasks. Existing medical VQA benchmarks focus on single-image analysis, failing to test temporal understanding of disease progression and treatment response.

**Method:** The authors construct LoMeVQA, a benchmark covering multiple imaging modalities and clinical scenarios that require reasoning across sequentially acquired images. The benchmark includes questions about change detection, disease progression, and treatment response that necessitate cross-temporal comparison.

**Results:** Evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs on LoMeVQA reveals significant performance gaps in longitudinal reasoning, demonstrating that current models struggle with temporal medical image understanding despite strong single-image performance.

**Why it matters:** Highly relevant to the reader's work on evaluation metrics and medical VQA; provides a new benchmark resource for assessing multimodal models on clinically realistic longitudinal radiology scenarios.


### 3. [ProgFormer: Hierarchical Voxel Diffusion Transformer for Longitudinal Brain MRI Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27537)
**Authors:** Dexuan Ding, Yuankai Qi, Luping Zhou, Jian Yang et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.27537`  
**Tags:** Brain MRI, Longitudinal Imaging, Diffusion Models

**TL;DR:** ProgFormer is a hierarchical voxel diffusion transformer that predicts future structural brain MRI by modeling subtle longitudinal changes while preserving global brain structure.

**Problem:** Predicting future structural MRI requires sensitivity to subtle, spatially localized longitudinal changes (e.g., atrophy from neurodegeneration) while maintaining fidelity to the stable global brain structure. Existing latent-space-based methods struggle to capture fine-grained disease progression.

**Method:** ProgFormer uses a hierarchical voxel diffusion transformer architecture that operates at multiple spatial scales. It decomposes the prediction task into preserving global anatomical structure and generating fine-grained local changes, guided by longitudinal conditioning from prior timepoints.

**Results:** ProgFormer outperforms existing longitudinal MRI prediction methods on neuroimaging datasets in terms of structural similarity and accuracy of predicted regional volumetric changes associated with disease progression.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to the reader's interest in medical imaging and self-supervised/generative models; introduces a diffusion-based approach to a clinically important longitudinal brain imaging task with direct implications for neurodegeneration research.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Scaling Vision-Language Models Is Not Enough to Mitigate Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28211)
**Authors:** Ioannis Sarridis, Ioannis Kompatsiaris, Symeon Papadopoulos  
**arXiv:** `2607.28211`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Bias Evaluation, CLIP

**TL;DR:** A large-scale empirical study of 194 VLMs across 16 model families finds that scaling model size and training data does not reliably mitigate spurious correlation biases in CLIP-style models.

**Problem:** Vision-language models like CLIP are foundational to multimodal systems, yet their robustness to spurious correlations is poorly understood at scale. It is unclear whether simply scaling up models or training data reduces bias.

**Method:** The authors systematically evaluate 194 publicly available VLMs spanning 16 model families, diverse sizes, 24 training datasets, and three evaluation benchmarks (ImageNet overall performance, bias-related spurious correlation benchmarks). They analyze how model size, data scale, and training recipe affect vulnerability to shortcuts.

**Results:** Scaling alone does not reliably mitigate bias; spurious correlations persist even in the largest models, and the relationship between scale and robustness is inconsistent across model families and benchmarks.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to the reader's work on CLIP-style foundation models and their evaluation; the findings have important implications for deploying VLMs in medical imaging where spurious correlations can be clinically harmful.


### 2. [Chimera: Designing and Chinchilla-Scaling Hybrid Visual Diffusion Transformers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28611)
**Authors:** Chongjian Ge, Hanwen Jiang, Tianyu Wang, Jiuxiang Gu et al. (12 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2607.28611`  
**Tags:** Diffusion Models, Video Generation, Efficient Architectures

**TL;DR:** Chimera is a hybrid visual diffusion backbone that combines linear and full attention in a principled scaling recipe for high-resolution image and long-video generation.

**Problem:** Visual generation at high resolution and long temporal horizon is bottlenecked by the quadratic cost of full self-attention over large token sequences spanning text, image, and video modalities.

**Method:** Chimera processes text, image, and video tokens in a single raster-ordered stream without positional embeddings, combining Kimi Delta Attention (KDA) for efficient linear-complexity local processing with periodic full-attention layers. A Chinchilla-style scaling analysis determines the optimal ratio of the two attention types as a function of compute budget.

**Results:** Chimera achieves competitive or superior generation quality on image and video benchmarks compared to full-attention baselines while significantly reducing computational cost, and the scaling recipe provides predictable performance gains with increased compute.

**Why it matters:** Relevant to the reader's interest in vision-language foundation models and efficient architectures; the hybrid attention and scaling methodology could inform design of large medical imaging models.


### 3. [CXR-Retrieve: Compositional Text-to-Image Retrieval in Chest Radiography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27779)
**Authors:** Tomer Erez, Moshe Kimhi, Chaim Baskin, Ehud Rivlin  
**arXiv:** `2607.27779`  
**Tags:** Medical Image Retrieval, Vision-Language Models, Chest X-ray

**TL;DR:** CXR-Retrieve introduces a compositional text-to-image retrieval benchmark for chest radiography, exposing that current biomedical VLMs fail on short clinical search queries despite strong report-to-image matching.

**Problem:** Large chest radiography archives lack structured clinical annotations, relying on free-text reports for organization. Current biomedical vision-language models are optimized for full report-to-image matching rather than short, compositional clinical queries (e.g., 'bilateral pleural effusion with cardiomegaly').

**Method:** The authors construct CXR-Retrieve, a benchmark of compositional text queries derived from clinical findings in chest X-ray reports, and evaluate a range of CLIP-style biomedical VLMs on text-to-image retrieval using this more clinically realistic query format.

**Results:** Existing biomedical VLMs substantially underperform on compositional short-query retrieval compared to report-level retrieval, revealing a gap between current model capabilities and clinical search use cases.

**Why it matters:** Directly aligned with the reader's interests in CLIP-style medical VLMs, chest X-ray analysis, and evaluation of generative/retrieval systems in radiology; provides a new benchmark that exposes shortcomings relevant to clinical deployment.
