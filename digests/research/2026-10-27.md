## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [MedVTok: A General-Purpose Medical Visual Tokenizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35842)
**Authors:** Chenglong Ma, Yuanfeng Ji, Junzhi Ning, Jiyao Liu et al. (21 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.35842`  
**Tags:** Medical Visual Tokenizer, Multimodal Medical AI, Foundation Models

**TL;DR:** MedVTok is a general-purpose visual tokenizer that maps heterogeneous medical images (2D and 3D, across modalities) into a shared discrete token space for downstream multimodal medical models.

**Problem:** Existing medical visual tokenizers are tied to one dimensionality or imaging modality, so multimodal medical systems end up with fragmented, poorly reusable visual representations.

**Method:** The authors build a single tokenizer that encodes 2D images and 3D volumes from different imaging modalities into a common token vocabulary. The design aims to serve as a shared visual interface for patient-condition modeling, so one encoder can feed language models or other multimodal heads regardless of input modality.

**Results:** The abstract (truncated) positions MedVTok as a reusable cross-modality, cross-dimension tokenizer; specific benchmark numbers are not visible in the provided text.

**Why it matters:** A unified medical visual tokenizer is directly upstream of radiology report generation and medical VLM work, and could replace modality-specific encoders in CLIP-style medical pretraining pipelines.


### 2. [MedAdaptAgent: An Automatic Harness for Adapting Medical Imaging Models to New Sites](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34884)
**Authors:** Hanxue Gu, Zhonghao Liu, Xingrui Gu, Kang Wang et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.34884`  
**Tags:** Domain Adaptation, Medical Imaging, Agentic Workflows

**TL;DR:** MedAdaptAgent is an automatic harness that adapts pretrained medical imaging models to a new hospital's data, choosing adaptation strategies based on architecture, domain shift, and available labels and compute.

**Problem:** A model trained for the same task often degrades at a new site because imaging protocols and preprocessing differ, and naive adaptation on a small labeled set can overfit while hurting performance on unseen data.

**Method:** The system wraps model adaptation in an agentic loop that inspects the pretrained model, characterizes the shift to the new site, and selects among adaptation options conditioned on label and compute budgets. It aims to automate the trial-and-error a human engineer would otherwise do per site.

**Results:** The truncated abstract frames the harness as improving site-level adaptation while guarding against degradation on held-out data; quantitative gains are not visible in the provided excerpt.

**Why it matters:** Cross-site generalization is the central deployment barrier for chest X-ray and other radiology models, and an automated adaptation harness is a practical complement to foundation-model pretraining.


### 3. [SAP-ViT: A Self-Attributing Patch Vision Transformer for Medical Image Classification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35593)
**Authors:** Wei Zhang, Wolfgang Nejdl, Ming Tang  
**arXiv:** `2610.35593`  
**Tags:** Vision Transformers, Interpretability, Medical Image Classification

**TL;DR:** SAP-ViT replaces the CLS token with direct aggregation of last-layer patch embeddings so that every medical image classification decomposes exactly into per-patch contributions.

**Problem:** Standard ViT classifiers predict from a global CLS token, which is poorly aligned with the local, region-level interpretability clinicians need in medical imaging.

**Method:** The classifier aggregates last-layer patch embeddings directly, making the class logit a sum of patch-level terms. This yields self-attributing predictions where the attribution map is the model's own computation rather than a post-hoc explanation.

**Results:** The abstract (truncated) reports that this design makes predictions decomposable by patch; competitive accuracy against CLS-based ViTs is implied but numbers are not visible.

**Why it matters:** Built-in patch attribution is attractive for chest X-ray classifiers and for grounding findings in report generation, offering an alternative to Grad-CAM-style explanations.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Do vision models know that three is three?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35857)
**Authors:** Ankit Sonthalia, Arnas Uselis, Seong Joon Oh  
**arXiv:** `2610.35857`  
**Tags:** Representation Learning, Linear Representation Hypothesis, Vision Models

**TL;DR:** This paper tests whether vision models encode concept transitions (e.g., 2→3, clean→dirty) as consistent linear directions in embedding space across different visual domains.

**Problem:** The linear representation hypothesis is well studied for language models, but it is unclear whether vision encoders represent the same abstract transformation with the same direction regardless of the surface domain in which it appears.

**Method:** The authors probe vision models with paired inputs that differ by one concept change across multiple domains, such as handwritten digits versus hand gestures, and measure whether the embedding difference vectors align. They compare across models and concept types to characterize when linear, domain-invariant directions emerge.

**Results:** The truncated abstract sets up the question of whether such directions exist; detailed findings on which models and concepts show alignment are not visible in the excerpt.

**Why it matters:** Understanding whether CLIP-style encoders carry linear, transferable concept directions bears directly on prompt-based steering, zero-shot transfer, and interpretability of medical vision-language models.


### 2. [LE-GDA: Layer Ensemble Gaussian Discriminant Analysis for Training-Free Few-Shot CLIP Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34901)
**Authors:** Pavodi Maniamfu, Vogel Kiketa, Zezina Kuzulu Kuetu, Jakin Mukendi Kabongo et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.34901`  
**Tags:** CLIP Adaptation, Few-Shot Learning, Training-Free Methods

**TL;DR:** LE-GDA builds a training-free few-shot CLIP classifier by combining Gaussian discriminant analysis across multiple intermediate layers rather than only the final projected feature.

**Problem:** Training-free few-shot adaptation methods use only CLIP's final image representation and ignore intermediate layers, and naively averaging class scores from different layers lets numerical scale rather than informativeness decide influence.

**Method:** The method fits Gaussian discriminant classifiers per layer and ensembles them with a calibration that normalizes the differing spread of layer-wise scores. It requires no gradient updates, keeping the adaptation cheap and reproducible.

**Results:** The truncated abstract positions the approach as improving over final-layer-only training-free baselines; exact accuracy gains are not visible in the excerpt.

**Why it matters:** Cheap, training-free few-shot adaptation of CLIP-family models is directly usable for medical imaging tasks where labeled examples are scarce and fine-tuning is risky.


### 3. [Beyond Distribution Matching: Self-Supervised Representation Forcing for Few-Step Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35594)
**Authors:** Junyi Chen, Haiyu Zhang, Zhoujie Fu, Tengfei Wang et al. (5 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.35594`  
**Tags:** Diffusion Distillation, Self-Supervised Learning, Video Generation

**TL;DR:** The paper augments Distribution Matching Distillation for few-step video diffusion with a self-supervised representation-forcing objective, since output-space matching alone leaves internal representations unconstrained.

**Problem:** DMD distills video diffusion models into few-step students using only an output-space objective, which can let the student's internal representations degrade even when outputs look faithful.

**Method:** The authors add a representation-level loss that forces the student's intermediate features to match self-supervised targets alongside the distribution-matching objective. The approach keeps DMD's flexibility across student architectures and avoids adversarial training.

**Results:** The truncated abstract claims improved few-step video generation over standard DMD; specific quality or speed figures are not visible in the provided text.

**Why it matters:** The idea that self-supervised representation targets improve generative distillation connects the reader's SSL interests to efficient generative models, including potential medical image synthesis.
