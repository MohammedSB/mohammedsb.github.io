## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [PLRS-IC: A Dual-Calibration Framework for Chest X-Ray Vision-Language Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39266)
**Authors:** Qixing Zhao, Jinpeng Li  
**arXiv:** `2609.39266`  
**Tags:** Chest X-ray, Vision-Language Models, Radiology Report Generation

**TL;DR:** A dual-calibration framework addresses projection-induced visual mismatch and patient-agnostic semantic overlap in chest X-ray vision-language alignment for zero-shot tasks.

**Problem:** Fine-grained vision-language alignment in chest radiography is hindered by two intertwined ambiguities: frontal and lateral radiographs exhibit projection-induced visual mismatch at the local feature level, and patient-agnostic semantic overlap conflates findings across patients at the global semantic level.

**Method:** The PLRS-IC framework introduces dual calibration: a local-level projection-aware calibration module that reconciles frontal/lateral view discrepancies, and a patient-level semantic calibration that injects patient-specific context to reduce inter-patient semantic confusion. These are integrated into a CLIP-style contrastive learning pipeline trained on paired radiology reports and multi-view radiographs.

**Results:** PLRS-IC improves zero-shot classification, grounding, and segmentation performance on chest X-ray benchmarks, demonstrating that addressing both calibration sources yields consistent gains over baselines that tackle only one.

**Why it matters:** Directly addresses the reader's core interests in CLIP-style vision-language foundation models for chest X-ray analysis, multimodal medical imaging, and zero-shot clinical tasks without task-specific annotations.


### 2. [GateSPINE: Gated Cross-View Fusion for Lumbar Spine MRI Report Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40091)
**Authors:** Hoang Nguyen Van, Cuong Vuong Tuan, Trang Mai Xuan, Bien Tran Van et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.40091`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Radiology Report Generation, Vision-Language Models

**TL;DR:** GateSPINE proposes a gated cross-view fusion mechanism for automated lumbar spine MRI report generation that adaptively combines multi-sequence and multi-plane imaging information.

**Problem:** Automated report generation for lumbar spine MRI is challenging because examinations comprise multiple sequences and imaging planes with complementary diagnostic information, and existing methods encode studies as single volumes or combine acquisitions by fixed rules, diluting findings visible in only one plane.

**Method:** GateSPINE introduces a gated cross-view fusion module that learns to selectively weight information from different MRI sequences and planes. A vision-language model backbone processes each view independently, and the gating mechanism dynamically controls information flow across views based on diagnostic relevance before generating the final report.

**Results:** The method outperforms baseline fixed-fusion approaches on lumbar spine MRI report generation metrics, demonstrating improved capture of plane-specific findings and more clinically accurate descriptions.

**Why it matters:** Squarely addresses the reader's interests in multimodal learning for medical imaging, radiology report generation, and vision-language models applied to MRI beyond standard chest X-ray settings.


### 3. [Towards Trustworthy AI for Glioma Diagnosis: A Task-Aware Evaluation of Uncertainty Quantification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39429)
**Authors:** Gonzalo Esteban Mosquera Rojas, Sebastian R. van der Voort, Carolin M. Pirkl, Sandeep Kaushik et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.39429`  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Uncertainty Quantification, Glioma Diagnosis

**TL;DR:** A task-aware evaluation of uncertainty quantification using Monte Carlo Dropout in a multi-task deep learning framework for MRI-based glioma diagnosis covering segmentation and genomic marker prediction.

**Problem:** Trustworthy AI for high-stakes medical image analysis requires uncertainty quantification (UQ), but most UQ evaluations treat all tasks uniformly without examining how uncertainty quality differs between tumor segmentation and molecular subtype prediction tasks in glioma diagnosis.

**Method:** The authors build a multi-task deep learning framework that jointly performs tumor segmentation and predicts IDH mutation status, 1p/19q co-deletion, and tumor grade from MRI. Monte Carlo Dropout (MCD) is applied throughout, and uncertainty estimates are evaluated in a task-aware manner—separately analyzing how well UQ signals correlate with errors in each sub-task.

**Results:** Task-aware analysis reveals substantially different UQ behavior across segmentation versus classification sub-tasks, with MCD providing more reliable uncertainty signals for some tasks than others, highlighting the need for task-specific UQ design in multi-task clinical models.

**Why it matters:** Highly relevant to the reader's interest in trustworthy AI for medical imaging, evaluation methodology for clinical AI, and uncertainty quantification in MRI analysis—complementing interests in radiology beyond chest X-ray.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Emergent Multi-View Geometry Through Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39227)
**Authors:** David Nordström, Thibaut Loiseau, Vincent Lepetit, Michael Felsberg et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.39227`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Multi-View Representation Learning, 3D Scene Understanding

**TL;DR:** Poincar3 is a self-supervised method that learns geometry-aware representations from multiple views through self-distillation without relying on RGB reconstruction, disentangling geometry from appearance.

**Problem:** Most visual representation learning operates on individual images, and methods that leverage multiple views typically rely on RGB reconstruction, which entangles geometric structure with visual appearance and prevents pure geometric understanding from emerging.

**Method:** Poincar3 uses self-distillation across multiple rendered views of scenes: an online network processes one viewpoint while a target network processes another, and the model learns to predict consistent representations across viewpoints without reconstructing pixel values. This forces the model to encode viewpoint-invariant geometric structure rather than appearance.

**Results:** The method produces representations that capture multi-view geometry without supervision, matching or exceeding reconstruction-based baselines on downstream 3D understanding tasks while being more computationally efficient and appearance-agnostic.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to the reader's interests in self-supervised learning and multimodal representation learning; the JEPA-style self-distillation approach and multi-view invariance learning are closely related to MaskCLIP and SLIP-family methods.


### 2. [Spherical Interpolation for Backward-Compatible Multimodal Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39836)
**Authors:** Simone Ricci, Niccolò Biondi, Federico Pernici  
**arXiv:** `2609.39836`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Backward-Compatible Retrieval, Contrastive Learning

**TL;DR:** Spherical interpolation enables backward-compatible upgrades of contrastive vision-language models by constructing intermediate embedding spaces that are simultaneously compatible with both old and new model representations.

**Problem:** When contrastive vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) are upgraded, the new embedding space is incompatible with the old one, forcing expensive recomputation of all gallery embeddings for cross-modal retrieval—a major practical barrier to iterative model improvement.

**Method:** The authors propose spherical interpolation (slerp) on the unit hypersphere to construct a transition embedding space between two independently trained models. By interpolating between old and new model representations in this curved geometry, they derive a compatibility transform that allows new query embeddings to retrieve from old gallery embeddings without recomputation.

**Results:** Spherical interpolation achieves backward-compatible retrieval performance competitive with methods that require explicit compatibility training, while requiring no additional training and respecting the normalized geometry of contrastive embedding spaces.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to the reader's focus on CLIP-style vision-language foundation models; addresses a practical deployment challenge for model upgrades that arises in medical and general retrieval systems alike.


### 3. [Seeing as Humans Do: Learning from Motion to Segment Anything Without Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39785)
**Authors:** Weijian Jian, Xiaoyue Zhang, Bin Xiao, Chunyu Xie et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2609.39785`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Semantic Segmentation, Foundation Models

**TL;DR:** Motion-Grounded Segment Anything learns unsupervised object segmentation at multiple granularities from motion cues, enabling SAM-scale segmentation without manual annotations.

**Problem:** SAM relies on massive manual annotations, creating a scaling bottleneck; existing unsupervised motion-based methods overfit to moving entities and lack multi-granularity understanding or the ability to generalize to static objects.

**Method:** The method uses optical flow and motion segmentation as free supervisory signal to train a multi-granularity segmentation model. Motion cues ground the learning of object concepts across spatial scales, and a curriculum from moving to static objects enables generalization beyond the motion domain. The resulting model can segment anything in a SAM-compatible interface without human labels.

**Results:** The approach achieves strong zero-shot segmentation performance on standard benchmarks, approaching supervised SAM on several metrics while requiring no manual annotations, and demonstrates multi-granularity segmentation of both moving and static objects.

**Why it matters:** Highly relevant to the reader's interests in self-supervised learning, foundation models for vision, and referring segmentation—particularly the question of how to learn rich visual representations without large annotation costs.
