1. PRISM: Pathology Reliability In Scarce-label Medicine. A Tile-level Benchmark of Pathology Foundation Models

Alicem Koyun  ·  2610.36517

HistopathologyFoundation ModelsModel Calibration

TL;DR: PRISM is a tile-level benchmark that evaluates pathology foundation models jointly on calibration, low-label performance, and cross-institutional robustness rather than just in-distribution accuracy.

Problem: Pathology foundation models are usually compared only by classification accuracy with full labels on in-distribution test sets. Calibration, behavior under scarce labels, and robustness to institutional shift are rarely measured together, so reported rankings may not reflect clinical reliability.

Method: The authors build a tile-level benchmark that scores pathology foundation models along four reliability axes, including calibration, performance across decreasing label budgets, and cross-institutional distribution shift. Models are evaluated as frozen encoders so that differences reflect representation quality rather than fine-tuning tricks.

Results: The benchmark reveals that rankings by in-distribution accuracy do not necessarily hold under low label budgets or institutional shift, and calibration varies substantially across foundation models (abstract truncated; specific numbers not available).

Why it matters: Directly addresses how medical foundation models should be evaluated beyond accuracy, which parallels your interest in evaluation metrics and self-supervised medical imaging representations.

2. Retrospective macromolecular pool fraction mapping from conventional clinical MRI: a physics-guided self-supervised deep learning framework

Jelmer van Lune, Miha Fuderer, Oscar van der Heide, Matteo Maspero et al. (8 authors)  ·  2610.37213

Quantitative MRISelf-Supervised LearningPhysics-Informed Deep Learning

TL;DR: A physics-guided self-supervised network estimates macromolecular pool fraction, a myelin-sensitive biomarker, directly from conventional clinical brain MRI without specialized acquisitions.

Problem: Macromolecular pool fraction (MPF) is a quantitative marker of myelin integrity but requires dedicated magnetization-transfer acquisitions and reconstruction, limiting its use in large retrospective studies. Conventional clinical MRI contrasts are abundant but lack a direct MPF readout.

Method: The framework uses a signal model of the clinical MRI sequences as a physics prior and trains a network in a self-supervised manner so that predicted tissue parameters reproduce the observed conventional images. This allows retrospective MPF estimation from routine T1- and T2-weighted scans without ground-truth MPF labels.

Results: The method recovers MPF maps from conventional clinical MRI that are consistent with reference quantitative acquisitions (abstract truncated; quantitative agreement figures not available).

Why it matters: A concrete example of physics-guided self-supervision unlocking quantitative biomarkers from routine imaging, a pattern that transfers to other modalities you work with.

3. Systematic Morphometric Brain Autoencoding for Representation Learning in Neurodegenerative Diseases

Nuria Balbás-Esteban, Albert Belenguer-Llorens, Elaheh Moradi, Jussi Tohka et al. (6 authors)  ·  2610.37074

Self-Supervised LearningNeuroimagingNeurodegenerative Disease

TL;DR: The paper systematically studies morphometry-aware brain MRI autoencoding as a self-supervised pretraining strategy for neurodegenerative disease representation learning.

Problem: Supervised models on structural T1-weighted MRI generalize poorly because annotations are scarce and scanner-induced distribution shift is common. Existing self-supervised approaches are borrowed from natural images and ignore the morphometric structure of brain anatomy.

Method: The authors design autoencoding objectives that incorporate brain morphometry rather than generic pixel reconstruction, and systematically compare architectural and objective variants. Learned representations are evaluated on downstream neurodegenerative disease tasks under limited labels and scanner shift.

Results: Morphometry-aware self-supervised representations improve downstream neurodegenerative disease prediction and robustness relative to natural-image-derived self-supervised baselines (abstract truncated; exact gains not available).

Why it matters: Directly in your lane of self-supervised medical imaging, with a useful argument that domain-specific pretext tasks beat ported natural-image recipes.

1. EmbeddingGemma 2: Unified and Efficient Multimodal Representations

Henrique Schechter Vera, Sahil Dua, Jovan Andonov, Sara Smoot et al. (73 authors)  ·  2610.37364

Multimodal EmbeddingsFoundation ModelsRepresentation Learning

TL;DR: EmbeddingGemma 2 is an open embedding model that maps text, images, audio, video, and their combinations into a single unified space.

Problem: Most embedding models cover one or two modalities, and mixed-modal inputs such as an image plus caption or a video with speech are poorly handled in a shared space. Open, efficient models with strong unimodal, cross-modal, and mixed-modal quality are lacking.

Method: The model uses simplified per-layer embeddings and a multi-stage training curriculum that progresses through modalities and objectives. This curriculum is designed to preserve unimodal quality while building cross-modal and mixed-modal alignment.

Results: The model achieves strong performance across unimodal, cross-modal, and mixed-modal evaluations as an open release (abstract truncated; benchmark numbers not available).

Why it matters: An open CLIP-style successor spanning five modalities is a natural backbone candidate for medical retrieval, report-image alignment, and educational multimodal tools.

2. Can Discrete Tokens Serve as Semantically Lossless Representations of Visual Signals?

Jialv Zou, Xiaotong Li, Zhengrong Yue, Xu Huang et al. (9 authors)  ·  2610.37662

Visual TokenizationDiscrete RepresentationsMultimodal Modeling

TL;DR: The paper challenges the assumption that quantization necessarily causes semantic loss, arguing discrete visual tokens can be semantically lossless representations of images.

Problem: Discrete visual tokens enable unified next-token prediction across vision and language, but quantization error is widely believed to cap their semantic fidelity. This belief positions continuous features as inherently superior for multimodal understanding.

Method: The authors analyze when and why quantization degrades semantics and propose a tokenization approach designed to preserve semantic content through the discrete bottleneck. They compare discrete and continuous representations on semantic understanding tasks under a shared next-token prediction objective.

Results: Discrete tokens can match continuous representations on semantic fidelity under the proposed design, undermining the assumed ceiling (abstract truncated; metrics not available).

Why it matters: Relevant to whether unified autoregressive vision-language models can serve radiology report generation without sacrificing fine-grained visual semantics.

3. PerceptRL: Making Visual Perception Rewardable in MLLMs from Answers to Interactions

Tongkun Guan, Zhibo Yang, Mingkun Yang, Haocheng Wang et al. (6 authors)  ·  2610.37471

Multimodal LLMsReinforcement LearningFine-Grained Perception

TL;DR: PerceptRL makes fine-grained visual perception directly rewardable during MLLM post-training, moving beyond answer-only rewards to interaction-level signals.

Problem: Fine-grained perception errors bottleneck reasoning-focused post-training of multimodal LLMs. Caption pretraining improves perception but its effect fades across later stages, and continuing caption supervision during RL is hard to integrate.

Method: The authors design reward signals that credit perceptual correctness from answers and from interactions rather than only final outcomes. This lets reinforcement learning optimize perception and reasoning jointly in post-training.

Results: Perception-aware rewards improve fine-grained visual accuracy and downstream reasoning compared with outcome-only RL (abstract truncated; benchmark details not available).

Why it matters: Chest X-ray report generation fails largely on perception of subtle findings, so RL rewards that target perception are directly applicable.
