## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [HarMoE: Multi-Source Chest Radiograph Pretraining with Dataset-Disentangled Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02252)
**Authors:** Haozhe Luo, Ziyu Zhou, Shelley Zixin Shu, Mauricio Reyes  
**arXiv:** `2608.02252`  
**Tags:** Chest X-ray, Multi-Source Pretraining, Mixture-of-Experts

**TL;DR:** HarMoE pretrains chest-radiograph representations by combining image-report alignment with many existing multi-label classification datasets, using dataset-disentangled experts to keep heterogeneous label sources from conflicting.

**Problem:** Chest X-ray vision-language models are overwhelmingly built on MIMIC-CXR image-report alignment, leaving the large pool of curated multi-label disease-classification datasets underused as a supervision source. Naively pooling those datasets is hard because their label spaces, annotation policies, and acquisition characteristics differ.

**Method:** The authors treat each classification corpus as a distinct supervision source and route it through dataset-specific experts inside a mixture-of-experts pretraining backbone, so source-specific label conventions and biases are absorbed by dedicated experts while shared visual structure accumulates in the common trunk. This disentangling lets multi-source label supervision complement (rather than corrupt) report-based alignment during pretraining.

**Results:** The paper reports that adding dataset-disentangled multi-label supervision yields stronger chest-radiograph representations than report-alignment-only pretraining, with gains on downstream CXR recognition transfer (the posted abstract is truncated, so exact benchmark numbers are not visible here).

**Why it matters:** This directly targets the reader's core area — vision-language pretraining for chest X-ray — and offers a concrete recipe for escaping MIMIC-CXR dependence, which is one of the main scaling bottlenecks for CXR foundation models and for report-generation pipelines built on top of them.


### 2. [Self-supervised DXA representations encode multi-system disease risk, biological aging and heritability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02208)
**Authors:** Gil Sasson, Zachary Levine, Smadar Shilo, Sarah Kohn et al. (12 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.02208`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, JEPA, Medical Imaging Biomarkers

**TL;DR:** LeDXA applies a joint-embedding predictive architecture to whole-body DXA scans and shows the learned self-supervised embeddings encode multi-system disease risk, biological aging, and heritable signal.

**Problem:** Whole-body DXA scans are acquired routinely for bone density and body composition, but their full spatial structure is discarded in favor of a handful of scalar summaries. It is unclear whether raw DXA images carry systemic health information beyond those clinical readouts.

**Method:** The authors train a JEPA-style vision model on raw DXA images without labels, learning to predict masked-region representations rather than pixels, and then probe the frozen embeddings against phenotypic, longitudinal outcome, and genetic data. Evaluations span prediction of incident disease across organ systems, estimation of biological age, and heritability analysis of the representation itself.

**Results:** The self-supervised representations recover disease-risk signal spanning multiple physiological systems, track biological aging, and show measurable heritability — indicating the embeddings capture systemic biology rather than only bone and fat geometry.

**Why it matters:** It is a clean demonstration that JEPA-style self-supervision on an under-exploited clinical modality yields transferable, biologically meaningful representations, which is a template the reader could apply to chest radiographs or other routinely-acquired imaging with linked EHR and genetic outcomes.


### 3. [Few-Shot Concept Prompt Learning for Segmentation Foundation Models via Visual Grounding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01663)
**Authors:** Rahul Venkataramani, Rachana Sathish  
**arXiv:** `2608.01663`  
**Tags:** Promptable Segmentation Foundation Models, Visual Grounding, Few-Shot Medical Segmentation

**TL;DR:** Few-shot concept prompt learning replaces hand-written text prompts to SAM3 / Medical SAM3 with learned visual-grounding-based concept prompts, arguing the language interface itself is the structural bottleneck for clinical segmentation.

**Problem:** Promptable segmentation foundation models advertise few-shot, language-specified segmentation, but their accuracy on clinical targets falls well short of task-specific models. The usual explanations — too little medical pretraining, or badly worded prompts — do not fully account for the gap.

**Method:** The authors diagnose the shortfall as a structural limitation of specifying anatomical or pathological concepts through natural language, then learn continuous concept prompts from a handful of annotated exemplars via visual grounding, keeping the foundation model frozen. The learned prompts act as drop-in replacements for text phrases in the model's existing promptable interface.

**Results:** Learned concept prompts substantially outperform text prompting and prompt-engineering baselines on medical segmentation tasks in the few-shot regime, closing much of the gap to task-specific supervision without retraining the backbone.

**Why it matters:** This sits squarely at the intersection of referring/promptable segmentation and medical foundation models, and its central claim — that the text interface, not medical-domain knowledge, is the limiting factor — is directly actionable for anyone building referring segmentation systems on SAM-family models for radiology.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [CAPEval: A Decoupled Caption Evaluation across Understanding and Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02589)
**Authors:** Zhipeng Liu, Haochen Wang, Zhaoxiang Zhang  
**arXiv:** `2608.02589`  
**Tags:** Caption Evaluation, Vision-Language Models, Text-to-Image Generation

**TL;DR:** CAPEval decouples caption quality into visual coverage and claim faithfulness, evaluating each separately for both multimodal understanding and text-to-image generation.

**Problem:** Caption quality is normally reported as a single scalar, which conflates how much of the image content a caption covers with how reliably the image supports the caption's claims. A caption can be rich but hallucinatory, or faithful but nearly empty, and one number cannot distinguish them.

**Method:** The authors build a decoupled evaluation protocol that scores coverage (fraction of visual information expressed) and support/faithfulness (whether each stated claim is grounded in the image) as separate axes. The two axes are then applied on both sides of the caption's role: as supervision for understanding models and as conditioning for text-to-image generation.

**Results:** The decoupled view separates captioners that a single aggregate score ranks equivalently, exposing systematic coverage-versus-faithfulness trade-offs across models and clarifying which failure mode drives downstream generation errors.

**Why it matters:** The coverage-versus-support decomposition is exactly the structure needed for radiology report evaluation, where clinical completeness and factual grounding are distinct failure modes that current single-score metrics (and even many clinical-efficacy metrics) blur together.


### 2. [HAFI-VLM: A Frequency Perspective for Diagnosing and Enhancing Visual Perception in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02124)
**Authors:** Jin Cui, Chuanchang Su, Jiayi Lu, Xinyue Long et al. (6 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.02124`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Fine-Grained Visual Perception, Frequency-Domain Analysis

**TL;DR:** HAFI-VLM identifies 'spectral response rigidity' — pretrained vision encoders keep encoder-specific layerwise frequency profiles that barely shift under fine-tuning — and fixes it to improve fine-grained visual perception.

**Problem:** VLMs remain unreliable when an answer depends on fine visual detail rather than gist. Existing explanations focus on token budgets or connector design and do not explain why fine-tuning fails to recover the needed detail.

**Method:** The authors analyze layerwise spectral responses of pretrained vision encoders and show each encoder imposes a persistent frequency profile that is nearly invariant to downstream fine-tuning, so high-frequency evidence images actually contain never reaches the language model. HAFI-VLM then introduces frequency-aware handling that adapts the encoder's spectral response to the image and task instead of leaving it fixed.

**Results:** Relaxing spectral rigidity improves performance on tasks that require fine-grained visual evidence, with the diagnostic analysis showing the improvement tracks recovered high-frequency response rather than added capacity.

**Why it matters:** Fine-grained perception failure is the dominant error mode when general VLMs are applied to radiology, where findings are small, low-contrast, high-frequency structures; this gives both a diagnostic tool for encoder choice and a concrete intervention.


### 3. [EOVSAM: Efficient Open-Vocabulary Segmentation with SAM 3 in One Pass](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02284)
**Authors:** Haomin Peng, Yongkang Li, Zhaoxiang Liu, Xiaojie Jin et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.02284`  
**Tags:** Open-Vocabulary Segmentation, SAM 3, Efficient Inference

**TL;DR:** EOVSAM turns SAM 3's noun-phrase-guided segmentation into a single-pass open-vocabulary segmenter, removing the exhaustive per-category vocabulary traversal that makes it prohibitively slow.

**Problem:** SAM 3 achieves competitive open-vocabulary segmentation only by running its promptable pipeline once per candidate noun phrase, so cost scales with vocabulary size and becomes impractical for large label sets.

**Method:** EOVSAM restructures inference so all target categories are resolved in one pass over the image, replacing sequential vocabulary traversal with a unified text-conditioned decoding step over shared visual features. This preserves SAM 3's promptable open-vocabulary behavior while decoupling runtime from the number of categories.

**Results:** The framework retains competitive open-vocabulary segmentation quality while cutting the computational overhead that grows with category count, making large-vocabulary segmentation tractable in a single forward pass.

**Why it matters:** Anyone building referring or open-vocabulary segmentation on SAM-family backbones hits this exact scaling wall; a one-pass formulation matters for medical settings where dozens to hundreds of anatomical and pathological concepts must be queried per study.
