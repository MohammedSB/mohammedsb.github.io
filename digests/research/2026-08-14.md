## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Mr3D-VL: A generalist vision language foundation model for Multiparametric 3D Magnetic Resonance Imaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12689)
**Authors:** Zhi Qiao, Xintong Wu, Yichu He, Feng Shi  
**arXiv:** `2608.12689`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Foundation Models, Multiparametric MRI, Radiology Report Generation

**TL;DR:** Mr3D-VL is a generalist vision-language foundation model for multiparametric 3D brain MRI that couples cross-modality volumetric encoding with natural-language interaction for diagnosis and reasoning.

**Problem:** Current AI models for multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) are narrow task-specific predictors that cannot converse, explain, or integrate spatial evidence across sequences, which blocks clinical use where cross-modal reasoning is required. The physical meanings of T1/T1c/T2/FLAIR differ substantially, making naive fusion and 3D token handling hard.

**Method:** The authors build a generalist VLM operating directly on multi-parametric 3D volumes, with modality-aware encoding to reconcile the differing physical semantics of MRI sequences and spatially-aware token handling to preserve volumetric localization. The model is instruction-tuned across a spectrum of brain-tumor tasks so a single set of weights supports description, question answering, and grounded reasoning over mpMRI.

**Results:** Reported as a generalist across brain tumor diagnosis and treatment-relevant tasks, with language-based interpretability and cross-modal reasoning that task-specific baselines lack.

**Why it matters:** This is close to the core of your work: a CLIP-style/VLM foundation model pushed into true 3D multimodal medical imaging with report-like language output — a direct architectural and evaluation reference point for report-generation and grounding work beyond chest X-ray.


### 2. [How Good are Foundation Models in Longitudinal MRI Disease Progression Reasoning?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13309)
**Authors:** Wafa Al Ghallabi, Ritesh Thawkar, Sara Ghaboura, Omkar Thawakar et al. (9 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.13309`  
**Tags:** Medical Vision-Language Benchmarks, Longitudinal MRI, Temporal Reasoning

**TL;DR:** A new benchmark shows that current foundation models largely fail at reasoning about disease progression across sequential, multi-view MRI timepoints.

**Problem:** Existing medical vision-language benchmarks are almost all single-timepoint and single-view, so they never test the temporal-spatial reasoning radiologists actually perform when comparing a study to priors. As a result, reported VLM competence on medical imaging is likely overstated.

**Method:** The authors construct a longitudinal MRI evaluation covering multi-view anatomical planes across sequential timepoints, with tasks requiring interval-change localization and progression characterization rather than one-shot description. Both general-purpose and medical foundation models are evaluated under this protocol.

**Results:** Foundation models struggle to align views across time and to precisely localize interval changes, exposing a large gap between single-image performance and longitudinal clinical reasoning.

**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to your interest in evaluation of generative radiology outputs — it argues that current metrics and benchmarks miss temporal comparison, which is exactly where report-generation systems fail clinically (prior-study comparison sentences).


### 3. [HounsWorld: A Multimodal World Model for Hidden Patient-State Readout, Reconstruction, and Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12904)
**Authors:** Yunhao Bai, Zhongwei Qiu, Guangyu Guo, Yiming Huang et al. (8 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.12904`  
**Tags:** Medical World Models, CT Imaging, Multimodal Representation Learning

**TL;DR:** HounsWorld reframes CT-centered clinical AI as inference over a hidden patient state that can be read out, reconstructed, and simulated, rather than as isolated scan-to-answer mappings.

**Problem:** Most volumetric medical models learn direct mappings from images to labels or text, ignoring that both dense imaging and sparse clinical language are partial observations of the same underlying patient condition. This makes models brittle when observations are incomplete.

**Method:** The paper formulates a multimodal world model in which volumetric CT (anatomy, attenuation, lesions) and clinical language are treated as complementary observations of a shared latent patient state. The model supports three operations over this state: readout (predicting clinical attributes), reconstruction (recovering imaging observations), and simulation (rolling the state forward or under hypothetical conditions).

**Results:** The unified latent-state formulation supports readout, reconstruction, and simulation within one model, positioned as a step beyond task-specific volumetric encoders.

**Why it matters:** A generative/latent-state view of imaging plus text is a natural extension of self-supervised multimodal pretraining for medical imaging, and the readout/simulate framing is a useful alternative to pure contrastive objectives for report-grounded modeling.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [A Controlled Study of Self-Supervised Image and Video Pretraining under Limited Resources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13183)
**Authors:** Brunó B. Englert, Gijs Dubbelman  
**arXiv:** `2608.13183`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Visual Pretraining, Compute-Efficient Training

**TL;DR:** A controlled comparison of self-supervised image and video pretraining objectives under a fixed, limited compute and data budget, isolating which SSL choices actually matter.

**Problem:** Visual foundation models are typically pretrained at scales that make objective comparisons impossible and that most labs cannot reproduce, so it is unclear which SSL objectives, data modalities, and design choices are genuinely responsible for downstream gains. Confounded comparisons across papers have made SSL folklore hard to falsify.

**Method:** The authors hold data, compute, architecture, and evaluation fixed while varying the self-supervised objective and the pretraining modality (images vs video). Downstream transfer is measured across image and video understanding tasks so that objective-level differences are attributable rather than confounded with scale.

**Results:** The study identifies which SSL objectives retain their advantages in the low-resource regime and shows that competitive representations are attainable well below frontier pretraining scale.

**Why it matters:** If you pretrain SSL encoders on medical images or video (ultrasound, endoscopy) where data and compute are far below web scale, this is the closest thing to a controlled recipe guide for which objective to pick.


### 2. [Scaling Representation Diversity: Modulated Attention and Reconstructive Regularization for Visual Grounding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12748)
**Authors:** Junyi Hu, Tian Bai, Fengyi Wu, Yian Huang et al. (10 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2608.12748`  
**Tags:** Referring Expression Comprehension, Visual Grounding, Representation Collapse

**TL;DR:** Identifies representation degeneration as the key obstacle to a single generalist visual grounding model and fixes it with modulated attention plus reconstructive regularization.

**Problem:** Referring expression comprehension is usually studied with dataset-specific fine-tuning, yielding specialists that fail to transfer across datasets. Attempts to scale one generalist model run into degenerate, low-diversity representations.

**Method:** The paper reframes REC as unified open-vocabulary grounding and diagnoses collapse in the learned representation space as the scaling bottleneck. It introduces a modulated attention mechanism together with a reconstructive regularization objective that explicitly preserves representation diversity while training a single model across datasets.

**Results:** The generalist model retains representation diversity and improves cross-dataset grounding relative to specialist fine-tuning baselines.

**Why it matters:** Referring segmentation and grounding in radiology suffer exactly this problem — small per-dataset specialists that do not transfer — and the diversity-preserving regularizer is a cheap idea to port into medical referring segmentation pipelines.


### 3. [StreamTTT: Reconciling Real-Time Perception and Long-Term Memory in Streaming VLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13416)
**Authors:** Joya Chen, Zeyun Zhong, Mike Zheng Shou  
**arXiv:** `2608.13416`  
**Tags:** Streaming Vision-Language Models, Test-Time Training, Long-Context Video

**TL;DR:** StreamTTT writes long-range video history into online-updated fast weights so a streaming VLM can keep sharp present-moment perception without sacrificing long-term recall.

**Problem:** Streaming video VLMs face a hard trade-off: shortening the context improves perception of the current scene but destroys long-range memory, while long contexts blunt real-time perception and cost grows without bound.

**Method:** Rather than storing history in the denoiser context or KV cache, StreamTTT compresses long-range history into fast weights that are updated online at test time, outside the attention window. The short attention context stays devoted to current-frame perception while the fast weights carry accumulated past evidence.

**Results:** The method reconciles real-time perception with long-horizon recall on streaming video benchmarks where prior context-length trade-offs forced one or the other.

**Why it matters:** Test-time fast-weight memory is a general mechanism for long medical sequences — longitudinal study series, cine imaging, or long procedural video — where full-context attention is infeasible.
