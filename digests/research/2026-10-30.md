## Top Medical AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [RepConTUR: A Report-Conditioned Volumetric Multimodal Model for Abdominal Tumor Segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40094)
**Authors:** Seunghwan Lee, Sangjoon Park, Jongmin Lee, Yoon Jo Kim et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.40094`  
**Tags:** Radiology Report Grounding, Tumor Segmentation, Vision-Language Models

**TL;DR:** RepConTUR conditions a single 3D volumetric segmentation model on the full radiology report to cut false-positive tumor delineations for radiotherapy planning.

**Problem:** Automatic abdominal tumor segmentation for radiotherapy is dominated by false positives that distort dose and add editing time. The accompanying radiology report localizes disease at the suborgan level, but existing text-promptable segmenters only accept short referring phrases and mostly operate in 2D.

**Method:** The authors build a report-conditioned volumetric multimodal model that takes the native 3D scan together with the free-text report as conditioning input. Rather than short referring expressions, the model ingests the report's suborgan localization cues to steer where tumor masks are predicted, in one unified network.

**Results:** The abstract reports that report conditioning reduces false positives relative to unconditioned and phrase-prompted baselines on abdominal tumor segmentation; exact figures are truncated in the listing.

**Why it matters:** This is a direct bridge between your two interests, radiology reports and referring segmentation, and shows reports used as 3D grounding signal rather than as generation targets, which is a pattern worth borrowing for chest CT or CXR work.


### 2. [Aligning Medical Vision Language Models at the Capability Boundary with Entropy Adaptive Rubric Grounding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39835)
**Authors:** Hongyu Bai, Jiaqi Zeng, Chunzheng Zhu  
**arXiv:** `2610.39835`  
**Tags:** Medical Vision-Language Models, Rubric-Based Alignment, Diagnostic Reasoning

**TL;DR:** An alignment scheme for medical VLMs that uses entropy to focus training on hard boundary cases and grounds rubric-based rewards in visual evidence rather than final-answer correctness alone.

**Problem:** Uniform supervised fine-tuning lets easy cases dominate medical VLM updates while clinically critical borderline cases saturate early. Sparse rewards that only check the final answer cannot tell whether the model's reasoning actually links visual findings to clinical knowledge.

**Method:** The method adapts the training signal per sample based on model entropy so that capability-boundary cases receive more weight. Rewards come from rubrics that are grounded in the image, scoring intermediate evidence-to-knowledge links instead of just the diagnosis label.

**Results:** The paper claims improved diagnostic reasoning alignment over uniform SFT and outcome-only reward baselines; numbers are not visible in the truncated abstract.

**Why it matters:** Verifiable, evidence-grounded reward design is exactly the gap in evaluating generated radiology reports, and the entropy-adaptive weighting is a practical idea for training on imbalanced CXR findings.


### 3. [LoRe-MIM: Lost-or-Replaced Masked Image Modeling for Self-Supervised Representation Learning in Whole-Body PET/CT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40284)
**Authors:** Amartya Bhattacharya, Bashirul Azam Biswas, Biratal Raj Wagle, Michael S. Leapman et al. (7 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.40284`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Masked Image Modeling, PET/CT

**TL;DR:** LoRe-MIM is a masked image modeling pretext task for whole-body PET/CT in which patches are either removed or replaced, and the model must detect which happened.

**Problem:** Robust whole-body PET/CT models for detection, treatment planning, and response assessment are bottlenecked by expensive expert annotation. Standard SSL recipes were designed for natural images and do not exploit the paired functional-anatomical structure of PET/CT.

**Method:** The authors propose Lost-or-Replaced masked image modeling, which corrupts volumetric patches by either dropping them or substituting them with other patches. The encoder learns to reconstruct and to discriminate lost from replaced regions, forcing it to model both anatomy and tracer uptake jointly.

**Results:** Pretrained representations are reported to transfer better to downstream PET/CT tasks than existing SSL baselines; specific metrics are truncated.

**Why it matters:** A concrete SSL variant for multimodal 3D medical volumes that you could adapt to CT or paired image-report pretraining, and it pairs well with the DINOv2-for-PET benchmark in the notable list.


## Top General AI Papers (Full Summary)

### 1. [Proving and Improving the Platonic Representation Hypothesis with JEPAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39302)
**Authors:** Randall Balestriero, Yann LeCun  
**arXiv:** `2610.39302`  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Representation Alignment, JEPA

**TL;DR:** Balestriero and LeCun prove a Platonic Representation Theorem showing that JEPAs trained on different modalities converge to the same representation, then use the proof to improve training.

**Problem:** The Platonic Representation Hypothesis conjectures that models trained on different modalities converge to a shared world representation as they scale, but without a proof it offers neither a guarantee nor a training recipe.

**Method:** The authors formalize the hypothesis for Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures and prove conditions under which independently trained JEPAs recover the same latent representation up to a transformation. They then derive from the theorem a recipe that encourages this convergence during training.

**Results:** The theorem is stated for JEPAs, and the derived recipe is reported to improve representation alignment empirically; details are truncated in the listing.

**Why it matters:** This gives theoretical grounding for cross-modal alignment that underlies CLIP-style medical image-text pretraining, and suggests JEPA-style objectives as an alternative to contrastive losses.


### 2. [Training Foundation Models with a Single Algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40564)
**Authors:** Yuda Song, Alvin Zhang, Fahim Tajwar, Daman Arora et al. (10 authors)  
**arXiv:** `2610.40564`  
**Tags:** Foundation Model Training, Reinforcement Learning, Unified Objectives

**TL;DR:** The paper shows that pretraining, midtraining, SFT, and RL can all be expressed as maximum-likelihood fitting to model-generated target distributions under a single algorithm.

**Problem:** Foundation models are trained in stages with different data and different learning algorithms, and it is unclear whether this algorithmic separation is fundamental or an artifact of practice.

**Method:** The authors derive RL objectives as maximum-likelihood fitting to target distributions produced by the model itself, which lets every stage share one update rule. The stages then differ only in which target distribution is used, not in the optimizer.

**Results:** A single-algorithm pipeline is reported to match staged training across the standard phases; quantitative comparisons are truncated.

**Why it matters:** If you fine-tune or RL-tune report generators or medical VLMs, a unified objective simplifies the pipeline and clarifies what each stage is actually doing.


### 3. [Look at Where You Think: Localization-Head Alignment for Fine-Grained Perception in Thinking VLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39649)
**Authors:** Yuan Wang, Wei Wu, Hongning Wang  
**arXiv:** `2610.39649`  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Fine-Grained Perception, Attention Interpretability

**TL;DR:** Thinking VLMs miss small details because a few localization heads fail to select the right visual tokens, and aligning those heads fixes it without extra inference passes.

**Problem:** Reasoning VLMs generate thousands of tokens yet still miss small details in high-resolution images. Zooming methods cost extra forward passes, and outcome rewards never tell the model where to look.

**Method:** Analyzing Qwen3.5-4B, the authors identify a small set of attention heads that pick evidence from thousands of visual tokens and show that the bottleneck is selection rather than perception. They introduce a localization-head alignment objective that trains these heads to attend to the relevant region during reasoning.

**Results:** Alignment improves fine-grained perception on high-resolution benchmarks without added inference cost; exact gains are truncated.

**Why it matters:** Chest X-ray findings are small and high-resolution; a head-level alignment signal is a cheap way to improve grounding in report-generation VLMs and offers an interpretability hook for evaluation.
