1. Comorbidity-Aware Radiology Report Generation.

IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging  ·  Sep 18, 2026

Hongze Zhu, Hong Liu, Yawen Huang, Chen Hu et al. (9 authors)

Radiology Report GenerationChest X-RayMedical Imaging

This paper tackles two long-standing problems in chest X-ray report generation: diseases usually co-occur, which explodes the space of possible label combinations and leaves each combination with very little training signal, and standard autoregressive training optimizes next-token prediction rather than clinical correctness. The proposed comorbidity-aware approach restructures the image-to-label mapping so the model generalizes across disease combinations instead of memorizing them.

3. Bridging the Gap in Ophthalmic AI: MM-Retinal-Reason Dataset and OphthaReason Model toward Dynamic Multimodal Reasoning.

IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging  ·  Sep 15, 2026

Ruiqi Wu, Yu'ang Yao, Na Su, Tengfei Ma et al. (14 authors)

Vision-Language ModelsMedical ReasoningBenchmark Datasets

The authors argue that existing medical multimodal LLMs only do shallow "basic reasoning" — matching visual features to labels — while real diagnosis requires integrating findings, history, and clinical knowledge over multiple inference steps. They release MM-Retinal-Reason, a dataset targeting complex ophthalmic reasoning, and OphthaReason, an RL-trained model designed to handle it.

4. Rapid patient-specific neural networks for X-ray to volume registration

Nature  ·  Sep 16, 2026

Vivek Gopalakrishnan, David-Dimitris Chlorogiannis, Andrew Abumoussa, Anna M. Larson et al. (9 authors)

Medical ImagingImage RegistrationDeep Learning

A Nature paper on training small neural networks on the fly, per patient, to register 2D X-ray images to 3D volumes such as CT. Because the network is fit to the individual anatomy rather than a population, it sidesteps the generalization failures of pretrained registration models and is fast enough for intraoperative use.

5. Adaptive concept rule learning for interpretable and generalizable medical image classification.

Medical Image Analysis  ·  Sep 15, 2026

Yibo Gao, Hangqi Zhou, Zheyao Gao, Bomin Wang et al. (7 authors)

Interpretable AIMedical ImagingModel Generalization

Concept-based classifiers ground their predictions in human-readable diagnostic signs, but they typically explain only one image at a time, leaving clinicians unable to audit the model's overall decision logic. This work learns adaptive concept rules that give a global, inspectable decision structure while also improving generalization across datasets.

6. Staged purpose-blinded evaluation of provenance risk from a general-purpose generator in breast ultrasound

npj Digital Medicine  ·  Sep 19, 2026

Zhenqi Zhang, Min Zhuang, Ziyue Hu, Yang Li et al. (24 authors)

Generative AI EvaluationMedical ImagingAI Safety

The study runs a staged, purpose-blinded evaluation to test whether breast ultrasound images produced by a general-purpose image generator can be traced back to their source — a provenance risk with implications for both patient privacy and dataset contamination. The blinding protocol is a useful template for evaluating synthetic medical imagery without biasing the assessors.

7. SurgDepth: Test-Time Adaptation of Depth Foundation Models for Surgical Scene Understanding.

IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging  ·  Sep 17, 2026

Shengyao Wang, Junpei Xue, Xincong Zhong, Yihang Bao et al. (8 authors)

Foundation ModelsTest-Time AdaptationSurgical Imaging

Depth foundation models trained on natural images collapse when applied to endoscopic video, and retraining them for every new camera and operating room is impractical. SurgDepth adapts these models at test time without requiring any surgical training data, making a general-purpose vision foundation model usable in a specialized clinical domain.

8. From Technical Performance to Clinical Readiness: A Phase-Based Framework for Evidence Standards in Clinical Artificial Intelligence

NEJM AI  ·  Sep 17, 2026

Ivan Nenadic Zak Loring Peter A. Noseworthy Faraz S. Ahmad Luiz Vasconcelos Marat Fudim and Venkatesh L. Murthy

Clinical AIEvaluation FrameworksTranslational Research

NEJM AI proposes a phased evidence framework, modeled loosely on clinical trial phases, for moving medical AI from benchmark accuracy to demonstrated clinical readiness. It offers concrete standards for what evidence a model should produce at each stage, which is directly useful when framing the evaluation section of an imaging or report-generation paper.
