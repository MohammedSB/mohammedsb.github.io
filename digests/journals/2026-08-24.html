Foundation ModelsMedical ImagingReview

A review that maps the fast-moving landscape of foundation models in medical imaging, organizing it around core model components, clinical applications across modalities and anatomies, and open challenges. 2. RankByGene: Gene-Guided Histopathology Representation Learning Through Cross-Modal Ranking Consistency.

IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging  ·  Aug 21, 2026

Wentao Huang, Meilong Xu, Xiaoling Hu, Shahira Abousamra et al. (12 authors)

Multimodal LearningSelf-Supervised LearningComputational Pathology

RankByGene learns histopathology image representations by supervising them with paired spatial transcriptomics data, using a cross-modal ranking-consistency objective instead of forcing direct pixel-to-gene alignment. Because ranking relationships survive spatial distortion and modality differences, the approach yields more robust image features than standard alignment-based cross-modal training.

3. Safety and security of large language models in healthcare

Nature  ·  Aug 19, 2026

Jan Clusmann, Oscar Freyer, Max Ostermann, Dyke Ferber et al. (15 authors)

LLM EvaluationClinical AIAI Safety

A Nature analysis of how large language models can fail or be attacked when deployed in healthcare, covering hallucination, data leakage, prompt manipulation, and model poisoning. It argues that clinical LLM deployment needs safety and security evaluation frameworks distinct from the general-purpose benchmarks currently used.

4. Building safer clinical agents: the case for residency-level benchmarks in medical artificial intelligence.

Lancet Digital Health  ·  Aug 20, 2026

Kameron C Black, Bryan J Bunning, Yixing Jiang, Jonathan H Chen

LLM EvaluationClinical AIMedical Education

This piece argues that exam-style medical question benchmarks are a poor proxy for clinical competence and proposes evaluating clinical AI agents against residency-level tasks instead — longitudinal reasoning, handoffs, and supervised decision-making under uncertainty. The framing borrows the graduated-autonomy structure of medical training as a template for judging when an agent is safe to act.

An analysis quantifying the lag between European CE marking and US FDA clearance for AI-enabled radiology software, showing systematic delays and asymmetries between the two regulatory pathways.
