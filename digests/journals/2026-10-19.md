### 1. [An all-in-one foundation model for multimodal medical image restoration and enhancement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01310-z)
*Nature Machine Intelligence · Oct 13, 2026*  
**Authors:** Luyang Cao, Zhijian Wu, Jian Zhang, Lei Qi et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Medical Imaging, Multimodal Learning

This Nature Machine Intelligence paper introduces a single foundation model that handles restoration and enhancement tasks across multiple medical imaging modalities, rather than training a separate network per task. It is directly relevant to self-supervised and multimodal pretraining strategies for medical images and could serve as a preprocessing or representation backbone for downstream chest X-ray work.

### 2. [EyeGraph: A Multi-granularity Medical Vision-Language Foundation Model with Knowledge Graph Reasoning for Ophthalmic Interpretation.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2026.3742614)
*IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging · Oct 13, 2026*  
**Authors:** Zhiyuan Cai, Yiwei Ren, Yixiang Liu, Jialin Li et al. (11 authors)  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Foundation Models, Hallucination

EyeGraph is an ophthalmic vision-language foundation model that combines multi-granularity image-text alignment with knowledge graph reasoning to reduce hallucinations in generated interpretations. The approach of grounding VLM outputs in a structured medical ontology is transferable to radiology report generation and its evaluation.

### 3. [Repurposing adult AI radiograph models for neonatal care through continual learning: an international multicentre study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01812-6)
*Nature Biomedical Engineering · Oct 13, 2026*  
**Authors:** Vishwanatha M. Rao, Emma Chen, Agustina Saenz, Oishi Banerjee et al. (75 authors)  
**Tags:** Chest X-Ray, Continual Learning, Domain Adaptation

This international multicentre study repurposes adult radiograph AI models for neonatal imaging using continual learning, addressing the severe distribution shift between adult and neonatal anatomy. It offers practical evidence on how pretrained chest X-ray models can be adapted to underserved populations without catastrophic forgetting.

### 4. [Noise2Match: Self-Supervised Low-Dose CT Denoising via Adaptive Inter-Slice Matching.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2026.3744092)
*IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging · Oct 16, 2026*  
**Authors:** Langrui Zhou, Guang Li, Xinyu Huang, Ziteng Zhou et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Medical Imaging, CT

Noise2Match proposes a self-supervised low-dose CT denoising method that exploits adaptive matching between adjacent slices, removing the need for paired noisy-clean training data. It sidesteps the pixel-wise independent noise assumption of classic self-supervised denoisers, making it relevant to label-free pretraining on volumetric medical images.

### 5. [Hierarchical visual prompt disentanglement for continual rare disease diagnosis.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2026.104355)
*Medical Image Analysis · Oct 17, 2026*  
**Authors:** Yang Hu, Yuanyuan Chen, Qingjie Zeng, Yong Xia  
**Tags:** Few-Shot Learning, Continual Learning, Medical Imaging

This Medical Image Analysis paper tackles few-shot class-incremental diagnosis of rare diseases by disentangling hierarchical visual prompts that mirror the taxonomic structure of medical ontologies. Exploiting label hierarchy rather than a flat label space improves generalization to fine-grained disease subtypes under extreme data scarcity.

### 6. [Extending synthetic medical education: generating OSCE cases with LLMs](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03421-8)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 19, 2026*  
**Authors:** Kaiz P. Esmail, Arya S. Rao, Richard S. Lee, Ali Ebada et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** AI For Medical Education, LLMs, Synthetic Data

This npj Digital Medicine study uses large language models to generate synthetic OSCE (objective structured clinical examination) cases for medical training. It extends prior work on synthetic medical education content and raises questions about quality assurance and clinical validity of LLM-authored assessment material.

### 7. [Sycophantic AI, critical-feedback tolerance, and the case for productive friction in medical training](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03389-5)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 16, 2026*  
**Authors:** Zhi Chen, Bo Jia, Kangxiang Ji, Huilin Sheng et al. (6 authors)  
**Tags:** AI For Medical Education, LLM Safety, Clinical AI

This perspective argues that sycophantic AI assistants undermine medical trainees' tolerance for critical feedback and calls for designing productive friction into educational AI tools. It connects LLM alignment behaviors to downstream effects on clinical reasoning development, a key concern for AI in medical education.

### 8. [GWDRO-Lung: Gromov–Wasserstein distributionally robust learning for calibrated pulmonary nodule malignancy prediction across imaging cohorts](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03323-9)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 17, 2026*  
**Authors:** Yubo Yan, An Yan, Wen Li, Jianlong Bu et al. (5 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Robustness, Calibration

GWDRO-Lung applies Gromov-Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization to pulmonary nodule malignancy prediction, targeting calibrated performance across heterogeneous imaging cohorts. It addresses the cross-site generalization and calibration failures that commonly plague thoracic imaging models deployed outside their training distribution.
