### 1. [Why high scores do not mean application readiness for health AI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04500-9)
*Nature Medicine · Jul 2, 2026*  
**Tags:** Clinical AI, LLM Evaluation, Foundation Models

This perspective argues that strong benchmark performance by health AI systems does not translate into real-world clinical readiness, highlighting the gap between leaderboard scores and deployment safety. It is directly relevant to anyone designing evaluation metrics for generative medical models, including radiology report generation.

### 2. [BoneCoT: multicentre validation of a whole-body skeleton foundation model for bone metastases guided by clinician-derived chain of thought](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01736-1)
*Nature Biomedical Engineering · Jul 2, 2026*  
**Authors:** Hui Zhao, Ruipeng Zhang, Zhiyu Wang, Yifeng Gu et al. (7 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Medical Imaging, Multimodal Learning

BoneCoT is a whole-body skeleton foundation model for detecting bone metastases that incorporates clinician-derived chain-of-thought reasoning and is validated across multiple centers. It combines foundation-model pretraining with structured reasoning, a pattern of interest for radiology AI and report-style outputs.

### 3. [Large language models are powerful electronic health record encoders](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02915-9)
*npj Digital Medicine · Jul 6, 2026*  
**Authors:** Stefan Hegselmann, Georg von Arnim, Tillmann Rheude, Noel Kronenberg et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** LLM, Clinical AI, Representation Learning

This study shows that large language models can serve as powerful encoders of electronic health records, producing representations that outperform prior approaches on clinical prediction tasks. It illustrates how foundation-model embeddings transfer to structured medical data, informing multimodal fusion strategies.

### 4. [Explainable foundation model for dementia screening and risk stratification using retinal fundus images](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02968-w)
*npj Digital Medicine · Jul 4, 2026*  
**Authors:** Changho Han, Jaewon Kim, Hyeokjong Lee, Hyunwoo Joo et al. (6 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Medical Imaging, Explainable AI

An explainable foundation model uses retinal fundus images to screen for dementia and stratify risk, linking an accessible imaging modality to systemic disease. Its emphasis on explainability and self-supervised imaging pretraining aligns closely with vision foundation-model research in medicine.

### 5. [Wavelet-inspired diffusion model with near-field constraint for real-time echocardiography dehazing.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2026.104204)
*Medical Image Analysis · Jul 2, 2026*  
**Authors:** Xue Gao, Peng Huang, Fangyan Tian, Wei Guo et al. (9 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Diffusion Models, Cardiac Imaging

This paper introduces a wavelet-inspired diffusion model with a near-field physical constraint to remove near-field haze from echocardiography in real time. It is a generative-imaging method addressing a concrete artifact-reduction problem relevant to computer vision for medical imaging.

### 6. [Co-assistant networks by pathology foundation model and convolutional neural network for gigapixel whole slide image analysis.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2026.104202)
*Medical Image Analysis · Jul 2, 2026*  
**Authors:** Zhuoran Liu, Junyi Shen, Lei Cui, Meilian Xu et al. (6 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Medical Imaging, Computational Pathology

The work pairs a pathology foundation model with a CNN in a co-assistant architecture to analyze gigapixel whole-slide images under multiple-instance learning, addressing limits of self-attention-only feature extraction. It is directly relevant to combining foundation-model features with task-specific networks in medical vision.

### 7. [Externally Tested AI Models for Malignancy Classification of Lung Nodules at CT: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.250331)
*Radiology: AI · Jul 2026*  
**Authors:** Oke Dimas Asmara, Eline G M Steenhuis, Kim de Jong, Anita Joseph et al. (9 authors)  
**Tags:** Radiology AI, LLM Evaluation, Clinical AI

This systematic review and meta-analysis pools the diagnostic accuracy of externally tested AI models for classifying lung nodule malignancy on chest CT, emphasizing generalization beyond the training distribution. It offers rigorous benchmarking evidence important for anyone evaluating radiology AI in realistic screening settings.

### 8. [AI as a Safety Net Reader for Mammograms Classified as Normal or Benign in the French Screening Program.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.250989)
*Radiology: AI · Jul 2026*  
**Authors:** Christophe Tourasse, Benoît Mesurolle, Maud Ottavy, Patricia Soler-Michel et al. (5 authors)  
**Tags:** Radiology AI, Clinical AI, Breast Imaging

This retrospective study evaluates whether AI can act as a safety-net second reader for mammograms initially classified as normal or benign in the French screening program. It demonstrates a concrete clinical-workflow integration of imaging AI aimed at reducing missed cancers.
