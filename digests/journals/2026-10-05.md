### 1. [Interpretable multimodal retrieval augmented diagnosis for breast ultrasound with multinational clinical validation and reader study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03272-3)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 3, 2026*  
**Authors:** Han Jang, Subin An, Sunghyun Jung, Hye Ji et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** Multimodal Learning, Medical Imaging, Retrieval-Augmented Diagnosis

This study presents an interpretable multimodal retrieval-augmented system for diagnosing breast ultrasound images. It was validated on clinical data from multiple countries and tested in a reader study comparing it with clinicians.

### 2. [Checklist for Artificial Intelligence in Medical Imaging (CLAIM): Explanation, Elaboration, and Examples.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260835)
*Radiology: AI · Sep 30, 2026*  
**Authors:** Tugba Akinci D'Antonoli, Lisa C Adams, Amine Amyar, Ken Chang et al. (16 authors)  
**Tags:** Reporting Standards, Medical Imaging, Research Methodology

The CLAIM Steering Committee gives a detailed explanation of each item in the updated Checklist for Artificial Intelligence in Medical Imaging, with worked examples. The aim is to cut down on inconsistent readings of the checklist so that imaging AI studies are reported more transparently and can be reproduced.

### 3. [Standardized pre-consultation by a large language model agent vs ophthalmology residents: a randomized clinical trial](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03232-x)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 5, 2026*  
**Authors:** Ming-Jie Luo, Yunxi Lai, Wei Wang, Shaowei Bi et al. (14 authors)  
**Tags:** LLM Agents, Clinical AI, Randomized Trial

A randomized clinical trial compared a large language model agent with ophthalmology residents at taking standardized pre-consultation histories from patients. It is a direct, trial-based comparison of LLM agents and trainee doctors in a real clinical workflow, which also makes it relevant to medical education.

### 4. [Cortex-grounded diffusion models for brain image generation.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2026.104339)
*Medical Image Analysis · Sep 29, 2026*  
**Authors:** Fabian Bongratz, Yitong Li, Sama Elbaroudy, Timo Grimmer et al. (7 authors)  
**Tags:** Diffusion Models, Synthetic Data, Neuroimaging

The authors propose diffusion models that use cortical anatomy to condition the generation of synthetic brain images, instead of weaker signals such as labels or text. The goal is anatomically realistic synthetic data that can make up for rare phenotypes, differences between scanners, and limited longitudinal coverage.

### 5. [AI-powered medical devices must be tested in real-world settings](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03046-7)
*Nature · Sep 29, 2026*  
**Tags:** Clinical AI, AI Evaluation, Regulation

This commentary argues that AI-powered medical devices should be tested in real-world clinical settings, not only on retrospective benchmarks. It points to the gap between lab results and how the devices actually perform once deployed.

### 6. [Availability of performance evidence of approved AI diagnostic software in pathology and hematology morphology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03356-0)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 5, 2026*  
**Authors:** Diponkor Mondal, Mikko Purhonen, Ana Marušić, Oscar Brück  
**Tags:** AI Regulation, Digital Pathology, Evidence Assessment

The study looks at how much performance evidence is publicly available for approved AI diagnostic software in pathology and hematology morphology. It shows how transparent, or not, the validation behind commercially approved medical AI products is.

### 7. [Deep Learning Analysis of Dual-Modality US Videos for the Characterization of Superficial Lymphadenopathy.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260131)
*Radiology: AI · Sep 30, 2026*  
**Authors:** Yangyang Zhu, Ran Cao, Zhibin Zhu, Jinxia Zhang et al. (14 authors)  
**Tags:** Deep Learning, Ultrasound, Video Analysis

A deep learning network trained on dual-modality ultrasound videos from five centers was developed to tell benign from malignant superficial lymph node enlargement. It also sorts malignant cases into subtypes, making use of the temporal and multimodal information in ultrasound.

### 8. [The clinician-artificial intelligence scientist: a proposed career pathway in medicine.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101077)
*Lancet Digital Health · Sep 29, 2026*  
**Authors:** Yilan Wu, Ariel Yuhan Ong, Haichao Chen, Gabriel Dawei Yang et al. (7 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Education, Clinical AI, Career Development

This Viewpoint proposes a formal 'clinician-AI scientist' career path, modeled on the traditional clinician-scientist. The idea is to train people who can connect AI development with useful clinical implementation.
