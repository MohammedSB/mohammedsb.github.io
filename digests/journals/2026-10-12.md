### 1. [An open vision-language model for diverse medical applications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04626-w)
*Nature Medicine · Oct 6, 2026*  
**Authors:** Andrew Sellergren, Sahar Kazemzadeh, Fereshteh Mahvar, Atilla Kiraly et al. (88 authors)  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Foundation Models, Medical Imaging

This paper presents an openly released vision-language model built to work across many medical tasks that combine images and text. Because it is open, researchers can study it, fine-tune it and compare against it, which proprietary multimodal medical models do not allow.

### 2. [Photography-based dental plaque detection and report generation among preschool children using PlaqueSAM](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03385-9)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 11, 2026*  
**Authors:** Jing Hao, Kaixin Guo, Zhiyi Shan, Yanqi Yang et al. (7 authors)  
**Tags:** Report Generation, Segmentation, Clinical AI

PlaqueSAM is a Segment Anything–based system that finds dental plaque in ordinary photographs of preschool children's teeth and writes a report from what it finds. It pairs segmentation with automated report writing in a low-cost screening setting, which is close to radiology report generation and referring segmentation.

### 3. [Artificial intelligence across the medical education continuum: institutional, learning, readiness and safety systems](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03362-2)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 10, 2026*  
**Authors:** Rushi Patel, Ceyhun Haziroglu, Akshith Ullal, Ahmed Soliman et al. (9 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Education, Clinical AI, AI Safety

This article lays out how AI is entering medical education at every stage, from medical school through residency to practicing clinicians. It is organized around four areas: institutional systems, learning, readiness and safety, which gives educators a framework for adopting AI responsibly.

### 4. [MAGIC: an international network for evaluating generative artificial intelligence in global health](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04706-x)
*Nature Medicine · Oct 6, 2026*  
**Authors:** Rui Yang, Weihao Xuan, Tianyu Liu, Danielle S. Bitterman et al. (66 authors)  
**Tags:** Generative AI Evaluation, Global Health, Benchmarking

MAGIC is an international network set up to test generative AI tools in global health settings, including low-resource ones. Its goal is evaluation that reflects diverse populations and real clinical conditions rather than benchmarks drawn mostly from high-income countries.

### 5. [Patient-Conditioned Adaptive Offsets for Reliable Diagnosis across Subgroups.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2026.3740613)
*IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging · Oct 6, 2026*  
**Authors:** Gelei Xu, Yuying Duan, Jun Xia, Ruining Deng et al. (6 authors)  
**Tags:** Algorithmic Fairness, Medical Imaging, Diagnostic AI

Many fairness methods remove patient attributes such as demographics, but in medicine those attributes often carry real diagnostic information. This work instead lets patient characteristics adjust the model's decision offsets, which keeps that information while making diagnoses more reliable across patient subgroups.

### 6. [ARNAI: Artifact Removal Network Based on Autoencoding and Inpainting for Robust Spinal Image Segmentation and Measurement.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.251134)
*Radiology: AI · Oct 7, 2026*  
**Authors:** Sang-Jin Park, Jinyoung Choi, Seokwon Kim, Seungeon Song et al. (16 authors)  
**Tags:** Radiography, Segmentation, Image Inpainting

ARNAI uses an autoencoder with inpainting to remove the artifacts caused by spinal implants on postoperative lateral lumbar radiographs. It then segments the spine and measures spinopelvic parameters automatically, and was tested on an internal dataset and an external one from a second institution.

### 7. [Decoupling Multi-Contrast Super-Resolution: Self-Supervised Implicit Re-Representation for Unpaired Cross-Modal Synthesis.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2026.3739662)
*IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging · Oct 9, 2026*  
**Authors:** Yinzhe Wu, Hongyu Rui, Fanwen Wang, Jiahao Huang et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, MRI, Super-Resolution

Most deep learning methods for multi-contrast MRI super-resolution need large paired low- and high-resolution datasets and work only at fixed upscaling factors. This paper uses self-supervised implicit neural representations so the model can learn from unpaired cross-modal data while still using population-level priors.

### 8. [FHIR-AgentEval: a modular sandbox for benchmarking clinical LLM agents with memory-augmented configurations](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03348-0)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 10, 2026*  
**Authors:** Youssef Mokssit, Kamalakkannan Ravi, Mengshu Nie, Junyoung Kim et al. (5 authors)  
**Tags:** LLM Agents, Clinical AI, Benchmarking

FHIR-AgentEval is a modular sandbox for benchmarking LLM agents that work with clinical data in the FHIR health-record standard. It lets researchers compare agent setups, including ones with added memory, in a reproducible environment.
