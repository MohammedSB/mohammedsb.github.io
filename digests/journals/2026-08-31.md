### 1. [Performance and label efficiency of traditional deep-learning models and a retina-specific foundation model for ocular and systemic disease detection: a retrospective comparative study.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101031)
*Lancet Digital Health · Aug 28, 2026*  
**Authors:** Samantha Min Er Yew, Xiaofeng Lei, Jocelyn Hui Lin Goh, Ke Zou et al. (29 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Self-Supervised Learning, Medical Imaging

This retrospective study benchmarks RETFound, a self-supervised retina-specific foundation model, against three ImageNet-pretrained supervised backbones (ResNet50, ViT-Base, SwinV2) for detecting ocular and systemic diseases from retinal images. It directly probes whether domain-specific SSL pretraining actually buys accuracy and label efficiency over strong generic baselines — the central question for anyone building medical vision foundation models.

### 2. [Evaluating foundation models on official German medical licensing examinations: Implications for high-stakes assessment and AI-assisted medical education](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03082-7)
*npj Digital Medicine · Aug 31, 2026*  
**Authors:** Lasse Cirkel, Johannes Knitza, Volker Schillings, Alexander Oksche et al. (6 authors)  
**Tags:** LLM Evaluation, Medical Education, Foundation Models

The authors evaluate current foundation models on Germany's official medical licensing examinations, a high-stakes, non-English benchmark that is less likely to be contaminated than USMLE-style question sets. Results are framed around what model performance means for assessment integrity and for AI-assisted medical education.

### 3. [nnMIL: a generalizable multiple instance learning framework for computational pathology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01767-8)
*Nature Biomedical Engineering · Aug 25, 2026*  
**Authors:** Xiangde Luo, Jinxi Xiang, Yuanfeng Ji, Ruijiang Li  
**Tags:** Computational Pathology, Multiple Instance Learning, Generalization

nnMIL is a multiple-instance learning framework designed to work out-of-the-box across many whole-slide pathology tasks rather than being hand-tuned per dataset. It targets the reproducibility and generalization gap in weakly supervised gigapixel image analysis, offering a standardized recipe on top of patch-level foundation model features.

### 4. [Toward unified and comprehensive automated electroencephalogram interpretation: a multicentre development and validation of an electroencephalogram foundation model.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101039)
*Lancet Digital Health · Aug 25, 2026*  
**Authors:** Chenxi Sun, Ioannis Karakis, Aline Herlopian, Marcus C Ng et al. (66 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Clinical AI, Multitask Learning

This multicentre study develops and externally validates a multidomain EEG foundation model that reaches expert-level performance across a broad range of interpretation tasks instead of one narrow classification problem. It is a useful template for how to scale pretraining and prove cross-site generalization for a non-image clinical signal modality.

### 5. [Surgical instrument-tissue characterization via multi-task self-supervised instrument segmentation and motion estimation.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2026.104279)
*Medical Image Analysis · Aug 28, 2026*  
**Authors:** Yuxuan Liu, Xinyao Zhou, Yating Luo, Yunfei Luan et al. (7 authors)  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Segmentation, Surgical Vision

The paper jointly learns surgical instrument segmentation and tissue motion estimation in a single multi-task self-supervised model, exploiting the fact that the two tasks constrain each other. Avoiding dense manual masks and motion labels makes this a good case study in self-supervised objectives for fine-grained medical video understanding.

### 6. [SF-DisenNet: Self-Supervised Function-Guided Disentanglement for Fine-Tuning-Free Brain Representation.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2026.3727625)
*IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging · Aug 26, 2026*  
**Authors:** Kaixiang Shu, Ronglin Zhang, Jiaqiang Li, Xuegang Song et al. (6 authors)  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Neuroimaging, Representation Learning

SF-DisenNet uses functional-connectivity information to guide self-supervised disentanglement of structural MRI representations, so one pretrained brain encoder serves many disorders instead of training a model per disease. Because the learned representation needs no fine-tuning downstream, it enables direct cross-disorder comparison — relevant to anyone chasing transferable medical image embeddings.

### 7. [A Deep Learning Model Incorporating Spatiotemporal Asymmetries on Longitudinal Mammograms to Predict Breast Cancer Risk.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.250888)
*Radiology: AI · Sep 2026*  
**Authors:** Zhengbo Zhou, Dooman Arefan, Margarita L Zuley, Jules H Sumkin et al. (5 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Risk Prediction, Deep Learning

This deep learning model predicts breast cancer risk by explicitly encoding bilateral and longitudinal asymmetries across sequential mammograms rather than scoring a single exam. Building temporal and left-right comparison into the architecture reflects how radiologists actually read priors, and the design idea transfers naturally to longitudinal chest X-ray analysis.

### 8. [AgentDS-BUS: a fine-tuning-free agentic breast ultrasound malignancy classification framework with decoupled segmentation and feature analysis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03144-w)
*npj Digital Medicine · Aug 29, 2026*  
**Authors:** Pengze Li, Lijie Huang, Tao Wu, Francisco J. Maldonado et al. (10 authors)  
**Tags:** Agentic AI, Vision-Language Models, Medical Imaging

AgentDS-BUS classifies breast ultrasound malignancy using an agent pipeline that decouples lesion segmentation from downstream feature reasoning, with no task-specific fine-tuning required. It is a concrete example of composing pretrained vision and language components into a training-free clinical imaging system, an alternative to end-to-end supervised models.
