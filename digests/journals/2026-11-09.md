### 1. [Collaborative generalist-specialist AI for safe worklist deprioritization of non-contrast brain computed tomography](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03447-y)
*npj Digital Medicine · Nov 5, 2026*  
**Authors:** Seungjun Lee, Wooyul Paik, Gihong Kim, Won Jung Chung et al. (6 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Clinical AI, Radiology Workflow

This npj Digital Medicine study pairs a generalist AI model with a specialist model to safely deprioritize non-contrast brain CT scans on radiology worklists, so normal studies can be read later without delaying urgent findings. It is directly relevant to radiology AI deployment, showing how collaborative model ensembles can be validated for triage rather than diagnosis.

### 2. [Performance of artificial intelligence versus clinician left-ventricular segmentation of cardiac MRI for mortality prediction: a retrospective, multicentre, prognostic study.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101042)
*Lancet Digital Health · Jun 16, 2027*  
**Authors:** George Joy, Karan Punjabi, George Searle, Zaid Abdulelah et al. (24 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Segmentation, Clinical AI

Across multiple centres, deep-learning segmentation of the left ventricle on cardiac MRI produced ejection fraction estimates that predicted all-cause mortality better than expert clinician segmentations. The result argues that AI-derived imaging biomarkers can be judged by downstream prognostic value rather than only by overlap with human contours, a useful framing for segmentation evaluation.

### 3. [Foundation artificial intelligence models for health recognition by use of face photographs (FAHR-Face): a model development and validation study.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101056)
*Lancet Digital Health · Jun 16, 2027*  
**Authors:** Fridolin Haugg, Grace Lee, John He, Leonard Nürnberg et al. (17 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Medical Imaging, Clinical AI

FAHR-Face is a foundation model pretrained on over 40 million facial photographs and then fine-tuned to estimate biological age and predict survival, with validation across diverse cohorts. It is a clean example of the pretrain-then-adapt recipe applied to a non-standard medical imaging modality, and of how visual foundation models can yield clinically meaningful risk signals.

### 4. [ISLES'24: Final stroke infarct prediction with multimodal imaging and clinical data. Where do we stand?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2026.104362)
*Medical Image Analysis · Nov 5, 2026*  
**Authors:** Ezequiel de la Rosa, Ruisheng Su, Mauricio Reyes, Evamaria O Riedel et al. (48 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Benchmarks, Multimodal Learning

The ISLES'24 challenge paper reviews the state of final stroke infarct prediction from multimodal acute imaging combined with clinical variables, summarising what top methods did and where they still fail. It is a useful snapshot of benchmark design and multimodal fusion in neuroimaging, including lessons on evaluation and generalisation.

### 5. [A gait foundation model for prediction of multisystem health phenotypes from 3D skeletal motion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04724-9)
*Nature Medicine · Nov 6, 2026*  
**Authors:** Adam Gabet, Sarah Kohn, Guy Lutsker, Shira Gelman et al. (14 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Digital Health, Self-Supervised Learning

This Nature Medicine paper introduces a foundation model trained on 3D skeletal gait data that predicts health phenotypes spanning multiple organ systems from how a person walks. It extends the foundation-model paradigm to motion data and shows that a single pretrained representation can transfer to many downstream clinical prediction tasks.

### 6. [Multimodal cell foundation models require synergistic integration beyond alignment](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01328-3)
*Nature Machine Intelligence · Nov 9, 2026*  
**Authors:** Till Richter, Eric Zimmermann, James Hall, Fabian J. Theis et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Multimodal Learning

This Nature Machine Intelligence perspective argues that multimodal cell foundation models need genuinely synergistic integration of modalities rather than the simple cross-modal alignment used in CLIP-style approaches. The critique of alignment-only training is relevant to anyone building multimodal medical models, including vision-language systems for imaging and reports.

### 7. [Development and validation of early warning scores predicting 24 h mortality addressing intercurrent medical interventions in Denmark: a multicentre retrospective cohort study of 2·08 million hospital encounters.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101067)
*Lancet Digital Health · Nov 5, 2026*  
**Authors:** Alexandros Katsiferis, Neil Scheidwasser, Tri-Long Nguyen, Theis Lange et al. (13 authors)  
**Tags:** Clinical AI, Model Evaluation, Risk Prediction

Using 2.08 million Danish hospital encounters, the authors compare the National Early Warning Score, simplified rules, and machine learning models for 24-hour mortality while explicitly handling the bias introduced by clinical interventions. The paper emphasises net clinical benefit over raw discrimination and asks whether added model complexity actually pays off, a question that applies broadly to clinical AI evaluation.

### 8. [Context is built, not given: context readiness as the next benchmark for clinical use of artificial intelligence.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101124)
*Lancet Digital Health · Nov 4, 2026*  
**Authors:** Taro Shimizu, Ethan Goh  
**Tags:** Clinical AI, LLM Evaluation, AI Deployment

This Lancet Digital Health comment proposes 'context readiness' as a new benchmark for clinical AI, arguing that the context a model needs for safe use must be deliberately constructed rather than assumed to exist. It is a short but thought-provoking piece on how evaluation frameworks should evolve as large language and multimodal models enter clinical workflows.
