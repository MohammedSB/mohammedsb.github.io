### 1. [Stochastic Siamese MAE Pretraining for Longitudinal Medical Images.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2026.3745973)
*IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging · Oct 22, 2026*  
**Authors:** Taha Emre, Arunava Chakravarty, Thomas Pinetz, Dmitrii Lachinov et al. (12 authors)  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Medical Imaging, Foundation Models

STAMP extends masked autoencoder pretraining with a stochastic Siamese design so that learned representations of 3D medical volumes capture temporal change across longitudinal scans. This addresses a known gap in MAE-style self-supervision, which learns strong single-image features but ignores disease progression over time.

### 2. [Interpretable Deep Learning for Opportunistic Screening of Left Ventricular Dysfunction Screening Using Chest Radiographs.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260161)
*Radiology: AI · Oct 21, 2026*  
**Authors:** Sowon Jang, Dong Hyun Lee, Hye Soo Cho, Sun-Ju Byeon et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** Chest X-Ray, Interpretable AI, Clinical AI

An interpretable deep learning model detects reduced left ventricular ejection fraction from routine chest radiographs using same-day CXR-echocardiography pairs from health checkup populations. The work frames CXR AI as an opportunistic prescreening tool to select who should receive echocardiography, a practical deployment angle for chest X-ray models.

### 3. [Examining reliance patterns on AI advice in medical imaging: a mixed-methods, randomised, crossover experiment.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101050)
*Lancet Digital Health · Oct 23, 2026*  
**Authors:** Susanne Gaube, Ekaterina Jussupow, Eesha Kokje, Timo K Koch et al. (13 authors)  
**Tags:** Human-AI Collaboration, Medical Imaging, Clinical AI

A randomised crossover experiment measured how experts and novices rely on AI advice when reading medical images, documenting both over-reliance and under-reliance. The results help explain why clinician-plus-AI teams often fail to beat either the human or the model alone, which matters for anyone designing or evaluating assistive radiology tools.

### 4. [Agentic Automation of BT-RADS Scoring Using an End-to-End System for Standardized Glioma Follow-up Assessment.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260234)
*Radiology: AI · Oct 21, 2026*  
**Authors:** Mohamed Sobhi Jabal, Jikai Zhang, Dominic LaBella, Jessica L Houk et al. (9 authors)  
**Tags:** LLM Agents, Radiology AI, Neuroimaging

A multi-agent system combining large language models with a convolutional network automates BT-RADS scoring for post-treatment glioma MRI, integrating imaging trends, medication effects, and radiation timing. Evaluated on over five hundred cases, it is a concrete example of agentic LLM pipelines producing standardized structured radiology assessments.

### 5. [TRAM-AI: A Framework for Transparency in Reporting of Medical Imaging Datasets for Artificial Intelligence Development.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260538)
*Radiology: AI · Oct 21, 2026*  
**Authors:** Kartik Bose, Pankaj Gupta, Lisa Adams, Rupesh Agrawal et al. (36 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Datasets, Reporting Standards

TRAM-AI is a Delphi-derived consensus checklist for verifying whether medical imaging datasets described as public are actually usable for AI development. It targets the common frustration of papers citing datasets that are inaccessible, undocumented, or incomplete in practice.

### 6. [Foundation artificial intelligence models for health recognition by use of face photographs (FAHR-Face): a model development and validation study.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101056)
*Lancet Digital Health · Jun 16, 2027*  
**Authors:** Fridolin Haugg, Grace Lee, John He, Leonard Nürnberg et al. (17 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Clinical AI, Computer Vision

FAHR-Face is a foundation model trained on over forty million facial photographs and fine-tuned for biological age estimation and survival prediction. It demonstrates the large-scale pretrain-then-finetune recipe applied to a non-radiological health signal, with validation of the downstream prognostic tasks.

### 7. [ConComp: Constraining and compensating latent representations for semi-supervised medical image segmentation.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2026.104354)
*Medical Image Analysis · Oct 24, 2026*  
**Authors:** Jie Yang, Yongchao Wang, Junchao Fan, Yang Wei et al. (7 authors)  
**Tags:** Semi-Supervised Learning, Medical Image Segmentation

ConComp tackles internal covariate shift in semi-supervised medical image segmentation by constraining and compensating latent representations, improving pseudo-label reliability during training. It is relevant to anyone working on segmentation with scarce annotations, where consistency regularization and pseudo-labeling are the standard tools.

### 8. [From simulation to pedagogy: multi-model and randomized evaluation of structured AI standardized patients](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03372-0)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 23, 2026*  
**Authors:** Ping Wu, Yu Han, Jing Zhang, Yunqi Li et al. (11 authors)  
**Tags:** AI For Medical Education, LLM Evaluation

This study evaluates structured AI standardized patients across multiple language models with a randomized design, moving from simulation feasibility toward pedagogical evidence. It offers a template for rigorously assessing LLM-driven tools in clinical training rather than relying on anecdotal demonstrations.
