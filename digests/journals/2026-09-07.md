### 1. [From algorithms to patient outcomes — lessons from one of the first randomized trials of AI in medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04633-x)
*Nature Medicine · Sep 7, 2026*  
**Authors:** Kristina Lång  
**Tags:** Clinical AI, Randomized Trials, AI Evaluation

This Nature Medicine piece draws lessons from one of the first randomized controlled trials of an AI tool in clinical practice, examining how algorithmic performance translated (or failed to translate) into patient outcomes. It highlights the gap between retrospective benchmark accuracy and real-world clinical benefit, and argues for prospective trial designs as the standard for evaluating medical AI.

### 2. [Limited benchmarks constrain the conclusions of a general-purpose versus clinical AI comparison](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04638-6)
*Nature Medicine · Sep 3, 2026*  
**Authors:** Brett Beaulieu-Jones, Shamim Nemati  
**Tags:** LLM Evaluation, Clinical AI, Benchmarks

This Matters Arising letter (with the authors' reply at index 3) critiques a prior comparison of general-purpose versus clinical-specialist AI models, arguing that the limited benchmark set undermines the conclusions. The exchange is a useful case study in how benchmark selection shapes claims about medical foundation model performance.

### 3. [Human-AI Collaboration in Radiology: The Blind Spots.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260325)
*Radiology: AI · Sep 2, 2026*  
**Authors:** Su Hwan Kim, Lisa C Adams, Benedikt Wiestler, Dennis M Hedderich  
**Tags:** Radiology AI, Human-AI Collaboration

This narrative synthesis argues that radiology AI research has overemphasized standalone algorithm performance while neglecting the human-AI interactions that determine real clinical value. It identifies three underrecognized determinants of effective collaboration, including cognitive factors in how radiologists integrate AI outputs into their reading workflow.

### 4. [Development and external validation of a contrastive learning foundation model for ECG-based prediction of cardiovascular diseases and outcomes.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101092)
*Lancet Digital Health · Sep 1, 2026*  
**Authors:** Michael Ko, Matteo Gadaleta, Eric J Topol, Evan D Muse et al. (5 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Contrastive Learning, Cardiology AI

The authors develop ECG-CLIP, a large-scale contrastive learning foundation model for electrocardiograms that reduces reliance on task-specific labels, and externally validate it for predicting cardiovascular diseases and outcomes. It is a direct translation of CLIP-style self-supervised pretraining to a non-imaging clinical signal, with lessons for label-efficient medical representation learning.

### 5. [Prototype-guided Multimodal Retrieval for Knowledge-assisted Interventional Radiology.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2026.3728817)
*IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging · Sep 1, 2026*  
**Authors:** Jingxiong Li, Chenglu Zhu, Yuxuan Sun, Yixuan Si et al. (9 authors)  
**Tags:** Vision-Language Models, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, Interventional Radiology

This work presents a prototype-guided multimodal retrieval framework for knowledge-assisted interventional radiology, addressing the fact that existing medical RAG methods are text-centric and miss fine-grained visual-textual evidence. It combines procedural images with domain knowledge to support joint visual-clinical reasoning in a specialized radiology setting.

### 6. [Image Quality in the Era of Artificial Intelligence: Understanding the Limitations of AI-based Image Reconstruction and Postprocessing.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260100)
*Radiology: AI · Sep 2, 2026*  
**Authors:** Jana G Delfino, Jason L Granstedt, Frank W Samuelson, Robert Ochs et al. (5 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Image Reconstruction, Radiology AI

This review examines how AI-based image reconstruction and postprocessing in radiology can produce images that look sharper and cleaner while introducing new failure modes that decouple perceived quality from actual diagnostic information content. It is essential reading for anyone whose downstream models or evaluations depend on AI-processed imaging inputs.

### 7. [A Deep Learning Model Incorporating Spatiotemporal Asymmetries on Longitudinal Mammograms to Predict Breast Cancer Risk.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.250888)
*Radiology: AI · Sep 2026*  
**Authors:** Zhengbo Zhou, Dooman Arefan, Margarita L Zuley, Jules H Sumkin et al. (5 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Breast Cancer Risk, Deep Learning

The authors build a deep learning risk model that explicitly encodes bilateral and longitudinal asymmetries across sequential mammograms to predict future breast cancer risk. The approach shows how temporal and contralateral structure in imaging can be exploited as an inductive bias beyond single-image classification.

### 8. [What LUNA25 Teaches Us about AI for Lung Cancer Screening.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260657)
*Radiology: AI · Sep 2026*  
**Authors:** Eduardo Moreno Júdice de Mattos Farina, Gilberto Szarf  
**Tags:** Lung Cancer Screening, Radiology AI, Benchmarks

This Radiology: AI commentary reflects on what the LUNA25 challenge reveals about the state of AI for lung cancer screening on chest CT, including where nodule detection and malignancy estimation models succeed and fall short. It offers a grounded perspective on community benchmarks as drivers of thoracic imaging AI progress.
