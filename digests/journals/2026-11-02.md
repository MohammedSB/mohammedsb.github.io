### 1. [A clinical multimodal vision language foundation model with fine-grain explainability](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04655-5)
*Nature Medicine · Oct 29, 2026*  
**Authors:** Yang Bai, Haoran Cheng, Yang Zhou, Jun Zhou et al. (49 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Medical Imaging, Vision-Language Models

This paper presents a clinical multimodal vision-language foundation model designed for medical imaging tasks with fine-grained explainability, bridging the gap between CLIP-style pretraining and clinical interpretability. It is directly relevant to work on vision-language models for radiology and chest X-ray analysis with explainable AI components.

### 3. [Understanding clinical reasoning variability in medical large language models: a mechanistic interpretability study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03423-6)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 31, 2026*  
**Authors:** Mirage Modi, Jordan E. Krull, Timothy D. Gauntner, Mingjia Li et al. (13 authors)  
**Tags:** Clinical AI, LLM Evaluation, Medical Education

This study investigates why large language models exhibit variability in clinical reasoning using mechanistic interpretability methods, identifying internal model behaviors that lead to inconsistent diagnostic conclusions. It is relevant to understanding LLM reliability in medical decision-support and AI-assisted medical education.

### 4. [Interpretable graph learning of spatial TME patterns in digital pathology for gastric cancer prognosis](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03398-4)
*npj Digital Medicine · Oct 30, 2026*  
**Authors:** Menglei Hua, Xiaohan Zheng, Xinzhuo Wang, Ruihao Qin et al. (12 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Computational Pathology, Clinical AI

This paper proposes an interpretable graph learning framework that models spatial tumor microenvironment patterns in digital pathology slides for gastric cancer prognosis. The approach connects spatial image features with clinical outcomes in an explainable way, relevant to multimodal and self-supervised learning in medical imaging.

### 5. [Automation Bias and Deskilling in Radiology AI: A Confidence-stratified Triage Model for Workflow Integration.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.260591)
*Radiology: AI · Oct 28, 2026*  
**Authors:** Nalan Karunanayake, Javin Schefflein, Binsheng Zhao, Lawrence H Schwartz  
**Tags:** Radiology AI, Clinical AI, Human-AI Interaction

This perspective piece examines automation bias and deskilling risks as radiologists increasingly rely on AI triage tools, proposing a confidence-stratified workflow integration model to mitigate these harms. It is highly relevant to responsible deployment of AI in radiology and understanding human-AI collaboration in clinical settings.

### 6. [Foundation artificial intelligence models for health recognition by use of face photographs (FAHR-Face): a model development and validation study.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101056)
*Lancet Digital Health · Jun 16, 2027*  
**Authors:** Fridolin Haugg, Grace Lee, John He, Leonard Nürnberg et al. (17 authors)  
**Tags:** Foundation Models, Clinical AI, Medical Imaging

FAHR-Face is a foundation model trained on over 40 million facial photographs and fine-tuned for biological age estimation and survival prediction, demonstrating that facial appearance encodes meaningful health signals. This work exemplifies the growing area of vision-based health foundation models applicable to multimodal medical AI.

### 7. [Re-timing mortality risk through individualized modelling of lifespan trajectories](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01822-4)
*Nature Biomedical Engineering · Nov 2, 2026*  
**Authors:** Ting Xu, Qingsheng Peng, Hengtong Li, Meng Wang et al. (17 authors)  
**Tags:** Clinical AI, Predictive Modeling, Digital Health

This study presents an individualized lifespan trajectory modeling approach to re-time mortality risk estimation, moving beyond population-level statistics to personalized predictions. It illustrates how machine learning can support longitudinal health risk stratification in clinical settings.
