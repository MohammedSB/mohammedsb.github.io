### 1. [Quantitative mapping from conventional MRI using self-supervised physics-guided deep learning: Applications to a large-scale, clinically heterogeneous dataset.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2026.104295)
*Medical Image Analysis · Sep 9, 2026*  
**Authors:** Jelmer van Lune, Stefano Mandija, Oscar van der Heide, Matteo Maspero et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** Self-Supervised Learning, Medical Imaging, MRI Reconstruction

This work uses self-supervised, physics-guided deep learning to derive quantitative tissue parameter maps from ordinary clinical MRI scans, removing the need for special acquisition protocols. It was validated on a large, clinically heterogeneous dataset, suggesting quantitative MRI biomarkers could be extracted retrospectively at scale.

### 2. [CORE: Suppressing Spurious Similarity via Confidence-Aware Prototypes for Few-Shot Medical Image Segmentation.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TMI.2026.3732553)
*IEEE Trans. Medical Imaging · Sep 10, 2026*  
**Authors:** Chuanzhen Xu, Shumeng Li, Haoran Wang, Jian Zhang et al. (8 authors)  
**Tags:** Few-Shot Learning, Medical Image Segmentation

CORE improves few-shot medical image segmentation by weighting foreground pixels according to confidence instead of treating them equally when building class prototypes, which suppresses misleading feature similarities. The result is more reliable segmentation of unseen anatomical structures from only a handful of annotated examples.

### 3. [DeepVEST: Deep Learning-based Vessel Segmentation and Erasure in Breast MRI for Improved Lesion Assessment.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.250630)
*Radiology: AI · Sep 9, 2026*  
**Authors:** Marco Cantone, Tianyu Zhang, Claudio Marrocco, Luyi Han et al. (11 authors)  
**Tags:** Medical Imaging, Segmentation, Breast Imaging

DeepVEST is a deep learning method that segments and then digitally erases blood vessels from dynamic contrast-enhanced breast MRI before generating maximum intensity projections. Removing vascular clutter made lesions easier to see and interpret, developed and tested on the public Duke breast MRI dataset.

### 4. [A Deep Learning Model to Predict Breast Cancer Recurrence Using Longitudinal Mammograms and Clinical Data.](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.250941)
*Radiology: AI · Sep 9, 2026*  
**Authors:** Chunyao Lu, Xin Wang, Tianyu Zhang, Xinglong Liang et al. (12 authors)  
**Tags:** Multimodal Learning, Clinical AI, Medical Imaging

A multimodal deep learning model combines sequences of mammograms taken over time with static clinical variables to predict both the risk and the subtype of breast cancer recurrence. Using longitudinal imaging rather than a single scan lets the model capture how a patient's tissue changes across years of follow-up.

### 5. [Evaluating AI-assisted detection of fetal intracranial malformations in prenatal ultrasound practice: a multicentre, self-crossover, randomised controlled trial in China.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.landig.2026.101040)
*Lancet Digital Health · Sep 10, 2026*  
**Authors:** Ting Lei, Ning Shang, BaiHong Xie, YingChun Luo et al. (10 authors)  
**Tags:** Clinical AI, Prospective Validation, Medical Imaging

A multicentre randomised self-crossover trial in China tested whether an AI system helps sonographers detect fetal brain malformations during real prenatal ultrasound exams. Notably, it also measured whether AI assistance changed detection of anomalies the system was never trained to recognise, probing automation bias in practice.

### 6. [Multi-Agent collaboration as a complementary architecture for AI-generated medical examination items](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-03187-z)
*npj Digital Medicine · Sep 14, 2026*  
**Authors:** Zhehan Jiang  
**Tags:** LLM Evaluation, AI For Medical Education, Multi-Agent Systems

Instead of prompting a single language model, this study has several LLM agents collaborate — drafting, critiquing and revising — to produce medical examination questions. The multi-agent setup is presented as a complement to single-model generation, improving item quality for medical assessment.

### 7. [Prospective evidence for conversational medical AI is hard, but non-negotiable](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04639-5)
*Nature Medicine · Sep 14, 2026*  
**Authors:** Mike Schaekermann, Anil Palepu, Adam Rodman, Ami Parekh et al. (14 authors)  
**Tags:** Clinical AI, LLM Evaluation, Conversational AI

This perspective argues that conversational medical AI, such as chatbots that talk directly with patients, cannot be judged by benchmark scores alone and requires prospective clinical studies. It acknowledges such trials are slow and expensive but treats them as a non-negotiable condition for deployment.

### 8. [Clinical usability of an explainable AI decision support tool and evaluation of multimodal models in NSCLC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04488-2)
*Nature Medicine · Sep 13, 2026*  
**Authors:** Arsela Prelaj, Vanja Miskovic, Matteo Sacco, Alberto Ferrarin et al. (124 authors)  
**Tags:** Explainable AI, Multimodal Learning, Clinical Decision Support

The authors evaluated multimodal models for non-small-cell lung cancer alongside an explainable AI decision support tool, testing not just accuracy but whether clinicians can actually use the explanations. It is a useful template for pairing multimodal model benchmarking with real usability assessment at the bedside.
