1. An explainable biomedical foundation model via large-scale concept-enhanced vision–language pretraining

Nature Biomedical Engineering  ·  Aug 17, 2026

Yuxiang Nie, Sunan He, Yequan Bie, Yihui Wang et al. (27 authors)

Foundation ModelsVision-Language ModelsExplainable AI

This paper introduces a biomedical foundation model pretrained on large-scale image-text pairs where medical concepts are explicitly injected into the vision-language objective, so the model's predictions can be traced back to human-interpretable clinical concepts rather than opaque embeddings. It is directly relevant to CLIP-style medical pretraining, offering a route to interpretability without sacrificing the transfer performance of contrastive image-text models.

2. Foundation models in biomedical imaging: turning hype into reality

Amgad Muneer, Kai Zhang, Ibraheem Hamdi, Rizwan Qureshi et al. (9 authors)

Foundation ModelsMedical ImagingEvaluation

A perspective piece assessing where biomedical imaging foundation models actually deliver value versus where claims outrun evidence, covering data scale, benchmark design, and generalization failures across sites and scanners. It lays out what rigorous evaluation of self-supervised and multimodal imaging models should look like before clinical deployment.

3. UniMedDiff: a knowledge-enhanced diffusion model for medical image generation from clinical reports

npj Digital Medicine  ·  Aug 13, 2026

Lexuan Liu, Peng Huang, Bowen Guo, Shuyu Liang et al. (8 authors)

Medical Image GenerationVision-Language ModelsDiffusion Models

UniMedDiff is a diffusion model that synthesizes medical images conditioned on free-text clinical reports, using structured medical knowledge to keep generated findings consistent with the described pathology. This is the inverse direction of report generation and is useful both for data augmentation and for probing how well text-to-image models encode radiological semantics.

4. Deep-learning triage of three-dimensional pathology datasets for comprehensive and efficient pathologist assessments

Nature Biomedical Engineering  ·  Aug 12, 2026

Gan Gao, Renao Yan, Andrew H. Song, Huai-Ching Hsieh et al. (23 authors)

Medical ImagingDeep LearningClinical Workflow

The authors use deep learning to triage large three-dimensional pathology volumes, flagging the regions most likely to matter so pathologists review a small fraction of the data instead of the whole stack. The design pattern — model as an attention router over huge volumetric data rather than a standalone diagnostic — transfers naturally to volumetric radiology workflows.

CliniSense AI automatically scores trainees' clinical skills and returns real-time feedback, replacing slow and inconsistent human observation-based assessment. 6. MultiMed-RAG: leveraging multi-source knowledge and agent collaboration for medical tasks

npj Digital Medicine  ·  Aug 17, 2026

Yuxing Lu, Sin Yee Goi, Xukai Zhao, J. Ben Tamo et al. (8 authors)

Retrieval-Augmented GenerationMedical LLMsMulti-Agent Systems

MultiMed-RAG combines retrieval over several distinct medical knowledge sources with a set of collaborating agents that divide up reasoning across clinical tasks. It is relevant as a grounding strategy: pulling in external evidence to reduce hallucination is the same problem faced when generating factually accurate radiology reports.

7. Uncertainty-inspired open-set model for identifying infantile fundus abnormalities

npj Digital Medicine  ·  Aug 15, 2026

Xinyu Zhao, Zhenquan Wu, Xuemei Zhu, Mengrui Zhang et al. (21 authors)

Medical ImagingUncertainty QuantificationModel Robustness

This model detects abnormalities in infant fundus images while explicitly handling open-set conditions, using uncertainty estimates to abstain on disease categories it was never trained on. Open-set awareness is a persistent weak point in medical imaging classifiers, which otherwise assign confident labels to anything they are shown.

Nature Medicine  ·  Aug 13, 2026

Paul Webster

Clinical AIEvidence GenerationDeployment

This piece examines the widening gap between how quickly AI clinical decision support tools are being deployed and how slowly rigorous evidence about their effects on patient outcomes accumulates. It argues for evaluation designs that keep pace with rapid rollout, a framing worth borrowing when defending metrics for generative clinical models.
