1. From algorithms to patient outcomes — lessons from one of the first randomized trials of AI in medicine

Nature Medicine  ·  Sep 7, 2026

Kristina Lång

Clinical AIRandomized TrialsAI Evaluation

This Nature Medicine piece draws lessons from one of the first randomized controlled trials of an AI tool in clinical practice, examining how algorithmic performance translated (or failed to translate) into patient outcomes. It highlights the gap between retrospective benchmark accuracy and real-world clinical benefit, and argues for prospective trial designs as the standard for evaluating medical AI.

3. Human-AI Collaboration in Radiology: The Blind Spots.

Radiology: AI  ·  Sep 2, 2026

Su Hwan Kim, Lisa C Adams, Benedikt Wiestler, Dennis M Hedderich

Radiology AIHuman-AI Collaboration

This narrative synthesis argues that radiology AI research has overemphasized standalone algorithm performance while neglecting the human-AI interactions that determine real clinical value. It identifies three underrecognized determinants of effective collaboration, including cognitive factors in how radiologists integrate AI outputs into their reading workflow.

4. Development and external validation of a contrastive learning foundation model for ECG-based prediction of cardiovascular diseases and outcomes.

Lancet Digital Health  ·  Sep 1, 2026

Michael Ko, Matteo Gadaleta, Eric J Topol, Evan D Muse et al. (5 authors)

Foundation ModelsContrastive LearningCardiology AI

The authors develop ECG-CLIP, a large-scale contrastive learning foundation model for electrocardiograms that reduces reliance on task-specific labels, and externally validate it for predicting cardiovascular diseases and outcomes. It is a direct translation of CLIP-style self-supervised pretraining to a non-imaging clinical signal, with lessons for label-efficient medical representation learning.

This review examines how AI-based image reconstruction and postprocessing in radiology can produce images that look sharper and cleaner while introducing new failure modes that decouple perceived quality from actual diagnostic information content.
