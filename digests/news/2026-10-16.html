1. Houthi missiles strike Yemen presidential palace, killing five

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Houthi forces fired ballistic missiles at the al-Maashiq presidential palace complex in Aden on Thursday, killing five people. According to Yemen's information minister, the prime minister and other ministers were unhurt. It was the first Houthi strike on the palace, which is the seat of the internationally recognised government.

Background: Fighting between the Iran-aligned Houthis and the Saudi-led coalition has escalated. The coalition has listed dozens of Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure in recent weeks, and Houthi forces are advancing on the besieged city of Taiz.

Why it matters: Striking the government's seat in its southern stronghold shows the Houthis' reach is growing as the war spills into Saudi Arabia. Regional events are already being disrupted: an EU-Gulf summit and a Saudi golf tournament have been postponed over security concerns.

Different views: BBC treats the Yemeni information minister as its main source and leads with the casualties and the ministers' safety. The Guardian places the strike within the wider Saudi-Houthi war and runs a piece in which a former Yemeni PM says the future of western security is being decided around Taiz. The Jerusalem Post foregrounds the Houthi warning to airlines about Saudi airspace and pairs the story with analyses on how Israeli air-defence technology could help Riyadh, and why arms for Yemen's government shouldn't come from the US or Israel. In this set, Al Jazeera covers the escalation only through its effect on Saudi sporting events.

2. 'We'll knock it out fast': Trump threatens Iran's Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site with strikes

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Trump threatened to strike Iran's Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site, saying 'we'll knock it out fast.' He ruled out using a nuclear weapon but would not deny reports that the US might use the GBU-43/B 'mother of all bombs' (MOAB). A CNN report cited US officials saying strike plans include using the MOAB against Iranian energy infrastructure and nuclear sites.

Background: The US maintains a blockade and sanctions on Iranian energy exports, though tanker data shows some Iranian gas and jet fuel is still getting out. Diplomats say Tehran has told Hezbollah to prepare for an American attack it considers 'a matter of time.'

Why it matters: Openly naming targets and weapons points to a possible new US-Iran war that could draw in Hezbollah and Israel. Shipping and energy markets are already under strain: BBC Verify counts a record 12 vessels struck near Hormuz in a single week.

Different views: Al Jazeera leads with Trump ruling out a nuclear strike and explains why that matters, treating restraint as the news. The Jerusalem Post leads with the threat and the attack plans, and surrounds it with Israeli and diplomatic-source stories: a Netanyahu adviser saying Israel is 'very close' to creating conditions for regime change, Iran warning Hezbollah, and unrest inside Iran. BBC's related coverage in this set focuses on the measurable shipping and oil-export effects around Hormuz rather than the strike rhetoric.

3. Israeli settlers continue progrom amid olive harvest in occupied West Bank

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Israeli settlers armed with clubs and guns attacked Palestinians in the occupied West Bank during the olive harvest, injuring at least two people, including a Washington Post journalist. Settlers have also set olive trees and vehicles on fire. Separately, Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi told Israeli President Isaac Herzog that Israel must stop the 'forced establishment' of settlements.

Background: The olive harvest is an economic and cultural mainstay for West Bank Palestinians, and settler attacks on harvesters and groves have become a recurring flashpoint each autumn.

Why it matters: The escalating violence, now reaching foreign journalists, is adding to diplomatic pressure on Israel over settlement expansion, including direct criticism from China.

Different views: Al Jazeera calls the attacks 'pogroms' and centres Palestinian harvesters and their destroyed property. BBC covers the settlement issue mainly as a diplomatic exchange between China and Israel's president. The Jerusalem Post has no settler-violence story in this set; its Palestinian coverage focuses instead on internal politics, such as challenges to Abbas's postponement of the election and polling on Hamas's popularity.
