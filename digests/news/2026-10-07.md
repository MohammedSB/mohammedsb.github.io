### 1. [Yemen civil war: what sparked latest escalation and who is involved?](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/07/yemen-civil-war-what-sparked-latest-escalation-and-who-is-involved)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Yemen's civil war has escalated sharply. A Houthi offensive has displaced more than 200,000 people, according to the UN migration agency. The Houthis have fired missiles at Aden airport and at the Saudi cities of Riyadh and Khamis Mushait. Saudi-backed government forces say they have 'secured' the Red Sea waterway near the Bab al-Mandab Strait, but reports conflict on whether they also took the port city of Mokha.

**Background:** The war is between the UN-recognised government, backed by Saudi Arabia, and the Iran-backed Houthis. It now reaches Saudi oil sites and one of the world's most important shipping chokepoints.

**Why it matters:** Fighting over Bab al-Mandab threatens global shipping and draws Saudi Arabia, and possibly its new defence partners Pakistan and Turkey, more directly into the war. It is also causing a fast-growing humanitarian emergency.

**Different views:** The Guardian and BBC focus on the government–Houthi conflict and its maritime stakes, and the BBC points out that the Saudi-backed forces' own claims contradict each other. The Jerusalem Post repeatedly calls the Houthis 'Iran-backed' and stresses their missile strikes on Saudi Arabia and the regional alliance angle. It also ran graphic footage of government troops running over unarmed Houthi fighters with their hands up, which the BBC and Guardian summaries do not mention.


### 2. [One year on, Trump's Gaza plan has not stopped Israeli fire or disarmed Hamas - explainer](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-910750)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Israelis marked three years since the Hamas-led 7 October 2023 attack with vigils and survivor accounts, while Gaza also reached one year of ceasefire under Trump's plan. The plan remains largely stalled: Hamas is still armed, Israeli forces hold most of Gaza, reconstruction has barely begun, and Israeli strikes continue. One strike this week killed at least four Palestinians, including a Civil Defence rescue volunteer.

**Background:** The ceasefire began a year ago under a US-brokered framework that was meant to lead to Hamas disarming, Israeli withdrawal and reconstruction. Tent cities keep spreading across Gaza's ruins.

**Why it matters:** The anniversary shows how little the ceasefire has resolved. Gaza's future governance, Israeli security demands and the humanitarian crisis are all still unsettled, and Israeli politics has moved further right.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera calls it a 'so-called ceasefire', says Israel attacked Gaza on nine days out of ten, and describes the past three years as genocide. The Jerusalem Post treats the stalled plan as a shared failure and puts Hamas's refusal to disarm next to the continued Israeli fire. The BBC and Guardian give much of their anniversary coverage to Israeli survivors and the rebuilding of kibbutzim such as Nir Oz. The BBC also carries an account of conditions in Gaza from its Gaza correspondent.


### 3. [US sets new demands for Iran deal: What are they?](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/7/us-sets-new-demands-for-iran-deal-what-are-they?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** US Vice President Vance has set out new US conditions for a deal with Iran, including reduced nuclear enrichment. Al Jazeera reads this as leaving some room for compromise, though large obstacles remain. At the same time, satellite images show renewed activity at a secret Iranian nuclear site that Israel bombed in March, and the White House is defending Trump's comment about letting Iran 'take out' Los Angeles and San Diego.

**Background:** The diplomacy follows open US/Israel–Iran hostilities this year, including Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities in March. A US senator says the Pentagon denied him access to a US base in Qatar to hide details of the Iran war.

**Why it matters:** Whether Washington and Tehran reach a deal or go back to war will shape regional security, nuclear proliferation risk and the Gulf states' position between the two.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera presents the US demands as a possible opening for diplomacy. The Jerusalem Post puts the Iranian threat first: rebuilding at the bombed site, an alleged Iranian drone plot that led US bombers to leave RAF Fairford (which Iran denies), and women getting military training for a 'self-sacrifice' campaign. The BBC covers the US domestic row over Trump's remarks, giving both the White House defence and Gavin Newsom's response that they were 'deranged'.
