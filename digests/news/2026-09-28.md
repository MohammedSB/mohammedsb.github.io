## Top Stories

### 1. [Trump rejects Iran’s seven-day peace deal to reopen strait of Hormuz](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/26/trump-dismiss-iran-seven-day-peace-deal-hormuz)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Donald Trump rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and resume nuclear talks in exchange for the US lifting its naval blockade of Iranian ports. His UN ambassador, Mike Waltz, said Iran was 'asking for everything up front' and was not negotiating in good faith. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran is still waiting for an official US answer through mediators, and a US official said separate mediated talks with each side are expected on Monday and Tuesday.

**Background:** The US-Iran war began with US-Israeli strikes on Iran in late February and has now lasted about six months. Iran presented the seven-day proposal on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly.

**Why it matters:** Brent crude rose more than 3% to above $107 a barrel and UK diesel hit a record price. Reports say Trump expects renewed strikes after the midterms, so the main diplomatic route out of the war looks stalled.

**Different views:** The Guardian leads with Trump's rejection, Waltz's bad-faith charge and the report of strikes after the midterms. Al Jazeera leads with the oil price shock and calls the conflict 'the US war.' The BBC leads with Araghchi's line that only negotiation can end the conflict, making the Iranian foreign minister the main voice. The Jerusalem Post runs many pieces that present the talks as still alive through mediators, and focuses on pressure for nuclear concessions, IRGC claims of seizing US underwater vehicles, and analysis that Washington wants 'something more to show' from the war.


### 2. [Inside Yemen's front-line city as Houthis battle for control](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw98005ndz7no?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Fighting between the Iran-aligned Houthis and Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces has escalated, including around the contested city of Taiz. The Houthis blamed Saudi F-15s for a strike on a Taiz market that they say killed seven and wounded about 40. They also threatened to hit Saudi bridges in retaliation, and schools in Riyadh switched to remote learning without warning.

**Background:** Yemen had about four years of relative calm after the Saudi-Houthi truce. The renewed fighting comes during the wider US-Iran war.

**Why it matters:** A reopened Saudi-Houthi front adds a second regional war tied to Iran. It threatens Saudi territory directly and is deepening a humanitarian crisis, with children leaving school to work.

**Different views:** The BBC reports from the front line alongside pro-government soldiers, so the battle is seen from their side. The Guardian focuses on civilians and children's lost schooling and covers neither side's military claims. The Jerusalem Post passes on Houthi casualty figures and accusations against Saudi strikes, noting that the Saudis say the targets were missile launch sites, and it also reports the disruption inside Riyadh. Al Jazeera has no Yemen coverage in this set.


### 3. [Israel does not have right to close UK consulate in Jerusalem, Foreign Office advised](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/sep/28/israel-no-right-close-jerusalem-uk-consulate-foreign-office-advised)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Former attorney general Dominic Grieve advised the UK Foreign Office that Israel has no legal right to close Britain's consulate in East Jerusalem, which Israel says it will shut on 8 October. At the same time, Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband is expected to tell Labour's conference that critics of the party's Gaza policy 'were right'. He will point to new UK sanctions on Israeli settlements as the response.

**Background:** The consulate in East Jerusalem has long served as Britain's mission to the Palestinians. Grieve's argument rests on East Jerusalem being occupied Palestinian territory, where Israel lacks sovereign authority over foreign missions.

**Why it matters:** This is a sharp worsening of UK-Israel relations. It pairs a UK policy shift toward sanctions with an Israeli move against a long-standing diplomatic presence for Palestinians.
