### 1. [US renews strikes on Iran as Trump threatens to attack underground nuclear site](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cdrv0p37k8jo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** The US carried out an 11th consecutive night of strikes on Iran, with Trump threatening to attack the underground 'Pickaxe Mountain' nuclear site near Natanz. Iran launched reprisals against US bases and allies, claiming hits on ships in the Strait of Hormuz and targets in Bahrain and Jordan, and the Pentagon acknowledged nearly 100 US troops injured in recent weeks.

**Background:** The US-Israel war on Iran began roughly five months ago; a ceasefire agreed in April has collapsed into the most sustained fighting yet, centered on Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz and its nuclear program.

**Why it matters:** A direct, prolonged US-Iran war is reshaping global energy markets and regional security, with US casualties and a $37.5bn cost driving political backlash in Washington.

**Different views:** Jerusalem Post frames it around Iranian desperation and Israeli calls for regime change to stop a near-complete bomb; Al Jazeera casts it as a costly 'war on Iran' and highlights the price tag and troop injuries; the Guardian frames it as Trump's self-made 'forever war' and a crisis of the global order.


### 2. [US to announce deal allowing Saudi Arabia a nuclear programme, reports say](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cj03r59z73po?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** The US is reported to be finalizing a deal granting Saudi Arabia a civilian nuclear program, which the Trump administration plans to submit to Congress within days. Reports indicate the pact makes no mention of normalization with Israel and reportedly lacks certain IAEA safeguards.

**Background:** A US-backed Saudi nuclear program fulfills a long-held Riyadh ambition and had previously been tied to potential Saudi-Israel normalization.

**Why it matters:** The deal could trigger regional proliferation concerns and reshape the US-Saudi-Israel triangle amid the ongoing Iran war.

**Different views:** BBC emphasizes the deal fulfilling Saudi ambitions but attracting concern in Israel and among US lawmakers; the Jerusalem Post stresses the absence of Israel normalization and missing IAEA safeguards as points of alarm.


### 3. [Houthis threaten to attack shipping tankers if they use Saudi Arabian ports on Red Sea](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/21/houthis-threaten-attack-tankers-saudi-arabian-ports-red-sea-shipping)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis warned shipping companies their tankers risk attack if they use Saudi Arabian Red Sea ports, moving to enforce a newly announced blockade. Saudi tankers reportedly made U-turns in the Red Sea to reroute via Suez.

**Background:** The Houthis have shut Bab al-Mandeb, and combined with Iran's chokehold on the Strait of Hormuz, key energy corridors are now under threat.

**Why it matters:** A Houthi blockade of Saudi ports opens a new front in the US-Iran war and could severely disrupt Saudi oil exports and global energy supplies.

**Different views:** The Guardian frames it as a threat to Saudi exports and global shipping; the Jerusalem Post frames the blockade as the Houthis opening a front against the US in its war with Iran.
