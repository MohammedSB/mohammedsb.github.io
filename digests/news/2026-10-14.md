### 1. [Houthi missile hits Aramco building after Saudi-led coalition strike on Yemen fuel depot](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/crx2d60y1py3o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** A Houthi missile hit Saudi Aramco's headquarters complex in Dhahran. The Saudi energy ministry said it caused a 'limited fire'. The strike came after the Saudi-led coalition hit a strategic fuel depot outside Sanaa, having first warned civilians to leave the area. The Houthis have vowed more attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities. Saudi defences intercepted further missiles aimed at Riyadh and Dhahran.

**Background:** The Houthis are an Iran-backed armed group. They have been fighting pro-government forces along Yemen's Red Sea coast, and their conflict with Riyadh has escalated sharply in recent weeks. In an earlier attack on Riyadh's international airport and another facility, 13 people were killed, and Saudi Arabia has promised a 'serious response'.

**Why it matters:** Direct missile exchanges between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis, now reaching core oil infrastructure, risk reopening a full Saudi-Houthi war. This comes as Gulf energy supply is already under strain from the US-Iran war and attacks in the Strait of Hormuz.

**Different views:** The BBC relies on the Saudi energy ministry's description of a 'limited fire'. The Jerusalem Post reports 'extensive damage' and ongoing explosions in Riyadh. It also carries Houthi threats, Trump's claim that the Houthis are attacking to drive up oil prices, and analysis urging Riyadh to work with Israel, the UAE and the US. The Guardian puts the Saudi strikes on Sanaa's fuel depot and the evacuation warnings to civilians first, and only then the missiles fired at Dhahran. The Jerusalem Post's Yemen aid report says the fighting is overwhelming hospitals, has cut aid to 800,000 people and has displaced more than 200,000.


### 2. [Iran steps up Hormuz surveillance: Attacks soar, oil flows crash again](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/14/iran-steps-up-hormuz-surveillance-attacks-soar-oil-flows-crash-again?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Ships in the Strait of Hormuz are being attacked more often than at any time since the US-Iran war began, and oil flows have fallen sharply again. Iran has stepped up its surveillance of the waterway. Sources say a container ship, the GFS Pride, sank near Hormuz after being hit by a projectile. According to UN International Maritime Organization data, it is the second vessel to sink since the war began.

**Background:** The US-Iran war began on 28 February. Washington reinstated its blockade of Iranian ports in July and in August launched 'Operation Economic Outcast' to cut Iran's remaining links with the global economy.

**Why it matters:** Hormuz is the world's most important oil chokepoint. Rising attacks there come as the IMF warns that emergency oil stocks are running low, so this is a source of global economic risk as well as a regional crisis.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera presents the shipping crisis as part of Iran actively increasing its control and surveillance of the strait. The Jerusalem Post's sinking report attributes the strike only to a 'projectile' and sources, and leans on IMO data. Its related coverage looks at whether Iran's economy is holding up under the blockade and at Trump's signals on talks.


### 3. [Board of Peace preparing for Gaza field survey to find locations for Hamas weapons handover](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-911631)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** The Board of Peace is preparing a field survey in Gaza to find locations where Hamas could hand over its weapons. Security officials say there is an enormous gap between how Hamas is behaving and US hopes of trading reconstruction for disarmament. Meanwhile, an Israeli air strike in Gaza City killed a Palestinian woman, and civilians rescued her young daughter from the burning apartment.

**Background:** The postwar framework for Gaza links reconstruction to Hamas disarmament, which is being overseen by the US-backed Board of Peace. Israeli strikes are still taking place in Gaza.

**Why it matters:** Whether Hamas disarms is the main obstacle to rebuilding Gaza and to any stable end to the war. Continued strikes show that violence on the ground has not stopped.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post frames Gaza around the disarmament process. It relies on security officials, focuses on Hamas's non-compliance and calls Hamas's response to Navi Pillay's Nobel Peace Prize opportunistic. Al Jazeera covers Gaza through civilian casualties from Israeli strikes, such as the woman killed and the girl rescued in Gaza City, and does not mention the disarmament negotiations.
