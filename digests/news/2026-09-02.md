## Top Stories

### 1. [US and Iran exchange more airstrikes, fuelling fears of wider return to hostilities](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/01/us-iran-airstrikes-launch)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US carried out a new wave of airstrikes on Iranian Revolutionary Guard targets, including a strike in the coastal town of Sirik that reportedly hit a private home hosting a wedding, killing four to five people — a child among them — and wounding 68. Iran responded by striking US bases and interests in Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan (US Marine base Camp Titin) and Iraq, with explosions reported near Eilat and sirens in southern Jordan. Iranian officials called the wedding strike a "war crime" and said Tehran "will no longer exercise restraint," threatening a response "several times larger."

**Background:** The exchange follows a flare-up last weekend that many hoped would stay contained, set against a US naval blockade around the Strait of Hormuz and an unraveling interim understanding between Washington and Tehran.

**Why it matters:** The strikes have spread the conflict onto the territory of four Gulf and Arab states hosting US forces, making a broad regional war — rather than a bounded US-Iran duel — the likeliest trajectory.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and the Guardian lead on the civilian toll and Iran's "war crime" accusation, with BBC Verify examining footage while noting the US insistence that it "never targets civilians"; the Jerusalem Post frames the same events as CENTCOM "completing a wave of strikes" in retaliation for attacks on US troops and stresses Iranian threats and the reach of its missiles.


### 2. [Two sailors killed in Iranian attack on ship in Strait of Hormuz, Saudi Foreign Ministry says](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-907312)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry said two sailors were killed in an Iranian attack on a ship in the Strait of Hormuz, while the IRGC said two oil tankers were disabled after striking sea mines while trying to transit the waterway. Separately, Saudi-loaded supertankers were hit by unknown projectiles in the Omani corridor, and the US reportedly began a "tanker for tanker" policy of directly striking Iranian ships rather than merely turning them back.

**Background:** Roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes through Hormuz; Iran has historically threatened the chokepoint, and a US-enforced blockade has now turned it into an active battlefield with mines and drifting cargo.

**Why it matters:** Killing foreign crew and mining the strait internationalizes the war economically, dragging Gulf producers and global shipping insurance into a conflict that had been mostly military — and explains the scramble for alternative crude, including the US-Venezuela oil deal.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post foregrounds Iranian mines and attacks on neutral Saudi-linked shipping as evidence of Iranian recklessness, while Al Jazeera's coverage frames the chokepoint mainly through the US blockade and the resulting global fuel-price and supply question.


### 3. [More than 900 killed in Gaza this year despite ceasefire, Hamas-run health ministry says](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c7708dy33pdo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Gaza's Hamas-run health ministry said more than 900 people have been killed in the Strip this year despite the ceasefire, putting the toll since 10 October above 1,300, including 301 children. On the same day Israel said special forces raided Gaza City to capture the head of Hamas's internal security service, with accompanying strikes killing four; the IDF separately confirmed killing two Hamas operatives, naming a Gaza City Brigade security commander.

**Background:** The 10 October ceasefire halted the full-scale war but left Israeli forces holding lines inside Gaza, with near-daily strikes and raids continuing; Hamas is simultaneously fighting armed clan-based militias contesting its control.

**Why it matters:** A ceasefire that has produced over a thousand deaths raises the question of whether the truce framework can survive, and each high-profile Israeli raid risks tipping it back into open war.

**Different views:** The BBC reports the toll while flagging its source as the Hamas-run ministry; the Jerusalem Post covers only the named militants killed and Hamas's internal security apparatus, presenting operations as targeted counterterrorism; Al Jazeera and the Guardian situate the deaths within a broader argument about Israeli conduct and Western complicity.
