1. Iran targets Bahrain and Jordan in retaliation as US strikes continue

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: The US launched a third consecutive night of strikes on Iran, hitting the port cities of Bushehr and Bandar Abbas, while Iran retaliated with missile and drone attacks on US allies (Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait, Oman) and struck oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. Trump announced the reinstatement of a naval blockade on Iran and demanded a 20% toll on all cargo passing through the strait, saying the US would take control of the waterway.

Background: The escalation follows earlier US-Israeli strikes on Iran and a breakdown of a memorandum of understanding, with Iran's attacks on Emirati and other tankers drawing condemnation from Gulf states.

Why it matters: A blockade and toll on the Strait of Hormuz — through which a large share of global oil transits — risks a direct US-Iran war and a global energy shock, with oil prices already surging over 3%.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames it as 'US bombs Iran again' emphasizing American aggression, while the Jerusalem Post stresses Gulf states uniting against the 'Islamic regime' and CENTCOM's precision; Iranian voices (via JPost) mock Trump's toll as transactional profiteering, and BBC/Guardian report the tit-for-tat exchanges more neutrally.

2. Israel sets October date for first elections since Hamas attacks in 2023

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Israel set 27 October as the date for national elections, the first since the Hamas-led attacks of 7 October 2023. The vote gives Israelis their first chance to pass judgment on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his coalition's handling of the wars in Gaza and Iran.

Background: Netanyahu's government has been under sustained domestic pressure over the 2023 attacks and the subsequent multi-front conflicts.

Why it matters: The outcome could reshape Israeli policy on Gaza, Iran, and Lebanon at a moment of acute regional escalation, and determine Netanyahu's political survival.

3. Yemen's Houthis launch missiles at Saudi Arabia after strikes on Sanaa airport

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis launched missiles at Saudi Arabia, saying they targeted Abha airport in retaliation for a strike on Sanaa airport, which they blame on Saudi Arabia. The strike on Sanaa followed a diverted Iranian flight carrying a Houthi delegation returning from Tehran.

Background: The exchange threatens a years-long de-escalation between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia and comes amid the broader US-Iran confrontation.

Why it matters: A renewed Houthi-Saudi war, alongside Houthi threats to Bab al-Mandeb, could open a second front and further imperil Red Sea and global shipping.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames the Sanaa airport strike and Yemen tensions around external aggression, while Yemen's Saudi-backed government (via the Guardian) casts the Iranian flights as an unacceptable violation of sovereignty carrying Houthi equipment; the Jerusalem Post analyzes why the Houthis appear intent on reigniting conflict with Saudi Arabia.
