1. Qatar PM to meet Trump: What’s the latest on US-Iran negotiations?

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Qatar's prime minister is meeting Donald Trump as Washington examines an Iranian two-day proposal developed with Qatari mediators. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the talks have made no progress and called Tehran's demands unacceptable, while Trump told CNN a return to war is 'highly unlikely' if Iran signs a strong agreement. The Jerusalem Post reports Israel and the US prepared a strike over the weekend that Trump held off on while mediation continued.

Background: The US-Israeli war on Iran began on February 28 and has since moved into a tense ceasefire-and-negotiation phase, with the US launching Task Force Iron Gate to choke Iran's land-border weapons trade and Iran threatening undersea cables and shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters: Whether the mediated proposal survives decides if the region returns to open war; Trump's post-midterm weakness and Israel's unresolved election leave both governments with narrow room to manoeuvre.

Different views: Al Jazeera foregrounds the Qatari-brokered proposal and Iran's domestic security crackdown, treating the mediation track as the main story. The Jerusalem Post foregrounds Rubio's rejection of 'outrageous' Iranian demands, sourced reporting that a strike was ready and only postponed, and Iranian spokesman Baghaei's framing of US pressure as a midterm diversion; its analysis pieces treat Israeli and US security sources as authoritative on the options.

2. Aramco Tower hit in heart of Saudi capital as Houthis launch fresh strikes

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: Houthi missile and drone strikes hit the Aramco Tower in Riyadh's central financial district, with BBC Verify authenticating footage of the damage. The Houthis say a Saudi air strike killed at least 33 people, which Saudi Arabia has not confirmed and says it is investigating. Separately, Syria confirmed it is sending troops to Saudi Arabia under a new defence cooperation agreement, while denying reports that Syrian forces are deploying inside Yemen.

Background: The Saudi-Houthi front has reignited alongside the wider Iran conflict, and al-Sharaa's Syria signed a defence pact with Riyadh on November 4 and is weighing membership of the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement, marking Damascus's shift into the Gulf-led Arab security system.

Why it matters: Strikes on the symbolic heart of Saudi finance plus a heavy claimed civilian toll in Yemen raise the risk of a second full-scale regional war, and Syrian troops on Saudi soil would formalise a realignment that cuts Iran out of Damascus.

Different views: BBC leads with its own verification of the Riyadh damage and explicitly flags that the 33-death figure is a Houthi claim Saudi Arabia has not addressed. The Jerusalem Post pairs the Houthi claim with the coalition's statement that it destroyed a missile launcher and notes Reuters could not verify the toll, and devotes several analyses to the Syrian-Saudi pact as a strategic win against Iran. Al Jazeera foregrounds Syrian officials' denial of a Yemen deployment and frames the move as 'security integration' rather than a military commitment.

3. Israel’s president picks Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot to form government

The Guardian · UK

What happened: President Isaac Herzog tasked Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party and Benjamin Netanyahu's strongest rival, with forming a government after last month's election left the Knesset deadlocked. Eisenkot began reaching out to Netanyahu's allies on Sunday, saying the prime minister had brought Israel to a historic crisis approaching a 'point of no return'.

Background: Netanyahu's coalition lost its majority in the election but neither bloc secured a clear path to power, leaving Israel without a settled government while the Iran ceasefire talks and the Gaza situation remain unresolved.

Why it matters: A former military chief replacing Netanyahu would reshape Israel's posture on Iran, Gaza and relations with Washington; failure would prolong paralysis or force another election at a moment of high regional risk.

Different views: The Guardian reports the mandate as a process story centred on Eisenkot's outreach and his criticism of Netanyahu. Al Jazeera's opinion piece reads the election result as proof that violent Israeli policies in the occupied territories and the region will continue regardless of who governs. The Jerusalem Post does not cover the mandate directly in this batch but folds the 'election shake-up' into its analysis of Iran war options, treating government formation chiefly as a constraint on military decision-making.
