1. Crowds gather as six-day funeral for former Iranian supreme leader begins

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Iran began a six-day series of mass funeral processions for former supreme leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, with crowds filling Tehran's Grand Mosalla mosque and authorities projecting up to 30 million participants nationwide. Processions are set to travel to Qom, Najaf and Kerbala.

Background: Khamenei was killed in February 2026 in the opening airstrike of a war launched by the US and Israel, ending his 37-year rule. His son Mojtaba has been named successor but remains out of public view after being wounded.

Why it matters: The funeral is a test of the Islamic Republic's stability and legitimacy after a decapitating strike, and a signal of whether a weakened Tehran will try to reassert regional influence.

Different views: The Guardian and BBC emphasize the scale of genuine popular mourning and millions of grievers; the Jerusalem Post frames it as staged 'theater of mourning' and focuses on 'kill Trump'/'death to America' chants and Iran's next strategic move, including that Mojtaba was barred from attending over assassination fears.

2. Palestinian officials slam decision to end UNRWA's role in Gaza as 'poltically motivated'

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: A 'Board of Peace' decision to end UNRWA's operational role in Gaza drew fierce condemnation from Palestinian officials, who called it politically motivated and vowed to do everything to preserve the agency.

Background: UNRWA is the main provider of aid, health and education to Palestinian refugees; Israel has long sought to dismantle it, accusing it of ties to Hamas.

Why it matters: Removing UNRWA from Gaza would reshape the entire humanitarian aid architecture during an ongoing catastrophe and carries symbolic weight for the Palestinian refugee cause.

Different views: Reported here via the Israeli Jerusalem Post, which quotes Palestinian officials describing the move as 'driven by the Israeli right wing, which seeks to destroy the Palestinian cause' — pro-Palestinian outlets would frame it as collective punishment, while Israeli framing casts UNRWA's removal as necessary reform.

3. Iran’s China envoy vows ‘special’ Hormuz treatment for ‘friendly’ countries

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: Iran's ambassador to China said Tehran will grant 'special' treatment to friendly countries transiting the Strait of Hormuz while charging new fees on vessels, insisting the charges will proceed despite US objections and Trump's claims to the contrary.

Background: The Strait of Hormuz is the world's most important oil chokepoint; Iran controls part of its territorial waters and has repeatedly threatened transit during periods of tension with Washington.

Why it matters: New fees or preferential treatment could disrupt global energy shipping, sharpen the US-Iran standoff after the war, and give Iran economic leverage over shipping nations.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames it as Iran defiantly rewarding 'friendly' states and asserting sovereignty; the Jerusalem Post (index 36) stresses the direct contradiction of Trump's denials and casts the charges as a provocative 'service fee' Iran insists is not a toll.
