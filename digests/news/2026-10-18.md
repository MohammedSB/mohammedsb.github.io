### 1. [European diplomats attacked by Israeli settlers in West Bank](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c962ydpmjm3zo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Israeli settlers attacked a group of European diplomats, a French mayor and the archbishop of Cambrai in Taybeh in the occupied West Bank during an attempt to stop Palestinians harvesting olives, injuring three people and damaging a vehicle. The Israeli military condemned the violence, while Netanyahu's office dismissed the episode as part of a 'smear' campaign; France and Belgium issued formal condemnations.

**Background:** Settler violence against Palestinian farmers spikes every autumn olive harvest, and this season has seen a sharp rise in attacks across the West Bank with few arrests or prosecutions.

**Why it matters:** An attack on accredited diplomats pushes settler violence from a Palestinian grievance into a direct diplomatic incident with EU states, and the split between the army's condemnation and the prime minister's denial exposes the gap between Israel's security establishment and its government.

**Different views:** The BBC gives equal weight to the army's condemnation and the prime minister's office calling it a 'smear', treating the official Israeli response as the story. The Guardian foregrounds the victims and names the attackers as 'settler militants' acting to block Palestinians from 'their own land', and Al Jazeera uses the word 'pogroms', frames the attack as part of a surge, and centres critics who say European condemnation without sanctions is hollow.


### 2. [Egypt to provide ‘necessary’ support to Saudi Arabia amid Houthi attacks](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/18/egypt-to-provide-all-necessary-and-possible-support-to-saudi?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Egypt's presidency said Cairo will provide Saudi Arabia with the 'necessary' support to defend its security after Houthi strikes on Riyadh's international airport and Saudi Aramco facilities, with Sisi seeking parliamentary backing for possible deployment. Separately, the UAE reportedly agreed with Riyadh to re-enter the Yemen conflict by resuming payments to southern government forces, and the Saudi-led coalition intercepted missiles headed for Yanbu as missiles hit Aden airport.

**Background:** The Yemen war had been largely frozen since a 2022 truce, but recent Houthi territorial gains and renewed cross-border strikes, reportedly aided by Iranian targeting intelligence, have pulled Gulf states and now Egypt back toward direct involvement.

**Why it matters:** A reactivated Gulf-Egyptian coalition against the Houthis, alongside Hormuz disruptions and oil above $100, risks folding Yemen back into the wider US-Israel confrontation with Iran and deepening what aid agencies already call a catastrophic humanitarian situation.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera stresses that any Egyptian role will be 'strictly defensive' and pairs the diplomacy with reporting on Yemen's humanitarian collapse. The Guardian treats the UAE's return and payments to southern forces as the key development, sourced to Yemeni insiders. The Jerusalem Post frames the escalation through Iran, leading with WSJ reporting on Iranian targeting intelligence for the Houthis and asking how Israel could help Saudi Arabia, positioning the war as part of a regional anti-Iran defence architecture.


### 3. [Iran's parliament approves Mehrdad Akhlaghi as defense minister after predecessor killed by Israel](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-912028)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Iran's parliament approved Mehrdad Akhlaghi, a four-decade veteran of the country's missile and aerospace industry previously sanctioned by the UN, as defence minister. He replaces interim minister Majid Ebn al-Reza, who stepped in after Aziz Nasirzadeh was killed in US-Israeli strikes in February.

**Background:** Iran has been rebuilding its security leadership and defence-industrial base since this year's strikes, while facing sanctions-driven economic strain and IRGC warnings about possible domestic unrest.

**Why it matters:** Appointing a missile-programme technocrat rather than a political figure signals Tehran's priority on rebuilding ballistic and drone capabilities at a moment of Hormuz mine incidents and renewed Houthi strikes, shaping how the US and Israel calculate the next phase of confrontation.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera profiles Akhlaghi as a 'tech-focused' minister and keeps the focus on his industrial and aerospace credentials. The Jerusalem Post leads with the fact that his predecessor was killed by Israel, highlights his UN sanctions record, and places the appointment alongside analyses of IRGC alertness, sanctions pressure and oil prices as drivers of possible escalation.
