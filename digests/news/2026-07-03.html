1. Mourners pay respects as Iran’s slain leader Ali Khamenei lies in state

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: Iran began a seven-day state funeral for Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, whose body lies in state in Tehran's Grand Mosalla as some 100 foreign delegations arrive. Khamenei was killed in February by US and Israeli strikes in the opening of a war now in its fourth month, and processions are planned across Tehran, Qom, Mashhad, and Iraqi cities.

Background: Khamenei led Iran for 36 years; his death during the US-Israel war leaves a leadership vacuum amid a tense ceasefire and ongoing regional confrontation.

Why it matters: The funeral is both a mass mourning event and a political stage that will shape Iran's succession, its posture toward Israel and the US, and regional stability.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames Khamenei neutrally as the 'slain leader' under a fragile ceasefire and the Guardian stresses the funeral's intended 'message of resistance,' while the Jerusalem Post foregrounds that he was 'killed by US and Israeli strikes' and amplifies opposition/diaspora calls to use the week for anti-regime protest.

2. Israel approves plan to establish 13 new settlements in occupied West Bank

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: Israel approved a plan to establish 13 new settlements in the occupied West Bank. Palestinian officials warned the move would further isolate East Jerusalem and undermine the territorial contiguity of any future Palestinian state.

Background: Settlement expansion in the occupied West Bank is considered illegal under international law and is a recurring flashpoint in the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

Why it matters: The expansion deepens the entrenchment of Israeli control and complicates prospects for a two-state solution, drawing likely international criticism.

Different views: Al Jazeera centers Palestinian warnings and the framing of occupation and isolation of East Jerusalem; Israeli coverage of settlement policy typically emphasizes security and sovereignty rationales rather than illegality.

3. Bomb blast at Damascus cafe kills nine, Syrian state media say

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: A bomb blast tore through a crowded cafe near the Palace of Justice in Damascus, killing nine people and wounding 22, according to Syrian state media. There was no immediate claim of responsibility.

Background: The attack comes as Syria's post-Assad transitional government works to consolidate control and form new institutions amid persistent instability.

Why it matters: The bombing signals the fragility of security under Syria's new leadership and the risk of renewed violence as the country attempts a political transition.
