1. Iraq celebrates National Sovereignty Day as US troops withdraw

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Iraq held 'National Sovereignty Day' celebrations marking the formal withdrawal of US and coalition troops, with military parades in Baghdad and Najaf. The withdrawal was completed under a deal agreed two years ago and represents the end of the anti-Islamic State coalition mission. Both US and UK forces have now departed Iraqi territory.

Background: US forces entered Iraq in 2003 and remained in various capacities through the ISIS campaign that peaked around 2014-2017. Pressure from Iran-aligned Iraqi political factions to expel foreign troops intensified after the 2020 US killing of Iranian General Qasem Soleimani on Iraqi soil.

Why it matters: The withdrawal marks a significant geopolitical shift in the region, reducing direct US military presence and raising questions about Iraq's ability to contain residual ISIS activity and manage its relationships with both the US and Iran.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames the withdrawal as a legitimate expression of Iraqi sovereignty and a milestone after decades of foreign military presence, foregrounding Iraqi government and public celebration. The BBC's coverage is more neutral, noting the withdrawal was 'a key demand of the Iraqi government' without editorialising on its merits. The Jerusalem Post's analysis is explicitly skeptical, arguing that Baghdad's framing as 'sovereignty' reveals ingratitude for the US role in defeating ISIS and maintaining order, and treats the withdrawal as a strategic loss for Washington rather than an Iraqi achievement.

2. Iran receives US feedback on seven-day trust-building plan, main issue is sequencing

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Iran has received US feedback — conveyed through Qatari mediators — on a proposed seven-day trust-building plan, with sequencing of steps being the main sticking point in ongoing nuclear negotiations. Separate talks in New York involved Qatari mediators, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, and a US delegation that included Jared Kushner. A parallel report suggests the two sides may return to confrontation after US midterm elections if a deal is not reached.

Background: The US and Iran have been engaged in indirect nuclear talks since the Trump administration re-imposed maximum pressure sanctions. Iran has simultaneously accelerated elements of its nuclear program, and Israel has been lobbying against any agreement it views as insufficiently restrictive.

Why it matters: The sequencing dispute — who takes the first step — is a classic deadlock in such negotiations and has derailed past nuclear deals. A failure to agree could push Iran closer to weapons-grade enrichment thresholds and trigger further military escalation involving Israel or the US.

Different views: Coverage of these negotiations is almost entirely from the Jerusalem Post, which foregrounds Israeli security concerns, Netanyahu's warnings to the UAE about Iranian nuclear construction, and the risk that a deal could constrain Israel's freedom of action. The Qatari PM's denial that Doha funded Hamas military operations and his accusation that Netanyahu is a 'war criminal' (article 44) — also covered only by the Jerusalem Post — adds a layer of regional diplomatic friction around Qatar's mediating role that other outlets do not address in this context.

3. Israel-bound passenger plane makes emergency landing in Saudi Arabia after reported fight between pilots

The Guardian · UK

What happened: A FlyDubai commercial flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv made an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia after an apparent physical altercation between the two pilots in the cockpit triggered distress signals, briefly raising fears of a hijacking. Israel scrambled fighter jets in response. An Israeli minister subsequently claimed a 'terrorist' had intended to crash the plane, though other accounts suggest the incident stemmed from a personal feud between the pilots.

Background: FlyDubai operates regular flights between Dubai and Tel Aviv under the Abraham Accords normalisation framework. Emergency diversions of Israel-bound flights carry heightened geopolitical sensitivity given the ongoing Gaza conflict and regional tensions.

Why it matters: The incident briefly threatened to escalate into a security crisis and illustrates the fragility of normalisation-era air links between Gulf states and Israel. The competing official explanations — terrorist act versus personal dispute — also point to politically motivated framing by Israeli officials.

Different views: The Guardian leads with the factual account of a pilot brawl and emergency landing, noting Israeli officials' fears of hijacking while also reporting that the incident appeared to stem from a feud between the pilots, implicitly treating the 'terrorist' claim with scepticism. The Jerusalem Post headline foregrounds Arab media's 'mixed' and 'sensational' reactions, framing the story partly through Israeli media's lens of potential terrorism. Al Jazeera separates the attack on the pilot from the emergency landing into two articles, keeping the framing more procedural and less security-inflected.
