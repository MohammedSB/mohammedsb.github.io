## Top Stories

### 1. [Houthis say they targeted Saudi capital with ballistic missiles](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cwly5d9v7r43o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Yemen's Houthis said they struck "sensitive" sites in Riyadh with ballistic missiles and drones, including an Aramco facility, hours after a large plume of black smoke was seen rising near Riyadh's airport and a reported strike on a fuel depot caused flight delays. Saudi authorities sent phone air-raid alerts to residents of the capital overnight — the first since the escalation with the Houthis began in July — said they intercepted a ballistic missile aimed at the city, and later issued an all-clear.

**Background:** Fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Iran-aligned Houthis escalated sharply from July, and the group had just accused Riyadh of backing "criminal attempts" in Sanaa and vowed retaliation. Saudi Arabia has signed a trilateral defence arrangement with Turkey and Pakistan, and both have now been asked to consider their obligations.

**Why it matters:** Strikes on the Saudi capital and on energy infrastructure move the Yemen war back into the Gulf's economic core, with oil already above $100 a barrel, and risk pulling treaty partners Pakistan and Turkey into a direct regional confrontation.

**Different views:** The BBC and Guardian accounts lead on the visible evidence — smoke over the airport, civilian phone alerts, flight delays — and present the Houthi claim alongside the Saudi interception statement; the Jerusalem Post pieces stress that Riyadh has not confirmed any target was hit, foreground the all-clear, and situate the attack within an Iranian buildup on Yemen's Red Sea coast. Al Jazeera's coverage centres a Saudi commentator promising a "very sizeable response" and the question of what Turkish and Pakistani backing would mean, rather than the damage itself.


### 2. [What are Iran’s latest conditions to end its war with the US?](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/20/what-are-irans-new-conditions-to-end-its-war-with-the-us?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Iran has conveyed conditions for re-entering talks and ending its war with the United States, relayed by Security Chief Mohsen Rezaei: a halt to fighting on all fronts, the unfreezing of Iranian funds, and an end to the US naval blockade. At the same time Iranian military officials claimed Washington is preparing to resume strikes and warned that any attack would trigger sustained retaliation against US bases and interests across the region.

**Background:** The conditions come after months of war and a severe economic crisis inside Iran, with regional escalation pushing oil past $100 per barrel. Thousands of IRGC and Basij volunteers marched through Tehran in a show of defiance the same week.

**Why it matters:** These are the clearest public terms Tehran has offered for de-escalation, and whether Washington treats them as an opening or a bluff will shape whether the Gulf war widens or winds down.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera frames the conditions as a substantive diplomatic move and links them to the economic pain of ordinary Iranians and rising oil prices; the Jerusalem Post reports the same terms more transactionally and pairs them with Iranian threats against US bases, exiled Crown Prince Reza Pahlavi's claims of accelerating defections, and regime-weakness framing that casts the offer as a product of internal erosion.


### 3. [Pact with Saudi Arabia threatens to draw Pakistan into Middle East conflict](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/19/pakistan-saudi-arabia-mecca-pact-joint-defence-agreement-houthis-yemen-conflict)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Riyadh's potential activation of its mutual defence pact with Pakistan, as fighting with the Houthis escalates, threatens to draw Islamabad directly into a Middle East conflict — a pact army chief Gen Asim Munir personally orchestrated and saw signed in Riyadh last September. Turkey's foreign minister separately said Ankara is ready to meet Saudi defence needs under the same framework, while Pakistan has said no military response from Islamabad has been discussed.

**Background:** The Saudi-Pakistan agreement, later widened into a trilateral arrangement including Turkey, was signed as a deterrence guarantee and had not previously been tested. Its first real test is arriving over Yemen rather than Iran.

**Why it matters:** If invoked, the pact would commit a nuclear-armed South Asian state and a NATO member to a Gulf conflict, turning a regional war into a much broader security entanglement.

**Different views:** The Guardian treats the pact primarily as a domestic and strategic problem for Pakistan, centring Munir and the costs to Islamabad; Al Jazeera frames Turkey's offer as a test of the trilateral arrangement and of Arab collective defence; the Jerusalem Post pairs Ankara's offer with Pakistan's denial that any military reaction is under discussion, emphasising the gap between the pact's promises and what partners will actually do.
