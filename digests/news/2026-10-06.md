## Top Stories

### 1. [Saudi-led coalition, Yemeni government forces launch massive three-pronged assault on Houthis](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-910640)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Yemeni government forces have launched a large three-pronged ground offensive against the Iran-backed Houthis, with air cover from the Saudi-led coalition. The coalition says about 100 fighter jets are hitting targets along the Red Sea coast and in Sanaa. Yemeni officials say they have retaken key areas around the Bab al-Mandab strait, but reports conflict on whether the port city of Mokha has fallen. The Houthis have hit back with strikes on Jazan and Najran airports in Saudi Arabia, wounding three people.

**Background:** The Houthis have held Sanaa and much of northern Yemen for years. Their September offensive and missile attacks on Saudi Arabia came as Riyadh was building a new defence pact with Pakistan and Turkey.

**Why it matters:** This is the biggest escalation in Yemen's war in years. It puts shipping through the Bab al-Mandab strait at risk and is the first real test of the Saudi-Pakistan-Turkey alliance. Iran has already warned that outside intervention would complicate the situation.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post treats the offensive as a regional realignment: Riyadh turning to Pakistan and Turkey after the US refused to step in, against a Houthi threat it calls existential. Its analysis also doubts that the divided government forces can hold together. The Guardian mostly reports coalition and Yemeni officials' claims of territory and targets taken. The BBC stresses that those claims conflict, for example over Mokha. Al Jazeera puts more weight on the Houthi strikes on Saudi airports, Pakistan's insistence that its role is only defensive, and hunger among Yemen's children.


### 2. [UK threatens to expel Israeli diplomats over planned East Jerusalem consulate closure](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/05/uk-threatens-to-expel-israeli-diplomats-after-closing-of-east-jerusalem-consulate)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Britain has threatened to expel one Israeli diplomat for every British diplomat forced out by Israel's decision to close the UK consulate in East Jerusalem later this week. That could mean up to 27 Israeli diplomats leaving the UK.

**Background:** Relations have been getting worse since the UK banned goods from Israeli settlements, which the UK considers illegal. The East Jerusalem consulate has long been Britain's main channel to the Palestinians.

**Why it matters:** Tit-for-tat expulsions on this scale would be a sharp break between Israel and one of its main Western partners. Closing the consulate would also weaken Britain's diplomatic presence with the Palestinians.


### 3. [Flydubai co-pilot originally planned attack for July, investigators believe](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/05/flydubai-co-pilot-originally-planned-attack-for-july-investigators-believe)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Investigators now believe the flydubai co-pilot who allegedly tried to take control of a Dubai–Tel Aviv flight on 30 September first planned the attack for July. He may have postponed it because a friend was on the crew. Passengers and crew stopped him. Reports say he planned to crash the plane into Tel Aviv airport or a building. Agencies in several countries are now looking into his background, including his years studying in Australia.

**Background:** The suspect, Omani national Hamam al-Hammami, studied engineering at RMIT in Melbourne from 2015 to 2017 but left without finishing. Flights between the Gulf and Israel have kept running despite the regional wars.

**Why it matters:** A hijacking by a serving pilot on an Israel-bound Gulf airline raises serious questions about how airlines vet flight crew, and about the security of normalised air links between the Gulf and Israel.

**Different views:** The BBC passes on reports describing him as a 'lone wolf' extremist. The Guardian focuses on the investigative detail: the timeline, the multinational inquiry and his time in Australia. The Jerusalem Post relies on an Israeli terrorism expert who links the plot to al-Qaeda and to inspiration from 9/11 and Bin Laden, framing it as a sign that jihadist networks in the Arabian Peninsula are still active. Al Jazeera has no coverage of the story in this set.
