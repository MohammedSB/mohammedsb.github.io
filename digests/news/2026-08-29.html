1. US clears Strait of Hormuz as 50,000 troops maintain blockade of Iran, CENTCOM chief says

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: CENTCOM's commander announced that the Strait of Hormuz has been cleared and that roughly 50,000 US troops are maintaining a naval blockade of Iran. He said nearly 1,500 vessels carrying about 750 million barrels of crude have transited the strait in recent months under US military protection. The announcement lands alongside reporting that Trump has walked away from the June memorandum of understanding that underpinned diplomacy over reopening the waterway (index 36).

Background: The US and Israel began large-scale strikes on Iran on 28 February 2026, and Iran's retaliation against Gulf targets turned the conflict regional, with the battle for Hormuz becoming its economic center of gravity. Roughly a fifth of the world's seaborne oil normally passes through the strait.

Why it matters: A US-policed strait plus a sustained blockade makes Washington the effective gatekeeper of Gulf energy flows, locking in a long-term military commitment and giving the US enormous leverage over both Iran and Gulf producers. Abandoning the MoU narrows the diplomatic off-ramp and raises the odds the confrontation becomes open-ended.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post frames the blockade and the collapse of the MoU approvingly — as pressure working and as escape from a deal 'too soft on Iran' — while Al Jazeera foregrounds the blockade's civilian cost, citing Iran's 35 percent collapse in trade, and the Guardian treats the whole campaign as Trump's war with global economic fallout.

2. Iran’s leaders acknowledge economic toll of war, pledge diplomacy, defence

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: Iran's leadership publicly acknowledged the economic damage of the six-month war, with the president saying sanctions and the US naval blockade have cut exports and imports by nearly 35 percent. Officials paired the admission with pledges to pursue diplomacy while continuing to rebuild defences.

Background: Iran has been under intensified US Treasury sanctions — including new measures targeting what Washington calls a 'critical node' of Iranian dollar access (index 34) — since strikes began in February 2026, and lost Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei on the war's first day.

Why it matters: An open admission of economic pain from a leadership that normally projects resilience is the clearest signal yet that Tehran may be positioning for negotiations, even as Washington hardens its terms. Whether that opening survives Trump's abandonment of the June MoU will shape the next phase of the war.

Different views: Al Jazeera presents the statements as evidence that sanctions and blockade are inflicting collective hardship on Iranians while Tehran still seeks a diplomatic path; Israeli coverage reads the same economic distress as validation of maximum pressure and an argument against easing terms; Western mainstream outlets focus on what it means for the odds of a deal rather than on who is to blame.

3. BBC tracks one week of Israeli settler violence in occupied West Bank

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: The BBC documented a single week of Israeli settler attacks on Palestinians in the occupied West Bank, reporting a sustained pattern rather than isolated incidents. The same period saw settlers surround a Palestinian home in Qusra with at least four people trapped inside while nearby land was set alight (index 0), and Israeli forces raid and order the closure of an UNRWA vocational centre (index 2).

Background: Settler violence in the West Bank has escalated sharply during and after the Gaza war, alongside Israeli measures against UNRWA, whose Qalandiya Training Centre in East Jerusalem is also under threat of closure (index 3).

Why it matters: West Bank violence and the dismantling of UNRWA's institutional presence are reshaping facts on the ground in ways that outlast any Gaza ceasefire, and they are a growing source of friction between Israel and European governments.

Different views: Al Jazeera covers the attacks as part of an organised campaign of dispossession and frames the UNRWA raids as an assault on Palestinian refugee rights; the BBC's approach is observational and evidentiary — tracking incidents over a week to establish a pattern without adopting either side's framing; the Jerusalem Post's Middle East coverage this cycle largely omits settler violence, focusing instead on Iran, Syria and Hezbollah.
