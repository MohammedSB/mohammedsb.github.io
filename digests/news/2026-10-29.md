### 1. [Coalition talks loom in Israel as Netanyahu and Eisenkot blocs set to fall short of majority](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/28/israel-election-exit-polls-suggest-netanyahu-eisenkot-blocs-tied-ntwnfb)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** With nearly all ballots counted, Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud-led bloc has lost its governing majority, projected at around 53 of 120 Knesset seats, while rival Gadi Eisenkot's bloc also falls short of 61. Turnout was the highest in nearly three decades, Palestinian-citizen parties made marked gains, and far-right Itamar Ben-Gvir underperformed. Netanyahu has alleged foreign interference in the vote, which President Isaac Herzog rebuked as tarnishing an entire community.

**Background:** This is the first national election since the Gaza war and the US-Israel confrontation with Iran, held with Netanyahu's coalition weakened by internal splits and public anger. Neither bloc can govern without either a unity arrangement or reliance on Arab parties, which Eisenkot has so far treated as a barrier.

**Why it matters:** The result decides whether Netanyahu survives, whether Israel is governed with Palestinian-citizen parties for the first time in a decisive role, or whether the country heads to yet another election, with direct consequences for Gaza, the West Bank and the Iran file.

**Different views:** The BBC frames the outcome as deadlock and deep division, laying out neutral scenarios including a repeat election; the Guardian treats the result as Netanyahu having lost his mandate and editorialises that Eisenkot should work with Palestinian parties, foregrounding their surge and Netanyahu's interference claims; Al Jazeera foregrounds the campaign's attacks on Arab parties and reads the deadlock as those attacks failing; the Jerusalem Post has no election analysis in this set and leads instead with the Iran track.


### 2. [Gulf states working on multipart peace deal to avert renewal of US-Iran war](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/28/gulf-states-multipart-peace-deal-avert-renewal-us-iran-war)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Gulf states, with Qatar as lead mediator, have presented Iran a multipart proposal to avert a renewed US-Iran war: reopening the Strait of Hormuz within days of sanctions relief, the return of IAEA inspectors, and a substantial Houthi withdrawal inside Yemen. In parallel, Vice President JD Vance sent a letter to Iran's security chief Rezaei pressing for a swift return to nuclear talks, while Trump warned Gulf states to 'prepare' and the White House said Iran is in 'very bad condition' under a continuing US blockade.

**Background:** The US-Iran war, which the Pentagon says has cost $47bn, is in a fragile pause with a US naval blockade in place and Trump reportedly weighing renewed strikes after the 5 November midterms. Iran's foreign minister says Tehran is in close contact with mediators on a seven-day plan.

**Why it matters:** Whether Tehran accepts the package in the coming days likely determines if strikes resume, with Hormuz shipping, oil prices, Yemen's war and the Gulf states' own security all riding on the outcome.

**Different views:** The Guardian centres the Gulf states as the authors of a peace package and frames the goal as averting a 'disastrous' war; the Jerusalem Post runs the story through Washington's lens, citing Axios and the White House on Iran's weakness and the threat of resumed strikes, and notes concern that Netanyahu could attack Iran this week; Al Jazeera pairs the Vance letter with Trump's warning to the Gulf and the war's domestic cost to US taxpayers, implying pressure on Washington as much as Tehran.


### 3. [Fighting rages near Yemen’s Taiz as UN urges humanitarian access](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/29/fighting-rages-near-yemens-taiz-as-un-urges-humanitarian-access?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Fighting has intensified near Taiz in Yemen, with the UN saying more than 281,000 people have been displaced since early August and urging humanitarian access. The Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile fired at the kingdom, with civil defence alerts issued in Najran and the Eastern Province.

**Background:** Yemen's war has re-escalated alongside the US-Iran conflict, and a Houthi pullback is now a component of the Gulf-brokered deal being offered to Tehran. Turkey's Erdogan also told Trump Ankara is working diplomatically on both Hormuz and Yemen.

**Why it matters:** Yemen has become a second front tied to the Iran negotiations, so a breakdown there could sink the wider deal, while renewed Houthi fire at Saudi territory threatens to pull Riyadh deeper into direct confrontation.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera leads with the UN displacement figures and the humanitarian toll on civilians; the Jerusalem Post leads with the Saudi military interception and the Houthi threat to the kingdom, with no mention of displacement; the Guardian treats Yemen only as a bargaining chip inside the Gulf states' Iran proposal.
