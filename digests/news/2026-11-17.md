## Top Stories

### 1. [Third US aircraft carrier brings thousands more troops to Middle East](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/16/aircraft-middle-east-trump-military)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** A third US aircraft carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, has arrived in the Middle East, US Central Command said on Monday. The Jerusalem Post puts the total at about 53,000 US troops in the region. Trump keeps changing his line: he has threatened new strikes on Iran, said the war is 'militarily effectively over', and said Washington doesn't need a deal with Tehran. Iran has rejected a state of 'no war, no peace'.

**Background:** The US-Israel war on Iran has been running since at least March, when an Iranian drone strike in Kuwait killed six US soldiers. According to the Jerusalem Post, three carriers were last in the region at once in April, and before that in 2003. The buildup comes as Trump heads to talks with Xi and Putin where Iran is expected to come up.

**Why it matters:** The war could either restart in full or settle into a long, unresolved standoff. The added firepower raises the risk of new strikes on Iranian infrastructure at the same moment that mine strikes in the Strait of Hormuz and Iranian attacks on Bahrain are spreading the conflict around the Gulf.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post treats the buildup as US-Israeli preparation for a possible war and surrounds it with Iranian threats: a CNN-sourced report on 'dirty bomb' planning, questions about missile bases near dams, and gas shortages it says could fuel unrest. Al Jazeera focuses on the impasse and on Iranians' fear of more strikes on energy sites, bridges, schools and hospitals, and quotes Iran's rejection of 'no war, no peace'. The Guardian's angle is Trump's contradictory statements about whether the war is over.


### 2. [Two oil tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz, IRGC says](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-915014)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Iran's Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) say six tankers caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday and Monday, including two oil tankers on Monday. Iran's regular navy separately said a fourth tanker had been hit by a mine east of the strait. Al Jazeera, also citing Iran, reported two vessels on fire after a mine hit.

**Background:** Roughly a fifth of the world's seaborne oil normally passes through the Strait of Hormuz. The strikes come during the US-Iran war, while Iran also says it has shot down a US MQ-1 drone near the strait.

**Why it matters:** Repeated mine strikes threaten one of the world's most important energy routes and could push up oil prices and shipping insurance costs. At the same time, Houthi advances in Yemen are putting pressure on the Bab al-Mandab, the other key choke point in the region. Almost all reporting so far comes from Iranian military statements, and none of the coverage here says who laid the mines.


### 3. [Yemeni forces retreat to defend port city from Houthis](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqvg2xgyvnjpo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces are pulling troops from the Bab al-Mandab Strait and parts of Marib to defend the interim capital, Aden, as the Houthis advance. The Houthis are besieging Taiz and have strengthened their hold on the highlands above Aden. The Jerusalem Post reports that Saudi Arabia has reshuffled its military command in response.

**Background:** Yemen has been split for years between the Iran-backed Houthis, who hold Sanaa and the north, and a Saudi-backed government based in Aden. The Houthis have previously attacked Red Sea shipping.

**Why it matters:** If the Houthis gain ground near the Bab al-Mandab, they could threaten Red Sea shipping just as Hormuz is disrupted. A threat to Aden would also badly damage Saudi Arabia's position in Yemen.

**Different views:** The BBC presents the advance mainly as a risk to the global economy and shipping. The Guardian focuses on anti-Houthi Yemeni factions accusing Riyadh of incompetence, treating Saudi strategy as the problem. The Jerusalem Post stresses that the Houthis are backed by Iran and asks whether Aden's defences will hold. None of the Al Jazeera articles in this set cover the Yemen advance.
