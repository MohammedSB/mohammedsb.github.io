### 1. [US and Iran both claim control of Strait of Hormuz amid attacks on vessels](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/17/us-and-iran-both-claim-control-of-strait-of-hormuz-amid-attacks-on-vessels?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** The United States and Iran are each claiming control of the Strait of Hormuz after two vessels were struck by naval mines this week, with a UK maritime agency's data showing a record 12 ships hit in seven days and oil exports slowing. Trump separately threatened strikes on Iran's Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site, while shipping data shows Iran still moving some LPG and jet fuel cargoes despite the US blockade.

**Background:** The strait carries roughly a fifth of global oil trade and has become the main pressure point of the US-Iran war, with a US naval blockade and sanctions set against Iranian mining and attacks on shipping.

**Why it matters:** A contested chokepoint with rising vessel strikes raises the risk of direct US-Iran naval escalation and keeps global energy prices elevated, with Trump's nuclear-site threat signalling the war could widen beyond the maritime front.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera frames the strait as genuinely contested, giving equal weight to the US and Iranian claims of control; BBC Verify sidesteps the rival claims and anchors its account in UK maritime agency incident data and export figures. The Jerusalem Post foregrounds US leverage, leading on Trump's strike threats, CENTCOM's claims that Iranian proxies are starved of cash, and a US advocacy group's analysis of Iranian sanctions evasion, treating US and pro-sanctions sources as authoritative.


### 2. [Palestinians and US journalist injured by Israeli settlers in West Bank harvest attack](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c65ynk7212vqo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Masked Israeli settlers attacked Palestinians harvesting olives across the occupied West Bank, injuring at least six people in Abu Falah including a child and four women, and beating Washington Post photographer Heidi Levine. The BBC witnessed one attack directly, and the violence followed a ruling by a prominent far-right rabbi permitting the seizure of Palestinian olives.

**Background:** Settler attacks on the autumn olive harvest recur every year, but rights groups report a sharp surge in violence since the Gaza war began and settlements have expanded under the current Israeli government.

**Why it matters:** The injury of an American journalist and a religious edict effectively sanctioning theft push the issue onto the diplomatic agenda, with China's foreign minister publicly telling Israel's president to halt 'forced' settlement building the same week.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera calls the attacks 'pogroms' and leads with the rabbi's edict as the trigger, foregrounding Palestinian victims and condemnation; the BBC reports as an eyewitness, describing 'masked settlers' and placing the incident in a 'surge' of similar attacks without attributing a cause. The Guardian describes a 'coordinated movement' to disrupt the harvest and highlights the injured US journalist. The Jerusalem Post carries no coverage of the attacks in this sample, so the Israeli government's or settlers' account is absent from all four outlets.


### 3. [UAE agrees with Saudi Arabia to re-enter Yemen conflict amid Houthi gains](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/17/uae-yemen-conflict-agreement-saudi-arabia-houthi-gains)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The UAE has agreed with Saudi Arabia to re-enter the Yemen conflict by resuming payments to government forces fighting the Houthis, as Houthi missiles and drones struck Aden's presidential palace and airport and the Saudi-led coalition intercepted a missile headed for Yanbu. A Wall Street Journal report says Iran is supplying the Houthis with targeting intelligence, and Trump said Saudi Arabia may accept Israeli help against the Houthis.

**Background:** Houthi forces have made rapid gains in recent weeks, threatening Taiz and Red Sea supply routes, in what analysts tie to the wider US-Iran war and Iranian support for its allies.

**Why it matters:** Gulf re-engagement, Israeli involvement, and Iranian targeting support would turn Yemen from a stalled civil war into a direct theatre of the regional confrontation, with Saudi oil infrastructure now within reach of Houthi strikes.

**Different views:** The Guardian foregrounds Gulf rulers' alarm and gives a former Yemeni prime minister space to cast the war as a test of 'western security'; Al Jazeera asks whether government forces can sustain their offensive at all and stresses Houthi pressure on supply routes. The Jerusalem Post foregrounds Iran's hand, leading with the WSJ intelligence report and Trump's suggestion of Saudi-Israeli cooperation and a revived Abraham Accords, and republishes Arab commentary likening the fight for Sanaa to the fall of Assad in Damascus.
