## Top Stories

### 1. [Turnout so far highest in decades as Israelis vote in pivotal election](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3j47l4kdez4o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Israelis voted Tuesday in the first general election since the 7 October 2023 attacks, with turnout reported at its highest in decades. Benjamin Netanyahu is fighting for political survival against former chief of staff Gadi Eisenkot, and most polls show his coalition losing its majority, though exit polls and coalition arithmetic may take days to resolve. A suspected stabbing attempt against Democrats leader Yair Golan was foiled during the day.

**Background:** The vote follows two years of war in Gaza, the February US-Israeli strikes on Iran and the ongoing confrontation with Tehran, plus Netanyahu's corruption trial and deepening international isolation.

**Why it matters:** The result will decide who governs Israel through the next phase of the Iran standoff, the Gaza plan and West Bank policy, and it lands days before US midterms that will shape Washington's posture toward both.

**Different views:** BBC and Guardian frame the vote as a referendum on Netanyahu and a hinge for Gaza and global geopolitics, with the Guardian also reporting he pressured the military over Iran and displaced Hebron families to burnish security credentials. Al Jazeera foregrounds divisions over the wars and isolation and quotes a former PLO official on a possible Netanyahu-Eisenkot unity deal, while the Jerusalem Post surveys how Arab and Iranian media are reading the election and carries Iran's line that the outcome 'doesn't matter'.


### 2. [Jordan carries out deadly strike in Syria in retaliation for church attack](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cq8ezg6dy6xlo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Jordan's air force struck what it called terrorist hideouts in rural Deraa and Suwayda in southern Syria, saying the targets were tied to Friday's gun attack on a Greek Orthodox church funeral in Karak that killed nine Christians. Syrian health officials said five people including women and children died, and Damascus said the strikes were not coordinated and opened an investigation. Jordan separately arrested three suspects after a shootout.

**Background:** The Karak church shooting was the deadliest attack on Jordan's Christian community in years and remains unclaimed. Southern Syria is weakly controlled by Damascus, with ISIS cells, Druze tensions and Israeli border raids complicating the picture.

**Why it matters:** A unilateral Jordanian strike inside Syria strains ties with the new government in Damascus and signals that neighbours will act on their own when they judge Syria unable to police its south.

**Different views:** BBC and the Guardian lead with Jordan's own statement about retaliation against 'terrorist groups' while noting Syrian casualty figures. Al Jazeera foregrounds the victims' families denying any link to the church attack and Damascus's claim of no coordination. The Jerusalem Post runs both the Jordanian justification and Syria's civilian-deaths account as separate stories and situates the strike within Damascus's broader struggle to control the south.


### 3. [Iran says US ‘not serious’ about talks amid Tehran meeting](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/10/27/iran-says-us-not-serious-about-talks-amid-tehran-meeting?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Officials from Iran, Pakistan, Turkey and Saudi Arabia met in Tehran under the Mecca defence pact framework to try to halt escalation, with Saudi defence minister Khalid bin Salman and Pakistan's army chief in attendance. Iran's security chief then briefed Khamenei, who stressed holding to Iran's conditions for ending the war, and Tehran said Washington is 'not serious' about talks. Separately, US officials were reported to be ready to strike Iran within 96 hours of a Trump order, with Israel pushing for a joint attack after the US midterms.

**Background:** The region has been in open confrontation since the 28 February US-Israeli strikes on Iran, with Iran squeezing shipping in Hormuz and Iran-backed Houthi attacks grounding most flights from Riyadh.

**Why it matters:** Gulf states are now talking directly to Tehran at the same moment Washington is readying renewed strikes, so the next weeks could bring either a regional security arrangement that sidelines the US or a second round of war.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera presents the Tehran meeting as a de-escalation effort and foregrounds Iran's complaint about US seriousness, and the Guardian amplifies Oman's call for a Gulf-Iraq-Iran security pact without US primacy. The Jerusalem Post frames the same talks against continuing Houthi attacks, Hezbollah rearmament and Khamenei's conditions, and reports the US 96-hour strike readiness and Israeli pressure for a joint attack as the main dynamic.
