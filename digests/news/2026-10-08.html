1. Houthis ramp up missile attacks on Riyadh as Saudi-backed war in Yemen widens

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis fired new missiles at Riyadh and Khamis Mushait in Saudi Arabia and at Aden's international airport, the main hub for the Saudi-backed government. Officials say Houthi attacks on Saudi airports killed three people. Houthi fighters also appear to be advancing slowly in south-west Yemen towards Taiz.

Background: Yemen's civil war between the Houthis and the UN-recognised government has flared again, and it is now tied to the wider US-Iran war and the disruption in the Strait of Hormuz. Turkey, under the new 'Mecca' defence pact, and possibly Syria are weighing military support for Saudi Arabia.

Why it matters: The war has moved from Yemen's interior to direct attacks on Saudi cities and regional infrastructure. It risks drawing Turkey, Syria and others into the fighting, and the UN migration agency says more than 200,000 people have been newly displaced.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post presents the fighting as 'an overspill of the US-Iran war', stresses the Houthis' Iranian backing, and follows the outside actors (Turkey's Mecca pact, Syria's possible aid). The Guardian and BBC focus on events on the ground in Yemen, such as front-line advances, the Aden airport strike and officially reported casualties, and the Guardian adds an explainer on the civil war's domestic roots. None of these items quotes the Houthis directly beyond their claims of responsibility.

2. ‘Palestinians will bear the cost’: UK consulate in East Jerusalem prepares to shut its doors

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Britain's consulate general in East Jerusalem is being forced to close after Israel acted against UK sanctions on West Bank settlements. Signs were taken down overnight. Ed Miliband said the building will be rebranded as a 'UK mission' that keeps vital services, but most UK diplomats will leave.

Background: In September Israel said it would respond to Britain's sanctions on illegal settlements. The consulate has long served as Britain's de facto mission to the Palestinians.

Why it matters: This is a low point in UK-Israel relations and sharply reduces Britain's diplomatic presence for Palestinians. There are reports that the UK is considering expelling Israeli diplomats in response.

Different views: The BBC's headline leads with Israel's account ('most UK diplomats to leave, Israel says') and sets it against Miliband's reassurance. The Guardian first describes both sides stepping back from a full confrontation, then foregrounds the cost to Palestinians and the possible expulsion of Israeli diplomats. Al Jazeera's video calls the area 'occupied East Jerusalem' and focuses on the physical closure. The Jerusalem Post items supplied here don't cover the story.

3. Trump asks Pentagon for Iran strike options before midterms - report

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: The White House has reportedly asked the Pentagon for options to strike Iran before the 3 November US midterms, though no decision has been made. At the same time, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said the Strait of Hormuz could reopen within a week and that 'the diplomatic process is still continuing'. Brent crude rose 5% to about $105 a barrel.

Background: The US and Iran are at war. Iran's blockade of Hormuz has disrupted global energy supplies, and Iranian attacks have driven US troops out of a base in Kuwait. A report also says Iran is targeting US bases in Germany.

Why it matters: New US strikes could reverse the tentative diplomatic opening on Hormuz. That would further stress energy markets: the World Bank forecasts Gulf economies will shrink 4.3% in 2026.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post covers the story mainly through military threats, including the strike options, Iranian targeting of US bases and Israel's earlier warnings to partners, while also carrying Araghchi's diplomatic message. The Guardian treats the strike report mostly as a driver of markets, and Al Jazeera looks at the war's human and economic costs through tanker crews' Hormuz bonuses and the diesel shortage.
