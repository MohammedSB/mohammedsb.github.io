## Top Stories

### 1. [First US military attacks on Iran in a month prompt retaliation](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/30/us-strikes-iranian-launchers-on-larak-island-amid-strait-of-hormuz-tensions)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US resumed direct military action against Iran for the first time in roughly a month, striking two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz, which US Central Command said were preparing to lay sea mines; two people were killed and two injured. Iran retaliated by firing missiles and drones at US bases in Jordan and the United Arab Emirates, with most interceptions reported successful and the UAE saying it downed a drone while denying its Al Menhad airbase was hit. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said Tehran still wants a negotiated end to the conflict, accusing Washington of not fulfilling its commitments.

**Background:** The exchange breaks a lull of more than a month in the US-Israel war on Iran, and centres on the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint for roughly a fifth of global oil traffic. It coincides with a G20 finance meeting where the EU pledged to keep up economic pressure on Tehran.

**Why it matters:** Strikes on Hormuz targets plus Iranian fire onto Gulf and Jordanian soil widen the war beyond Iran-US bilateral exchanges and put Arab states hosting US forces directly in the line of fire. It also tests whether Pezeshkian's diplomatic track survives renewed escalation.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post foregrounds the US justification — launchers 'preparing to fire sea mines' — and argues Iran deliberately picked Jordan and the UAE because they are unlikely to hit back, while Al Jazeera runs an analysis casting the sea-mine claim as implausible and frames events as a 'US-Israel war on Iran' with regional economic fallout. BBC and the Guardian take a more procedural line, leading on casualty figures, the month-long gap in strikes, and Pezeshkian's offer of diplomacy.


### 2. [Turkiye, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan hold first Mecca defence pact meeting](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/8/31/turkiye-saudi-arabia-pakistan-hold-first-mecca-defence-pact-meeting?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian*

**What happened:** Senior officials from Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan held the first meeting under the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement, discussing joint military cooperation, defence production and coordination mechanisms. Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said the pact is not aimed at any specific country and that members are weighing a mechanism to admit new states, while separately calling Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu an 'enemy of humanity'.

**Background:** The Mecca Agreement was signed as US-Iran hostilities intensified, binding a NATO member, the Gulf's dominant power and a nuclear-armed South Asian state into a mutual defence framework. Its first working session lands as Gulf states absorb Iranian strikes on their territory.

**Why it matters:** If it acquires real command structures and new members, the pact would be the most consequential new security bloc in the region in decades, hedging against both Iran and reliance on US protection. It arrives alongside deepening Israel-Greece defence ties, pointing to hardening rival alignments.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera treats the meeting as a test of whether the pact has substance and asks who joins next, presenting it as legitimate regional self-organisation; the Jerusalem Post reads it primarily through an Israeli security lens as part of a geopolitical reshuffle to be watched warily, pairing it with Israel-Greece agreements and amplifying Fidan's attack on Netanyahu as evidence of hostile intent.


### 3. [Iran’s economy in crisis as US launches new sanctions campaign](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/31/iran-economy-crisis-usa-launches-sanctions-inflation-trump)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US launched a new sanctions campaign against Iran branded 'Operation Economic Outcast', with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent saying new secondary sanctions will be rolled out weekly, starting with banks. Iranians already face runaway food inflation, closed petrol stations and a collapsing currency, and the campaign is expected to deepen poverty. Enforcement is reaching third countries: Washington threatened to revoke dollar access for Banque Misr's UAE arm, alleged to be a 'critical node' in Tehran's dollar access.

**Background:** The campaign layers onto years of existing sanctions and a wartime economy already in crisis. The EU used this week's G20 meeting to say it will work with Washington to sustain economic pressure.

**Why it matters:** Secondary sanctions on Gulf and Egyptian banks extend the economic war to Iran's neighbours and trading partners, raising the cost of neutrality across the region. Whether financial strangulation changes Tehran's behaviour — or only its population's living standards — is now the central bet of US strategy.

**Different views:** The Guardian centres the humanitarian toll on ordinary Iranians, and Al Jazeera argues outright that the package will fail to achieve Washington's objectives; the Jerusalem Post presents the pressure approvingly as escalating leverage, though it also quotes an Israeli scholar cautioning that economic collapse alone is unlikely to topple the regime.
