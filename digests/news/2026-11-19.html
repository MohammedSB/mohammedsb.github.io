1. Kuwait says it repelled first Iranian attack in two months after US strikes island

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: Kuwait said it repelled its first Iranian attack in two months, and Bahrain also reported being attacked. Both came after a US air strike on Iran's Qeshm Island in the Gulf on Tuesday. Qatar and Oman are reported to be pursuing mediation talks with Tehran.

Background: The US and Iran are at war, and the US is blockading Iranian ports. The two-month lull in Iranian strikes on Gulf states followed earlier rounds of fighting, including the June 2025 strikes on Iran's nuclear sites.

Why it matters: Renewed Iranian strikes on Gulf states that host US forces could widen the war across the region. They also put pressure on the Gulf-led mediation and raise the risk of escalation in the Strait of Hormuz.

Different views: The BBC headline leads with the sequence of events, presenting Kuwait's account of the attack as coming 'after US strikes island.' The Jerusalem Post headline reads 'Iran attacks US bases in Gulf countries despite diplomatic efforts,' casting Tehran as the side undermining diplomacy and putting the Qeshm strike in the subheading, and its analysis frames the attacks as Iran 'testing US resolve.' Al Jazeera focuses instead on the US side's claims, asking whether there is any evidence for Trump's allegation that Iran is developing bioweapons.

2. Israeli tanks cross ‘yellow line’ boundary into Hamas-controlled central Gaza

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Israeli tanks crossed the 'yellow line' boundary into Hamas-controlled central Gaza on Wednesday. It was the first ground operation across that line since the ceasefire, and it forced dozens of displaced Palestinians to flee their tents. The Israeli military says it has finished the operation, which it said was to destroy a 'terror tunnel.' Hamas called it a 'flagrant' violation of the ceasefire.

Background: The yellow line has divided Israeli-held and Hamas-held parts of Gaza since a fragile ceasefire was agreed in October 2025 under a US-led, UN-backed peace plan. In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot is trying to form a government without Netanyahu, who now leads a caretaker government.

Why it matters: This is the most serious test of the ceasefire's boundary so far, and it comes during political uncertainty in Israel. Aid groups already say the wider peace plan is failing.

Different views: The BBC sets the Israeli military's stated reason (a 'terror tunnel') against Hamas's condemnation and gives the two roughly equal weight. The Guardian leads with the human impact on displaced Palestinians and ties the incursion to Israeli coalition politics. Al Jazeera's related coverage relies on aid groups, who describe the peace plan as 'displacement by design' that has collapsed. Among the articles here, the Jerusalem Post has no coverage of the incursion.

3. Saudi-led coalition intercepts Houthi missiles targeting Riyadh and south

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted Houthi missiles fired at Riyadh and southern Saudi Arabia. Falling debris caused material damage, and the Jerusalem Post reports that the PIF Tower in Riyadh's financial district was hit. The strike came shortly after Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman met US Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper.

Background: Fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis has restarted. It has killed or wounded thousands and displaced hundreds of thousands in Yemen, and Riyadh is reportedly preparing for a campaign lasting months.

Why it matters: Hitting Riyadh's financial centre shows that the Yemen war has spread into Saudi Arabia itself. It is becoming a major front alongside the US-Iran war, with consequences for shipping, oil prices and Saudi international events.

Different views: Al Jazeera and one Jerusalem Post report both pass on the coalition's statement with little added framing. Other Jerusalem Post pieces link the strike to the crown prince's meeting with the CENTCOM chief, call the Houthi front 'the most active front in the wider Iran war,' and treat the Houthis mainly as an Iranian proxy. Al Jazeera's report here does not mention Iran.
