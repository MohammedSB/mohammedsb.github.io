1. US military says it struck three Iranian tankers after American warships came under missile attack

The Guardian · UK

What happened: The US military said it struck three Iranian oil tankers after US Navy warships — an aircraft carrier and a destroyer — came under ballistic missile attack in the Gulf region, with CENTCOM warning it would destroy Iran's 'limited and exposed' oil fleet if necessary. Iran's IRGC in turn claimed it targeted three US-affiliated ships and three oil tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Parliament speaker Ghalibaf vowed any further attack on Iranian interests would meet a 'faster, heavier, more painful' response.

Background: The exchanges are the latest round in a months-long US–Iran conflict that has moved into the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint for roughly a fifth of global seaborne oil, with both sides using shipping as leverage over the waterway.

Why it matters: Direct strikes on naval vessels and oil tankers put the world's most important energy chokepoint at risk and create escalation ladders that could pull in Gulf states, Israel and Iran-aligned groups across the region.

Different views: One account leads with the US Navy ships being fired on first and quotes CENTCOM's commander framing the tanker strikes as proportionate economic punishment, while another presents the tit-for-tat as a symmetrical 'tanker war' between two powers contesting the strait and centres the risk to the waterway itself; a fourth strand foregrounds a former US defense secretary's warning that this is a drifting 'forever war' with a Pentagon in turmoil, a criticism the strike-focused accounts do not carry.

2. ‘Guilt by association’: why seven international aid workers were killed in Israeli airstrike

The Guardian · UK

What happened: A Guardian investigation reported that the seven World Central Kitchen aid workers killed in an Israeli airstrike were marked as targets under a military practice of assigning 'guilt by association', citing a senior Israeli military prosecutor among its sources. The reporting presents the convoy strike not as an isolated error but as the product of a widely used targeting method.

Background: The WCK convoy strike on the road to Rafah drew international condemnation and was officially attributed by Israel to misidentification and procedural failures, with a small number of officers disciplined.

Why it matters: If association-based targeting is systemic rather than exceptional, it reframes aid-worker and civilian deaths in Gaza as policy-driven, with direct implications for war crimes investigations and for states supplying arms.

Different views: The investigative account treats internal Israeli military sources as authoritative to argue the killings followed doctrine rather than error; coverage from other outlets in this cycle does not engage the targeting-policy claim at all, instead covering Gaza through mass funerals for bodies recovered from rubble or, from an Israeli vantage point, through Gazan voices who blame Hamas for the war.

3. Civilians among at least 60 killed in Yemen as ground fighting escalates

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Fighting between Yemen's Saudi-backed government forces and the Iran-aligned Houthis killed more than 60 people, including civilians, as the Houthis pressed an offensive on the western coast — 26 government troops and 31 Houthi fighters among the dead. Government forces claimed capture of a strategic district amid intensified clashes around Taiz, while the Houthis pushed toward the port of Mocha, under 50 miles from the Bab al-Mandab strait.

Background: Yemen's front lines had been largely frozen for years under a fragile de facto truce; this is among the deadliest ground fighting since it took hold.

Why it matters: A Houthi advance on Mocha would extend their control over Bab al-Mandab, a second global shipping chokepoint, at the same moment the Strait of Hormuz is under attack — compounding pressure on Red Sea trade and drawing Saudi Arabia further in.

Different views: One account frames the fighting primarily as a humanitarian toll with civilians among the dead and government forces gaining ground, while another foregrounds the Houthis' strategic objective of the Bab al-Mandab chokepoint and their claimed strikes on Saudi forces using domestically made missiles — emphasising capability and Iranian alignment over casualties.
