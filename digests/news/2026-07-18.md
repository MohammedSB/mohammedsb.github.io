## Top Stories

### 1. [US strikes hit Iran for seventh consecutive night](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cy748n8zx8ro?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** US forces struck Iran for a seventh consecutive night, hitting bridges, energy facilities, ports and military sites, while Iran retaliated against US allies in the Gulf — with Kuwait reporting a power and water plant hit and sirens sounding in Bahrain. The fighting is escalating around the Strait of Hormuz, weeks after a short-lived ceasefire collapsed.

**Background:** Trump declared the Iran peace deal over roughly 10 days ago, resuming a bombing campaign that follows an earlier 38-day spring air war against Iran.

**Why it matters:** A widening US-Iran war threatens regional energy shipping through Hormuz, drags in Gulf states hosting US bases, and carries major economic and political stakes ahead of US midterms.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and the Guardian foreground US strikes on civilian infrastructure (water, bridges) and frame the war as an unlawful Trump-driven fiasco; the Jerusalem Post emphasizes CENTCOM 'holding Iran accountable' and degrading military capabilities, while presenting Iran's retaliation claims skeptically; BBC covers both the strikes and Iranian counterstrikes more neutrally.


### 2. [Israeli ministers announce plans for new illegal settlements in Gaza and West Bank](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/18/israel-plan-new-illegal-settlements-gaza-west-bank-occupied-palestine)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Israel's defence and finance ministers announced plans for three 'Nahal' settlement outposts in Gaza plus over $400m to expand West Bank construction, as Israel's regional commander said the military now controls 65% of the Gaza Strip. Critics say the moves violate the Trump-brokered ceasefire.

**Background:** The announcement extends Israel's territorial hold in Gaza and settlement expansion in the occupied West Bank amid an already fragile truce.

**Why it matters:** New settlements in Gaza would mark a major shift toward permanent Israeli presence, undercutting ceasefire terms and any two-state framework.

**Different views:** The Guardian frames the outposts as illegal settlements and ceasefire violations; Israeli outlets tend to present such territorial control as security necessity, and pro-Palestinian coverage (e.g. Al Jazeera's West Bank barrier reporting) casts it as strangling Palestinian communities.


### 3. [Will Mahmoud Abbas actually let Palestinians vote? Critics say he's testing the waters first](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-902794)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Palestinian Authority President Mahmoud Abbas announced plans for Palestinian elections, but analysts question whether he will actually allow a vote or is merely 'testing the waters.' One Palestinian analyst framed it as an effort to remove Israel's claim that Palestinians are 'not a partner for peace.'

**Background:** Palestinians have not held general elections since 2006; Abbas has repeatedly postponed votes amid the Fatah-Hamas split and questions over his legitimacy.

**Why it matters:** A genuine Palestinian election would reshape PA legitimacy and diplomacy, but skepticism about Abbas's intentions signals continued internal political paralysis.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post frames the announcement skeptically as a maneuver by Abbas rather than a genuine democratic opening.
