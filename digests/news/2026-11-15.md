## Top Stories

### 1. [Iran's IRGC launches drone attack on US base in Bahrain, triggering hostile escalation](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-914895)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Iran's IRGC launched drones at a US radar installation at an air base in Bahrain, home to the US Navy's Fifth Fleet; the IRGC said six drones were used and called the strike retaliatory. Bahrain's Defence Force said its air defences thwarted several Iranian aerial attacks, and its interior ministry told residents to go to the nearest safe location. Bahrain accused Iran of attacking its territory to settle a score with the US, while Jordan, Egypt, Saudi Arabia and Qatar condemned the attack.

**Background:** The strike comes amid an ongoing US-Israel war on Iran, with a US blockade and repeated rounds of strikes. Tehran says attacks on countries cooperating with the US are justified under international law.

**Why it matters:** It marks a resumption of strikes in the Gulf and directly pulls a Gulf Arab state hosting US forces into the confrontation. That raises the risk of a wider regional war and further disruption to energy and shipping.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera's headlines carry Iran's own terms — 'retaliatory' strikes on a 'US military radar' — and give Tehran's legal justification alongside Bahrain's accusation, while its explainer leads with the Bahraini military's interception claim. The Jerusalem Post frames it as an IRGC attack 'triggering hostile escalation' and foregrounds the civilian shelter warning; the Guardian's live blog foregrounds the regional condemnations relayed by Bahrain's state news agency and Bahrain's role as Fifth Fleet host.


### 2. [Trump tells advisers he may hold off on Iran attacks barring new provocations](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-914862)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Trump has reportedly told advisers he may hold off on new attacks on Iran unless there are fresh provocations. Current and former officials say he has alternated between threatening escalation and winding down hostilities and has not made up his mind. Meanwhile Iran's foreign minister Araghchi is due in Oman for talks, and Israel's military is raising its alert level for fear of a surprise US strike.

**Background:** The US and Israel have carried out rounds of strikes on Iran, and nuclear talks are running in parallel, with Oman a traditional intermediary. A separate report says Iran still has up to 2,500 missiles despite those strikes.

**Why it matters:** Trump's decision determines whether the war escalates or moves toward a negotiated wind-down. The Bahrain strike is exactly the kind of event that could count as a 'new provocation'.

**Different views:** This thread is carried almost entirely by the Jerusalem Post, which builds it from US and Israeli official accounts: Iran's remaining missile stocks, Israeli fears of being caught off guard, and Rubio calling the Iranian regime an 'extraordinary danger'. Al Jazeera's items in this batch instead refer to the conflict as the 'US-Israel war on Iran' and foreground Tehran's legal argument and the war's energy costs for import-dependent Asian states; neither it nor the Guardian covers Trump's deliberations here.


### 3. [Eisenkot condemns Netanyahu’s ‘racist’ campaign against Israel’s Arab citizens](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/13/eisenkot-accuses-netanyahu-of-racist-campaign-against-israels-arab-minority)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Israeli prime ministerial hopeful Gadi Eisenkot condemned what he called a 'racist and aggressive campaign of delegitimisation' against Israel's Arab citizens, after caretaker prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu accused Arab parties of electoral fraud without evidence. Eisenkot called the Arab minority 'an integral part of the Israeli story' as he tries to form a government. Separately, Arab party leader Mansour Abbas is being pressed by the change bloc to denounce Hamas as a condition for a coalition role.

**Background:** Israel is in post-election coalition talks, with Netanyahu serving as caretaker and Eisenkot seeking to assemble a governing majority. That majority may depend on the participation or support of an Arab party.

**Why it matters:** The outcome decides whether Netanyahu is replaced and whether an Arab party takes part in governing during a regional war. It also tests the place of Arab citizens in Israeli politics.

**Different views:** The Guardian foregrounds Eisenkot's defence of Arab citizens and states that Netanyahu's fraud claims are made without evidence. Al Jazeera foregrounds the pressure from Eisenkot's own bloc on Mansour Abbas to denounce Hamas, so the bloc appears as the one imposing conditions on an Arab leader. The Jerusalem Post has no article on the coalition talks in this batch.
