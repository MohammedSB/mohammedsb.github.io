### 1. [Iran targets US bases as Israel warns sanctions could drive Tehran to ‘desperate steps’](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/03/iran-targets-us-bases-as-israel-warns-sanctions-could-drive-tehran-to-desperate-steps)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Iran struck air bases used by US forces in the UAE and Kuwait, demonstrating a continued capacity to hit Gulf targets six months into the war and despite a new sanctions regime the Trump administration calls an 'economic D-day.' Israel's defence chief said the sanctions and economic pressure have Iran's leadership fearing regime collapse and could push Tehran toward 'desperate steps.' US officials, including Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, say the combined military and financial pressure may trigger an internal uprising or fracture the IRGC.

**Background:** The US-Israel-Iran war has run roughly six months, featuring US strikes inside Iran, Iranian missile and drone retaliation against Gulf bases, and an Iranian effort to disrupt shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Washington has layered on sanctions aimed at asphyxiating the IRGC and Iran's oil revenue.

**Why it matters:** Iran retains strike capability against Gulf states hosting US forces, which risks dragging UAE, Kuwait and other partners into a widening regional war, while the collapse-or-capitulate bet in Washington and Jerusalem carries a serious chance of escalation instead of surrender.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post frames the picture as Iran losing — regime in 'death throes,' 13 Iranian military dead, failing Hormuz blockade, unpaid professors — while Guardian coverage stresses Iran's persistent reach and the risk that squeezing a cornered regime produces desperation; Al Jazeera pivots to the war's costs for Washington, arguing rising prices and anti-war sentiment before US midterms may force Trump to de-escalate.


### 2. [Far-right Israeli minister outlines plan for removal of all Palestinians from Gaza](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/03/israel-far-right-minister-itamar-ben-gvir-plan-removal-palestinians-gaza)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Israeli far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir publicly set out a plan for the forced expulsion of the entire Palestinian population of the Gaza Strip — roughly 2 million people — to destinations abroad, calling the proposal 'realistic' and 'concrete.' He made the remarks in the run-up to a closely contested Israeli election.

**Background:** Forced transfer of a protected population from occupied territory is prohibited under international humanitarian law and is a grave breach of the Fourth Geneva Convention. Ben-Gvir leads the Otzma Yehudit party and has repeatedly pushed 'voluntary emigration' schemes for Gaza from within the security cabinet.

**Why it matters:** A sitting Israeli minister campaigning on mass expulsion moves what was fringe rhetoric into mainstream electoral politics, and shapes how Arab states, European governments and international courts assess Israeli intent in Gaza.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and the Guardian both lead with the illegality, using the words 'illegal forced expulsion' in their headlines and treating the plan as evidence of intent to ethnically cleanse Gaza; Israeli right-leaning coverage largely does not foreground the story at all, instead framing the election period around Iran policy and security, with US Ambassador Huckabee's criticism of settler violence appearing as the main internal critique of the Israeli far right.


### 3. [Israelis shoot dead two Palestinian teenagers after settlers encircle village](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/03/israelis-shoot-dead-two-palestinian-teenagers-after-settlers-encircle-village)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Two Palestinian teenagers, Khalil Abu Alia and Omar al-Naasan, were shot dead in the West Bank village of al-Mughayyir after Israeli settlers encircled the village escorted by Israeli troops. Separately, Israeli forces razed an entire village outside Hebron. The village council says the teens were trying to stop settlers stealing sheep; the Israeli military says they threw stones at its troops.

**Background:** Settler attacks on West Bank villages have surged since 2023, frequently with military escort or acquiescence, and al-Mughayyir has been repeatedly targeted. Palestinian communities in the South Hebron Hills face demolition orders and forced displacement.

**Why it matters:** The killings and demolitions mark an accelerating campaign of displacement in the occupied West Bank that runs parallel to the Gaza war, and it is straining Israel's standing even with sympathetic voices in Washington.

**Different views:** The Guardian describes the events flatly as 'Israeli terror and ethnic cleansing' and centres the settlers' encirclement of the village; the BBC gives both accounts in balance — the council's sheep-theft version against the military's stone-throwing claim — and follows with a bereaved father's testimony; the Jerusalem Post approaches settler violence obliquely, via US Ambassador Huckabee warning that it is damaging Israel's reputation in America rather than through the killings themselves.
