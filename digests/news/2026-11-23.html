1. Iran says it will use ‘all available means’ to counter US blockade

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Iran's Ministry of Foreign Affairs said it will use 'all available means' to confront what it calls a US naval siege and to defend itself, and it threatened to respond to any attack. Data cited by the Jerusalem Post shows Iranian attacks on ships near the Strait of Hormuz have reached their highest level since the war began. Kpler reports that Iran's oil on water has fallen to zero because the blockade is stopping its exports.

Background: The US has imposed a naval blockade on Iran during an ongoing conflict, and the Strait of Hormuz is a key route for oil shipped out of the Gulf. Israeli coverage describes a pending Trump decision between a deal with Iran and renewed strikes.

Why it matters: With Iran's oil exports cut off and its threats rising, the risk of escalation in the Strait of Hormuz is growing, and that would affect global energy markets and the wider region. Whether Washington chooses a deal or military action is still open.

Different views: Al Jazeera leads with Iran's official statements and uses Tehran's term 'naval siege', which makes Iran's defensive position the main story. The Jerusalem Post foregrounds Iranian attacks on shipping and the economic damage to Iran, and runs commentary from Israeli experts and a former diplomat who say a deal could endanger Israel for generations and that Trump has a window to strike before January. The Guardian's only angle in this set is the environmental cost of the war, told through a swimmer crossing the strait.

2. Israel’s destruction of Gaza has created a ‘toxic wasteland’, experts find

The Guardian · UK

What happened: An Oxfam report based on scientific and satellite findings says Israel's military campaign has left Gaza with poisoned water, contaminated soil and toxic air. It warns that this threatens the systems the population depends on to survive and will harm public health 'for generations to come'. Al Jazeera reports that the pollution affects 99% of the territory.

Background: Gaza has been heavily destroyed over the course of the war. Earlier humanitarian concern focused mostly on deaths, famine and displacement, not long-term environmental damage.

Why it matters: The report shifts attention to long-term damage that will affect rebuilding and health well beyond any ceasefire. It may also feed into legal and accountability arguments, including 'environmental crimes' claims.

Different views: Al Jazeera uses the strongest framing, with headlines saying Israel is 'blamed' for 'environmental crimes' across 99% of Gaza. The BBC attributes the findings to Oxfam and uses more cautious wording ('signs of possible contamination'). The Guardian presents the findings as what 'experts find' and stresses the health risks across generations. None of the provided pieces includes an Israeli government response, and the Jerusalem Post does not cover the report in this set.

3. Netanyahu rival condemns ‘Jewish terrorism’ after settler attacks on Palestinian villages

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Dozens of Israeli settlers raided Palestinian villages in the occupied West Bank on Saturday night. They set fire to vehicles and several homes, and the Jerusalem Post counts four separate attacks that included revenge graffiti. Opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot called the attacks 'Jewish terrorism' and accused ministers Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich of bringing the West Bank to the brink of anarchy.

Background: Settler violence has been increasing, and far-right ministers hold important security and finance posts in Netanyahu's government. Al Jazeera reports that closed military zones are being taken over by settlers as de facto settler land.

Why it matters: The attacks have become a domestic political fight in Israel, with the opposition directly blaming coalition ministers. They also add to the de facto annexation dynamics that shape the future of the West Bank.

Different views: The Guardian centres Eisenkot's 'Jewish terrorism' quote and treats the political row inside Israel as the main story. The Jerusalem Post calls the attackers 'Jewish extremists', lists the attacks as discrete incidents, and frames the story around Eisenkot's criticism of Ben-Gvir and Smotrich. Al Jazeera's related coverage is structural rather than about one incident: it describes military zones as 'staging posts for pogroms' and presents the violence as systematic land seizure rather than the work of extremists.
