### 1. [US signs landmark nuclear deal with Saudi Arabia](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cj03r59z73po?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** The US and Saudi Arabia signed a landmark 'peaceful' civil nuclear cooperation agreement giving US firms access to the Saudi nuclear program. Trump stressed the deal is contingent on Saudi Arabia joining the Abraham Accords and recognizing Israel, though reporting differs on whether it bans or permits domestic uranium enrichment.

**Background:** Past US presidents also tied nuclear cooperation to Saudi-Israel normalization, but this deal is being struck amid an active US-Iran war and while Washington insists Iran can never enrich uranium.

**Why it matters:** It could reshape Gulf alignments and normalization with Israel, while critics warn it risks a regional nuclear arms race and a double standard versus Iran.

**Different views:** BBC frames it neutrally as a 'landmark' deal that jettisons old demands; the Guardian and commentators like Kenneth Roth call it 'absurd,' stressing enrichment fears and Iran hypocrisy; the Jerusalem Post highlights Israeli officials' alarm that it could yield Saudi weapons and discourage normalization, while also casting the Accords link as a positive incentive.

### 3. [Houthis claim attack on oil tankers as US launches more strikes on Iran](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cpw9xzx9r4ko?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** The Houthis claimed attacks on Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea, the first such strikes since declaring a 'maritime embargo' against Saudi Arabia, prompting multiple tankers to make sharp U-turns and reroute away from Bab al-Mandeb.

**Background:** With the Strait of Hormuz already disrupted by Iran, Saudi Arabia had shifted energy exports toward the Red Sea, which the Houthi blockade now threatens as a second chokepoint.

**Why it matters:** Threatening a second maritime chokepoint could spike global oil prices and shipping insurance costs and open a new front tying the Houthis directly into the US-Iran war.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and BBC report the disruption factually as an expanding blockade; the Jerusalem Post frames it as a 'dangerous escalation' and Iranian-backed threat to Gulf economies, stressing risk to Saudi/US resources rather than Yemeni grievances.
