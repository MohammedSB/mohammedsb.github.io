1. Iranian attacks on ships near Strait of Hormuz hit highest level since war began, data shows

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Shipping data shows Iranian attacks on vessels near the Strait of Hormuz have reached their highest level since the war began. UKMTO said three vessels were struck by projectiles on Monday, though all continued their journeys and all crew were accounted for. Separately, the IRGC told IRNA that three oil tankers hit mines and caught fire while under US military escort. US officials quoted by the WSJ also named Iran as a possible culprit in cyberattacks and bomb threats against as many as 50 ships.

Background: The attacks are happening during an ongoing US-Iran war that followed Khamenei's killing. A US blockade has cut Iran's oil exports so sharply that Kpler reports Iran's oil on water has fallen to zero.

Why it matters: Hormuz is the world's most important oil chokepoint, and Iran appears to be answering the blockade by making the strait dangerous for everyone, which tests whether US escorts can protect shipping. It also adds pressure to Trump's choice between a deal with Iran and further strikes, a choice the Gulf states and Israel are watching closely.

Different views: All the Hormuz coverage in this batch comes from the Jerusalem Post. It sets UKMTO and US-official (WSJ) accounts next to an IRGC statement via IRNA that stresses the failure of US escorts, and runs Israeli-expert analyses warning that a US-Iran deal would endanger Israel. Al Jazeera's Iran coverage instead centres Iran's lawsuits in US and EU courts over alleged war crimes, along with expert doubts that they can succeed. It does not report the shipping attacks.

2. Hamas, Palestinian factions agree to Gaza disarmament roadmap, Board of Peace says

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: The Board of Peace says Hamas and other Palestinian factions have agreed to a roadmap for disarmament in Gaza. The board demanded that Hamas carry out its commitment to disarm without delay, and it called on Israel to allow building and other materials into the territory. Israeli strikes went on at the same time: one killed Hamas internal security chief Naeem Abu Nahel in Gaza City, and Gaza health officials reported seven people killed in a day.

Background: The Board of Peace is the body set up under the US-brokered ceasefire framework to oversee Gaza's transition after the war. Hamas disarmament has been the main obstacle to later phases such as reconstruction and Israeli withdrawal.

Why it matters: If the roadmap holds, it could unlock the stalled next stage of the ceasefire and reconstruction. Continued Israeli strikes on Hamas officials, however, show how fragile the arrangement still is.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post leads with the Board of Peace's demand that Hamas disarm immediately and with COGAT meeting UN officials on winter aid, so its Gaza coverage is about diplomacy and aid logistics. The other outlets' Gaza coverage in this batch does not mention the roadmap. Al Jazeera and the BBC focus on continued Israeli strikes, using Gaza health and interior ministry figures, and on a meningitis outbreak. The BBC and the Guardian both report Oxfam and expert findings that Israel's assault has left Gaza a 'toxic wasteland'.

3. Iraq orders takeover of oilfields in areas under Kurdish control

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered the federal government to take over oilfields in areas under Kurdish control. He also ordered it to seize all border crossings in the Kurdish region, following an inquiry into oil smuggling. Al-Zaidi is also travelling to Cairo to discuss regional security and economic cooperation with Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi.

Background: Baghdad and the Kurdistan Regional Government have argued for years over control of oil production, exports and revenue. Iraqi courts and international arbitration have already restricted Erbil's independent oil sales.

Why it matters: This is the most direct challenge to Kurdish economic autonomy in years and could lead to a political or security confrontation between Baghdad and Erbil. It could also disrupt Iraqi oil flows at a time when Gulf energy supplies are already under strain.
