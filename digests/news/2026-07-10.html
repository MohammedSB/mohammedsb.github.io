1. US and Iran trade escalating strikes as supreme leader is buried after days-long funeral

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: The US and Iran traded their most intense strikes since a June interim memorandum, with the US hitting targets across Iran (including port cities Bandar Abbas and Sirik) and Iran retaliating against US-allied Gulf states Kuwait and Qatar. Trump declared the fragile truce 'over' and threatened further escalation unless Iran stops attacking ships in the Strait of Hormuz.

Background: The escalation followed Iranian attacks on three tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and coincided with the days-long funeral of late supreme leader Ali Khamenei.

Why it matters: A renewed US-Iran war threatens global oil supply through the Hormuz chokepoint, drags in Gulf states, and could pull Israel into direct conflict.

2. How Hamas Governed Gaza

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: Hamas is dissolving its government in Gaza and handing power to a new technocratic administration, ending direct Hamas rule over the enclave.

Background: The move follows Israel's devastating war and ceasefire arrangements, with a new committee (NCAG) and PA figures like Nasser al-Shaath now involved in Gaza's governance.

Why it matters: It marks a potential structural shift in who administers Gaza's 2 million people and reshapes the postwar political order, though questions remain over sovereignty and real power.

Different views: Al Jazeera commentary argues the change alters who administers Gaza but not who holds ultimate power over Palestinian life, while JPost frames it through PA coordination and the formation of the new governing panel.

3. First Palestinian legislative elections in 20 years announced

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: The first Palestinian legislative elections in 20 years have been announced.

Background: The last such elections in 2006 produced a Hamas victory and the subsequent Fatah-Hamas split; no legislative vote has been held since amid the West Bank-Gaza political division.

Why it matters: Elections could reshape Palestinian political legitimacy and leadership at a pivotal moment when Gaza's governance is being restructured after the war.
