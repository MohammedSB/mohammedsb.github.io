## Top Stories

### 1. [US hits dozens of targets in Iran overnight as peace efforts under threat](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/30/us-strikes-iran-war-widening-iraq)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The United States carried out a major wave of airstrikes on Iran overnight — reportedly roughly twice the scale of previous strikes — targeting multiple locations including Bushehr, Qeshm Island, Kish Island, and Ahwaz. Iran retaliated by striking US allies in the region, including hitting targets in Kuwait and launching drones that struck tankers at Egypt's Damietta port. Mediators are struggling to pull both sides back to the negotiating table.

**Background:** The US-Iran conflict has been escalating for months in a tit-for-tat exchange of strikes. The latest US bombardment came after Iran attempted attacks on American troops, and follows a brief pause in hostilities that both sides have now abandoned.

**Why it matters:** This is the most dangerous escalation in the US-Iran conflict in decades, with strikes now hitting Iranian soil, US allies being targeted in retaliation, and regional energy infrastructure at risk — raising serious fears of an all-out regional war.

**Different views:** BBC and Guardian frame the escalation as a dangerous cycle that both sides may be partially choosing, with BBC noting Iran may prefer 'limited war' to peace as a strategic posture. Al Jazeera emphasizes Iranian retaliation and US aggression, headlining IRGC strikes on Kuwait. The Jerusalem Post focuses on the scale of US strikes and Israeli intelligence on Iran's underground sites, highlighting a Trump-Netanyahu divide over how far to push Iran militarily.


### 2. [Trump says ‘complete disarmament’ of Hamas agreed in Gaza but Israel yet to accept](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/31/trump-hamas-disarmament-agreement-gaza-board-of-peace)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Donald Trump announced a 'complete disarmament' agreement for Hamas in Gaza brokered through his newly created 'Board of Peace,' with Hamas officially agreeing to a phased handover of weapons to a new Gaza administration. However, Israel has not accepted the deal, with Israeli sources telling the Jerusalem Post there will be no IDF withdrawal until Gaza is fully demilitarized, and Hamas officials warning the deal is void without Israeli approval.

**Background:** The Gaza war has been ongoing since the October 7, 2023 Hamas attacks on Israel. Previous ceasefire and hostage deals have repeatedly stalled, with Hamas's armed status and Israel's military presence being the core sticking points in any long-term resolution.

**Why it matters:** If implemented, Hamas disarmament would be the most transformative development in the Gaza conflict since October 7 — but the gap between US announcements, Hamas's conditional agreement, and Israel's outright skepticism means the deal remains fragile and its prospects deeply uncertain.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post is highly skeptical, stressing Israel's firm rejection and reporting that IRGC officials urged Hamas not to sign — framing the deal as premature and potentially a Hamas tactical maneuver. The Guardian and BBC give more weight to the potential diplomatic breakthrough while noting Israeli far-right opposition. Al Jazeera foregrounds Palestinian skepticism on the ground in Gaza, questioning whether any deal will bring real change or end occupation.


### 3. [Saudi Arabia prepares sea and possible land offensive against Houthis](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/30/saudi-forces-planning-major-offensive-against-houthis-central-yemen)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Saudi Arabia is reportedly concentrating military forces for a major sea and possible land offensive against the Houthis in central Yemen, according to Yemeni sources. The move comes as the Houthis, in coordination with Iraqi militias, have been striking Saudi territory and choking oil shipping through the southern Red Sea. The recent joint US-Saudi strikes marked the first time Riyadh publicly joined Washington in hitting Houthi targets.

**Background:** The Houthis have been a persistent threat to Saudi Arabia and regional shipping since Yemen's civil war escalated. Their alignment with Iran's 'axis of resistance' has deepened as the Gaza conflict expanded into a broader regional confrontation involving Iran, Iraq-based militias, and Hezbollah.

**Why it matters:** A Saudi land offensive in Yemen would be a major escalation after years of costly stalemate, and would further widen the regional war already engulfing Iran, Iraq, and the Red Sea — with direct implications for global oil markets and Gulf stability.

**Different views:** The Guardian and Jerusalem Post both report the Saudi preparations, framing it as a defensive move to break a Houthi stranglehold on oil exports. The Jerusalem Post additionally highlights the Iran-Houthi-Iraqi militia coordination angle, emphasizing the broader Iranian 'axis' threat. Al Jazeera has not prominently featured the Saudi offensive framing, consistent with its generally more sympathetic coverage of Houthi grievances.
