## Top Stories

### 1. ['Economic D-Day': Trump threatens economic warfare against countries aiding Iran](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-906049)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Trump announced what he called an 'economic D-Day' against Iran, threatening 'economic warfare and isolation on an unprecedented scale' against any country that trades with or assists Tehran, following the US Treasury's announcement of its toughest-ever sanctions package. The UAE responded by suspending all trade, commercial exchanges and financial transactions with Iran, while Treasury Secretary Bessent said allies must decide whether they 'are with us or against us' and predicted the sanctions would 'squash' Iran's economy and 'collapse' its regime. Iranian officials — FM Araghchi, Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf and IRGC spokesman Mohebbi — dismissed the campaign as doomed and threatened a more destructive response if war resumes.

**Background:** The pressure campaign follows a US-Israeli military confrontation with Iran that failed to force Tehran back to the negotiating table, prompting a pivot from kinetic strikes to secondary sanctions on third countries. Iran's main trade lifeline runs through China and Gulf intermediaries, making the extraterritorial threat the core of the strategy.

**Why it matters:** Secondary sanctions on Iran's trade partners would set up a direct confrontation with China and force Gulf states like the UAE to pick sides, restructuring regional economic alignments. Israeli officials read the move as lowering the near-term risk of war expansion — a deliberate delay until after the US midterms — which makes the sanctions the main arena of the Iran file for months.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post frames it as effective coercion that reduces escalation risk and highlights Gulf compliance, while the BBC reports the regime-collapse rhetoric more clinically as a US ultimatum to allies; the Guardian stresses that strikes 'failed' and casts the pivot as a China confrontation in the making, and Iranian voices relayed via Israeli and Qatari outlets present Tehran as unbowed and even victorious.


### 2. [Israel opens up bids for highly sensitive West Bank settlement project](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cly50gn1e54o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Israel opened tenders for construction in the E1 area of the occupied West Bank between Jerusalem and Ma'ale Adumim, and separately re-established a previously evacuated settlement where some thirty 'pioneer families' have moved in. The UK, France, Germany, Italy and Canada issued condemnations, with the British foreign secretary calling the move 'an unacceptable and destructive act.'

**Background:** E1 has been frozen for years under US and European pressure precisely because building there would sever the northern and southern West Bank and cut East Jerusalem off from its Palestinian hinterland. The current Israeli government has made settlement expansion a central policy plank.

**Why it matters:** Analysts say E1 construction would physically partition the West Bank and foreclose a contiguous Palestinian state, converting a diplomatic dispute into an irreversible fact on the ground. It also tests whether European and Canadian pressure, following recent statehood-recognition moves, translates into consequences beyond statements.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and the Guardian foreground illegality under international law, the end of Palestinian statehood, and settler violence — including a near-doubling of attacks in Areas A and B under Palestinian rule; the BBC centers the settlers' own nationalist framing of 'pioneer families' alongside Palestinian fear, while the Jerusalem Post covers the West Bank largely through the lens of the resulting economic crisis and Israeli security restrictions rather than settlement legality.


### 3. [Turkey denies Israeli claims, says no troops were at Syrian airbase struck by IDF](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-906085)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Turkey's defense ministry denied Israeli claims that Turkish troops were present at a Syrian airbase struck by the IDF, saying Israel's military interventions against neighbouring states 'must not be accepted.' Israel maintains Ankara and Damascus were 'on the verge' of breaking the status quo, while US Ambassador Mike Huckabee told Al Arabiya that Turkish forces had been 'further south than supposed' and characterised the Israeli strike as a reaction rather than a provocation.

**Background:** Since Assad's fall and the rise of the Sharaa government, Turkey has expanded its military footprint in Syria while Israel has struck repeatedly to prevent hostile capabilities near its border. Netanyahu previously ran a deconfliction channel with Russia in Syria; the open question is whether the same can be built with Erdogan.

**Why it matters:** Two US-aligned militaries — one a NATO member — are now operating in overlapping Syrian airspace without an agreed deconfliction mechanism, creating a live risk of direct clash. Repeated Israeli strikes are also pushing Damascus toward alternative partners such as Pakistan and hardening Saudi and Gulf reluctance on Abraham Accords expansion.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post presents the strike as pre-emptive enforcement of a status quo Ankara and Damascus were about to break, and amplifies the US ambassador's exculpatory account; Turkish denials relayed in the same coverage frame it as unacceptable aggression against a sovereign neighbour, and pro-Palestinian and left-leaning outlets situate such strikes within a broader pattern of Israeli regional military action rather than defensive necessity.
