## Top Stories

### 1. [Houthis seize key Yemeni port of Mocha in drive to take control of Red Sea coast](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/10/houthis-seize-key-port-mocha-yemen-red-sea-coast-iran-saudi-arabia-us)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Houthi forces seized the Red Sea port of Mocha and, per witnesses, the island of Perim at the mouth of the Bab al-Mandab strait, in the heaviest fighting with the Saudi-backed government in years. Around 1,000 people have been killed or wounded and tens of thousands displaced. The Yemeni government says a counteroffensive toward Sanaa has begun, and Trump reportedly declined a Saudi request for direct US strikes on the Houthis.

**Background:** The advance comes amid the ongoing US-Iran war, in which the Strait of Hormuz has been contested and Saudi Arabia has rerouted oil exports through Red Sea ports that depend on transit past Bab al-Mandab.

**Why it matters:** If Iran-aligned forces hold both Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab, the region's two maritime chokepoints fall under their leverage, threatening Gulf oil exports and potentially opening a new front in the US-Iran war.

**Different views:** The Guardian foregrounds the economic stakes for Saudi Arabia and global shipping and ties the offensive to the US-Iran war; Al Jazeera frames the Houthis as an 'Iran-backed force' threatening the alternative to Hormuz while stopping short of assessing Iranian direction. The Jerusalem Post calls the Houthis a 'terror group', treats Yemeni military claims of captured IRGC maps and Iranian arms and advice as authoritative, and gives prominence to the government's counteroffensive, which the other outlets barely mention. The BBC hedges the Perim capture as 'witnesses say'.


### 2. [Iran war won't end until after crucial November elections, says Trump](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5ydld0gykpo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Trump said the war with Iran will not end until after the November midterm elections, adding that oil prices will stay high until then and claiming Iran is trying to influence the vote. He said he does not regret launching the war. Separately, Gulf foreign ministers are reportedly set to meet Iran's foreign minister in Salalah, Oman, on Monday to seek a deal on the Strait of Hormuz.

**Background:** The US and Iran have been fighting around Hormuz for weeks; Iranian strikes badly damaged the US naval base in Bahrain and aircraft at Muwaffaq Salti base in Jordan, while the IAEA reports new construction at Iran's Pickaxe Mountain site that Trump has threatened to strike.

**Why it matters:** Explicitly linking the war's duration to US electoral timing signals months more of conflict and $105 oil, while the Gulf-Iran channel in Oman is the first visible diplomatic track that could bypass Washington.

**Different views:** The BBC flags that Trump's claim of Iranian election interference was made 'without evidence'; the Jerusalem Post relays the same allegation without that caveat and pairs it with his 'no regret' remarks and the Hormuz-renaming taunt. Al Jazeera emphasises US vulnerability, headlining a US official's admission that Iran 'blew the hell out of' the Bahrain base, while the Jerusalem Post reports the Jordan base damage more clinically and leads instead on Iran's depleted missile production and sanctions evasion via China.


### 3. [Algeria to sever diplomatic ties with UAE](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cx2zpgpngvpo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Algeria announced it is severing diplomatic ties with the United Arab Emirates. The foreign ministry gave no specific reason but said it had exhausted all other means.

**Background:** Algiers has long accused Abu Dhabi of backing Morocco on Western Sahara and of meddling in the Sahel and Libya; relations had deteriorated for years without a formal break.

**Why it matters:** A full rupture between two major Arab states deepens the Maghreb-Gulf fault line, and lands the same week Spain's parliament backed citizenship for Sahrawis amid its own tensions with Morocco, sharpening the Western Sahara dispute on several fronts.
