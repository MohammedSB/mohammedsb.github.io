1. Ali Khamenei’s six-day funeral expected to draw millions in Iran

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Iran began a six-day series of mass funeral processions for former Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, with crowds estimated in the millions filling Tehran's Grand Mosalla and ceremonies planned across Tehran, Qom, Mashhad, Najaf and Karbala. His body will lie in state before a burial in Mashhad, and Iranian leaders used the occasion to vow they will 'never surrender.'

Background: Khamenei's 37-year reign was ended in February by the opening airstrike of the ongoing war launched by the US and Israel; the funeral had been delayed for months.

Why it matters: The scale of the mourning and the defiant 'resistance' messaging signal how Iran's leadership intends to consolidate legitimacy and project strength amid an active war now in its fourth month.

Different views: Al Jazeera and the Guardian foreground the sheer scale of grief and national mourning, while the Jerusalem Post frames the event around security anxieties — the absence of successor Mojtaba Khamenei over assassination fears (35) and generals' threats of 'harsh response' to any attack (43).
