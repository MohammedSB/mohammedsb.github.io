## Top Stories

### 1. [Trump planning new military assault or tighter sanctions, say Iranian officials](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/11/trump-planning-military-assault-or-tighter-sanctions-iranian-officials)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Senior Iranian officials say they believe the US is preparing either new military strikes or a tighter economic squeeze, and an adviser to chief negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warned Tehran could be pushed into pre-emptive strikes if the stalemate continues. Ghalibaf separately said Iran remains open to a deal if Washington 'refrains from excessive demands', while sources told the Jerusalem Post that Qatar's prime minister admits major gaps remain in the mediated talks.

**Background:** The US and Israel have been at war with Iran since February, with strikes on Iranian military infrastructure, attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and a US campaign to cripple Iran's economy. Qatar and other mediators are trying to broker a US-Iran agreement.

**Why it matters:** The talks are the main route out of a war that has disrupted Gulf shipping and drawn in neighbouring states. A collapse risks renewed escalation, including the pre-emptive Iranian action the adviser warned of.

**Different views:** The Guardian and Al Jazeera build their reports on Iranian officials' own account, foregrounding Tehran's fear of US attack and its conditional openness to a deal. The Jerusalem Post relies on unnamed sources on the mediation and on reports of Iran's remaining capabilities (about 1,700 ballistic missiles, 50 produced a month), foregrounding the gaps in the talks, Trump's push for a deal and the continuing Iranian threat.


### 2. [Israeli airstrike on Nasser hospital in Gaza kills surgeon and five others](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/12/israeli-airstrike-nasser-hospital-gaza-kills-surgeon)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** An Israeli airstrike hit Nasser hospital in Khan Younis early on Thursday, killing six people, including a surgeon and a disabled man, and wounding 28, according to the hospital director. Witnesses said a missile struck a building housing the surgical wards and intensive care units. The Israeli military confirmed in a statement that it carried out the strike.

**Background:** Nasser is the largest hospital in southern Gaza and one of the few medical facilities still functioning in the strip. More than 940,000 displaced people there are also facing winter storms in flood-prone areas.

**Why it matters:** The strike further degrades Gaza's remaining medical capacity and is likely to sharpen international scrutiny of Israeli attacks on health facilities.

**Different views:** The Guardian attributes the toll to the hospital director and leads with the victims' identities and the scarcity of remaining hospitals, while the BBC attributes the deaths to the health ministry and witnesses and leads with which wards were hit. The Jerusalem Post has no article on the strike in this set; its Gaza piece instead presents an HonestReporting investigation arguing that Hamas-issued flooding figures were relayed by international media without verification.


### 3. [Iran attacks shipping navigation systems on Oman's Didamar island near Strait of Hormuz](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-914624)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Iranian strikes targeted maritime navigation infrastructure on Oman's Didamar island near the Strait of Hormuz, though the extent of the damage and the effect on commercial shipping remain unclear. Separately, Saudi Arabia said debris from intercepted Houthi missiles fell in the Mecca region, the Houthis claimed new Saudi airstrikes, and Iran said a tanker hit a mine in Hormuz.

**Background:** Tankers in the strait were hit by missiles or sea mines throughout October, and the Guardian reports hundreds of oil spills since the war began in February. Oman has traditionally acted as a neutral go-between for Iran and the West.

**Why it matters:** A strike on Omani territory widens the war to a state that has stayed out of it and further endangers navigation through one of the world's most important oil chokepoints. Combined with renewed Saudi-Houthi exchanges, it points to the conflict spreading across the Gulf and Yemen.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post frames the Hormuz and Yemen events around Iranian and Houthi attacks and Gulf states' closer cooperation in response. The Guardian covers the same waterway through the environmental damage of what it calls the 'US war on Iran', and Al Jazeera asks whether US bases in the Gulf have become liabilities for their hosts; neither has an article on the Didamar strike in this set.
