### 1. [Israel's new parliament to be sworn in as leaders compete to form government](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/crd939lpzjp9o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Israel's new Knesset is being sworn in after the election, with Netanyahu's main rival Gadi Eisenkot given the first chance to assemble a coalition and oust the prime minister. The opposition's immediate priority is replacing the Knesset speaker, a post that controls the legislative agenda, even though it is still struggling to assemble a governing majority.

**Background:** Netanyahu remains caretaker prime minister while the coalition arithmetic is unresolved, retaining control of security decisions and the machinery of government during the deadlock.

**Why it matters:** Whether Netanyahu survives determines Israel's posture on Gaza, the West Bank and the Iran confrontation, and a prolonged caretaker period leaves a wartime government with weakened democratic mandate making consequential decisions.

**Different views:** BBC foregrounds Eisenkot's formal mandate and treats the process as an orderly constitutional handover; the Guardian frames the speakership as the opposition's real lever of power and stresses how far it remains from a coalition; Al Jazeera centres Netanyahu's interim powers and presents him as actively using caretaker status to disrupt his rivals rather than as a passive outgoing leader.


### 2. [Four killed in Gaza after sand embankment collapses onto tents, health ministry says](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqd090ee8px5o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** A sand embankment collapsed onto tents at a displacement camp south of Deir al-Balah after torrential rain, killing a woman and three children according to Gaza's health ministry, with witnesses describing a frantic effort to dig out buried family members. The deaths followed a storm that flooded around 70,000 tents, prompting civil defence to declare a maximum emergency and Gaza authorities to warn that more than 2,000 damaged buildings risk collapse.

**Background:** Hundreds of thousands of Palestinians are entering a third winter in tents after Israel's assault on Gaza, and winter shelter supplies have been a recurring point of dispute between Israel, Hamas and international bodies.

**Why it matters:** The floods show the ceasefire-era humanitarian system is unable to protect displaced civilians from predictable seasonal weather, and they are intensifying pressure on Israel over the volume and speed of aid it allows in.

**Different views:** The Guardian and Al Jazeera foreground the scale of displacement and place the flooding in the context of Israel's war and the third winter in tents, treating Gaza civil defence and government figures as the primary account; BBC attributes the deaths to the health ministry and keeps to the incident itself; the Jerusalem Post frames the story around the IDF's confirmation that tents and winter supplies have entered Gaza, presenting Hamas's blame of Israel as a claim being rebutted, while also reporting the Board of Peace's call for Israel to authorise more winter aid as an immediate emergency.


### 3. [Trump says more US attacks on Iran 'highly unlikely'](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cq5yny4nl2d3o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Trump said further US attacks on Iran are 'highly unlikely' and that Washington is maintaining the status quo, while insisting it remains 'locked and loaded' if conditions change. After meeting Qatar's prime minister he said Saudi Arabia and Qatar want a peaceful resolution, as Iran defended its two-day peace proposal and rejected Rubio's criticism, and Tehran announced it was lifting the 2,000km range limit on its ballistic missiles.

**Background:** The US and Iran remain deadlocked after the Iran war, with a Strait of Hormuz blockade in place, a US task force targeting Iran's land trade, and Trump demanding that Israel and Gulf allies repay the cost of the conflict.

**Why it matters:** The signals from Washington will shape whether the standoff moves toward negotiation or renewed strikes, and Iran's missile-range decision and the diversion of US aircraft from a UK base show both sides are still preparing for escalation even while talking about diplomacy.

**Different views:** BBC headlines the de-escalatory line that new attacks are unlikely; the Jerusalem Post leads with US readiness to strike and the Gulf states' support for keeping the Hormuz blockade, treating Trump's account of Gulf positions as authoritative; Al Jazeera presents the deadlock as requiring mutual, verifiable concessions rather than one side moving first, reads Trump's repayment demand as posturing, and gives Iran's spokesman space to cast US pressure as a post-midterm diversion.
