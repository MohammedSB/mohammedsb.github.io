1. Two US troops killed and one missing in Jordan after Iranian attacks

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Iran launched a wave of strikes on US allies across the Gulf, killing two US service members (one missing) at Muwaffaq Salti Airbase in Jordan and hitting infrastructure in Kuwait and Bahrain. The US retaliated with its seventh and then eighth consecutive nights of strikes on Iran's IRGC and military sites, as tensions spiked in the Strait of Hormuz.

Background: The exchange follows the collapse of a month-long US-Iran ceasefire, with Washington resuming a bombing campaign and blockade against Tehran.

Why it matters: The first US combat deaths mark a major escalation that risks pulling the US into a prolonged regional war and threatens Gulf energy infrastructure and shipping through Hormuz.

Different views: Al Jazeera foregrounds US bombing of Iranian civilian infrastructure and an 'eighth-straight night' of US aggression; the Jerusalem Post frames it as 'sinful Iranian aggression' and lawful CENTCOM retaliation for slain soldiers; the Guardian centers criticism of Trump for reviving an 'unlawful' and politically risky war.

2. Israeli ministers announce plans for new illegal settlements in Gaza and West Bank

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Israel's defence and finance ministers announced plans for three 'Nahal' outposts in Gaza and over $400m to expand West Bank construction, as the regional military commander said Israel now controls 65% of the Gaza Strip. Critics say this violates the Trump-brokered ceasefire.

Background: The moves extend a campaign by far-right minister Bezalel Smotrich to entrench Israeli control over occupied land.

Why it matters: Formalizing settlements inside Gaza signals a shift toward permanent occupation and undermines the ceasefire framework, with implications for any future Palestinian statehood.

Different views: Al Jazeera and the Guardian describe the settlements as illegal and part of a strategy to force Palestinians out, while Israeli outlets largely omit or downplay the settlement expansion in favor of the Iran war coverage.

3. Lebanon's Aoun marks first trip to Trump's White House with Hezbollah disarmament plan

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: Lebanese President Joseph Aoun made his first visit to Trump's White House — the first by a Lebanese head of state in nearly 20 years — bringing a plan for Hezbollah disarmament. Aoun, a former US-backed army commander, arrives amid reports Hezbollah is weighing escalation against the Lebanese army.

Background: Lebanon has been under pressure to rein in Hezbollah following the group's weakening in recent conflict with Israel.

Why it matters: A credible Lebanese state effort to disarm Hezbollah, backed by Washington, could reshape Lebanon's balance of power and reduce a flashpoint on Israel's northern border.
