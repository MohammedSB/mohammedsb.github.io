### 1. [Twelve killed and 300 injured in attack on airport in Saudi capital Riyadh](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw33x4y8k82no?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** A missile strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday killed 12 to 13 people and injured about 300. It was the deadliest attack inside Saudi Arabia since May 2015. On Sunday another projectile hit a passenger terminal, killing one person and injuring 11, and Saudi Arabia extended its suspension of all flights at the airport. The Houthis claimed the strikes, warned that 'the worst is yet to come', and told airlines and travellers to avoid Saudi airports.

**Background:** Saudi Arabia is running an air campaign against the Iran-backed Houthis, who are fighting a ground war in Yemen against the Saudi-backed government. The Houthis have repeatedly got past stretched Saudi air defences.

**Why it matters:** Striking civilian aviation in the Saudi capital is a major escalation. It damages Riyadh's reputation for stability and could pull in other countries: the UAE has expressed solidarity and Pakistan has pledged military backing. It also undermines Washington's effort to keep the wars in Iran and Yemen on hold.

**Different views:** The BBC centres casualty figures, CCTV footage and the Saudi minister's promise of a 'serious response', and relies on US and Saudi officials for attribution. The Guardian puts more weight on the political fallout: commentary argues Trump's strategy and old Gulf alliances look shaky. The Jerusalem Post stresses the Iran connection, the Houthis' own threats and Israeli and US experts who expect Saudi retaliation, comparing the strikes to Iran's attacks on US bases. Al Jazeera's coverage emphasises Saudi vows of resilience and the Yemeni forces fighting back against the Houthis on the ground. Death tolls differ between outlets (12 vs 13).


### 2. [Maps show how Israel’s ‘Yellow Line’ keeps squeezing Palestinians in Gaza](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/12/maps-show-how-israels-yellow-line-keeps-squeezing-palestinians-in-gaza?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** A year into the US-brokered ceasefire, Israel's 'Yellow Line' restricts Palestinian access to about 65% of Gaza and confines 2.1 million people to the rest. Over 1.4 million Gazans are acutely short of food. A US general has separately warned that Netanyahu's government is stalling Trump's Gaza plan, and the Board of Peace is reported to expect reconstruction only after the Israeli election and the US midterms.

**Background:** The October 2025 ceasefire set up a Board of Peace to oversee Gaza's future. Disarming Hamas and Israel's withdrawal remain unresolved.

**Why it matters:** The ceasefire has frozen into a territorial and humanitarian standoff. Gaza's future now looks tied to Israeli and US electoral calendars, and the humanitarian crisis continues in the meantime.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera foregrounds Palestinian experience: shrinking territory, hunger, displacement, and analysts who say even a change of Israeli government would change little. The Jerusalem Post frames it as a diplomatic and security impasse, citing a US general and the Board of Peace on Israeli stalling and the scale of the Hamas threat. It also reports conflict-of-interest allegations against Kushner, which the Board of Peace denies. Civilian conditions get far less attention there.


### 3. ['Insufficent': Iranian regime rejects US response to conditions set to reopen Strait of Hormuz](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-911369)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Iran's security chief Rezaee called the US response to Tehran's seven conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz 'insufficient'. He said the strait would stay closed to the Americans and no negotiations would take place. Separately, a tanker was hit by a drone near Oman's Sohar port.

**Background:** The Strait of Hormuz has been at the centre of the US-Iran war, and the Pentagon is still revising its count of US service members killed in that conflict. The IEA head has called the situation the largest energy security threat.

**Why it matters:** Keeping the strait closed puts pressure on global energy supplies and fuel prices. It also makes a lasting US-Iran settlement less likely as Iran's Houthi allies escalate against Saudi Arabia.
