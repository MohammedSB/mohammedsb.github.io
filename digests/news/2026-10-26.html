1. What you need to know about the most consequential election in Israel’s history

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Israel votes on Tuesday in its first general election since the 7 October 2023 Hamas attacks, with Benjamin Netanyahu seeking a new mandate against challengers including Gadi Eisenkot. A final mass rally against the government was held in Tel Aviv's Hostages Square on Saturday, organised by families of the 7 October victims and survivors.

Background: The vote follows three years of regional war spanning Gaza, Lebanon, Syria and two rounds of conflict with Iran, and comes days after a deadly attack on Israeli soldiers near Hebron.

Why it matters: The outcome will shape Gaza's future, West Bank policy, whether Israel joins a renewed US strike on Iran, and the trajectory of Israeli democracy itself, with regional actors from Tehran to Riyadh positioning for either result.

Different views: BBC frames the vote around 'security and democracy' as the central issues and treats Israeli voters' concerns as the story; the Guardian describes the post-October 7 campaign as a 'war of annihilation' and reports that Gaza's catastrophe is 'campaign fodder' with Palestinian views foregrounded; Al Jazeera asks what changes for Palestinians and tracks Knesset voting records on bills 'disfavouring Palestinians', while the Jerusalem Post largely covers the election through its regional security implications, carrying Netanyahu's warning of an 'imminent existential threat' from Iran.

2. Israeli soldiers raze neighbourhood in Hebron, driving families from homes

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Israeli troops have levelled a neighbourhood in Hebron and taken over homes, displacing hundreds of families after a gun attack on an army checkpoint killed two soldiers. Netanyahu vowed 'to destroy this place', and the IDF has announced West Bank crossings will close on election day with restrictions on Palestinian workers.

Background: The operation comes in the final days before the Israeli election and alongside a broader pattern of expulsions and infrastructure destruction across the occupied West Bank.

Why it matters: The scale of displacement in the West Bank's largest city, and its timing, signals that West Bank policy will harden regardless of the election result and risks a further flashpoint during voting.

Different views: The Guardian leads with 'collective punishment' and ties the operation explicitly to Netanyahu proving security credentials before the vote, citing the Hebron municipality's estimate of 35 hectares destroyed; Al Jazeera situates Hebron within a deliberate Israeli strategy of 'forced expulsions' to impose a 'new reality' and highlights Palestinians sheltering the displaced; the Jerusalem Post presents it as 'IDF operations' following the soldiers' deaths and sources the displacement figures (407 households, 2,649 people) to Hebron's relief committee without characterising the action; BBC frames the crossing closures as a 'crackdown' linked to election security.

3. US ready to strike Iran 'within 96 hours' of Trump's order, Israel pushes for joint attack - report

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: US forces are reportedly ready to strike Iran within 96 hours of a Trump order, with Israel pushing for a joint attack, though Trump has ruled out an immediate strike and says no final decision will come until after the US midterms. Tehran has reportedly threatened retaliation against energy and civilian targets and nuclear escalation, and Iranian officials have allegedly described US diplomats as 'fair game' for assassination.

Background: This follows earlier US-Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear and missile sites, ongoing Houthi missile attacks on Saudi Arabia that have grounded most Riyadh flights, and a planned meeting in Tehran between Iranian officials and Mecca defence pact members including Saudi Arabia's defence minister and Pakistan's army chief.

Why it matters: A renewed US-Israeli campaign against Iran would come as interceptor stockpiles in Israel and the Gulf are depleted and as Gulf states are hedging between Washington, Tehran and a new Saudi-Turkish-Pakistani-Egyptian alignment, raising the risk of a wider regional war.

Different views: The strike-readiness, assassination-plot and Iranian retaliation-threat stories come exclusively from the Jerusalem Post, sourced to unnamed US and Israeli officials and treating Israeli security assessments as authoritative; Al Jazeera's coverage of the same theatre focuses instead on the Houthi-Saudi exchange of fire and the Pakistan-Iran border, giving equal weight to Tehran's claim that its cargo is blocked, and does not report the US strike timeline at all.
