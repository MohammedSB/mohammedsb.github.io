1. Saudi Arabia shuts down key oil pipeline, blaming drones from Iraq

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Saudi Arabia shut down its 1,200km East-West pipeline after drone strikes damaged the export route, with Riyadh blaming militants operating from Iraqi territory. Iraq confirmed the launch site was in Maysan province near the Iranian border, seized a drone-launching platform, dismissed a military commander, opened an investigation and closed the Shalamcheh crossing with Iran. Estimates cited put the potential disruption at up to 4% of global oil supply.

Background: Riyadh had ramped up use of the pipeline — which carries crude from the Eastern Province to the Red Sea — to bypass the closure of the Strait of Hormuz since the start of the US-Israel war on Iran.

Why it matters: It removes one of the few remaining workarounds to a closed Hormuz, threatening a fresh oil price spike, and drags Iraq directly into the Iran confrontation by exposing Baghdad's inability to control armed groups on its soil.

Different views: One account centres Saudi and Iraqi official statements on the investigation and the sacked commander as a sovereignty-and-accountability story; another leads with the market arithmetic of lost barrels and fuel prices; a third treats the episode primarily as proof of the reach of Iran-backed militias, while a fourth foregrounds Iraqi enforcement action and the tracing of the launch site rather than attributing a sponsor.

2. Iran’s Houthi allies capture strategic island on vital oil shipping route

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Houthi forces seized the strategic island of Mayun/Perim at the narrowest point of the Bab al-Mandab strait and advanced rapidly along Yemen's Red Sea coast, including toward Mocha. Nearly 50,000 civilians have fled, with about 1,400 crossing to Obock in Djibouti in a single day, while Saudi and Yemeni government aircraft struck to slow the advance and the Houthis claimed a drone and missile attack on a Saudi base, damaging a mosque and homes.

Background: The Bab al-Mandab is the Red Sea chokepoint now carrying traffic diverted by the Hormuz closure, and Yemen's government has been Riyadh-backed since the Houthis took Sanaa in 2014.

Why it matters: Control of both ends of the Red Sea corridor gives the Houthis and Iran leverage over global shipping at the moment alternative oil routes are already failing, and risks turning Yemen's frozen conflict into a permanent regional front.

Different views: One telling frames the advance as Iran extending its reach through allied fighters and stresses the shipping and oil stakes for Saudi exports; another dwells on the scale of civilian displacement and the risk of a 'forever war'; a third argues the escalation may backfire by uniting rivals in the Red Sea; and a fourth locates the cause in Yemeni government dysfunction — 'ghost names' padding army rosters — and distributes blame among Yemeni leaders, Washington and Riyadh rather than Tehran.

3. Israeli attack on Gaza kills two Palestinians

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: An Israeli strike hit a vehicle in the Tel al-Hawa neighbourhood of western Gaza City, killing two people. Israel said the target was a car carrying Hamas operatives; the deaths were reported as Palestinians killed despite the US-brokered ceasefire.

Background: A ceasefire brokered by Washington is formally in place in Gaza, but strikes have continued intermittently since it took effect.

Why it matters: Each strike during the truce tests whether the US-brokered arrangement holds and shapes the diplomatic pressure on Israel, including the new UK settlement-related measures.

Different views: One report leads with two Palestinians killed and places the strike in a pattern of attacks continuing despite a ceasefire it puts in scare quotes; the other leads with the military's account that the vehicle carried Hamas fighters, sourcing the scene to circulated footage and treating the identification as the frame rather than the ceasefire question.
