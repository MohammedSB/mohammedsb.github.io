1. Iran warns vessels to coordinate passage amid claims of ships hitting mines in Hormuz

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Iran has told shipping to coordinate passage through the Strait of Hormuz, amid claims that ships have hit mines. Tehran has told Washington that normal traffic will not resume until its demands are met, including the lifting of the US naval blockade of Iranian ports. UKMTO separately reported three vessels struck by projectiles in the strait on Monday, and US officials told the WSJ that Iran may be behind cyberattacks and bomb threats against as many as 50 ships.

Background: The US and Iran are at war, and the US is blockading Iranian ports. Trump says the war effort is funded until roughly the end of January, and that the new Congress could limit his military options. Reports say his officials are split over whether to target Iranian leaders.

Why it matters: Hormuz carries a large share of the world's oil and gas exports, so a standoff over shipping there could hit global energy markets and the Gulf states. It also gives Tehran leverage in any talks to end the war.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post's coverage centres on Iranian threats: mines, attacks on ships, hackers, and an analysis of chemical and biological weapons programmes. It relies on US officials, UKMTO and Israeli security experts, and one analysis argues Trump is walking into an Iranian trap at Israel's expense. Al Jazeera looks instead at how the sanctions and blockade are hurting Iran, with electricity rationing ahead of winter. It also asks whether Netanyahu could use a new war with Iran to hold on to power as opposition leader Eisenkot tries to form a government. The BBC and Guardian items in this batch do not cover the Iran war.

2. ‘They don’t believe in freedom’: Yemenis plan their escape as Houthis advance on Aden

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Houthi forces are closing in on Aden, the temporary capital of Yemen's Saudi-backed government. Residents and NGOs are preparing to evacuate, and government forces have pulled back from strategic coastal positions. Saudi Arabia has launched airstrikes on the Houthis, including at Hodeidah port, and the UK is stepping up military support. Houthi strikes have hit Riyadh's tallest tower twice in a few days, injuring 11, and drones have targeted the Ras Tanura oil hub.

Background: The internationally recognised government made Aden its temporary capital after the Houthis took Sanaa and much of the north. For years the distance between Aden and the front line gave displaced Yemenis some safety.

Why it matters: If Aden falls, the Houthis would control most of Yemen and its coast near the Bab el-Mandeb shipping lane. That would put Saudi territory and oil infrastructure under direct pressure and pull outside powers, including the UK, further in.

Different views: The Guardian tells the story through displaced civilians like Hussein and through aid groups preparing to leave, and calls the Houthis Iran's proxy fighters. The Jerusalem Post focuses on the military and strategic side: Saudi airstrikes and evacuation warnings, Houthi retaliation threats, UK support, risks to shipping, and attacks on Saudi landmarks and oil sites. It relies mostly on Saudi official statements and the coalition's account.

3. Israeli air strike kills Hamas internal security chief in Gaza

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: An Israeli air strike in Gaza killed Naeem Abu Nahel, who had been Hamas's internal security chief for two months. He was among seven Palestinians reportedly killed in Israeli strikes on Tuesday. Al Jazeera, in what appears to be the same strike, reports that a Hamas official was killed in his apartment along with his wife and children.

Background: The strikes come as the Board of Peace says Hamas and other Palestinian factions have agreed to a Gaza disarmament roadmap. The board is demanding that Hamas disarm without delay and that Israel allow materials into Gaza. Humanitarian conditions remain severe, and Gaza's health ministry has issued a meningitis epidemic alert.

Why it matters: Killing a senior Hamas security official just as a disarmament roadmap is announced tests whether the post-war framework can hold. It also raises the risk of a return to wider fighting.

Different views: Al Jazeera puts the civilian toll first (the official's wife and children), frames the strike as part of escalating Israeli attacks, and sources it to Gaza's Interior Ministry. The BBC names the official and his role, and gives the day's death toll as reported figures. The Jerusalem Post's Gaza coverage that day centres on Hamas's duty to disarm and on COGAT's work with the UN on winter aid. The Guardian highlights the meningitis outbreak, citing Gaza's health ministry.
