### 1. [US expands Iran attacks as Trump warns Tehran and Houthis over Red Sea strikes](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/24/us-expands-iran-attacks-as-trump-warns-tehran-and-houthis-over-red-sea-strikes)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US expanded its air campaign against Iran, striking targets as far north as the Caspian Sea, while Trump threatened "major military punishment" for Tehran and its Houthi allies over attacks at the entrance to the Red Sea. Iran's Revolutionary Guards retaliated with drone and missile attacks on US military assets and infrastructure in Bahrain, Jordan and Kuwait, including a data centre in Bahrain, and halted three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. By Saturday there were no new US strikes reported for the first time in about two weeks, with both Washington and Tehran confirming talks are underway even as Iran rejected an Iraqi-brokered ceasefire proposal.

**Background:** The war follows earlier US and Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear and missile sites, and has since widened from a bilateral exchange into a regional conflict drawing in Gulf states hosting American bases. Control of the Strait of Hormuz — through which a large share of global oil moves — has become the central sticking point in ceasefire diplomacy.

**Why it matters:** Iranian strikes on Bahrain, Jordan and Kuwait mark the effective regionalisation of the war and put Gulf states that host US forces directly in the firing line. Any sustained closure of Hormuz would deliver a global economic shock, making the reported pause and back-channel talks the most consequential variable in the region right now.

**Different views:** The Guardian frames the escalation as a spiral with "no end to fighting in sight" and stresses Iran's growing accuracy and stretched Gulf defences; the Jerusalem Post emphasises Iranian intransigence (rejecting a ceasefire, rebuilding missile bases faster than expected) and IDF readiness for renewed fighting; Al Jazeera leads with Iran blaming Washington for the Hormuz dispute and foregrounds the diplomatic track and the pause in US strikes.

### 3. [Houthi attacks raise fears of wider Middle East conflict and more global economic damage](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvgve0vxy1yo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Houthi attacks have widened, with the Iran-aligned group threatening to target Saudi vessels in a vital shipping route, raising fears of a broader Middle East conflict and further global economic damage. Saudi Arabia struck Houthi military sites in the port city of Hodeidah — with Houthi-affiliated media saying state telecoms facilities were hit — and a Greek air-defence system deployed in Saudi Arabia intercepted missiles fired from Yemen. Hormuz tanker crossings have fallen to their lowest in more than two months as shipping reroutes.

**Background:** The Houthis fought a years-long war against a Saudi-led coalition that had largely quieted under a fragile truce, and have attacked Red Sea shipping since 2023. Their re-entry into a regional war alongside Iran now pulls Riyadh back into direct hostilities.

**Why it matters:** With both the Red Sea and Hormuz chokepoints under threat, the conflict is now squeezing global energy and container traffic simultaneously, and a Houthi-Saudi exchange could unravel the Yemen truce and drag the Gulf's largest economy fully into the war.

**Different views:** The BBC frames the Houthi threat primarily through the lens of global economic risk and the danger of a wider war; the Jerusalem Post reports the same front operationally — Saudi strikes on Hodeidah, intercepts over Saudi territory, shipping data — casting Riyadh as retaliating against an Iranian proxy; Houthi-aligned media quoted in that coverage stress civilian telecoms infrastructure being hit, while Al Jazeera's Yemen coverage focuses on the civilian toll of the country's front lines.
