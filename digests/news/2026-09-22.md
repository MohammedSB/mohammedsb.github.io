## Top Stories

### 1. [Houthis move to seize strategic terrain after Trump calls off airstrikes at last minute](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/21/houthis-seize-strategic-terrain-yemen-trump-calls-off-airstrikes-last-minute)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Houthi forces are advancing to seize strategic high ground in Yemen that would sever the Red Sea coast from territory held by Saudi-backed government forces, after Donald Trump reportedly aborted planned US airstrikes on the group at the last minute — said to be while munitions were already being loaded onto aircraft. The advance follows the Houthis' effective control of the Bab al-Mandab strait, and the UN puts the toll of the renewed fighting at nearly 700 killed and thousands wounded.

**Background:** Yemen's civil war between the Houthis and the Saudi-backed government has reignited as a spillover of the US-Israel war on Iran begun in late February, with Tehran's regional allies entering the fray. Bab al-Mandab is the chokepoint linking the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and, via Suez, to European trade.

**Why it matters:** Houthi control of both the strait and the adjoining highlands would give an Iranian-aligned force a durable grip on one of the world's most important shipping chokepoints, and a US decision not to strike pushes the burden of response onto Riyadh and its European partners.

**Different views:** The Guardian and Al Jazeera place the Houthi offensive inside a causal chain running from the US-Israeli war on Iran, treating Trump's aborted strike as the story's pivot and the Saudi-led campaign as a prior source of Yemen's ruin; the Jerusalem Post foregrounds the same aborted strike as an abandonment of Riyadh, casting the Houthis mainly as one arm of an Iranian proxy network alongside Iraqi militias and Hezbollah and treating Israeli and Saudi military assessments as the authoritative read on what happens next.


### 2. [Iran ready to reopen Strait of Hormuz if US eases military pressure, lifts blockade](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-909300)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Iranian officials signalled that Tehran is prepared to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days provided Washington takes reciprocal steps to ease military pressure and lift the blockade — a marked softening from the seven onerous preconditions Iran had previously attached to lifting the closure. The shift emerged as Iranian President Pezeshkian travelled to the UN General Assembly vowing to spotlight US 'oppression', and as Foreign Minister Araghchi denounced the 'free hand' he says Washington has given Israel.

**Background:** Iran closed or restricted Hormuz — through which a large share of the world's seaborne oil passes — during the war that began with US and Israeli strikes in late February. Roughly a fifth of global oil supply normally transits the strait.

**Why it matters:** A reopening on reciprocal terms would be the first concrete de-escalation step since the war started and would ease energy prices, but it also tests whether the Trump administration is willing to trade military pressure for anything short of capitulation.

**Different views:** The Guardian presents the move as Tehran relaxing its own preconditions and slots it into a broader UNGA narrative of a fracturing international order, alongside commentary arguing the war has united Iranians rather than turning them against their government; the Jerusalem Post frames the same offer as conditional on US concessions and runs it beside coverage of Iranian recruitment of children for 'self-sacrifice' campaigns and IRGC threats to change the 'weaponry and geography' of the war, leaving the diplomatic signal looking less like a concession than a tactical pause.


### 3. [UK to give support to Saudi jets in attempt to counter Houthi fighters](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/sep/21/uk-to-give-support-to-saudi-jets-in-attempt-to-counter-houthi-fighters)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The UK agreed to provide air-to-air refuelling for Saudi jets conducting what it calls 'defensive' operations against Houthi forces, following a direct Saudi request. Prime Minister Andy Burnham tied the decision to restoring Middle East stability and driving down domestic cost-of-living pressures, announcing it ahead of his first meeting with Trump. Separately, the G7 demanded Iran stop arming the Houthis and condemned Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia as a threat to global energy security.

**Background:** Britain has been one of Saudi Arabia's largest arms suppliers for decades, and UK support for the previous Saudi-led Yemen campaign drew sustained legal and parliamentary challenge over civilian casualties.

**Why it matters:** With the US declining to strike, a European power is stepping directly into the Yemen conflict's logistics chain — an escalation of Western involvement that arrives without the political debate that accompanied the last round.

**Different views:** The Guardian leads on the domestic political framing — a new prime minister justifying military support by reference to household energy bills before meeting Trump — while the BBC presents it primarily as a defensive response to a Saudi request, and the Jerusalem Post foregrounds the long-standing UK-Saudi arms relationship and situates the refuelling within an argument that Riyadh's existing alliances are proving insufficient. None of the three dwells on the civilian-casualty record that shaped the previous UK debate over Yemen.
