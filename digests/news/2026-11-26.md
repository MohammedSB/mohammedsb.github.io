### 1. [Hamas political official and wife among eight Palestinians killed in Israeli strikes on Gaza](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqvg2n4mzl1jo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Israeli strikes on Gaza killed eight Palestinians, including a Hamas political official and his wife. Al Jazeera's report of eight people killed (index 19) names the senior figure as Khaled Abu Nada, undersecretary of Gaza's Education Ministry, and says Netanyahu confirmed his killing. The two reports appear to describe the same strikes. In a separate strike (index 29), Mohammed Abu al-Ouf, head of Hamas's internal security service in northern Gaza, was killed with his wife and two teenage daughters, according to doctors.

**Background:** The BBC describes these killings as the latest in a series of near-daily Israeli attacks on what Israel says are Hamas targets.

**Why it matters:** Israel is still regularly killing senior Gaza figures and their families. Whether those figures count as militants or as civil administrators shapes how the strikes are judged.

**Different views:** The BBC calls the main victim a 'Hamas political official' and attributes the targeting to Israel's own account ('what it says are Hamas targets'). Al Jazeera calls him a 'senior education official' and gives his civil ministry title, and it cites Netanyahu's confirmation as evidence of a deliberate killing. The Jerusalem Post carried no report of the strikes in this batch.


### 2. [Saudi Arabia launches airstrikes against Houthis in Yemen as UK steps up military support](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-916066)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Saudi Arabia launched airstrikes against the Houthis in Yemen. It also said it had intercepted several drones over its Eastern Province, including four at Ras Tanura, an important centre of the kingdom's oil industry. Britain is stepping up military support for the Saudi side and has sanctioned the Houthis and their leaders.

**Background:** The Guardian reports that Houthi forces are encircling Aden, the temporary capital of Yemen's Saudi-backed government, and that residents and NGOs are preparing to evacuate.

**Why it matters:** If Aden fell, the internationally recognised government would lose its last major stronghold. Houthi drones reaching Saudi oil sites also draw Riyadh and London deeper into the conflict.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post (55) focuses on state military action: Saudi strikes, the drone threat to oil infrastructure, and UK support. The Guardian (41) tells the story through displaced Yemenis and aid groups in Aden and calls the Houthis 'Iran's proxy fighters'. Al Jazeera (5) frames the UK sanctions as 'symbolic' and asks what they will actually achieve.


### 3. [Iran warns vessels to coordinate passage amid claims of ships hitting mines in Hormuz](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-916095)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Iran told vessels to coordinate their passage through the Strait of Hormuz, amid claims that ships have hit mines there. Tehran has told Washington that normal shipping will not resume until its demands are met, including the lifting of a US naval blockade of Iranian ports.

**Background:** This comes during an active US war effort against Iran. In a separate interview (index 59), Trump said the war is funded until roughly the end of January, called Israel's goal of toppling the Iranian regime 'probably doable' but not his main objective, and acknowledged that the new Congress could limit his options.

**Why it matters:** Hormuz is the world's most important oil and gas chokepoint. Iran is using control of shipping there as leverage against the US blockade, which raises the economic stakes of the war well beyond the region.

**Different views:** Only the Jerusalem Post covered the Hormuz standoff or Trump's remarks on Iran in this batch. The mine strikes are reported as claims rather than confirmed facts, and the story is told mainly through Tehran's conditions and Washington's blockade.
