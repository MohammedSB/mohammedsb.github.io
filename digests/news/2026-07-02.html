1. US-Iran talks in Doha: What were the outcomes and what’s next?

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: US and Iranian negotiators held technical talks in Doha, mediated by Qatar, aimed at an interim deal building on a recently signed US-Iran memorandum of understanding, including monitoring mechanisms. Both sides signaled the process is continuing, with the next round set to follow funeral processions for Iran's late supreme leader, and Trump reportedly willing to extend past the August 18 deadline.

Background: The negotiations follow earlier Israeli and US strikes on Iran and a fragile ceasefire, with Witkoff and Kushner laying groundwork while VP Vance pushes for durable Iranian commitments.

Why it matters: The outcome will determine whether the region avoids a return to open war between Iran, Israel and the US, and shapes the future of Iran's nuclear program.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames the Doha talks neutrally as diplomacy with concrete outcomes, while Jerusalem Post coverage emphasizes American skepticism and leverage — Vance demanding guarantees, Huckabee insisting Washington won't accept a merely 'pragmatic' Iran, and reports that Trump weighed renewed war.

3. Syria forms first post-Assad parliament to kickstart new era of 'managed democracy'

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: Syria formed its first post-Assad parliament, with President Sharaa naming the final 70 appointed lawmakers to sit alongside 140 elected seats in a 210-member chamber that will hold limited power under a presidential system. Only fifteen women were included, and the body is being described as 'managed democracy.'

Background: The assembly follows the fall of Bashar al-Assad and Sharaa's consolidation of power, with two-thirds of seats chosen by regional electoral colleges.

Why it matters: It marks a pivotal institutional step in Syria's transition, but its constrained authority raises questions about how genuine the country's democratization will be.

Different views: BBC highlights the low representation of women and the electoral college mechanics as a mainstream institutional story, while Jerusalem Post frames it skeptically as 'managed democracy' and cites polling showing low Syrian confidence and demands for Damascus-Sweida talks.
