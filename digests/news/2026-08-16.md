## Top Stories

### 1. [Eleven killed in Israeli strikes on southern Lebanon, authorities say](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c4gjygx4gjeo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Two Israeli airstrikes hit homes in the southern Lebanese villages of Ansar and Deir al-Zahrani, killing 11 people — including three children — and wounding around 19, the deadliest day in Lebanon since the June truce. Israel's military said it struck 'Hezbollah terror infrastructure' in retaliation for an explosive drone attack that seriously wounded three of its soldiers, and reportedly carried out further strikes early Sunday. Hezbollah said the strikes would be 'met accordingly.'

**Background:** A US-brokered lull that followed the 2024 Israel-Hezbollah war was extended in June, but both sides have accused each other of violations and Israel has continued near-daily strikes on what it calls Hezbollah rearmament efforts.

**Why it matters:** This is the sharpest test yet of the truce and risks reopening a second front while the US-Iran war is still running, with Lebanon's government caught between demands to disarm Hezbollah and an escalating Israeli air campaign.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post leads with the three wounded Israeli soldiers and frames the deaths as a response to Hezbollah ceasefire violations, while Al Jazeera asks why Israel is escalating 'despite the ceasefire' and the Guardian foregrounds the civilian toll and dead children; the BBC attributes both claims without endorsing either.


### 2. [UAE says Iran attacked ADNOC vessel in Hormuz, urges Iran to halt attacks, reopen waterways](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-905582)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** The UAE accused Iran of attacking an ADNOC-operated vessel in the Strait of Hormuz — the third such attack in under a week — and publicly called on Tehran to halt what it called unprovoked strikes, end hostilities and fully reopen the waterway. The attacks come as Yemen's Mocha port also closed under Houthi missile fire and as US naval logistics in the Gulf reportedly strain under Iranian strikes on Bahrain-based facilities.

**Background:** The Strait has been effectively disrupted since the start of the US-Iran war, with Iran targeting shipping and Washington escalating rhetorically; Trump has floated the idea of making Hormuz US territory.

**Why it matters:** Abu Dhabi naming Iran directly marks Gulf Arab states shifting from quiet hedging to open confrontation, and a prolonged Hormuz closure reroutes global energy flows and pulls the Gulf monarchies deeper into the war.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post relays the UAE's 'unprovoked attacks' framing and pairs it with Iranian bounties on US soldiers and sanctions-busting oil sales to China; Al Jazeera treats Trump's Hormuz threats as outlandish brinkmanship, centres the war's devastating effect on ordinary Iranians' cost of living, and looks past the crisis to how a 'post-Hormuz era' could benefit African shipping routes.


### 3. [‘Spearhead of annexation’: Israel hands West Bank rule to civilian police](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/16/spearhead-of-annexation-israel-hands-west-bank-rule-to-civilian-police?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian*

**What happened:** Israel has transferred policing authority in the occupied West Bank from the military to civilian police forces, a step critics describe as the 'spearhead of annexation' that also empowers armed settler militias. It lands in the same week that settlers besieged the village of Qusra for seven days, encircling homes and cutting off supplies until the area was declared a closed military zone.

**Background:** The West Bank has been governed under Israeli military law since 1967, a legal distinction that has underpinned the territory's status as occupied rather than annexed; settler violence and land seizures have accelerated sharply in the past two years.

**Why it matters:** Replacing military administration with civilian Israeli policing erodes the last formal marker of occupation and hardens de facto annexation, undercutting the Palestinian Authority and the post-war governance track being negotiated for Gaza.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera presents the move as an annexation milestone inseparable from settler violence at Qusra; Israeli coverage largely passes over it, instead framing Palestinian politics through polling that shows collapsing support for Hamas and the PA and through Hamas's negotiating posture in Cairo.
