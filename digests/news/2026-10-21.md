### 1. [UK to deploy jets to defend Saudi Arabia from Houthi attacks](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckr5yqe1r1llo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** The UK announced it will deploy RAF Typhoon fighters, an A400M transport aircraft and military advisers to fly defensive missions over Saudi cities and critical infrastructure as Houthi attacks on the kingdom escalate. The move expands assistance first announced in September and comes alongside reports that Turkey will seek a parliamentary mandate to send military personnel to Saudi Arabia.

**Background:** Fighting between the Saudi-backed Yemeni government and the Houthis has intensified since September, with the Houthis striking Yemeni government camps and Saudi territory, and Iran offering to mediate while warning against Egyptian intervention.

**Why it matters:** Western and regional allies are now directly intervening in the Yemen war's new phase, raising the risk of a wider Red Sea and Gulf conflict that overlaps with the unresolved US-Iran confrontation.

**Different views:** The BBC and Jerusalem Post report the deployment largely as a defensive technical announcement, while the Guardian frames it as 'the most significant' escalation of Western intervention in a widening conflict; Al Jazeera foregrounds the century-long British-Saudi military bond, treating the deployment as continuity of a colonial-era relationship rather than a response to Houthi attacks.

*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

Meanwhile US-Iran talks have stalled over Iran's enriched uranium, and Pakistan's army chief arrived in Tehran to push mediation.

**Background:** A US-Israeli war with Iran earlier this year ended in a fragile diplomatic process; Trump reportedly blocked an Israeli plan for multi-day strikes on October 10 to avoid turbulence before the US midterms, and the Strait of Hormuz remains partly disrupted.

**Why it matters:** A resumption of strikes would reopen a major regional war, with Israel's October 27 election and the US midterms shaping timing on both sides.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera presents the enriched uranium dispute as a mutual sticking point in a diplomatic push, whereas the Jerusalem Post quotes officials saying Iran is deliberately prolonging talks to punish Trump and foregrounds Netanyahu's regime-change rhetoric and Israeli strike planning; the Guardian's Hormuz feature instead centres on ordinary Iranians living under US-Israeli strikes, a perspective absent from the Israeli coverage.


### 3. [Israel's far right find fertile ground among first-time voters](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmvg632xgndeo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Israel's October 27 election is a week away, with more than half a million first-time voters set to cast ballots and polling showing the far right gaining ground among young people. Netanyahu's own polling is poor, but the fractured opposition has yet to show it can form a government.

**Background:** The election follows three years of war in Gaza, Lebanon and with Iran, and Netanyahu is campaigning on confronting Iran, including the renewed strike threats reported this week.

**Why it matters:** The outcome will determine whether Israel escalates against Iran and Hezbollah or shifts toward negotiations, and will shape Trump's push for Israel-Saudi normalisation.

**Different views:** The BBC foregrounds the demographic pull of the far right among young voters as a neutral trend, while the Guardian's opinion coverage treats a Netanyahu defeat as a prerequisite for democratic repair and Palestinian rights; the Jerusalem Post does not cover the race directly, instead framing the election as a complicating factor for US-Saudi diplomacy and Iran strike timing.
