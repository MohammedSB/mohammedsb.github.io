### 1. [At least 20 killed after Israeli-hit building in Gaza City collapses](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/16/building-gaza-city-damaged-israeli-strikes-collapses-killed)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** A multi-storey residential building in Gaza City that had been damaged by an Israeli strike collapsed overnight, killing at least 20-21 people, including eight children, according to Gaza's civil defence. Around 100 displaced Palestinian families — roughly 10 families in the building itself — had been sheltering there, and rescuers say dozens remain trapped, with the search hampered by a lack of heavy machinery. The UN human rights chief responded by accusing Israel of war crimes over remains found under Gaza rubble.

**Background:** Gaza's housing stock has been extensively destroyed, pushing displaced families into structurally unsafe buildings already hit in earlier strikes, while equipment for rescue and rubble clearance remains scarce under restrictions on entry of heavy machinery.

**Why it matters:** It illustrates how the war's destruction keeps killing civilians long after the strikes themselves, and it is feeding directly into international legal accusations against Israel over strikes on residential buildings.

**Different views:** The Qatari and UK left-leaning outlets present the collapse as a direct consequence of an Israeli strike, foregrounding child deaths, the 100 trapped and the absence of rescue equipment; the Israeli outlet frames the toll and the presence of families as a claim attributed to Gaza's civil defence director and labels it a 'report', while separately covering the UN rights chief's war-crimes accusation as an allegation he 'claimed'. The UK public broadcaster splits the difference — attributing the toll to rescuers while stating as fact that the building had been hit by an Israeli strike last year.


### 2. [Houthis threaten more Saudi strikes as UN warns of humanitarian crisis in Yemen](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/15/houthis-threaten-saudi-arabia-strikes-un-warning-humanitarian-crisis-yemen)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Fighting in Yemen escalated sharply, with Saudi Arabia saying it shot down a Houthi drone south of Mecca and warning of a 'red line', while the Houthis denied targeting the holy city and claimed attacks on a Saudi airbase at Khamis Mushait, Aramco facilities at Yanbu, and the downing of a Saudi F-15 over Marib. The Houthis also seized the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands near the Bab al-Mandab strait, and the UN reported over 100,000 Yemenis displaced, with Taiz camps overflowing.

**Background:** The Iran-aligned Houthis have renewed a full-scale offensive against the Saudi-backed Yemeni government, reviving cross-border strikes on the kingdom that had largely paused during years of truce talks.

**Why it matters:** Houthi control of the Bab al-Mandab chokepoint plus strikes on Saudi energy infrastructure threatens global oil shipping at a moment when prices are already above $100 a barrel, and risks pulling Saudi Arabia fully back into a regional war.

**Different views:** The Israeli outlet centres the threat to shipping and Saudi energy infrastructure, relays Houthi military claims the Saudis have not confirmed, and consistently labels the group a 'terror group' while emphasising Iranian sponsorship; the UK left-leaning and public broadcasters foreground the humanitarian toll and displacement figures and give prominent placement to the Houthi denial that Mecca was targeted. The Qatari outlet covers the same escalation almost entirely through the lens of displaced civilians in Taiz rather than the military or oil-market dimension.


### 3. [US House votes for third time to end Iran war after $38bn cost is revealed](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/15/iran-war-cost-cbo-report)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The US House voted for a third time to end the war with Iran under a war powers resolution, hours after a Congressional Budget Office report put the conflict's cost at $38bn and rising by $3bn a month, with defensive missile stockpiles potentially taking five years to rebuild. A Pentagon inspector separately confirmed munitions shortfalls and resupply bottlenecks, contradicting President Trump's claim that supplies are 'virtually limitless'. Meanwhile a Panama-flagged tanker was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz with two sailors missing, Iran claimed to have downed a third US drone, and Turkey called on both Iran and the US to halt attacks in the Gulf.

**Background:** The US-Israel war on Iran has spilled into the Gulf's shipping lanes, with the Strait of Hormuz repeatedly disrupted and regional states pressing for a return to the pre-war status quo.

**Why it matters:** Domestic legislative pressure plus documented munitions depletion suggests real constraints on how long Washington can sustain the campaign, while attacks on shipping keep pulling in third countries.

**Different views:** On the tanker attack, Iran's account is that the vessel struck mines while the US says it was hit by a missile and then a drone — the UK public broadcaster presents both claims side by side, the Israeli outlet reports the incident largely through the rescue operation without adjudicating cause, and the Qatari outlet's related coverage foregrounds China's statement that the war exposes flaws in the region's US-led security architecture. The cost and munitions-shortfall story is carried prominently by the UK outlets but is largely absent from the Israeli coverage, which instead emphasises Iran's unsafeguarded nuclear material and regional military coordination.
