1. Iran refutes Trump’s claim to ‘control’ Hormuz: What’s the latest in talks?

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: Iran publicly rejected Donald Trump's claim that the US now 'controls' the Strait of Hormuz, saying the waterway stays closed until Washington meets its demands and changes its 'behaviour', as global energy prices climb. In parallel, a senior Iranian official said Tehran and Washington are not currently discussing an extension of the 60-day ceasefire window, even as Pakistani officials briefed that the two sides are nearing 'some sort' of arrangement, and CENTCOM struck a Panamanian-flagged vessel in the Gulf of Oman for allegedly breaching the US blockade on Iran.

Background: The closure follows the US-Iran war and the strikes on Iranian nuclear sites including Fordow; the ceasefire deal set an extendable 60-day period for reaching a final agreement limiting Iran's nuclear programme in exchange for sanctions relief.

Why it matters: Hormuz carries a large share of the world's seaborne oil, so a prolonged closure plus tit-for-tat maritime enforcement risks both a global energy shock and an accidental return to open war before any nuclear deal is signed.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames the standoff as Iranian leverage against US overreach — Tehran keeping the strait shut until Washington changes its conduct — while the Jerusalem Post frames it as a US 'blockade' successfully choking Iran's economy, quoting Iranian voices calling the blockade 'worse than war' and costing Iran $18bn, and casts the naval interdictions as legitimate enforcement rather than escalation.

2. Syrian court sentences Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia for war crimes

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: A Syrian court sentenced ousted president Bashar al-Assad and his brother Maher to death in absentia for war crimes and crimes against humanity, including premeditated murder of civilians and children, torture and arbitrary detention during the 14-year civil war. Both men fled to Moscow after Assad's overthrow and remain beyond the reach of Syrian authorities.

Background: Roughly 500,000 people died in the civil war that began in 2011; Assad was toppled by a rebel offensive and the successor government has been building legal cases against the former leadership.

Why it matters: It is the first formal capital verdict against Syria's former ruler and a milestone for the new government's transitional-justice claims, though enforcement depends entirely on Russia ever surrendering him.

Different views: Coverage largely converges on the facts; the Guardian and BBC foreground the civil-war death toll and the symbolism of an in-absentia verdict, while the Jerusalem Post emphasises the specific charges of murder and torture and reads the ruling alongside Damascus's new cooperation with Washington and the IAEA.

3. Houthis reportedly kill six in first deadly attack since start of Iran war

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: Houthi forces struck a cargo ship in the Red Sea near Bab el-Mandeb, killing at least six crew members — reported as four Pakistanis and one Indonesian in Israeli coverage — in their first deadly maritime attack since the start of the Iran war. The group said it targeted a vessel carrying Saudi military equipment; UKMTO confirmed a ship was hit by an unidentified projectile off Al Mokha, Yemen.

Background: The Houthis have attacked shipping intermittently since 2023 and paused during earlier lulls; the resumption comes with Hormuz already closed and US-Iran tensions unresolved.

Why it matters: With both Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb now under threat, two of the world's critical maritime chokepoints are simultaneously compromised, raising shipping and insurance costs and pulling Saudi Arabia closer to a conflict it has tried to avoid.

Different views: The BBC labels the Houthis 'Iran's proxy' and centres the attack on wider US-Iran war tensions, while the Jerusalem Post leads with the dead foreign crew and treats it as evidence of Iranian-directed escalation; Al Jazeera did not cover the strike in this window, and the Houthis' own stated justification — Saudi military cargo — appears only as a claim relayed second-hand.
