### 1. [Twelve killed and 300 injured in attack on airport in Saudi capital Riyadh](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw33x4y8k82no?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** A missile attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh on Saturday killed 12 people and injured more than 300, the deadliest strike inside Saudi Arabia since 2015. The Saudi-led coalition and US officials blame Yemen's Houthis, who have warned airlines and travellers against using Saudi airports and said 'worse is yet to come' if Saudi strikes continue. Saudi Arabia has vowed a response, the UAE has declared solidarity, and Donald Trump says he is weighing whether the US will join Saudi strikes on the group.

**Background:** The attack follows a Yemeni government campaign, backed by Saudi airpower, to retake Sanaa from the Houthis, which has brought intense bombing of the capital. It lands against the backdrop of the wider US-Israel war with Iran, of which the Houthis are a key ally.

**Why it matters:** The strike re-exports Yemen's war into the Gulf's economic core, threatens civil aviation across Saudi airspace, and could pull the US directly into a new front just as Washington was trying to park the Iran and Yemen conflicts.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera foregrounds Riyadh's 'war crime' language but pairs it with reporting on civilians under Saudi-backed bombardment in Sanaa, presenting the strike as an escalation within an expanding Yemeni war; the BBC sticks to casualty figures and notes the Houthis had not yet claimed it. The Guardian treats the story mainly as a problem for Trump's strategy and the prospect of US participation in Saudi strikes, while the Jerusalem Post frames the Houthis explicitly as Iran's proxies mirroring Tehran's tactics against US bases, and foregrounds their threats to airlines and claim that Saudi interceptors failed.


### 2. [US bombs cargo vessel in Gulf of Oman ‘trying to run Iran blockade’](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/10/11/us-bombs-cargo-vessel-in-gulf-of-oman-trying-to-run-iran-blockade?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** A US fighter jet bombed a commercial cargo vessel in the Gulf of Oman that CENTCOM says was attempting to run the US blockade of Iran. Separately, Iran's IRGC said an oil tanker exploded and caught fire after striking a naval mine while transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Western intelligence assessments reported by the Jerusalem Post say Iran has preserved key missile and drone factories despite months of US-Israeli strikes.

**Background:** The US and Israel have been at war with Iran for months, with the US enforcing a naval blockade; the Pentagon has also just revised upward its count of US service members killed in the conflict.

**Why it matters:** Strikes on merchant shipping and mines in Hormuz show the blockade is tightening into a direct threat to global energy flows, while evidence that Iran can still rebuild its arsenal undercuts claims the campaign has achieved its military aims.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera presents the vessel strike through CENTCOM's claim in quotation marks and separately charts the war's hit to global energy supply, treating the civilian and economic cost as the central story; the Jerusalem Post reports the strike as a precision action against a blockade-runner and adds the IRGC's mine account, while running a separate piece on Iran's intact missile production and a denied report that Washington pushed Israel to strike Iran alone before the US midterms.


### 3. [Palestinian Authority President Abbas postpones November legislative elections until late 2027](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-911222)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Palestinian Authority President Mahmoud Abbas on Saturday postponed Palestinian Legislative Council elections scheduled for November 28 until September 2027, citing Israeli military attacks, settlement expansion, settler violence, movement restrictions and withheld revenues. Palestinian critics accused him of disregarding the hopes of millions, while analysts argue Hamas quietly welcomes the delay as time to rebuild legitimacy and keep influence in Gaza through an allied list.

**Background:** The vote was tied to the governance track of Trump's Gaza peace plan, which marks its one-year anniversary this week with hostages returned and aid up but Hamas disarmament unfinished and Israel's own elections looming.

**Why it matters:** Another indefinite delay deepens the PA's legitimacy crisis and leaves the 'day after' in Gaza without a credible Palestinian political process, strengthening Hamas's hand on the ground where a former Shin Bet official says it still controls the Strip and earns up to $5 million a day.

**Different views:** Only the Jerusalem Post reports the postponement, and it does so from three angles: one piece relays the PA's stated Israeli-occupation reasons, another foregrounds Palestinian anger at Abbas, and a third casts the delay as a gift to Hamas. Al Jazeera and the Guardian instead mark the moment through Gaza's three-year anniversary, with the Guardian describing a 'genocide' and the West's lost authority and Al Jazeera questioning Biden officials' revisionism, while the Post carries Kushner's progress-focused account of the peace plan's first year.
