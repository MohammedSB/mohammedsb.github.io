### 1. [US pauses attacks on Iran for a second day as peace talks continue](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/26/us-pauses-trump-netanyahu-attacks-on-iran-talks-hormuz)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US paused airstrikes on Iran for a second consecutive night as diplomatic negotiations continued, with Qatar and Pakistan acting as mediators. Both Washington and Tehran submitted responses to a peace proposal, signaling a possible off-ramp from nearly five months of open conflict. Netanyahu is expected to visit the White House on Tuesday, adding another layer of complexity to the diplomatic picture.

**Background:** The US has been conducting strikes on Iran for roughly two weeks as part of an escalating conflict that has drawn in regional actors including the Houthis, Saudi Arabia, and Israel. The war has strained US munitions stockpiles, particularly interceptor missiles.

**Why it matters:** A second night without strikes represents the most significant pause in hostilities since the conflict began, and the active mediation effort by Qatar and Pakistan suggests a genuine diplomatic window may be opening. The outcome will shape the broader Middle East security order and the future of Iran's nuclear program.

**Different views:** The Guardian and Al Jazeera emphasize US restraint and diplomatic momentum, framing the pause as a potential de-escalation. The Jerusalem Post focuses more on Iran's conditional posture ('attack for attack'), Trump's unresolved military options, and warnings about Iran's next supreme leader being more hawkish on nuclear weapons — framing the pause as fragile rather than hopeful.


### 2. [President Ahmed al-Sharaa: Syria seeking security deal with Israel](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/26/president-ahmed-al-sharaa-syria-seeking-security-deal-with-israel?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian*

**What happened:** Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa told Al Jazeera that Damascus is actively seeking a security deal with Israel, marking a striking diplomatic overture from the post-Assad government. He simultaneously warned of potential chaos in Lebanon and stated Syria has no intention of intervening militarily there, though discussions with Beirut are ongoing.

**Background:** Syria's new government, which came to power after the fall of Bashar al-Assad, is navigating complex relationships with neighboring states including Israel, which still occupies the Golan Heights. The UN Secretary-General visited Damascus this weekend for the first time in 17 years, underscoring Syria's push for international reintegration.

**Why it matters:** A security arrangement between Syria and Israel would be a geopolitical watershed, potentially reshaping the Levant's security architecture and reducing one of Iran's key axes of regional influence. It also signals how dramatically Syrian foreign policy has pivoted under the new leadership.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera presents Sharaa's statement neutrally but notes his insistence that Syria will not relinquish its claim to the Golan Heights. The Jerusalem Post covers the same statement but pairs it with the UN chief's assertion that the Golan Heights are Syrian territory, framing that as a point of friction for Israel.


### 3. [Iran-backed Houthis claim missile attack on Saudi Arabia as regional tensions escalate](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/25/houthis-vow-retaliate-saudi-strikes-yemen-iran-war)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Yemen's Houthi rebels claimed a ballistic missile attack on the southern Saudi city of Jizan, which Saudi air defenses intercepted. The attack came after the Houthis vowed retaliation against Saudi Arabia for strikes earlier in the week, marking a significant escalation of the Houthi-Saudi front of the broader regional conflict.

**Background:** The Houthis have been a persistent regional actor throughout the Gaza war and the US-Iran conflict, attacking Red Sea shipping and Gulf state targets. Saudi Arabia had been pursuing a fragile détente with the Houthis in recent years, making this renewed direct strike on Saudi territory a notable reversal.

**Why it matters:** The Houthi attack on Saudi territory risks drawing Riyadh back into active military confrontation and complicates US-Saudi relations at a sensitive moment when a bilateral nuclear deal is being discussed. It also illustrates how the US-Iran conflict is metastasizing into multiple simultaneous regional flashpoints.
