*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US launched overnight airstrikes on Iran killing at least 14 people, and Iran retaliated by striking Gulf countries, in the most intense exchange since a shaky interim ceasefire was extended last month. ### 2. [Huge crowds gather in Mashhad for burial of Iran's supreme leader](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c5yzm8m4peeo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Huge crowds gathered in Mashhad for the burial of Iran's supreme leader Ali Khamenei at the Imam Reza shrine, Iran's holiest Shia site, capping six days of mass mourning processions that also passed through the shrines of Najaf and Karbala in Iraq.

**Background:** Khamenei's death and multi-day funeral come amid Iran's ongoing confrontation with the US and Israel and questions over succession and regime stability.

**Why it matters:** The transition at the top of Iran's leadership during an active military crisis carries major implications for the direction of Iranian policy and regional power dynamics.

**Different views:** BBC and Al Jazeera report the mass processions largely straight as a moment of national mourning, while the Guardian frames the funeral as a deliberate regime 'show of strength' and defiance, and the Jerusalem Post highlights footage of Khamenei's strike-destroyed prayer room, underscoring Iranian vulnerability.


### 3. [Iran retaliates with strikes on US Gulf assets as regional sirens sound](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-902037)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Iran retaliated against the US strikes by attacking US-linked assets across the Gulf, with Kuwait reporting it engaged a cruise missile, three ballistic missiles and 10 drones and one person injured by falling shrapnel. Sirens sounded in Bahrain, Kuwait and Qatar, and missiles were intercepted over Jordan.

**Background:** The strikes on Gulf states followed US bombing of Iranian targets, with the IRGC claiming responsibility and threatening to close the Strait of Hormuz.

**Why it matters:** Direct Iranian attacks on Gulf Arab states widen the conflict beyond a US-Iran bilateral confrontation, threatening the security and neutrality of key oil-producing US partners.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post and IRGC statements (Ghalibaf: 'if you strike, you'll get hit') frame the attacks as Iranian defiance and warning, while Al Jazeera emphasizes the spillover endangering civilians in Gulf states and Pakistan's mediation appeal, and Gulf governments like Kuwait cast their sovereignty as a 'red line.'
