## Top Stories

### 1. [Oil prices soar above $108 as Houthis strike Saudi infrastructure](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/sep/14/oil-prices-rise-drone-attacks-saudi-arabia-east-west-pipeline)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Drone attacks attributed to the Houthis forced Saudi Arabia to shut its 1,200km east-west crude pipeline running to the Red Sea, and Brent crude surged past $107-$108.5 a barrel. Satellite imagery reviewed by reporters showed damage Riyadh has not yet detailed publicly, and traders warn Saudi export stocks will run down within days if the line is not restarted, putting roughly 4-5% of global supply at risk. The pipeline outage compounds disruption in the Strait of Hormuz, where Washington says it is working to clear traffic.

**Background:** The east-west pipeline is Saudi Arabia's main workaround for Hormuz, moving crude from the Eastern Province fields to Red Sea terminals; it has been a Houthi target before, most notably in 2019. Its loss removes the kingdom's principal hedge against a Gulf chokepoint closure at the same moment Hormuz is disrupted.

**Why it matters:** Losing both the Hormuz route and its overland bypass simultaneously strips Saudi Arabia of export redundancy and turns a regional military escalation into a direct shock to global energy prices.

**Different views:** One set of reports leads with the market mechanics and the technical supply arithmetic, citing traders, satellite imagery and Saudi silence on damage; another frames the shutdown primarily as an explainer on why the pipeline matters to global oil, with less emphasis on who struck it; a third foregrounds the question of attribution and consequences, pointing at Iraqi Iran-aligned militias and Baghdad's reluctance to act rather than treating the Houthis as the sole author.


### 2. [Houthis claim major missile, drone attack on Saudi air base as rebels seize Red Sea islands](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-908510)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** The Houthis said they hit a military base in southern Saudi Arabia with drones and missiles, targeting what they described as weapons depots and command-and-control centres, and claimed the seizure of a strategic island in the Bab al-Mandab strait. They framed the strike as retaliation for more than 300 Saudi airstrikes on Yemen over five days. Yemen's Riyadh-backed government called the rebels' rapid advance along the Red Sea coast 'painful,' while claiming its own forces had advanced in western Taiz after repelling a Houthi offensive; reporting also pointed to 'ghost names' padding Yemeni army rosters as one reason resistance collapsed around Mocha.

**Background:** The Houthis have been pushing toward Marib, Yemen's oil-producing heartland, and Taiz, whose position controls access between the highlands and the Red Sea coast. Control of the Bab al-Mandab gives leverage over shipping at the southern end of the Red Sea, complementing Iranian leverage at Hormuz.

**Why it matters:** A Houthi hold on both the Bab al-Mandab and the approaches to Saudi Arabia's oil infrastructure would let a non-state actor squeeze two maritime chokepoints at once and directly pressure Riyadh's war calculus.

**Different views:** Coverage that centres Yemeni government claims leads with territorial gains in Taiz and treats the Houthi assault as repelled; coverage that centres the Houthi statement leads with the strike on Saudi soil and the island seizure, and relays the rebels' own justification of hundreds of prior Saudi airstrikes; coverage that centres Gulf and Israeli security assessments presents the advance as a deliberately sequenced Iranian pressure campaign against Gulf states, while Iran's foreign ministry is quoted insisting it does not interfere in Yemen.


### 3. [Israel accused of trying to ‘eliminate’ living conditions for Palestinians in West Bank](https://www.theguardian.com/world/ng-interactive/2026/sep/14/israel-palestinian-living-conditions-eliminated-west-bank-human-rights-report)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The Israeli human rights group B'Tselem published a major report arguing Israel is systematically working to eliminate the conditions that sustain Palestinian collective life in the occupied West Bank, detailing five mechanisms it says are used to dismantle that life. The report lands alongside mapping of more than 500 settlements and outposts housing some 750,000 settlers, and reporting of record levels of settler violence.

**Background:** West Bank settlement expansion and settler attacks have accelerated sharply since October 2023, alongside expanded Israeli military raids, land seizures and movement restrictions. B'Tselem is an Israeli organisation, which is part of why its conclusions carry weight in the argument.

**Why it matters:** It reframes West Bank policy from a series of separate incidents into an alleged deliberate structure, feeding directly into legal and diplomatic arguments over annexation and the viability of any Palestinian state.

**Different views:** One outlet reports the findings as a documented 'assault on the conditions required for Palestinians' existence,' quoting the report at length and treating B'Tselem as the authoritative account; another presents the same findings with added regional framing about eliminating Palestinian presence, paired with settlement mapping and the language of 'pogroms'; Israeli right-leaning coverage in this batch does not engage the report at all, and where Israeli government voices appear it is to attack critics — as in the culture minister's call to strip Oscar-winning Israeli documentary makers of citizenship for 'treason.'
