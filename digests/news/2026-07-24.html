1. Saudis must recognise Israel for nuclear deal, says Trump

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: President Trump announced a landmark US-Saudi civilian nuclear cooperation agreement, then declared that final approval hinges on Saudi Arabia recognizing Israel and joining the Abraham Accords. The US Department of Energy framed it as a 'peaceful' deal giving American firms broad access to the Saudi nuclear program.

Background: Riyadh has long sought US nuclear technology; Washington historically demanded strict non-enrichment and non-proliferation safeguards, which this deal appears to relax even as Trump insists Iran must never enrich uranium.

Why it matters: The deal could reshape Gulf security architecture, trigger a regional arms-race debate, and hinge Israeli-Saudi normalization on nuclear concessions — all while the US wages war on Iran over its own program.

Different views: BBC frames it as a bold move drawing bipartisan and Israeli criticism; Guardian commentators (Roth) call it a hypocritical 'sweetheart deal' handing Saudis the very options denied to Iran; Jerusalem Post shifts to Israeli strategy — Netanyahu backs normalization while analysts urge Israel to 'shape not stop' the terms to protect its security.

2. Donald Trump says decision on ‘massive attack’ against Iran is close

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Trump said he is close to deciding whether to launch a 'massive attack' on Iran 'bigger than ever before,' as US strikes on Iran entered their 13th consecutive night. Iran rejected an Iraqi-brokered ceasefire proposal and threatened US bases across the region, with reported Iranian strikes on Gulf states including Kuwait and Bahrain.

Background: The US-Israel war on Iran has been running for weeks following the reported killing of Ayatollah Khamenei, drawing in Iraq, the Gulf states, and Yemen's Houthis, with the UN chief warning the crisis is 'getting out of control.'

Why it matters: A larger US assault risks a region-wide war spanning Iraq, the Gulf, and shipping lanes, with direct threats to US bases and allied infrastructure and no diplomatic off-ramp in sight.

Different views: Guardian and Al Jazeera emphasize escalation risks, UN alarm, and the human/economic toll; Jerusalem Post foregrounds Iranian aggression, IDF readiness, Rubio's 'head for an eye' threat, and Israeli officials hinting at an imminent strike; Al Jazeera counts US strikes as an ongoing assault 'against Iran' rather than a defensive campaign.

3. Four Palestinians and two Israelis killed in West Bank shooting

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: A shooting in the northern West Bank village of Tal (near Nablus) left four Palestinians and one to two Israelis dead, with several seriously injured. Palestinians say villagers came under a settler attack that also involved the Israeli military.

Background: The incident comes amid documented acceleration of Israeli settlement expansion and settler violence in the occupied West Bank, a chronic flashpoint the EU is being pressed to confront.

Why it matters: It marks one of the deadliest single West Bank incidents recently and highlights escalating settler-Palestinian violence running in parallel to the wider regional war.

Different views: Al Jazeera and the Guardian lead with a 'settler attack' on the village allegedly abetted by the military; the BBC balances both accounts, noting the Israeli military's claim that a Palestinian stole a weapon and opened fire on hikers; casualty counts differ across outlets (one vs. two Israelis killed).
