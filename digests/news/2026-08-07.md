### 1. [Iran says a deal with Oman to reopen the strait of Hormuz is close to being finalised – as it happened](https://www.theguardian.com/world/live/2026/aug/06/iran-oman-strait-of-hormuz-yemen-houthis-israel-lebanon-gaza-us-donald-trump-middle-east-crisis-live-news)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Iran says an understanding has been reached with Oman on coordinates for a shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, with a deal to reopen the waterway close to finalisation. Trump said the strait is "sort of open right now" and that Iran "can't go much longer," while Tehran is reportedly weighing legislation to bar US and Israeli-linked vessels from transiting. Shipping industry sources say the proposed arrangement — outbound traffic routed between Iran and Oman, with Tehran retaining a right to intervene on inbound traffic — is not workable commercially.

**Background:** The strait has been disrupted since the US-Israel war on Iran began, choking a chokepoint that carries roughly a fifth of global oil. Washington has been pressing European allies to join a naval escort mission, which the UK and France have resisted.

**Why it matters:** Hormuz is the single largest source of escalation risk and global economic spillover in the current war, and whether a deal holds determines if the conflict de-escalates or widens to Gulf shipping and energy markets.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera frames Iran's chokepoint leverage as real but time-limited and casts Trump as trapped in an "escalation trap" of his own making; the Guardian presents Tehran as deliberately dragging out talks to damage Trump before the midterms; the Jerusalem Post emphasises that Trump's military leverage is nearly exhausted and that the passage deal is unworkable, implying Iran is extracting concessions it hasn't earned.


### 2. [Saudi ⁠Arabia, Pakistan and Turkiye sign defence deal amid regional turmoil](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/7/saudi-%e2%81%a0arabia-pakistan-and-turkiye-sign-defence-deal-amid-regional-turmoil?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian*

**What happened:** Saudi Arabia's crown prince, Turkey's president and Pakistan's prime minister signed a joint defence agreement in Mecca stating that an attack on any one of the three is an attack on all. The pact places three of the Muslim world's most influential states — including a nuclear-armed Pakistan — into a NATO-style mutual defence structure. It was signed as Riyadh faces intensifying Houthi missile and drone attacks and warns of coordinated strikes from Iraqi militias as well.

**Background:** Saudi Arabia has spent the period since the US-Israel war on Iran began absorbing cross-border Houthi attacks while questioning the reliability of US security guarantees. Ankara and Islamabad have both sought deeper Gulf strategic and financial ties.

**Why it matters:** A mutual-defence clause binding Riyadh to Ankara and nuclear-armed Islamabad is the most significant realignment of Sunni regional security architecture in years, and could reshape deterrence calculations for Iran, Israel and Washington alike.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and the Guardian treat the pact as a serious strategic bloc formed in response to Iranian-aligned pressure and thinning US cover; the Jerusalem Post runs counter-programming, quoting a former Israeli national security head calling Saudi Arabia a "paper tiger" that militarily "cannot get through the day," recasting the deal as a sign of Saudi weakness rather than emerging strength.


### 3. [Houthi attacks reportedly kill at least 30 Yemeni government forces](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckgdmdr0lmgo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Coordinated Houthi missile and drone strikes killed at least 30 Yemeni government forces, with the death toll expected to rise. The Houthis said they targeted "Saudi military build-ups" in the central provinces of Marib and Hadramawt. A Saudi official said Riyadh expects coordinated attacks from both the Houthis and Iraqi militias, having observed drones and missiles being moved.

**Background:** The Houthi–Saudi front had been relatively frozen under a de facto truce until the US-Israel war on Iran reactivated it. Marib and Hadramawt hold Yemen's main energy infrastructure and are the strategic prize of the wider war.

**Why it matters:** The scale and coordination of the strikes suggests the Houthis are preparing a major ground offensive, which would collapse Yemen's truce and pull Saudi Arabia directly into the Iran confrontation.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera frames the escalation analytically as Houthi expansion driven by the US-Israel war on Iran, with Saudi Arabia bracing for the consequences; the Jerusalem Post foregrounds the Iranian command-and-control angle — Houthis plus Iraqi militias acting on Tehran's behalf; the BBC reports the casualty figures and the competing Houthi and Yemeni government claims without attributing regional direction.
