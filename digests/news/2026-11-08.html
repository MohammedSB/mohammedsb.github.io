1. US carries out another round of ‘minor’ strikes on Iran, Trump says

The Guardian · UK

What happened: The US carried out a new round of limited strikes on Iran, targeting an air defence system on Qeshm Island, with Trump describing the action as 'minor.' Explosions were reported by Iranian state media at multiple locations. This follows an earlier round of strikes made public days before, as diplomatic efforts have simultaneously accelerated.

Background: The US and Israel have been engaged in an escalating military and diplomatic confrontation with Iran over its nuclear programme. Qatar has been acting as lead mediator, and the US has also launched Task Force Iron Gate to restrict Iranian weapons shipments and land commerce.

Why it matters: Continued US strikes on Iranian territory represent a dramatic escalation with potentially destabilising regional consequences, while simultaneous diplomacy — including Qatari-mediated nuclear inspection proposals and Turkish diplomatic outreach — signals the conflict is at a pivotal and unpredictable juncture.

Different views: The Guardian foregrounds the diplomatic track, noting Qatar's efforts to persuade Iran to allow UN inspectors access to bombed nuclear sites and framing the strikes as potentially undermining deal-making; it also contextualises the strikes against Trump's post-midterm political weakness. The Jerusalem Post focuses more on Iran's military vulnerabilities, Iranian defiance of a blockade, and the failure to destroy Iranian missiles as a strategic error, treating Israeli and US security concerns as the primary lens. The BBC leads with the $20m US reward for information on Iran's nuclear programme, foregrounding the intelligence-pressure dimension rather than either diplomacy or strikes.

2. Israel's president tasks opposition leader Eisenkot with forming government

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: Israeli President Isaac Herzog tasked opposition leader and former army chief Gadi Eisenkot with forming a new government, giving him 28 days to build a coalition after last month's election left neither Netanyahu's bloc nor the opposition with a clear majority. This is a significant break from Netanyahu, who had led Israel for most of the past two decades.

Background: Israel held elections after Netanyahu's coalition lost its majority. Eisenkot, leader of the centrist Yisrael Yisrael (Yashar) party, is a former IDF chief of staff seen as a stark contrast to Netanyahu in style and political alignment, particularly on issues of judicial reform and relations with the US.

Why it matters: If Eisenkot succeeds, it would end Netanyahu's long dominance of Israeli politics and could significantly shift Israel's posture on Gaza, Iran, and relations with the incoming post-Trump US political landscape. The US Democrats' midterm sweep, which weakens Netanyahu's closest international ally, adds urgency.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames Eisenkot as 'the anti-Netanyahu' but questions whether he can avoid continuing Netanyahu's legacy, emphasising risks of coalition-building and scepticism about meaningful policy change. The Guardian stresses the opportunity for opposition unity and the role of the US midterm results in motivating disparate opposition factions. The BBC and Jerusalem Post report the mandate more neutrally, focusing on the procedural and coalition arithmetic, with the Jerusalem Post emphasising the difficulty of forming a stable government from a divided opposition.

3. Floods in Gaza leave tens of thousands without shelter

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Heavy rains flooded temporary shelters across Gaza, leaving tens of thousands of displaced Palestinians without shelter. Civil Defence reported over 1,500 emergency calls, with thousands of tents destroyed. Israeli restrictions on humanitarian aid are hampering the emergency response.

Background: Gaza's population has been under sustained displacement throughout the ongoing war between Israel and Hamas. With most permanent structures damaged or destroyed and aid access severely constrained, displaced civilians are almost entirely reliant on temporary tent camps with minimal infrastructure.

Why it matters: The flooding compounds an already catastrophic humanitarian situation, with winter conditions now adding a new layer of acute danger for a population with no viable shelter options and severely limited access to relief supplies.

Different views: Al Jazeera explicitly foregrounds Israeli restrictions on aid as a cause of the inadequate response, framing the humanitarian crisis in the context of ongoing Israeli policy. The BBC reports the flooding and displacement factually without directly attributing blame for the aid shortfall. The Guardian's photo coverage similarly centres on Israeli-imposed aid restrictions as a compounding factor.
