1. Momentous decision looms after judges call for Netanyahu charges to be dropped

The Guardian · UK

What happened: The judges in Benjamin Netanyahu's corruption trial have asked prosecutors to end the proceedings. The Guardian calls this a highly unusual step. The decision now falls to Israel's attorney general. Netanyahu faces charges of bribery, fraud and breach of trust.

Background: The trial has run for years alongside Netanyahu's time as prime minister. It has been a central dividing line in Israeli politics and in arguments over judicial independence.

Why it matters: Dropping the charges after the judges stepped in could set a precedent for how Israel's courts treat a sitting leader. It would also shape Netanyahu's political future while questions about Israel's next government are still open.

Different views: The Guardian treats the judges' move as irregular. It focuses on the attorney general's decision and the risk of a damaging precedent. Al Jazeera's explainer asks whether Netanyahu could 'escape' his most serious charges, which frames the story around accountability. The Jerusalem Post has no story on the trial in this batch. Its only mention of Netanyahu is Trump avoiding a question about him and Israel's next government.

2. Hamas political official and wife among eight Palestinians killed in Israeli strikes on Gaza

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: BBC reports that Israeli strikes on Gaza killed eight Palestinians, including a Hamas political official and his wife. The Jerusalem Post, citing a report, says Israel struck the last surviving son of Hamas leader Khalil al-Hayya in Gaza City. His fate is unknown and six people were wounded. The Guardian cites Gaza's education ministry saying Israeli strikes have killed at least 16 schoolchildren since the start of November, including two sisters aged 15 and 13.

Background: Israel is carrying out near-daily strikes in Gaza that it says target Hamas. Rights groups and aid agencies describe severe humanitarian conditions, including stunting among Gaza's children.

Why it matters: The steady pace of strikes, including on senior Hamas figures and their families, shows that large-scale violence in Gaza continues. Civilian deaths keep adding to international scrutiny of Israel's conduct.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post identifies the target by his link to a Hamas leader and reports injuries without naming victims. BBC names who was killed and attributes the targeting rationale to Israel ('what it says are Hamas targets'). The Guardian puts child victims first and relies on Gaza's education ministry. Al Jazeera's related coverage leads with Amnesty's finding that Israel's attacks on women and girls aim to 'destroy life' and 'prevent births'. That is a claim of intent that the other outlets do not take up.

3. Houthi attack on Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery kills worker, halts some operations

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: A Houthi attack on Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery killed a worker and halted some operations. It followed Houthi strikes a day earlier on Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia's largest refinery and a major crude export hub. Meanwhile, Houthi media say a Saudi strike killed 11 civilians in Yemen's Taiz.

Background: Saudi Arabia and the Iran-backed Houthis are fighting directly again. This is happening alongside a wider US–Iran war in which Iran has struck Gulf states.

Why it matters: Attacks on Saudi Arabia's main oil export sites threaten global energy supply and the kingdom's image of security. Renewed Saudi strikes deepen Yemen's humanitarian crisis.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post leads with the Houthi attacks on Saudi oil sites and calls the Houthis an 'Iran-backed terrorist group'. Al Jazeera leads with Saudi strikes killing Yemeni civilians, citing Houthi media, and frames the story around Yemen's humanitarian toll. BBC reports from Riyadh, where residents say they trust the government to protect them, and focuses on whether the kingdom's image of security holds.
