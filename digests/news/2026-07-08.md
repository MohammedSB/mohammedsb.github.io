### 1. [US and Iran trade strikes around strait of Hormuz as ceasefire is violated again](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/08/iran-us-war-ceasefire-peace-agreement-strikes-strait-of-hormuz)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US and Iran traded direct military strikes around the Strait of Hormuz after three commercial vessels were hit, with US Central Command striking Iranian missile sites, air defenses and naval boats while Iran launched missiles and drones at US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain. Trump declared the June ceasefire/MoU effectively 'over,' revoked a license authorizing Iranian oil sales, and threatened further strikes.

**Background:** A ceasefire and Memorandum of Understanding signed in June had paused the US-Israel war on Iran; each side now accuses the other of violating it, with Iran insisting it will not cede control over the Strait of Hormuz shipping lane.

**Why it matters:** A renewed US-Iran shooting war centered on the world's most critical oil chokepoint threatens global energy markets and could reignite a full regional conflict just weeks after the last one ended.

**Different views:** Jerusalem Post frames the escalation as proof Iran is stalling on its nuclear/chemical weapons program and testing US and Gulf resolve; Al Jazeera foregrounds Trump 'fuming' and US aggression against a fragile truce; the Guardian and BBC present it more neutrally as a ceasefire unraveling with mutual blame.


### 2. [Tehran teemed with Khamenei mourners, but divisions – and demands for change – remain](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/07/millions-mourners-iran-regime-social-base)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Iran held a multi-day, multi-city funeral for slain former supreme leader Ali Khamenei, with his coffin processing through Tehran, Qom and on to Najaf, Iraq, drawing crowds reported in the millions. The regime cast the turnout as a show of defiance and resilience after its war with the US and Israel.

**Background:** Khamenei was killed during the recent US-Israel strikes on Iran, and his death has opened questions about succession and the regime's stability amid domestic unrest.

**Why it matters:** The scale and messaging of the funeral is a barometer of the Islamic Republic's cohesion and legitimacy at a moment of acute vulnerability and looming leadership transition.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera emphasizes the massive, reverent mourning crowds; the Guardian notes many mourners harbor demands for change and that divisions persist beneath the show of unity; the Jerusalem Post frames the event as a sign of weakness (e.g. inviting Western influencers) and juxtaposes it with the IRGC's January massacres of protesters.


### 3. [Bombs explode near Damascus hotel housing Macron on Syria visit](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/07/explosions-rock-damascus-near-hotel-where-french-president-was-staying)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Two IED explosions rocked Damascus near the hotel housing French President Emmanuel Macron during his visit, wounding at least 18 people including police officers. Macron continued his visit and meeting with Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa, pledging French help to rebuild Syria and its banking sector.

**Background:** Macron's trip was meant to bolster al-Sharaa's post-Assad government and project stability, and comes as Syria tightens security after back-to-back deadly blasts.

**Why it matters:** The attack undercuts the new Syrian leadership's effort to signal it can provide security and attract Western reconstruction support, testing its fragile international rehabilitation.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera stresses Macron's warning that Syria must not be 'destabilised'; the Jerusalem Post frames the visit primarily as evidence of Damascus's rising regional status and al-Sharaa's diplomatic momentum ahead of NATO-summit meetings; the Guardian casts it as a setback to the government's stability narrative.
