1. UK announces sanctions on West Bank settlements prompting furious Israeli response

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: The UK announced a comprehensive sanctions regime and import ban on goods from Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, with Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband accusing settlers of carrying out the 'ethnic cleansing' of Palestinians. Israel responded by shutting the UK consulate in East Jerusalem, while a domestic row erupted after the chief rabbi warned the move could endanger British Jews and senior rabbis publicly disagreed.

Background: British governments have condemned settlements as illegal for decades without imposing trade penalties; the UK move follows similar steps by France and Canada and comes amid escalating settler violence and demolition orders on communities such as Khan al Ahmar in the E1 corridor.

Why it matters: It is the strongest action against Israel ever taken by a UK government and tests whether Western allies will move from rhetoric to economic pressure; the muted US response and Australia's refusal to join the trade ban show how far the coalition extends and where it stops.

Different views: BBC leads with Israel's furious response and the consulate closure, treating the diplomatic rupture and the chief rabbi's safety warning as the core story, while the Guardian foregrounds domestic and Israeli voices endorsing the sanctions, frames them as a hard-won win for Palestine activists, and asks why they don't go further. Al Jazeera carries only an opinion piece by a barred British MP casting the episode as Israel evading scrutiny, and the Jerusalem Post does not cover the sanctions at all in this window.

2. Houthis seize key Yemeni port of Mocha in drive to take control of Red Sea coast

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Houthi forces seized the Red Sea port of Mocha, less than 50 miles from the Bab al-Mandab Strait, in the most serious fighting with the Saudi-backed Yemeni government in years. The government says it has launched a counteroffensive toward Sanaa and conducted strikes in Marib, and claims to have captured intelligence showing an IRGC plan to take over Bab al-Mandab.

Background: The Houthis have escalated attacks on Saudi Arabia and shipping as the US-Iran war widens, and Riyadh has so far avoided being drawn back into a full Yemen campaign.

Why it matters: Iranian-aligned control of both Hormuz and Bab al-Mandab would give Tehran leverage over two global shipping chokepoints, and the Houthi thrust tests Saudi restraint and the untested Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan defence pact.

Different views: The Guardian frames the seizure as 'Iran and its proxies' moving toward control of two chokepoints and a strategic disaster for Saudi Arabia; the Jerusalem Post labels the Houthis a 'terror group', relays Yemeni government claims of captured IRGC maps and a Sanaa offensive largely at face value, and asks whether Pakistan or Turkey will respond. Al Jazeera treats it as a multi-front war with both sides seeking to expand control, and its opinion coverage centres on Riyadh's dilemma rather than on Iranian design.

3. Iran referred to UN Security Council for nuclear non-compliance

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: The IAEA Board of Governors passed a resolution referring Iran to the UN Security Council for non-compliance with its non-proliferation obligations, after more than a year without inspections of key sites. Tehran condemned the resolution, saying US and Israeli strikes on its nuclear facilities have made inspector access unsafe.

Background: The move follows a June 2025 finding of non-compliance and comes as the US-Iran war continues, with analysts reporting a construction surge at Iran's deeply buried Pickaxe Mountain site.

Why it matters: A Security Council referral opens the door to snapback sanctions and international legal cover for further action, while Iran's stated justification links inspection access directly to an end to the strikes.

Different views: BBC and Al Jazeera both place Iran's rebuttal in the second sentence, with Al Jazeera's headline putting 'noncompliance' in scare quotes and stressing that the watchdog itself has been unable to inspect; the Jerusalem Post presents the resolution as a continuation of last year's finding, omits Iran's safety argument from the summary, and pairs it with reports on Iranian sanctions evasion and underground construction.
