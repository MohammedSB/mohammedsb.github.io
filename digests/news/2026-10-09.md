## Top Stories

### 1. [Three Saudis killed in Riyadh airport attack claimed by Houthis](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/09/riyadh-airport-flights-suspended-houthis-claim-attack)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Yemen's Houthis claimed missile attacks on Riyadh's King Khalid airport late on Thursday. Saudi officials said three Saudi citizens were killed and several other people were wounded. It was the deadliest attack inside the kingdom since Saudi Arabia and its allies launched a major offensive against the Houthis in Yemen. The airport has since reopened, and US and European bodies have issued travel advisories. The EU aviation safety agency, EASA, has also widened its warning against flying in Saudi airspace.

**Background:** The Iran-backed Houthis have attacked Saudi Arabia several times in recent days, mostly targeting airports. Fighting has escalated between the Houthis and the Saudi-backed Yemeni government, against the wider backdrop of the US-Iran war and Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz.

**Why it matters:** The attacks show the Houthis can now kill people in the Saudi capital, which turns the Yemen war back into a direct threat to the kingdom. Other states could be drawn in: Turkey is assessing help under a defence pact with Pakistan, and Syria is reportedly weighing military aid.

**Different views:** The BBC and Guardian treat Saudi officials as the main source for the death toll. They place the attack within the Saudi-Houthi conflict in Yemen and describe it as an escalation. Al Jazeera's headline focuses on the airport reopening and on airports being the Houthis' main target. The Jerusalem Post repeatedly calls the Houthis 'Iran-aligned' and describes the Yemen fighting as an overspill of the US-Iran war. It also runs related pieces on Syrian and Turkish involvement and on Houthi indoctrination of children against Jews, which links the story to Israel's own conflict.


### 2. [Most UK diplomats to leave East Jerusalem consulate, Israel says, as Miliband says 'vital services' to remain](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw33xd6vegnno?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Israel said most UK diplomats will leave the British consulate general in East Jerusalem. UK Energy Secretary Ed Miliband said the building will be rebranded as a 'UK mission', that 'vital services' will continue, and that only seven diplomats will stay. Both governments appear to have stepped back from a full diplomatic confrontation.

**Background:** Israel threatened in September to retaliate after Britain imposed sweeping sanctions on trade with illegal Israeli settlements in the West Bank and condemned 'ethnic cleansing' by settlers. The consulate has an unusual status: it serves Palestinians and is not accredited to Israel.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the sharpest breaks in UK-Israel relations in years. It reduces Britain's diplomatic presence for Palestinians, and it touches on the disputed status of Jerusalem.

**Different views:** The BBC headline leads with Israel's announcement and adds Miliband's reassurance as a counterpoint. The Guardian's news report leads with Miliband's account that the consulate will stay open and calls the outcome mutual de-escalation. A separate Guardian analysis says Israel's main aim was to deter sanctions and to advance its ambition to annex the whole of Jerusalem. The Jerusalem Post and Al Jazeera did not cover the story in this set.


### 3. [Zamir says return to war with Iran may postpone Israel election as Pentagon prepares plans - report](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-911125)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Israel's military chief, Eyal Zamir, reportedly said that a return to war with Iran could postpone Israel's election. Separately, the New York Times reported that the Pentagon has drawn up a plan for a three-day campaign against Iran and that three US aircraft carriers will soon be in the Middle East. Oil prices rose 5% to about $105 a barrel amid reports that the White House has asked to see options for more strikes.

**Background:** The US and Israel have already fought a war with Iran, which included Iranian strikes on US bases in Kuwait and Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. Iran now says Hormuz could reopen within a week, depending on the US response.

**Why it matters:** Renewed US strikes on Iran would affect global energy markets, the security of Gulf states and Israeli domestic politics. The Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia are already widening the regional conflict.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post bases its report on Israeli military leadership and US operational planning. Its coverage also includes Israeli intelligence exclusives, such as the account of why Israel killed Iran's navy chief and reports of Iranian plots against US bases in Germany. Al Jazeera asks whether Trump is timing a return to war around the US midterms and gives prominent space to his denial that he would strike before the vote. The Guardian covers the story mainly through oil and bond market reactions.
