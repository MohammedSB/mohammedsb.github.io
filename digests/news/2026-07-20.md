### 1. [US expands Iran offensive as diplomats claim talks ongoing](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/20/us-strikes-iran-strait-of-hormuz-oil-prices)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US expanded its aerial offensive against Iran, striking a city hosting a nuclear power plant and southern targets, after Iranian attacks killed multiple US service members in Jordan and Iraq. Oil surged past $90 a barrel as a vessel caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, while diplomats insisted back-channel talks continued.

**Background:** A Trump-brokered ceasefire tied to an Islamabad MoU has collapsed, reigniting direct US-Iran strikes now in their ninth night.

**Why it matters:** This is the most consequential escalation of the conflict, drawing in US troops, threatening global oil supply through Hormuz, and risking a full regional war.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post frames it as CENTCOM legitimately degrading Iranian capabilities threatening civilian shipping and casts Iran as the aggressor, while the Guardian and Al Jazeera emphasize ongoing diplomacy, US military deaths, and shifting domestic (Democratic) opposition to the offensive.


### 2. [Iran targets Kuwaiti infrastructure to send 'calibrated signal' to US following direct strikes](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-902961)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Iran's IRGC struck infrastructure and US-linked military sites across Gulf states — Kuwait's Camp Arifjan, Bahrain, and Jordan's Aqaba — with sirens sounding as governments condemned the attacks. Iran cast the Kuwait strikes as a 'calibrated signal' to Washington in response to direct US strikes.

**Background:** Iran has widened its targeting to US allies in the Gulf while conspicuously avoiding direct hits on major US naval assets and Israel.

**Why it matters:** Dragging neutral Gulf states into the conflict marks a dangerous new phase that could force Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan off the sidelines and internationalize the war.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post describes 'sinful Iranian aggression' and highlights angry Gulf citizens and Jordanian denials of any Aqaba threat, whereas Al Jazeera analyzes the strikes as a deliberate pressure tactic on Washington that stops short of confronting US forces or Israel directly.


### 3. [Hamas names Khalil al-Hayya as new leader after run-off with Khaled Meshaal](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/20/hamas-names-khalil-al-hayya-as-new-leader-after-run-off-with-khaled-meshaal?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian*

**What happened:** Hamas elected Khalil al-Hayya as the new head of its political bureau following a run-off against Khaled Meshaal. Al-Hayya, a veteran ceasefire negotiator and former member of the five-man leadership council, is the first to hold the top post since Yahya Sinwar was killed in October 2024.

**Background:** Hamas has cycled through leaders during the war after the deaths of Ismail Haniyeh and Sinwar, conducting a wartime internal election.

**Why it matters:** The choice of a known negotiator signals Hamas's posture toward future ceasefire and hostage talks and shapes the group's direction during the ongoing Gaza war.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera profiles al-Hayya as a veteran negotiator assuming legitimate Palestinian leadership, while the Jerusalem Post foregrounds his 'wartime negotiator' role and his succession to Sinwar, framing continuity in Hamas's militant leadership.
