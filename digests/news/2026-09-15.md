### 1. [Houthis seize strategic Red Sea islands as analysts warn of impending oil crunch](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/15/houthi-rebels-seize-red-sea-hanish-islands-saudi-oil-warning)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Houthi forces seized the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands in the Red Sea near the Bab al-Mandab chokepoint, while claiming a large missile and drone attack on a Saudi air base and striking Saudi cities, wounding at least 13 people. A drone attack also shut Saudi Arabia's east-west pipeline to the Red Sea, with satellite imagery showing damage that traders say could remove up to 4% of global oil supply if not repaired within days, pushing crude above $108 and 10-year US Treasury yields past 5%.

**Background:** The Iran-aligned Houthis have spent years contesting Yemen's Red Sea coast against the Saudi-led coalition, and the wider US-Iran confrontation has turned the Bab al-Mandab and Hormuz corridors into a single theatre. Riyadh has sought outside help, meeting the CENTCOM chief and asking Britain for assistance.

**Why it matters:** Control of the Bab al-Mandab plus damage to Saudi export infrastructure gives a non-state actor leverage over a significant share of global energy flows, transmitting a regional war directly into oil prices, bond markets and inflation worldwide.

**Different views:** Coverage foregrounds different actors: one account centres the market and supply consequences with satellite verification of pipeline damage and analyst warnings of an oil crunch, another leads on Houthi claims that the strikes answered more than 300 Saudi airstrikes in five days and on Saudi-Israeli intelligence cooperation, while a third emphasises Saudi coalition vows to respond 'firmly' and civilian impact in Saudi cities, with Houthi-run footage of an emptied Mocha airport used as evidence of battlefield gains.


### 2. [Two sailors missing after tanker attacked in Strait of Hormuz, Oman says](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3n07ydqdzlvo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** The Panama-flagged oil tanker El Gaia was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz, with two crew still missing and 23 evacuated by an Omani naval vessel. Iran said the ship caught fire after striking mines; US Central Command rejected that, saying the vessel had been hit by an Iranian missile last month and was struck again by an Iranian drone.

**Background:** Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of seaborne oil, and shipping there has become a pressure point in the US-Iran conflict. Gulf states have just postponed talks with Tehran on the strait's future.

**Why it matters:** Whether the damage was a mine or a deliberate Iranian strike determines whether this is drifting wartime debris or a direct attack on commercial shipping — and shapes the case for escalation or naval intervention.

**Different views:** One account presents the Iranian and US versions side by side as competing claims from Omani sourcing, another reports the incident largely as a maritime casualty and rescue operation centred on the missing crew, while a third leads with CENTCOM's denial of the mine explanation and treats the US attribution to an Iranian missile and drone as the established sequence of events.


### 3. [Israel accused of trying to ‘eliminate’ living conditions for Palestinians in West Bank](https://www.theguardian.com/world/ng-interactive/2026/sep/14/israel-palestinian-living-conditions-eliminated-west-bank-human-rights-report)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The Israeli human rights group B'Tselem published a major report arguing Israel is systematically working to 'eliminate' the conditions sustaining Palestinian collective life in the occupied West Bank, describing a broad assault on the requirements for Palestinians' existence there. The report landed alongside a diplomatic row over Western sanctions on Israeli settlements and a letter from more than 130 UK legal experts saying British measures fall far short of ICJ obligations.

**Background:** The ICJ has found the occupation unlawful, and settlement expansion and settler violence in the West Bank have accelerated alongside the Gaza war. B'Tselem is an Israeli organisation, which complicates attempts to dismiss its findings as external hostility.

**Why it matters:** It shifts the legal and diplomatic argument from Gaza to the West Bank and raises the pressure on European governments to convert ICJ findings into concrete sanctions rather than symbolic measures.

**Different views:** One treatment leads on the report's documentary findings and the legal experts' demand for stronger UK action; another packages it within a weekly Palestine round-up framed around a 'project of elimination' and the diplomatic crisis over settlement sanctions; Israeli-outlet coverage does not engage the report directly, instead covering adjacent items such as the UN rights chief's war crimes accusation over remains found under Gaza rubble and Palestinian Authority electoral restructuring.
