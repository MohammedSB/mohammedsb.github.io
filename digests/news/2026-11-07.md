### 1. [US carries out another round of ‘minor’ strikes on Iran, Trump says](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/07/us-carries-out-another-round-of-minor-strikes-on-iran-trump-says)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The United States carried out a second round of limited strikes on Iran, targeting an air defence system on Qeshm Island, with Trump describing the action as 'minor.' Iranian state media reported explosions in the area, and Iran separately claimed it downed a US MQ-1 drone over the island. Diplomatic efforts, led by Qatar, are accelerating in parallel with the strikes.

**Background:** The strikes follow an earlier US attack on Iran that was made public days prior, amid a broader escalation involving Houthi attacks in the Red Sea and Iranian activity in the Strait of Hormuz. Qatar has been acting as the primary mediator between Washington and Tehran.

**Why it matters:** Active US military strikes on Iranian territory represent a dramatic escalation risk in the region, with the Strait of Hormuz — through which a significant share of global oil passes — already under threat. The simultaneous military action and diplomacy signal deep uncertainty about whether the conflict will expand or be contained.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera foregrounds Qatar's mediating role and Iran's perspective on the strikes, emphasising diplomatic off-ramps and citing the Qatari PM's statement that the US remains open to a deal. The Guardian focuses on Trump's unpredictability and the threat of further, more devastating strikes, quoting his 'you'll know soon enough' remarks prominently. The Jerusalem Post centres on Iranian military responses — the downed drone, missile reports — and US/Canadian sanctions on Iran-linked firms, framing Iran as the primary aggressor. The BBC leads with satellite imagery showing construction near Iranian missile bases, adding a longer-term military build-up dimension absent from the others.


### 2. [Strait of Hormuz could reopen as part of Qatari plan for Iran nuclear inspections](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/07/strait-of-hormuz-qatar-plan-iran-nuclear-inspections)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Qatar, acting as lead mediator in the US-Iran conflict, is seeking to persuade Tehran to allow UN nuclear weapons inspectors access to bombed Iranian nuclear sites, including those storing highly enriched uranium. In exchange, the Strait of Hormuz could be reopened as part of the arrangement. Trump is still considering further strikes on Iranian nuclear sites, though the credibility of that threat has been questioned following comments by Vice President Vance.

**Background:** The Strait of Hormuz closure and US strikes on Iran follow a prolonged standoff over Iran's nuclear programme. Qatar has historically served as a back-channel between Washington and Tehran, and its PM has now publicly confirmed the US remains willing to seek a negotiated solution.

**Why it matters:** A deal allowing UN inspectors into Iran's nuclear sites would be a significant diplomatic breakthrough, potentially de-escalating a conflict that threatens global energy supplies. The reopening of Hormuz would be an immediate economic benefit affecting oil markets worldwide.

**Different views:** The Guardian presents this as a credible diplomatic pathway and treats Qatar's mediation framework with some optimism, noting the softening of US strike threats after Vance's remarks. Al Jazeera's coverage, via the Qatari PM's statement, is framed as confirmation of US willingness — lending institutional credibility to Qatar's own role. The Jerusalem Post's parallel reporting on US rewards for information on Iran's nuclear programme and new sanctions sits in tension with the diplomatic framing, implying continued US pressure rather than a pivot to negotiation.


### 3. [US midterm results may help unite Israeli coalition to unseat Netanyahu](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/07/us-midterm-results-may-help-unite-israeli-coalition-to-unseat-netanyahu)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The Democrats' sweep of the US midterm elections — in which staunch Republican support for Israel was cited as a significant liability — may help unite Israel's fractured opposition to form a coalition capable of unseating Prime Minister Netanyahu. Netanyahu's close public alignment with Trump and the Republicans has become a political vulnerability as US-Israel ties face new uncertainty.

**Background:** Netanyahu has staked much of his political identity on his relationship with Trump and the Republican Party. Israeli elections recently returned a parliament in which Arab parties made gains, creating both new coalition arithmetic and new pressure on the opposition to broaden its alliances.

**Why it matters:** A change in Israeli government could significantly alter the country's approach to Gaza, Iran, and Saudi normalisation talks. The intersection of US domestic politics and Israeli coalition dynamics is a rare and consequential alignment.

**Different views:** The Guardian frames the midterm results as an opportunity for Israel's left and centre to overcome divisions, explicitly noting that Arab party inclusion may be necessary — a framing that is sympathetic to a broader, more inclusive Israeli coalition. The BBC focuses more narrowly on Israeli anxiety about the future of the US-Israel relationship given Republican losses, treating Israeli security concerns as the central issue. The Jerusalem Post's coverage of Saudi normalisation being blocked by Netanyahu adds a parallel dimension: that Netanyahu's departure may be a prerequisite not just for domestic political change but for regional diplomacy.
