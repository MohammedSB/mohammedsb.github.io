1. Middle East crisis live: Netanyahu rejects Trump’s Gaza plan and vows no withdrawal until Hamas disarms

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu publicly rejected US President Donald Trump's 15-point "Board of Peace" plan for Gaza, saying Israel will not withdraw its forces from the territory until Hamas fully disarms. The central sticking point is sequencing: Israel insists disarmament comes first, while Hamas demands Israel halt attacks before it begins implementing the arms provisions. The rejection came despite Trump having publicly hailed the plan.

Background: The plan would have installed an international transitional governance mechanism for post-war Gaza alongside a phased Israeli drawdown. It arrives amid a broader regional crisis in which Washington is simultaneously consumed by its war with Iran and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters: An open Israeli rebuff of a signature Trump initiative signals a rare public rupture between the two governments and removes the main diplomatic track for ending the Gaza war, leaving the conflict without a negotiated off-ramp.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames it flatly as Israeli rejection of a peace framework and emphasizes Netanyahu's refusal to withdraw; the Guardian foregrounds the mutual sequencing deadlock and gives weight to Hamas's counter-condition; the Jerusalem Post's coverage largely subordinates Gaza to the Iran front, treating it as a "secondary front" rather than the main diplomatic story.

2. Iran issues tough demands to reopen strait of Hormuz as deal still out of reach

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Iran's Supreme National Security Council issued expanded conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, demanding the US "correct its behaviour" — with an IRGC commander spelling out six conditions — even as Foreign Minister Araghchi said an Oman-brokered shipping-route accord was in its "final stages." In parallel, the UAE said one of its ADNOC vessels was targeted by an Iranian missile, with ADNOC claiming 15 of its ships have been hit since the war began.

Background: The strait, which carried roughly a fifth of global oil and LNG shipments before the war, has been effectively closed for months amid the US-Israel war with Iran, stranding up to 20,000 seafarers in the Gulf.

Why it matters: Hormuz has become Iran's principal source of leverage over Washington and the global economy, so the terms of any reopening will define who is seen to have won the war and set energy prices worldwide.

Different views: Al Jazeera presents Iran's demands as a legitimate negotiating position and casts both sides as "talking past each other"; the Guardian frames the deadlock as the product of Trump's strategic miscalculation and empty threats; the Jerusalem Post channels Israeli security analysts arguing Iran will never willingly relinquish control of the strait and that Trump may settle for declaring victory with no nuclear deal.

3. Yemen’s Houthis claim responsibility for attack on Saudi oil refinery

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Yemen's Houthis claimed responsibility for a drone strike on Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery in southwestern Saudi Arabia. The Saudi Energy Ministry confirmed a fire was extinguished with no casualties but gave no cause. The attack came two days after Riyadh signed a defence pact with Turkey and Pakistan, and as Houthi forces struck Marib again with the UN warning Yemen is nearing wider conflict.

Background: The Tehran-aligned Houthis had largely refrained from striking Saudi territory since the 2022 truce; the new Turkey-Saudi-Pakistan pact was a response to regional instability generated by the US-Israel war on Iran.

Why it matters: A direct hit on Saudi energy infrastructure would widen the Iran conflict into the Gulf's largest oil producer and test whether the new defence pact functions as a real deterrent or draws its members into the war.

Different views: Al Jazeera leads with the Saudi ministry's account of an extinguished fire and treats the Houthi claim as unverified; the Guardian links the strike causally to the defence pact and Iran's Hormuz demands; the Jerusalem Post presents it as further evidence of an Iranian proxy network attacking Gulf and shipping targets.
