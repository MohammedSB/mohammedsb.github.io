### 1. [Israeli troops force families from homes amid settler terror campaign in West Bank](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/13/israeli-troops-force-families-from-homes-amid-settler-terror-campaign-in-west-bank)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Israeli troops forced two Palestinian families out of their besieged homes in the West Bank village of Qusra and took over eight additional houses, telling residents to leave and using some properties as barracks. This came a day after a senior Israeli commander had personally pledged to end the settler campaign that had cut off water, electricity and medical access to the trapped families. A former Israeli prime minister described the events as a deliberate attempt at ethnic cleansing.

**Background:** Qusra, near Nablus, has been a flashpoint for settler violence for years, and the siege escalated over roughly a week with about 15 Palestinians, including two children, confined in three homes. Settler expansion and displacement in Area C have accelerated sharply since 2023 with limited enforcement by Israeli security forces.

**Why it matters:** It marks a shift from settler harassment to state forces directly evicting families and occupying homes, a template that could be replicated across the West Bank. It also drew a rare public rebuke from the US ambassador to Israel, exposing friction inside Israel's closest alliance.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera frames it as a siege on civilians and blocked humanitarian access, the Guardian centres settler 'terror' and an Israeli ex-PM's ethnic-cleansing charge, and the BBC reports it more procedurally as troops telling families to leave and using homes as barracks; Israeli right-leaning coverage largely omits the story in favour of Palestinian Authority culpability and media-casualty disputes.


### 2. [Iran claims control of Strait of Hormuz, rejecting Trump’s claim of US total dominance](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-905395)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Iran's newly appointed Basij commander declared the Strait of Hormuz is 'under Iran's control and management,' directly rejecting President Trump's claim of 'total control' of the waterway. US Treasury Secretary Bessent simultaneously promised economic isolation 'like the world has never seen before' alongside a continued blockade stopping traffic in or out, while Defense Secretary Hegseth insisted the Navy can sustain an indefinite blockade. Shipping traffic through the strait has fallen further amid the competing claims.

**Background:** The strait carries roughly a fifth of global oil, and the current confrontation follows the Iran war that has already rerouted regional air and sea traffic. Both sides are asserting control rhetorically because neither can fully enforce it.

**Why it matters:** An open-ended blockade of Hormuz would keep global energy prices elevated and risks a direct US-Iran naval clash from a single miscalculation. It also sets a precedent for using maritime chokepoints as instruments of sanctions enforcement.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera questions the blockade's sustainability, highlighting reports of poor conditions in the US fleet, while the Jerusalem Post foregrounds Iranian bravado as hollow and treats the pressure campaign as effective coercion; both sides' official claims of 'control' are mutually exclusive and neither is independently verified.


### 3. [Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan to build joint political and military pact mechanisms](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-905411)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Turkey, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan signed the 'Mecca Joint Defense Agreement' on 7 August and are now building joint political and military mechanisms under it. The pact unites three Sunni-majority US partners responding to a regional conflagration that has brought Iranian missile fire into the Gulf. Analysts describe it as the emergence of a self-organising 'middle power' bloc.

**Background:** Riyadh, Ankara and Islamabad have historically been rivals or uneasy partners, and all three have hedged against declining confidence in US security guarantees. Pakistan is the only nuclear-armed member, which gives the arrangement an implicit deterrence dimension.

**Why it matters:** It signals a restructuring of the regional security order away from exclusive reliance on Washington, and could formalise a Sunni counterweight to Iran outside existing US-led frameworks. Any nuclear umbrella implication would be a major proliferation development.
