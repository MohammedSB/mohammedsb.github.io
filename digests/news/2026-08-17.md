## Top Stories

### 1. [Trump envoy Kushner meets Netanyahu after Hamas talks on peace plan](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c77gg5vn44yo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Jared Kushner held a rare direct meeting with Hamas political chief Khalil al-Hayya in Cairo on Sunday — alongside Tony Blair and Board of Peace head Nickolay Mladenov — covering Hamas disarmament, dismantling of tunnels and weapons infrastructure, IDF withdrawal and reconstruction. On Monday he met Benjamin Netanyahu in Jerusalem to try to secure Israeli buy-in for the same package. Israel had just rejected the Board of Peace's 15-point Gaza roadmap, insisting Hamas disarm first, and eight Arab and Islamic states issued a joint statement accusing Israel of derailing Trump's plan.

**Background:** The Gaza ceasefire has been stalled for months, with the US-backed Board of Peace framework meant to move it from a pause to a permanent settlement covering governance, disarmament and reconstruction. Washington has generally avoided direct contact with Hamas, making the Cairo meeting a notable break with precedent.

**Why it matters:** This is the closest the Trump administration has come to brokering a permanent end to the Gaza war, and it is now openly pressuring Israel rather than only Hamas. If Netanyahu holds out against the 15-point plan, the US faces a choice between forcing its ally's hand and letting its own roadmap collapse.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and the Guardian foreground Netanyahu as the obstacle — a leader "under pressure after rejecting" the plan — while the Jerusalem Post centres Hamas's refusal to disarm as the real blockage and highlights Palestinian Authority officials' unease at Washington legitimising Hamas. JPost also reports the Arab/Islamic statement blaming Israel as a claim by those governments rather than as established fact, and frames Hamas's participation through polling showing its declining popularity.


### 2. [US-Iran talks hit new impasse as MoU's 60-day deadline expires with no deal](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-905755)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** The 60-day US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding expired Monday with no agreement, after Trump had already declared the MoU "over" within a month of signing it and both sides continued trading attacks despite a nominal ceasefire. Al Arabiya reported the two had nonetheless agreed to extend the ceasefire. Trump separately confirmed a backchannel to the IRGC and threatened to "bomb the s***" out of Oman if it "gets in the way" of a deal, saying munitions used against Iran so far were "peanuts."

**Background:** The MoU followed this year's US-Iran war, which drew in Gulf states, disrupted Strait of Hormuz shipping and left an unstable ceasefire in place. Oman has historically been the main mediator between Washington and Tehran.

**Why it matters:** The expiry removes the only written framework restraining a US-Iran escalation, and threatening the chief mediator narrows the diplomatic channels that remain. Reports of Gulf frustration with Trump's approach and of Iran having prepared for a wider war suggest the region is hedging against renewed fighting.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera treats the collapse as largely Trump's doing — he unilaterally declared the deal dead weeks after signing — while the Jerusalem Post frames it as a mutual impasse and pairs it with WSJ-sourced reporting that Iran was secretly preparing a broader war, casting Tehran as the bad-faith actor. The Guardian gives most prominence to the Oman bombing threat itself as evidence of erratic US conduct; JPost reports the same quote alongside Trump's IRGC backchannel as leverage rather than recklessness.


### 3. [Iraqi Kurdish PM Barzani’s office targeted in drone attack](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/17/iraqi-kurdish-pm-barzanis-office-targeted-in-drone-attack?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian*

**What happened:** Explosive drones struck the office of Iraqi Kurdistan's prime minister and the residence of the region's protection agency chief, with no casualties reported. Three drones were intercepted over Erbil. Kurdish authorities said the attacks were launched from Iranian territory. Separately, Axios reported that Trump had used KRG President Nechirvan Barzani as a secret channel to the IRGC during May peace talks.

**Background:** Iraq has spent this year's US-Iran war trying and failing to stay neutral, while Baghdad struggles to bring Iran-aligned militias under state control. Erbil, home to US personnel and Western energy interests, has repeatedly been targeted in such attacks.

**Why it matters:** Striking the top of the Kurdish government — apparently over its role as a US-Iran conduit — signals that Iran or its proxies are willing to attack the very intermediaries brokering talks. It also exposes how little control Baghdad has over armed groups on its own territory.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera reports the attack largely as an unattributed incident, noting the targets and lack of casualties; the Jerusalem Post leads with the Kurdish claim that the drones came from Iranian soil and links it analytically to Iran-backed militias and Baghdad's weakness. JPost also ties the targeting directly to Barzani's role as Trump's secret IRGC backchannel, an angle absent from the pro-Palestinian coverage.
