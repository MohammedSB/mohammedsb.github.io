## Top Stories

### 1. [Iran targets US military base in Bahrain with drone attack](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqn4e2py48kwo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Iran launched a drone attack on a US military base in Bahrain on Sunday, its second attack on the country in three days. Bahrain, home to the US Navy's Fifth Fleet, told citizens and residents to stay calm and go to the nearest safe location. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan and Egypt all condemned the attack and expressed solidarity with Bahrain.

**Background:** The US and Iran are in a stand-off over the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian strikes have resumed since September, and the IRGC says several tankers have hit mines in the strait in recent days. The USS Theodore Roosevelt has just arrived, putting three US carrier strike groups in the region.

**Why it matters:** Repeated direct Iranian attacks on a US base in a Gulf state make the doubtful US-Iran peace talks even less likely to succeed and raise the risk that the US-Iran war resumes. They also threaten shipping through one of the world's most important energy chokepoints.

**Different views:** The Guardian presents the strikes as Iranian retaliation and asks what they mean for peace talks, while the BBC places them within the Hormuz stand-off. The Jerusalem Post's headline calls it a 'hostile escalation' by the IRGC, relies on Bahrain's interior ministry, and pairs it with reports that Iran still has up to 2,500 missiles. Al Jazeera puts less weight on the attack itself and more on the US carrier build-up and Trump's messaging, asking whether Washington is preparing to restart the war.


### 2. [Yemeni forces withdraw from Bab el-Mandeb Strait, leaving Iran-backed Houthis in control](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-914920)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces have pulled troops back from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait to defend the interim capital, Aden, from the Houthis. The Houthis are also besieging Taiz and have tightened their hold on the highlands above Aden.

**Background:** Yemen's internationally recognised government and the Houthis have been fighting for years. The Houthis have repeatedly attacked Red Sea shipping, and Bab el-Mandeb links the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden.

**Why it matters:** If the Houthis control more of the strait, they gain leverage over a major global shipping route at a time when the Gulf is already tense because of the US-Iran confrontation at Hormuz.

**Different views:** The BBC relies on the government forces' own account and describes the move as a redeployment to protect Aden. The Jerusalem Post describes it as a retreat that leaves 'Iran-backed Houthis in control' and focuses on Iran's links and the danger to shipping. An Al Jazeera opinion piece argues that naval deployments cannot secure the Red Sea and that only rebuilding the Yemeni state can.


### 3. [UK and allies urge Israel to allow rapid humanitarian access to Gaza](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cwm2mllj1pp8o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** The UK, Australia, Canada and Italy jointly urged Israel to allow rapid humanitarian access to Gaza. They said Israeli authorities are holding vital supplies at the borders, including 150,000 tents.

**Background:** Gaza's displaced population faces cold weather and flooding while living in damaged or makeshift shelter. Who controls aid deliveries into the territory remains a major point of dispute.

**Why it matters:** A joint statement from close Western allies puts diplomatic pressure on Israel over aid restrictions as winter conditions get worse for Gaza's displaced people.

**Different views:** The BBC treats the four governments' statement as authoritative and places responsibility for the shortage of shelter on Israeli border restrictions. The Jerusalem Post focuses on a different cause: citing a Palestinian Authority official through Jusoor News International, it reports that Hamas threatened staff to stop UN-backed weatherproof shelters from being built, and it does not mention Israel's border controls.
