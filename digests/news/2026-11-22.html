1. What’s behind Iran’s escalation of attacks in the Gulf?

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Iran has stepped up attacks on Gulf states and shipping. Bahrain says an Iranian strike hit a civilian industrial facility and started a fire. UKMTO reported two tankers struck within 50 minutes of each other near Khasab, while Iran said one tanker hit a mine in a restricted area. Iran is also reportedly assembling a convoy of more than 14 oil tankers to challenge the US naval blockade, and IRGC-linked media are threatening Gulf energy facilities.

Background: The US and Iran are at war, and Washington is running a naval blockade and sanctions campaign that is hurting Iran's economy. In the US, there is debate over whether Congress could cut off funding for the war, and analysts say winding it down may require easing the blockade.

Why it matters: The fighting is spreading to Gulf states' civilian infrastructure and to tanker traffic near the Strait of Hormuz, which raises the risk of a wider regional war and a shock to global energy supplies. The planned tanker convoy could lead to a direct naval confrontation with US forces.

Different views: Al Jazeera explains Iran's escalation as a response to economic pressure from the US blockade, and its analysis pieces focus on Washington's choices: easing the blockade, or Congress using its power over funding. The Jerusalem Post reports incident by incident, relying on statements from Bahrain, UKMTO and CENTCOM (for example, CENTCOM's denial that Iran damaged an F-16). It puts IRGC threats front and centre and gives little attention to the blockade's role in driving the escalation.

2. Israeli settlers attack several Palestinian villages in occupied West Bank

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Israeli settlers attacked several Palestinian villages in the Ramallah and el-Bireh areas of the occupied West Bank. They burned 12 vehicles and spray-painted graffiti on homes. The Jerusalem Post counted four separate attacks across the West Bank.

Background: Settler violence in the West Bank has risen during a period when far-right ministers Itamar Ben-Gvir and Bezalel Smotrich hold senior security and finance posts in the Israeli government.

Why it matters: Repeated settler attacks, with critics inside Israel saying the territory is close to anarchy, threaten to destabilise the West Bank further. They also add to international pressure on Israel over the occupation.

Different views: Al Jazeera describes the attackers as 'Israeli settlers' in the 'occupied' West Bank, calls the graffiti 'racist slogans', and centres the Palestinian villages that were targeted. The Jerusalem Post calls the attacks 'Jewish extremist' incidents with 'revenge graffiti', which points to retaliation as the motive. It treats the story as a dispute within Israel, quoting a Yashar chairman who blames Ben-Gvir and Smotrich, and says less about the Palestinian victims.

3. Saudi Arabia says it intercepts three Houthi missiles targeting Riyadh

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Saudi Arabia says it intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh. This came days after an earlier attack damaged the capital's tallest skyscraper.

Background: Houthi cross-border strikes on Saudi Arabia have intensified while Iran, the Houthis' main backer, is at war with the US and attacking Gulf states directly.

Why it matters: Repeated missile fire at the Saudi capital opens a second front against Gulf states during the Iran war and threatens to drag Saudi Arabia into the wider conflict.
