## Top Stories

### 1. [Child among seven killed by Israel in Gaza on ‘ceasefire’ anniversary](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/10/israeli-forces-kill-six-in-gaza-including-a-child?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Israeli strikes killed at least seven Palestinians in Gaza, including a child, on the first anniversary of the US-brokered ceasefire. Gaza's Health Ministry says at least 1,481 Palestinians have been killed in Israeli attacks since the truce began. At the same time, Jared Kushner marked the anniversary of Trump's peace plan by citing hostage returns and more aid, while saying Hamas disarmament is still unfinished.

**Background:** The ceasefire under Trump's Gaza plan, overseen by his 'Board of Peace', began a year ago, about two years into the war that started on 7 October 2023. Israel still controls a large share of the Strip, and Hamas has not disarmed.

**Why it matters:** The anniversary tests whether the US-led framework has produced a lasting settlement or only a lower-intensity conflict. Ongoing deaths, the collapse of healthcare and the unresolved disarmament question all bear on whether the truce lasts.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera puts 'ceasefire' in scare quotes and leads with Palestinian casualties, Health Ministry figures, collapsing healthcare and analysts who call the Board of Peace a failure. The Jerusalem Post leads with Kushner's account of progress, a former Shin Bet official saying Hamas controls Gaza and earns up to $5m a day, and a security official warning that Hamas sees the war as a victory, so it treats Israeli and US officials as the authoritative sources and Hamas's grip as the main obstacle.


### 2. [Three Saudi nationals killed in Houthi attacks on Riyadh airport](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmz7xe37g5wro?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Three Saudi nationals were killed and several others wounded in two missile attacks on Riyadh's King Khalid airport, which Yemen's Houthis claimed. It was the deadliest attack inside Saudi Arabia since the kingdom and its allies launched a major military offensive against the Houthis. The US and European groups issued travel advisories, and the EU aviation regulator EASA told airlines to avoid more parts of Saudi airspace.

**Background:** Fighting has escalated between the Iran-backed Houthis and Saudi Arabia, which backs Yemen's internationally recognised government and recently started a new offensive with its allies.

**Why it matters:** A deadly strike on the Saudi capital's main airport shows the Yemen war spilling back into the Gulf. It also threatens regional aviation and adds to wider Iran-linked tensions.

**Different views:** The BBC and the Guardian place the attack inside the escalating Saudi-Houthi war, and the Guardian points out it is the deadliest since Riyadh's own offensive began. The Jerusalem Post focuses on aviation-security fallout, such as travel advisories, EASA airspace restrictions and the Saudi authority saying operations are back to normal, and labels the Houthis 'Iran-aligned' without the context of the Saudi offensive.


### 3. [Zamir says return to war with Iran may postpone Israel election as Pentagon prepares plans - report](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-911125)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Israeli military chief Eyal Zamir reportedly said a return to war with Iran could delay Israel's election. The New York Times reports that three US aircraft carriers will soon be in the region and that the Pentagon has drawn up plans for a three-day campaign against Iran. A separate report says Washington pushed Israel to strike Iran on its own so the US could avoid political fallout before the midterms.

**Background:** The US and Israel have carried out months of strikes on Iran, including B-2 bombing. Western intelligence assessments say Iran has kept key missile and drone factories, while Trump says the US stopped Iran from getting a nuclear weapon and that the war will end soon.

**Why it matters:** A new round of US-Israeli war with Iran would put the whole region at risk, could change Israel's election timetable, and is tied up with US midterm politics and fuel prices.

**Different views:** Most of the coverage of military planning comes from the Jerusalem Post, which draws on Israeli and US officials, the NYT and Western intelligence, and treats Iran's remaining capabilities as the central threat. Al Jazeera's related coverage instead foregrounds Iran's response, presenting Tehran's dismissal of Rubio's 'civilisational' rhetoric as an 'anachronism' rather than covering the military planning itself.
