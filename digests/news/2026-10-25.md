### 1. [Israeli soldiers raze neighbourhood in Hebron, driving families from homes](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/24/israel-soldiers-raze-west-bank-neighbourhood-hebron-families-from-homes)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Israeli forces have razed a neighbourhood in Hebron, demolishing homes, converting others into military posts and driving out hundreds of Palestinian families after two Israeli soldiers were killed by gunmen at a checkpoint outside the city. Netanyahu vowed 'to destroy this place', and the military announced it will seal all West Bank land crossings and restrict Palestinian workers' movement through Tuesday's Israeli general election.

**Background:** Hebron is the largest West Bank city and the only one with Israeli settlers embedded in its core; it has been under escalating lockdown and settler expansion since the Gaza war began.

**Why it matters:** The scale of demolitions, described by the Hebron municipality as dozens of hectares, marks a shift from targeted raids to area-wide expulsion that threatens the West Bank's economic hub and sets a precedent on the eve of a vote that could entrench the hard right.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera frames the operation as an 'assault' and foregrounds mass expulsions, blockades and settler control, treating Palestinian and municipal accounts as authoritative. The BBC leads with the killing of the two soldiers as the trigger and the election timing, presenting the demolitions as a 'crackdown' responding to an attack. The Guardian uses the term 'collective punishment', anchors its figures in the Hebron municipality's estimate and highlights Netanyahu's own threat; the Jerusalem Post does not cover the demolitions in this set.


### 2. [Iran trying harder to target US personnel in Middle East, intelligence chief warns](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cmn8w1ddg2x9o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** US intelligence has warned that Iran is in advanced stages of planning attacks on American diplomats and officials in the Middle East, prompting the State Department to adjust staffing at regional posts; Secretary Rubio reportedly received the warning directly. Reports also say CENTCOM has drawn up strike plans on Iran for after the US midterms, while Tehran threatened to hit energy and civilian targets and escalate its nuclear programme if attacked.

**Background:** The region remains on a war footing after the earlier US-Israel confrontation with Iran, with interceptor stockpiles depleted in Israel and Gulf states and Iran running a nationwide IRGC 'self-sacrifice' mobilisation campaign.

**Why it matters:** The combination of assassination warnings, publicly reported strike plans and Iranian retaliation threats suggests both sides are preparing for a renewed round of conflict rather than a return to negotiations, with Gulf energy infrastructure and US personnel in Iraq as likely flashpoints.

**Different views:** The BBC centres the US intelligence chief's warning and the practical diplomatic staffing response. The Jerusalem Post treats the Axios and US intelligence reporting as authoritative, foregrounds Iranian officials calling diplomats 'fair game' and emphasises Tehran's threats against energy targets and interceptor shortages. Al Jazeera leads instead with Trump mocking a reporter's question about strikes, gives space to Tehran's claim that Washington derailed diplomacy, and questions what new strikes could actually achieve.


### 3. [As a divided Israel prepares to go to the polls, can Netanyahu pull off his greatest political trick yet?](https://www.theguardian.com/news/ng-interactive/2026/oct/24/divided-israel-election-benjamin-netanyahu)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Israel votes on Tuesday in a general election pitting Benjamin Netanyahu against former general Gadi Eisenkot, with Netanyahu putting rivals on the defensive despite the war's toll. The ultra-Orthodox draft dispute and both main candidates' vows to end Hamas rule dominate the campaign, while the humanitarian situation in Gaza is largely absent from debate.

**Background:** Netanyahu has dominated Israeli politics for three decades; his coalition has been strained by Haredi resistance to conscription and by the Gaza war's length and cost.

**Why it matters:** The outcome will determine the direction of the Gaza endgame, West Bank policy and the Iran confrontation, and the Guardian places it alongside the US, Brazil and Serbia votes as a test of whether the global authoritarian trend continues.

**Different views:** The Guardian foregrounds Netanyahu's political survival skills and Palestinians' fear that Gaza's suffering has become 'campaign fodder', citing a death toll near 75,000. The BBC frames the decisive issue as the domestic ultra-Orthodox draft row and whether it could bring down the government, with little on Gaza. The Jerusalem Post's election-adjacent coverage focuses on security threats from Iran, Syria and Iraq rather than the campaign itself.
