### 1. [Pilot who stabbed co-pilot on Israel-bound plane may have tried to crash it, says Netanyahu](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/30/israel-bound-passenger-plane-emergency-landing-saudi-arabia-pilots-feud)

### 2. [Pentagon says it has formally completed withdrawal of US troops from Iraq](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/30/pentagon-formal-withdrawal-troops-iraq)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The Pentagon formally completed the withdrawal of all US and UK troops from Iraq, ending a 12-year mission against the Islamic State that began in 2014. The withdrawal was conducted under a deal agreed two years ago following sustained pressure from the Iraqi government and Iran-aligned factions. Trump called it 'a great day for America' while Iran and its allies celebrated it as a victory.

**Background:** The US-led coalition entered Iraq in 2014 at Baghdad's invitation to counter the rapid territorial expansion of ISIS. Iraq's government, under pressure from Iran-backed militias and nationalist sentiment, had been demanding full sovereignty and an end to the foreign military presence for years.

**Why it matters:** The withdrawal marks a significant shift in the US military footprint in the Middle East and is widely seen as a geopolitical win for Iran, which has long sought to end Western military presence in neighboring Iraq. It also raises questions about Iraq's long-term security capacity against potential IS resurgence.

**Different views:** The Guardian leads with Trump's self-congratulatory framing but prominently notes Iran and its allies celebrated the departure as a victory, and quotes the Iraqi perspective on restored sovereignty. The Jerusalem Post runs an analysis piece framing the withdrawal with irony — noting Baghdad originally asked the US to return — and foregrounds the strategic benefit to Iran. The BBC reports it straightforwardly as the completion of a deal agreed two years ago, emphasizing it was a key Iraqi government demand. Al Jazeera's separate piece on Kurdish Peshmerga provides regional context about post-withdrawal security dynamics without directly addressing the withdrawal's geopolitical implications.

The Guardian notes the US is seven months into a war with Iran, suggesting kinetic conflict is ongoing alongside diplomacy, adding acute urgency to any diplomatic track.

**Why it matters:** The sequencing dispute is a classic and historically intractable obstacle in US-Iran nuclear talks, and Rubio's expulsion of the Iranian delegation signals the diplomatic window may be narrowing. A breakdown could accelerate Iran's nuclear program advancement, which Netanyahu separately told UAE President MBZ is already accelerating.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post covers this through multiple pieces emphasizing Israeli and hawkish US perspectives — a Trump aide says Iran will be 'resolved imminently' with no dilution of demands, and a former Israeli national security chief warns a US-Iran deal could still harm Israel. The Guardian's framing, referencing an ongoing US-Iran war and midterm political pressures, reflects a very different assumed baseline reality. Al Jazeera and the BBC do not appear to cover this specific diplomatic track in the provided articles, leaving it largely a Jerusalem Post and Guardian story with sharply divergent geopolitical assumptions underlying each outlet's framing.
