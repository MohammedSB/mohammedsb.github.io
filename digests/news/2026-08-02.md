### 1. [Trump cancels Iran strikes subject to deal being made 'rapidly'](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cjwx74qgld2o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Trump announced he had cancelled planned US military strikes on Iran, saying the "perimeters of a deal" had been agreed and that an agreement covering Iran's nuclear programme and the full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz must be reached "rapidly." He claimed Iran, Israel and other regional states had asked Washington to hold off, while simultaneously warning the US remains "locked and loaded." Iranian officials publicly denied asking for a pause, and Israel reportedly raised its alert level for a possible resumption of strikes.

**Background:** The pause follows weeks of an expanding US-Iran war that has drawn in at least five other countries, with attacks and counterattacks across Iraq, the Gulf and the Red Sea and disruption to Hormuz shipping. Saudi Arabia's crown prince is reported to have urged Trump to prioritise dialogue.

**Why it matters:** A deal would be the first off-ramp from a regional war that threatens global oil transit through Hormuz; its collapse would likely trigger the largest US strike campaign of the conflict. The gap between Trump's claims and Tehran's denials makes the ceasefire fragile and the next 72 hours decisive.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post foregrounds Rubio's regime-change rhetoric and Iranian denials as "blackmail" claims, presenting the pause as leverage won by military pressure; the Guardian frames it as a "climbdown" in a war spinning out of control, while Al Jazeera questions whether Trump's "deal" has any substance at all.


### 2. [Palestinian Islamic Jihad agrees to Board of Peace's proposed Gaza disarmament deal - report](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-904313)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Palestinian Islamic Jihad reportedly agreed to the Board of Peace's proposed Gaza disarmament framework announced by Trump, which requires complete disarmament of Hamas and all armed groups in Gaza in exchange for an IDF withdrawal. Hamas has also signalled a pledge to disarm, but verification mechanisms, the sequencing of Israeli withdrawal and post-war governance remain unresolved. An Israeli-backed militia figure inside Gaza told the Jerusalem Post he does not believe Hamas will actually disarm.

**Background:** The Board of Peace is the transitional governance body created under Trump's Gaza plan to oversee the strip after the war, operating outside the UN framework. Gaza remains devastated, with students sitting Tawjihi exams amid catastrophic conditions.

**Why it matters:** Disarmament is the linchpin of the entire post-war plan — without a credible verification and governance mechanism, the deal risks collapsing back into fighting or entrenching armed collaborator militias inside Gaza.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post treats disarmament as the central test of Palestinian good faith and gives voice to Israeli-aligned Gazan militia figures doubting Hamas; Al Jazeera argues the Board of Peace is a "failed body" that should be abandoned entirely, with the Palestinian question returned to the UN and international law rather than a US-run trusteeship.


### 3. [EU to hold urgent meeting of interior ministers on Tuesday over Ceuta crisis](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/01/spain-pedro-sanchez-calls-for-eu-meeting-ceuta-border-crossing)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** More than 50,000 Moroccans crossed into the Spanish North African enclave of Ceuta in a matter of days, many swimming the strait, prompting the EU to convene an emergency meeting of interior ministers. Twenty-two member states signed a joint letter demanding a "strengthening of external borders," while divisions persist within the bloc over how to respond. In Ceuta itself, hundreds of residents mobilised to shut down a planned far-right anti-migrant rally.

**Background:** Ceuta is one of two Spanish enclaves on the North African mainland and a recurring flashpoint in Madrid-Rabat relations, where Morocco's control of its border has previously been used as diplomatic leverage.

**Why it matters:** A crossing of this scale is among the largest single migration events in the EU's recent history and will shape both European border policy and Spain's leverage-laden relationship with Morocco.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera centres the migrants themselves, describing a deadly "game" driven by Moroccan economic desperation and highlighting local resistance to the far right; the Guardian emphasises the limbo facing arrivals alongside EU institutional paralysis, while the 22-state letter frames the same event primarily as a border-security failure.
