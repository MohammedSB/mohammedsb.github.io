1. US-Iran talks continue, but ‘deal unlikely’ before midterm elections

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Indirect US-Iran talks are continuing through mediators, with separate sessions expected on Monday and Tuesday focused on an amended Iranian seven-day ceasefire-style proposal floated at UNGA. Trump publicly vowed victory in the Iran conflict 'very soon' while simultaneously denying offering sanctions relief or frozen funds in exchange for nuclear concessions. A high-level official told the Wall Street Journal that Iran had come close to compromising on its nuclear program earlier this year during talks with JD Vance.

Background: The US and Iran have been in an active military conflict since late February, when US-Israeli strikes on Iran began. Seven months of war have strained Iran's economy severely, while Iran has tightened control over the Strait of Hormuz, raising global energy prices.

Why it matters: A negotiated outcome or its failure will shape Gulf security, global oil prices, and the nuclear non-proliferation order. The proximity to US midterm elections adds domestic political pressure on both sides to either reach or avoid a deal.

Different views: Al Jazeera foregrounds Iran's leverage — its Hormuz stranglehold and the concessions it is extracting — framing Iran as a rational actor seeking relief for allies like the Houthis. The Jerusalem Post clusters multiple articles emphasising Iranian economic collapse, internal dissent, IRGC propaganda, and crypto sanctions-evasion, treating Iran's negotiating position as one of weakness and bad faith. Trump's denial of sanctions offers is reported neutrally by the Post but its broader framing treats any Iranian compromise as something to be extracted rather than negotiated.

2. Iran suspected in bomb plot against RAF base used by US Air Force, UK police investigating - report

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: UK police are investigating a suspected bomb plot against RAF Fairford, a base also used by the US Air Force, after five men with three vans were found near the perimeter in the early hours of Sunday. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called it 'a very serious situation' involving a 'foreign actor.' All five were released on bail without immediate charge.

Background: RAF Fairford is a strategically significant base that has historically hosted US B-52 bombers and other heavy assets. Iran has been accused of escalating covert operations targeting Western infrastructure amid the ongoing US-Iran conflict.

Why it matters: If Iranian involvement is confirmed, it would mark a significant escalation of Iran's campaign against Western targets on European soil, with major implications for NATO allies and the diplomatic track of US-Iran talks.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post leads with Iran as the suspected actor and frames the plot within a pattern of Iranian aggression. The Guardian is more cautious, noting Iran's 'mixed track record' on such plots, that the men were released on bail, and that investigators are far from determining the cause — foregrounding legal uncertainty over Iranian culpability. Al Jazeera reports Rubio's 'foreign actor' framing without attributing it to Iran, keeping attribution open.

3. ‘You were right’: Ed Miliband tells critics Labour was wrong on Gaza

The Guardian · UK

What happened: UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told the Labour Party conference that the party had been wrong on Gaza and that critics who raised the plight of Palestinians 'were right.' He announced sanctions on Israeli settlements and pledged that Labour would not 'accept the status quo.' Separately, former Attorney General Dominic Grieve advised the Foreign Office that Israel does not have a legal right to close the British consulate in East Jerusalem, which Israel has threatened to shut on 8 October.

Background: Labour faced significant internal and electoral pressure over its Gaza stance throughout 2024–25, losing support among Muslim-majority constituencies. Israel's plan to close the East Jerusalem consulate is tied to broader tensions over UK recognition of Palestinian statehood and settlement sanctions.

Why it matters: The UK's posture shift — combining settlement sanctions, a legal challenge to the consulate closure, and a public apology on Gaza — signals a meaningful recalibration in a major Western ally's relationship with Israel, with potential ripple effects on EU and US diplomatic positioning.
