1. US launches new wave of strikes against Iran as Tehran says diplomacy has proven ‘futile’

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: US and Iranian forces exchanged heavy missile and drone attacks over the weekend and into Monday, with the US launching a new wave of strikes on Iran while Tehran targeted US bases across the Gulf and announced it was closing the Strait of Hormuz to shipping. Traffic through the strait plummeted to its lowest level in weeks, and Trump rejected Iran's closure claim, vowing the US would become 'guardian of the strait.' The June ceasefire is at the point of collapse.

Background: A ceasefire agreed last month is unraveling after roughly a week of tit-for-tat exchanges centered on control of the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint for global oil traffic; Iran says diplomacy has proven 'futile.'

Why it matters: A closure or sustained fighting in the Strait of Hormuz threatens global energy supplies and risks dragging Gulf states and the wider region into open war.

Different views: Israeli JPost emphasizes Iran 'getting its a** kicked' and Trump's vow to control and be paid for the strait, framing Iran as the aggressor stretching a 3,000km front; pro-Palestinian Al Jazeera frames it as US-driven escalation risking wider regional war; the Guardian foregrounds Bahrain's accusation that Iran targeted civilians and casts Trump's 'guardian' rhetoric skeptically.

2. Former Emir of Qatar Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani dies aged 74

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, the former Emir of Qatar, died aged 74. He took power in 1996 in a bloodless coup against his father and transformed Qatar into a gas-rich regional power, founding Al Jazeera and advancing women's reforms during his 18-year rule.

Background: Sheikh Hamad ruled until abdicating in 2013 in favor of his son; he reoriented Qatar's foreign policy and media influence, making it a diplomatic heavyweight punching above its size.

Why it matters: His legacy shaped modern Qatar's outsized diplomatic role — mediating in Gaza, Afghanistan and beyond — and the institutions he built continue to influence Middle East politics and media.

Different views: Al Jazeera, which he founded, runs an affectionate farewell emphasizing women's empowerment; BBC notes the bloodless coup and gas-driven transformation neutrally; Israeli JPost analyzes how he 'reshaped the modern Middle East' from a more strategic, arm's-length vantage.

3. UK to list Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps as terrorist organisation

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: The UK announced it will list Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) as a terrorist organization, with the Home Office banning support for the group. The move follows IRGC links to death threats and intimidation on British soil.

Background: The proscription is a major escalation of diplomatic tensions with Tehran and comes amid the broader US-Iran military confrontation in the Gulf.

Why it matters: Designating a branch of a state's official military as a terrorist organization is a significant diplomatic step that further isolates Iran and could trigger retaliation or a rupture in UK-Iran relations.

Different views: BBC and the Guardian cover it similarly as a domestic-security-driven escalation; framing across outlets is largely consistent, with the Guardian stressing the diplomatic rupture with Tehran.
