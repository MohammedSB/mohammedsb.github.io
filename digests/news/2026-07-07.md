## Top Stories

### 1. [Iran fires missiles at two commercial ships in Strait of Hormuz, causing major damage](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-901664)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** Three tankers, including the Qatari LNG carrier Al Rekayyat, were struck within hours near the Strait of Hormuz close to Oman. Qatar warned Iran it would bear full legal responsibility, calling it an unacceptable attack on international navigation and energy supplies; Iran blamed the US.

**Background:** The strikes come amid renewed maritime pressure after the US-Israel-Iran conflict, with Oman having proposed a new coastal shipping corridor that Iran opposes because it wants to charge ships for passage.

**Why it matters:** The Strait of Hormuz carries a large share of global oil and LNG; sustained attacks risk a wider Gulf conflict, drag in the US and Gulf states, and threaten global energy supplies.

**Different views:** Qatar (via Guardian) stresses Iran's legal responsibility; Jerusalem Post frames it as Iran deliberately testing Gulf and US resolve and firing missiles/drones; Iran itself blames the United States, deflecting attribution.


### 2. [Hamas offers to hand over authority in Gaza to US-backed administration](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/06/hamas-offers-hand-over-authority-gaza-us-backed-administration)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Hamas announced it would hand over governing authority in Gaza after two decades in power and invited a US-backed interim administration to take over. Its statement made no promise to disarm unilaterally, which Israel and the US have demanded.

**Background:** The offer comes amid ceasefire negotiations and mounting humanitarian collapse in Gaza, with disarmament remaining the central sticking point.

**Why it matters:** A genuine transfer of civil authority could reshape Gaza's postwar governance, but the omission of disarmament leaves the core of the Israeli-US demands unmet.

**Different views:** The Guardian reports the offer relatively straightforwardly as a potential opening; Jerusalem Post analysts and Palestinian officials call it a 'trick' to retain weapons and 'pull the wool over everyone's eyes,' arguing Hamas never actually renounced power.


### 3. [Millions join funeral procession for Iran’s supreme leader Ali Khamenei](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/06/ali-khamenei-funeral-procession-iran-supreme-leader)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Millions of mourners filled Tehran for the multi-day funeral procession of assassinated former supreme leader Ali Khamenei, killed in US-Israeli airstrikes in February. Crowds waved flags and red vengeance banners chanting for revenge, with ceremonies extending to Qom and Najaf.

**Background:** Khamenei's death in the February strikes marked an extraordinary reversal for a regime that, months earlier, had faced widespread anti-government street protests.

**Why it matters:** The mass turnout, however engineered, is a display of regime resilience and a signal of Iran's intent to retaliate, shaping the trajectory of the post-conflict standoff with the US and Israel.

**Different views:** BBC and Guardian note the spectacle was partly state-engineered and that underlying divisions and demands for change persist among mourners; Jerusalem Post frames Iran's outreach to Western influencers at the funeral as a 'sign of weakness,' and highlights diaspora protests flying Israeli and pro-Pahlavi flags against the regime.
