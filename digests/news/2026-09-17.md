## Top Stories

### 1. [US opts not to back Saudi Arabia in Yemen after meeting Houthi leaders](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/16/us-officials-decide-against-backing-saudi-arabia-yemen-meeting-houthi-leaders)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** US officials met Houthi leaders at the American embassy in Muscat over the weekend, and Washington subsequently decided not to intervene militarily to help Saudi Arabia push back the Houthi advance along Yemen's Red Sea coast. The Houthis reportedly gave assurances that Red Sea commercial shipping and the existing ceasefire understanding with the US would be respected. Saudi Arabia has responded on its own, flying dozens of airstrikes across Yemen, while the Yemeni government has asked the UN Security Council to act.

**Background:** The Houthis have advanced rapidly along the Red Sea coastal plain toward Bab al-Mandeb and Taiz, threatening a chokepoint that carries a large share of global trade and, with Saudi export infrastructure under attack, alternative crude routes out of the Gulf.

**Why it matters:** If Washington declines to defend Riyadh from an Iran-aligned force seizing a global chokepoint, the credibility of the US security umbrella in the Gulf is in question, pushing Saudi Arabia toward hedging arrangements with Egypt, China and potentially Israel.

**Different views:** One set of coverage frames the Muscat contacts and US non-intervention as evidence that the American security guarantee to the Gulf is hollow and that the Houthis are now a party Washington negotiates with; another frames the same facts as Riyadh being dangerously isolated and encircled by Iranian proxies, treating Saudi and US-UK statements about Iranian sponsorship as the authoritative account and giving little space to Houthi assurances or to the war's effect on Yemenis.


### 2. [At least 21 killed after Israeli-hit building in Gaza City collapses](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/16/building-gaza-city-damaged-israeli-strikes-collapses-killed)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** A six-storey residential building in Gaza City that had been struck by Israel last year collapsed overnight, killing at least 21 people including eight to eleven children, according to Gaza's civil defence. Around ten displaced families — roughly 100 people — had moved into the structurally compromised building for lack of any other shelter, and rescuers dug by hand because heavy machinery was unavailable. Dozens, including many children, were reported still trapped.

**Background:** Large parts of Gaza's housing stock are war-damaged but reoccupied by displaced families, and the territory's civil defence has been stripped of excavators, fuel and cutting equipment through the course of the war.

**Why it matters:** The collapse shows that casualties in Gaza are now being generated by the accumulated physical and humanitarian damage itself, not only by active strikes, which bears directly on reconstruction, rubble clearance and shelter obligations under any post-war arrangement.

**Different views:** Accounts differ mainly in attribution and emphasis: some lead on the building having been hit by an Israeli strike and on the absence of rescue equipment as a consequence of the war; others present the collapse as that of a 'war-damaged' building and attribute the casualty figures and family details to a named Gaza civil defence official, signalling that the numbers are a claim rather than an established fact.


### 3. [Iran school bombing: grounds to believe US was behind atrocity, UN finds](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/17/iran-school-bombing-un-mission-us-military-behind-attack)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** A UN fact-finding mission concluded there are reasonable grounds to believe the United States carried out February military strikes on a school and a sports facility in Iran that killed some 156 civilians, including 120 children, and that these amounted to war crimes. The same UN process also found the Iranian government committed crimes against humanity in its handling of protesters. The findings land as Trump says Washington is in direct talks with Tehran and 'hopefully toward the end' of the war.

**Background:** The US-Iran war has been running alongside a domestic American fight over war powers, with six resolutions brought in the House and a growing number of Republicans defecting as midterms approach.

**Why it matters:** A formal UN war-crimes finding against the US changes the legal and political terrain of the conflict, giving congressional opponents of the war and Washington's critics abroad a documented basis for pressure just as negotiations are reportedly opening.

**Different views:** One version leads with the US strikes and the child death toll as the headline finding, detailing the school and sports-facility attacks; another pairs the two halves of the UN output in a single frame — US war crimes alongside Iranian crimes against humanity — which distributes culpability and softens the singularity of the finding against Washington.
