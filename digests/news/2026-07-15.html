1. Trump threatens to bomb bridges and power plants unless Iran resumes talks

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: The US and Iran exchanged fire for a fourth consecutive day as Washington resumed blockading Iranian ports and struck Iranian targets. Trump threatened to bomb Iran's bridges and power plants next week unless Tehran returns to negotiations, while Iran threatened to close the Strait of Hormuz and halt regional energy exports.

Background: The confrontation has run more than four months since US strikes on Iran began in late February; a fragile US-Iran deal/MoU signed weeks earlier has collapsed, with the Strait of Hormuz the central flashpoint.

Why it matters: A full blockade and threats to civilian infrastructure risk a wider Gulf war, spiking oil prices and drawing in Gulf states, Iraq, and the Houthis; control of Hormuz affects a large share of global energy trade.

Different views: Al Jazeera frames it as the US restarting an aggressive war and reneging on a peace MoU while foregrounding Iranian claims of hitting US bases; the Jerusalem Post emphasizes Iranian aggression against tankers, regime survival calculus, and nuclear-breakout risk; BBC and the Guardian stress Trump's struggle to end the war and his about-face on Hormuz tolls.

2. 'They don't want you there': Trump tells Netanyahu to pull IDF troops from Syria, Lebanon - report

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: According to an Axios report, Trump told Netanyahu to pull IDF troops out of Syria and Lebanon, saying the local populations 'don't want you there.' Israeli military sources said the report caught them by surprise and that there had been no change on the ground.

Background: Israel has held buffer-zone positions in southern Syria and southern Lebanon; Syria's new president Ahmed al-Sharaa has told Trump that Israel refused to include 'withdrawal' in any agreement, while Israel and Lebanon are simultaneously holding US-brokered talks in Rome over IDF withdrawal and Hezbollah disarmament.

Why it matters: US pressure on Israel to withdraw signals a shift in Washington's posture toward post-Assad Syria and Lebanon and could reshape Israel's northern security arrangements and the Hezbollah disarmament process.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post frames withdrawal skeptically, questioning whether there is any strategic need to leave and whether Lebanon's army can contain Hezbollah, while presenting Sharaa's account as a 'report'; the framing centers Israeli security interests rather than Syrian or Lebanese sovereignty claims.

3. Israeli air attack on Gaza apartment kills family of three

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: An Israeli air strike on an apartment in Deir el-Balah, Gaza, killed a family of three with one child surviving, part of what Palestinian sources describe as escalating Israeli attacks across the territory. A separate strike on a police post in northern Gaza killed seven, including a senior Hamas-run police officer.

Background: The strikes come despite an October 'ceasefire'; Israel says it is targeting terrorists, while Palestinian officials and analysts say civilians and civil officials are being killed.

Why it matters: Continued deadly strikes undermine the ceasefire's durability and Gaza's post-war governance, raising questions about whether the truce is holding in practice.

Different views: Al Jazeera presents the attacks as deliberate Israeli sabotage of the ceasefire and killing of civil officials to ensure 'perpetual chaos'; the BBC relays Israel's military claim that it targeted 'terrorists' and notes a Hamas police officer among the dead, using 'officials say' attribution and neutral framing.
