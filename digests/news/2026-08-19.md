### 1. [Trump declares Strait of Hormuz as 'new US territory,' day after MoU deadline expires](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-905890)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** President Trump declared the Strait of Hormuz "new US territory" a day after the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding with Iran expired, and declined to say whether he was seeking to extend it. Iran's top negotiator Ghalibaf responded that the waterway will remain closed until Washington meets the interim deal's conditions, while Trump simultaneously claimed the strait was open. Traffic data shows crossings still in the single digits, with Iraq approving a three-month workaround mechanism to export crude via alternative channels.

**Background:** The strait carries roughly a fifth of global seaborne oil; Iran closed it during the current US-Iran war, and a temporary ceasefire MoU brokered in Islamabad has now lapsed without renewal. A senior Iranian official says Tehran will shift to a "fully offensive" posture now that Washington has ruled out an extension.

**Why it matters:** The expiry of the MoU removes the last formal restraint on a renewed US-Iran shooting war, and a sustained Hormuz closure would push global energy markets and Gulf economies into crisis while forcing states like Iraq to rebuild export routes from scratch.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post frames Tehran's stance as hostage-taking of global shipping by a regime scrambling to hold internal control, while Iranian officials quoted across coverage (Araghchi, Ghalibaf) cast the war as an Iranian victory in which the US "begged for negotiations"; Al Jazeera's framing centres on the war's civilian and third-country costs rather than the maritime balance of power.


### 2. [UAE suspends trade, financial transactions with Iran after attacks](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-905988)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** The UAE suspended all trade and financial transactions with Iran after what Abu Dhabi says were Iranian attacks on its territory, including reports of two ballistic missiles and a spate of strikes on UAE-owned tankers. Emirati air defences detected an incoming missile before giving the all-clear; Tehran denies carrying out any attack on the UAE.

**Background:** The UAE has long been Iran's most important sanctions-evasion channel, with Dubai serving as a re-export and financial hub worth tens of billions of dollars a year to the Iranian economy. The rupture comes as Saudi Arabia is quietly tightening money flows to the UAE, signalling a widening rift inside the Gulf bloc.

**Why it matters:** Closing the Dubai channel could shut Iran's single biggest economic escape route at the moment it most needs one, and it marks the Gulf states abandoning the hedging posture they maintained through previous rounds of the conflict.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera hedges the trigger as an allegation ("Iran allegedly targets the UAE") and foregrounds the embargo's economic strangulation of ordinary Iranians, while the Jerusalem Post treats the attacks as established fact and part of a pattern of Iranian aggression; Tehran flatly denies the missile launch, leaving the casus belli for the embargo genuinely disputed.


### 3. [US condemns 'unnecessary escalation' after Israeli airstrikes on Syrian airbase](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c62vy3wl31lo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Israel struck the Abu al-Duhur airbase near Idlib in northwestern Syria, and the US publicly condemned the attack as an "unnecessary escalation." Damascus called it "an unjustified act of aggression"; Turkey rejected Israel's claim that its troop deployment at the base violated the regional "status quo." US envoy Tom Barrack told the Jerusalem Post the strike risked scrambling Turkish jets and triggering direct Israel-Turkey confrontation, and Washington is now working to set up an Israel-Turkey-Syria deconfliction mechanism.

**Background:** Since Assad's fall, Turkey has moved to entrench military influence in Syria's new government, while Israel has insisted on a demilitarised southern and central Syria. Israel says it recently passed the US intelligence on Syrian efforts to rebuild military capabilities.

**Why it matters:** This is the sharpest open rift yet between Israel and Washington over Syria policy, and it puts two US partners — Israel and NATO member Turkey — on a path toward direct military contact.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post leads with Israeli and expert justification — Syria "armed to the teeth," Ankara's deployment as an intolerable threat — while the BBC leads with the American rebuke and Damascus's protest; the Turkish position, relayed in both, calls Israel's claims "untenable," and pro-Palestinian coverage situates the strike within a pattern of unilateral Israeli action across the region.
