## Top Stories

### 1. [Iran says it destroyed US military radar in Bahrain in retaliatory strike](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/13/iran-us-radar-bahrain-sirik-hormuz)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps says it targeted and destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, in retaliation for a US airstrike on a communications tower in Sirik, overlooking the Strait of Hormuz. It is described as Iran's first direct attack on US military installations in months, and Iran has threatened an unlimited response over the strait. Trump in turn threatened to renew strikes on Iran 'without hesitation', said no deal with Iran is necessary, and redirected JD Vance away from potential peace talks.

**Background:** The US war on Iran began in February and has settled into what analysts call a 'no war, no peace' standoff, with a US blockade and continuing attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. Four tankers were reportedly hit by explosions in the strait this week.

**Why it matters:** A direct exchange of strikes between the US and Iran, combined with Trump stepping back from negotiations, raises the risk of a return to open war. It also threatens Gulf states hosting US bases and a waterway vital to global oil shipping.

**Different views:** The Guardian presents the strike as retaliation and states the US airstrike on Sirik as fact, while the Jerusalem Post attributes both the radar hit and the Sirik attack to Iranian claims ('what they described as an air attack') and separately foregrounds Trump's threats and the blockade's damage to Iran's economy. Al Jazeera leads with Iran's threat of an unlimited response and treats Trump's 'no deal necessary' stance as a puzzle to be explained, alongside an opinion piece urging Gulf states to build security independent of Washington.


### 2. [Israeli airstrike on Nasser hospital in Gaza kills surgeon and five others](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/12/israeli-airstrike-nasser-hospital-gaza-kills-surgeon)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** An Israeli airstrike hit Nasser hospital in Khan Younis, southern Gaza, early on Thursday, killing six people including a surgeon and a disabled man, and wounding 28, according to the hospital director. Witnesses said the missile struck a building housing surgical wards and intensive care units. The Israeli military confirmed it carried out the attack and issued a statement on what it had struck.

**Background:** A truce was announced last October, but killings in Gaza have decreased rather than stopped. Nasser is the largest hospital in southern Gaza and one of the few still functioning, as winter rains flood shelters and damage power systems.

**Why it matters:** A strike on a working hospital during a nominal ceasefire undermines the truce and further erodes Gaza's remaining healthcare capacity. It comes as a new Israeli government is forming, with commentators doubting that it will change policy on Gaza.

**Different views:** The Guardian covers the strike heavily (a news report, video testimony from a doctor, an editorial and a column), relying on the hospital director and staff and arguing that the ceasefire is not holding. The BBC reports the death toll with attribution to the health ministry and witnesses. The Jerusalem Post has no article on the strike in this set, and its Gaza coverage instead highlights IDF figures on tents and winter supplies and the claim that Hamas is exploiting aid. Al Jazeera's Gaza item focuses on rain damage to shelters, power and healthcare.


### 3. [UN watchdog 'increasingly concerned' over Iran nuclear site](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cm75261e3pdko?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** The UN nuclear watchdog, the IAEA, says it is 'increasingly concerned' about a uranium enrichment plant at Isfahan that Iran announced more than a year ago but that inspectors have never been able to visit. The agency says it does not know the plant's exact location, its safeguards status, or whether it contains nuclear material. It called on Iran to reopen its nuclear facilities to inspection.

**Background:** Inspections have been cut off amid the war and the US-Israeli strikes on Iranian military and nuclear infrastructure. Iran is reported to retain substantial missile production capacity despite those strikes.

**Why it matters:** With no verification, there is no way to know the state of Iran's enrichment work at a moment when Trump says no deal is needed. That uncertainty strengthens the case of those arguing for further military or economic pressure.

**Different views:** All three outlets report the same IAEA findings, but the headlines differ. Al Jazeera frames it as the watchdog calling on Iran to reopen facilities, the BBC as growing concern over a never-inspected site, and the Jerusalem Post as the IAEA raising an alarm. The Jerusalem Post also runs it alongside an ex-Mossad director saying a deal Iran would respect is unlikely and a blockade is the best way to stop its nuclear ambitions.
