1. Iran pitches seven-day roadmap to end conflict with US

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Iran has given Washington a seven-day roadmap to end its conflict with the US. It offers to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within about six days and to start nuclear talks the day after. In return it wants US sanctions on its oil exports lifted, the war ended on all fronts including Lebanon, and some Iranian detainees released. Tehran says it is waiting for Donald Trump to respond.

Background: The plan builds on a June memorandum of understanding between the two countries. That deal led to a ceasefire that later collapsed, and the US-Israel war on Iran has since kept the strait largely closed and pushed fuel prices up worldwide.

Why it matters: Reopening Hormuz would ease global energy prices and could reduce pressure on Trump before the midterm elections. Because Iran ties the offer to Lebanon and other regional fronts, a deal would reach well beyond the US-Iran relationship.

Different views: Al Jazeera presents the plan as an offer to end the conflict and stresses the fuel-price and midterm pressure on Trump. The Guardian leads with Iran's list of demands. The Jerusalem Post treats it as a report built on the June MoU that applies only if conditions are met, and runs it next to stories on Israel being ready to strike Iran again, Chinese parts reaching Iran's drone program, and President Pezeshkian saying he consults Khamenei directly, which casts Tehran's offer as tactical rather than conciliatory.

2. Saudi Arabia intercepts wave of Houthi missiles as oil climbs to one-week high

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Saudi Arabia says it intercepted six Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Taif and the Yanbu area on the Red Sea, and oil prices rose to a one-week high. The Houthis say they fired missiles and drones at Riyadh and at Aramco facilities in Yanbu. France is sending forces to protect the Yanbu terminal, and Pakistani and Turkish military chiefs are going to Riyadh for urgent talks.

Background: The Houthis have advanced quickly down Yemen's Red Sea coast against the Saudi-backed government and have repeatedly fired into Saudi Arabia. With Hormuz disrupted, Yanbu is the kingdom's main alternative route for exporting oil.

Why it matters: The attacks threaten Saudi Arabia's last major bypass for oil exports while Hormuz is disrupted, and they could pull Pakistan, Turkey and European states into Yemen's war.

Different views: The Guardian leads with the interceptions and oil markets, relying mainly on the Saudi-led coalition's statements. The Jerusalem Post runs the Houthis' own claim of hitting Riyadh and Aramco, highlights a Greek-operated Patriot battery shooting down a missile, and consistently calls the group Iran-backed. Al Jazeera focuses on Saudi allies coming forward and on how cautious Pakistan and Turkey still are about getting involved.

3. Netanyahu defends Israeli military action as delegates walk out before UN speech

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: Dozens of delegates walked out as Benjamin Netanyahu went to the podium at the UN General Assembly. Other delegates applauded and chanted 'Am Yisrael Chai'. In his speech Netanyahu defended Israel's military campaigns and called those who left 'moral cowards'. Outside, police in New York detained protesters, including actors Susan Sarandon and Hannah Einbinder, at a Jewish Voice for Peace demonstration.

Background: Netanyahu faces an International Criminal Court arrest warrant, growing diplomatic isolation over the Gaza war as it nears its fourth year, and his toughest re-election race to date.

Why it matters: The walkout was one of the most visible rebukes of Israel at the UN during this conflict, and the speech gave Netanyahu a platform for defiance ahead of a close election at home.

Different views: The BBC's headline leads with Netanyahu's defence of Israeli military action and his 'moral cowards' retort, giving his framing top billing. The Guardian leads with the walkout, the protests, the arrests and his political isolation, and also publishes an op-ed calling for his prosecution. Al Jazeera features the walkout and footage of an Israeli official taking notes as delegates left. None of the Jerusalem Post articles in this batch cover the speech or the walkout.
