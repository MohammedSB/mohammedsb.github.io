### 1. [What we know about stabbing on Flydubai flight to Israel](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cqjdv7pmj9dno?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** On a Flydubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv, the Omani co-pilot allegedly stabbed the Indian captain, Smit Machchhar, and the plane dropped about 17,000ft in under two minutes. Machchhar, though wounded, opened the cockpit door. Passengers and off-duty pilots then overpowered the attacker, steadied the aircraft and diverted to Tabuk in Saudi Arabia. Saudi security services are questioning the co-pilot, and the UAE and Israel have opened investigations.

**Background:** The flight is a product of the UAE–Israel normalisation that began with the Abraham Accords. Saudi Arabia, which has no formal ties with Israel, ended up handling the emergency landing and the first stage of the investigation.

**Why it matters:** The incident is testing Gulf–Israeli security cooperation while a US–Iran war is under way. Netanyahu has said Iran may have been involved, which could turn an aviation crime into a geopolitical flashpoint.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post calls it a 'hijacking' and a 'terrorist attack', leads with Netanyahu's vow that those behind it 'will pay a heavy price' and the UAE official Gargash's terror label, and treats the Iran question as likely to be answered soon. The BBC and Guardian say 'alleged' and 'apparent', present Netanyahu's 'Islamist indoctrination' remark as his claim, and keep other explanations open: the Guardian notes investigators are looking at a mental health incident, and a BBC headline leads with his 'too early to say' on Iran. Al Jazeera and much of the Guardian's coverage centre on the Indian pilot's own account of the attack rather than on Israeli official statements.


### 2. [US sends third aircraft carrier to Middle East amid stalemate in Iran war](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/02/us-third-aircraft-carrier-middle-east-iran)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The US is sending a third aircraft carrier strike group, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, plus an amphibious assault group to the Middle East, with about 10,000 extra troops. President Trump has warned that new strikes on Iran could be coming. At the same time, Washington has imposed new sanctions on Iranian industries and, according to a report, ordered Iran's UN General Assembly delegation to leave the US after talks stalled.

**Background:** The US is at war with Iran, but the conflict has reached a stalemate and indirect nuclear talks have stalled. Iran has reportedly offered inspectors access to its nuclear sites in exchange for sanctions relief.

**Why it matters:** Three carriers in the region by the end of November would give the US far more ability to resume large-scale strikes. That raises the risk of a wider regional war and further disruption to Gulf energy flows.

**Different views:** The Guardian calls it 'Trump's war against Iran' and ties the buildup to a stalemate and to US domestic politics before the midterms. The Jerusalem Post, relying on US and Israeli defence officials, describes it as widening Trump's 'options' and says Iran's 'room for maneuver has narrowed'. Al Jazeera puts the question of escalation in its headline and adds up the force posture near Iran.


### 3. [Israeli court ruling a ‘new threshold’ in Palestinian suppression](https://www.aljazeera.com/video/newsfeed/2026/10/2/10-2-ziad-abu-rish-israel-politics-qt-mp4?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Israel's Supreme Court told the Palestinian citizen politician Sami Abu Shehadeh to withdraw from the upcoming Knesset election or be disqualified, and he has withdrawn. Al Jazeera presents the ruling as a 'new threshold' in suppressing Palestinian political participation.

**Background:** Israel holds an election in less than a month that could remove Netanyahu, and Palestinian participation in the vote has been shrinking.

**Why it matters:** The ruling narrows political representation for Israel's Palestinian citizens in an election that could change the government. It will also feed the debate over how democratic Israel's electoral process is.

**Different views:** Only Al Jazeera covered the ruling, and it framed it as suppression. The Guardian's election piece is built on the views of Jewish Israelis in Jerusalem divided over Netanyahu, and the Jerusalem Post's election coverage in this set is about a Palestinian Authority candidate, not Palestinian citizens of Israel. So the court ruling is absent from the other outlets' election coverage.
