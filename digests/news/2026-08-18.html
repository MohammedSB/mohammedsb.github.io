1. US-Iran talks hit new impasse as MoU's 60-day deadline expires with no deal

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: The 60-day Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding between Washington and Tehran expired with no deal, and Trump declined to extend it. Iran's top negotiator Ghalibaf said the Strait of Hormuz will stay closed until US conditions are met, a senior Iranian official warned Tehran would shift to a "fully offensive" posture, and Trump separately declared Hormuz "new US territory" and threatened to bomb Oman if it "gets in the way" of ending the Iran war. Saudi-owned Al Arabiya briefly reported an extension had been agreed, which was not confirmed.

Background: The MoU followed this year's US-Iran war and functioned as a temporary ceasefire while negotiations proceeded, with Oman and Qatar acting as intermediaries and Hormuz closure as Iran's main leverage. Hormuz transits remain in single digits, choking a corridor that normally carries roughly a fifth of global oil.

Why it matters: The lapse removes the only formal restraint on renewed US-Iran fighting while both sides reload depleted missile and interceptor stocks, and a closed Hormuz keeps global energy supply hostage to the next miscalculation.

Different views: Jerusalem Post coverage foregrounds Iranian threats, internal unrest Tehran is preparing to "crush," and Iranian officials' triumphalism (Araghchi's "we won the war, won diplomacy") as evidence of bad faith; Al Jazeera and the Guardian instead lead on Trump's bombing threats against Oman and the US refusal to extend as the escalatory act, while Iranian officials themselves frame the impasse as American desperation and "heavy political defeat."

2. Netanyahu and Kushner talks make no breakthrough on stalled Gaza deal

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Jared Kushner met Netanyahu in Jerusalem after a rare meeting with Hamas's political chief in Egypt, but produced no breakthrough on the stalled Gaza ceasefire. Netanyahu insisted Israeli troops will not withdraw from the Strip until Hamas disarms first, while calling the talks constructive. A parallel understanding emerged for a US general to oversee transfer of Hamas weapons to a Gaza government and international stabilisation force.

Background: Netanyahu publicly rejected the US-backed 15-point peace plan earlier this month in a rare open break with the Trump administration, leaving the ceasefire framework in limbo. Kushner's shuttle diplomacy is an attempt to salvage it without conceding on sequencing.

Why it matters: Sequencing — disarmament before or after withdrawal — is the single knot holding up any durable end to the Gaza war, and failure keeps a resumption of full-scale fighting on the table.

Different views: The BBC relays Kushner's upbeat "very good meeting" line; the Guardian stresses the absence of any breakthrough and Netanyahu's hard precondition; the Jerusalem Post reports Kushner explicitly pledging US backing for an Israeli resumption of war if Hamas misses a 60–90 day disarmament timeline, and highlights Palestinian Authority anger that Washington is sidelining Abbas by dealing with Hamas directly.

3. 'Unnecessary escalation': Damascus, US envoy Barrack accuse Israel of striking Syrian airbase

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: Eight air strikes hit the Abu al-Duhur military airbase in eastern Idlib countryside early Tuesday. Damascus and US envoy Tom Barrack both attributed the attack to Israel, with Barrack calling it an "unnecessary escalation" and saying Ahmed al-Sharaa's government wants de-escalation. Israel has not claimed responsibility.

Background: Israel has repeatedly struck Syrian military sites since Assad's fall, arguing it is denying capabilities to the new Damascus government, while Washington has been sponsoring Israel-Syria de-escalation talks. Separately, Syrian soldiers were filmed chanting anti-Jewish threats in Damascus.

Why it matters: A serving US envoy publicly rebuking Israel over a strike marks unusual daylight between Washington and Jerusalem and threatens the US-brokered Syria track just as al-Sharaa consolidates power.

Different views: Al Jazeera reports the strike flatly as an Israeli attack on a sovereign state's base; the Jerusalem Post hedges attribution ("Israel yet to take responsibility"), pairs it with footage of Syrian troops chanting death threats against Jews, and links the timing to Israel's rejection of the Gaza plan — implicitly casting the strike as defensive against a hostile new Damascus.
