### 1. [Kuwait says it repelled first Iranian attack in two months after US strikes island](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw4gn9jeqjq7o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Kuwait said it repelled its first Iranian attack in two months, and Bahrain said it also intercepted Iranian air attacks. Both came after a US air strike on Iran's Qeshm Island in the Gulf on Tuesday. The IRGC said it targeted a US radar in response to US drones entering Iranian airspace, while a US military official told the Jerusalem Post the US had struck an IRGC missile launch site in response to IRGC aggression against US personnel. Separately, Trump said Washington takes 'very seriously' a reported Iranian 'dirty bomb' plot against the US capital, which Tehran called 'baseless'.

**Background:** The exchanges come amid an ongoing US-Iran confrontation and a surge in attacks around the Strait of Hormuz, where an LPG tanker was set ablaze this week after a reported strike. Qatar and Oman are trying to mediate with Tehran.

**Why it matters:** Renewed fire on Gulf states after a two-month lull puts US bases, Gulf shipping and the Qatari-Omani mediation at risk. A Jerusalem Post report says Tehran has hardened its negotiating stance since the US midterms and is weighing escalation in Hormuz.

**Different views:** The BBC's headline puts the Iranian attack after the US strike on Qeshm, so it reads as a response. The Jerusalem Post's headline says Iran attacked US bases 'despite diplomatic efforts', relies on a US military official to describe the US strike as retaliation, and runs analysis linking the attacks to a Tehran strategy to destabilise the Gulf, including Bahrain's life sentences for 64 Shi'ites accused of Iran-linked terrorism; Iran's own reason (drone incursions) appears only in a subheading. On the dirty-bomb claim, the Guardian leads with the White House saying there are 'no credible threats', the BBC carries Tehran's denial, and the Jerusalem Post leads with CNN-sourced US intelligence, adding only lower down that it is not evidence of an imminent attack.


### 2. [Houthis advance toward Aden as Saudi-backed Yemeni forces suffer major setbacks](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-915138)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** Houthi forces are advancing toward Aden, forcing Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces to retreat, and Saudi Arabia has reshuffled its military command. The Saudi-led coalition says it carried out precision strikes on 60 Houthi military sites, while 'fierce battles' are reported in Taiz.

**Background:** Aden is the base of Yemen's internationally recognised government, which the Saudi-led coalition has backed against the Iran-aligned Houthis since 2015.

**Why it matters:** If the Houthis threaten Aden, the government's last major stronghold could collapse, Saudi Arabia's long Yemen campaign would be badly damaged, and the Houthis would gain more control over Red Sea and Gulf of Aden shipping lanes, all while the wider Iran confrontation intensifies.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera's headline leads with the coalition's 60 strikes and calls them 'precision strikes on key military targets', putting Saudi military action first. The Jerusalem Post leads with Houthi gains, the retreat of Saudi-backed forces and the Saudi reshuffle, presenting the story as a coalition setback.


### 3. [Israeli parliament elects new speaker backed by Netanyahu’s opponents](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/17/israeli-parliament-elects-new-knesset-speaker-backed-netanyahus-opponents)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The Knesset elected Matan Kahana as speaker. Kahana is an ally of Gadi Eisenkot, who is trying to form Israel's next government, and the vote is seen as a first step toward reversing policies of Benjamin Netanyahu's outgoing government.

**Background:** The vote follows last month's general election. Monitors recorded a 400% rise in anti-Arab hate speech online around the vote, alongside disproven fraud allegations against Arab Israeli parties, including from Netanyahu.

**Why it matters:** This is the first concrete sign in parliament of a handover of power away from Netanyahu. A new government could change Israel's policies on the judiciary, the West Bank and Gaza.
