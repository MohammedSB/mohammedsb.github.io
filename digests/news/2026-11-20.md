## Top Stories

### 1. [Israeli tanks cross ‘yellow line’ boundary into Hamas-controlled central Gaza](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/18/israeli-tanks-cross-yellow-line-boundary-into-hamas-controlled-central-gaza)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** On Wednesday, Israeli tanks crossed the 'yellow line' into Hamas-controlled central Gaza. It was the first ground operation across that boundary since the ceasefire was agreed last October, and dozens of displaced Palestinians fled their tents. The Israeli military has since said the operation is complete and that its aim was to destroy a 'terror tunnel'. Hamas called it a 'flagrant' violation of the ceasefire.

**Background:** Under the October ceasefire, Gaza was divided along a 'yellow line', with Israeli forces on one side and Hamas in control of the rest. The incursion comes as Gadi Eisenkot tries to form an Israeli government without Netanyahu.

**Why it matters:** This is the first ground breach of the ceasefire's main boundary. It tests whether the truce can hold, and it is happening while Israeli politics is unsettled.

**Different views:** The BBC leads with the Israeli military's account that the operation was finished and aimed at a 'terror tunnel', and gives Hamas's condemnation as the reply. The Guardian leads with Israeli tanks crossing the line, displaced Palestinians fleeing their tents and the fact that this is the first such breach, and ties it to Israeli coalition politics. It does not centre the tunnel explanation. Neither Al Jazeera nor the Jerusalem Post has an item on the incursion in this set.


### 2. [US targets Iran’s ship-to-ship transfers: Can Tehran break the blockade?](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/20/us-targets-irans-ship-to-ship-transfers-can-tehran-break-the-blockade?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** US Central Command says vessels that use ship-to-ship transfers to get around the US blockade of Iran can be stopped, boarded or seized by US forces. Trump says Washington will 'see what happens' in its conflict with Iran after 1 January. Israeli figures, including the ambassador to Washington, are signalling that Israel would join renewed strikes.

**Background:** The US, Israel and Iran are at war, and the Strait of Hormuz has become a battlefield. Since the June 2025 strikes on Iran's nuclear sites, Iran has not let International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) inspectors into the bombed facilities.

**Why it matters:** Tighter enforcement at sea, talk of a 1 January deadline and open predictions of a joint US-Israeli strike all point towards the war escalating again. That would affect global energy flows through Hormuz.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera presents the story as a blockade that Iran is trying to break and asks whether it can. The Jerusalem Post leans on an unnamed US official (who says Iran is firing deadlier missiles at ships), a former Israeli navy chief who predicts a strike within two weeks, and the Israeli ambassador. In that coverage Iran's side appears mainly as rebuttal: Araghchi mocking the ambassador, and the foreign ministry likening US chemical-weapons claims to the case made for the 2003 Iraq war. The Guardian covers the same war through its environmental damage, with oil slicks spreading across the Gulf.


### 3. [PIF Tower in Riyadh’s financial district damaged after Houthis launch missile towards Saudi Arabia](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-915410)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** A Houthi missile damaged the PIF Tower in Riyadh's financial district. The blast came shortly after Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman met the head of US Central Command, Admiral Brad Cooper. The next day Saudi Arabia intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile and drones over the south. Houthi-run Al-Masirah TV reported Saudi airstrikes on Sanaa. The Saudi-led coalition urged civilians to leave parts of Hodeidah port, and the Houthis are reported to be preparing strikes on Saudi Red Sea ports.

**Background:** Saudi Arabia and the Houthis are fighting openly again. The Houthis have repeatedly targeted southern Saudi cities, and they are besieging the Yemeni city of Taiz.

**Why it matters:** A hit on a landmark in central Riyadh marks a serious escalation. The Saudi-Houthi front may now be merging with the wider confrontation between the US and Iran.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post covers this most heavily. It consistently calls the Houthis 'Iran-aligned' or 'Iran-backed terrorists', draws on Saudi state sources (SPA and Civil Defense), and quotes Houthi media mainly for its reports of Saudi airstrikes. The BBC's related coverage is a civilian-focused report from inside Taiz under Houthi siege, not the missile exchanges. Al Jazeera and the Guardian have no items on the Saudi-Houthi fighting in this set.
