1. Trump emphasizes end of US-Iran ceasefire, claims Iran requested to resume negotiations

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: President Trump declared the three-week-old US-Iran ceasefire 'over' after mutual strikes, while claiming Iran has asked to resume negotiations. Iran launched attacks on US military infrastructure in Gulf states following US strikes on Iran's southern provinces, and both sides traded escalating threats.

Background: A fragile ceasefire had followed an earlier round of direct US-Iran conflict; regional mediators including Qatar have been trying to preserve a memorandum of understanding.

Why it matters: A full breakdown risks reigniting a direct US-Iran war that could draw in Israel and Gulf states and destabilize the entire region.

Different views: Jerusalem Post foregrounds Trump's claim that Iran begged to resume talks and frames Iran as the aggressor, while Al Jazeera emphasizes Iranian vows to 'never surrender' and questions whether the US and Iran are effectively at war again.

2. US wants Iran to pledge to stop shooting at ships in Strait of Hormuz

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: US officials, including VP JD Vance, are joining talks resuming in Oman where Washington demands Iran publicly pledge to stop attacking shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. Europe is separately weighing proposals to allow non-compulsory navigational fees in the strait, while a UN shipping agency urged countries to reject Iran's claim of sovereignty over the waterway.

Background: Iran attributed recent attacks on shipping to 'an errant part of their system' as the vital oil-transit chokepoint became a flashpoint in the wider US-Iran confrontation.

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz carries a large share of global oil; disruption or Iranian control threatens energy markets and international freedom of navigation.

Different views: Jerusalem Post and the UN framing stress rejecting Iranian 'control' and sovereignty claims, the Guardian focuses on European toll-and-diplomacy mechanics, and coverage differs on whether Iran's attacks were deliberate policy or a rogue malfunction.

3. US begins process to remove Syria from terrorism list, signaling major shift in relations

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: The US has begun the process of removing Syria from its list of state sponsors of terrorism, signaling a major shift in bilateral relations, with a commitment to remove obstacles to Syrian reconstruction. The move coincides with Macron's Damascus visit opening the door to French investment.

Background: This follows the fall of the previous regime and a broader Western reengagement with Syria's new government, which has also been cracking down on ISIS cells.

Why it matters: Delisting could unlock reconstruction funding and reintegrate Syria into the regional and international economy after years of isolation.
