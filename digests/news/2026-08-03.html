1. Trump cancels Iran strikes subject to deal being made 'rapidly'

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: Trump announced he had called off a planned large-scale US military assault on Iran — which he described as what would have been the biggest attack since World War II — saying the "perimeters" of a deal covering Iran's nuclear program and the full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz had been agreed. He claimed Iran and other regional states asked Washington to hold off. Reporting indicates Israel was slated to join the strike package and has since raised its alert for a resumption, while CENTCOM is canvassing "creative, unconventional" options against Tehran.

Background: The pause follows an intense US air campaign on Iran last week — Thursday's wave was reportedly about twice the size of the previous night's — waged amid a war that has choked commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters: The credibility of the claimed deal now determines whether a direct US-Iran war resumes and whether the world's most important oil chokepoint reopens.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post foregrounds Trump's "locked and loaded" leverage, Rubio's call for regime change, and Israeli complaints that the strikes were pointless unless they hit regime and economic targets; the Guardian frames it as Trump backing down from a "forever war" he can't win; Al Jazeera stresses analyst skepticism that anything beyond a narrow Hormuz arrangement is achievable and notes a pattern of the US "backing off before markets open," while Iranian officials call Trump's account an attempt to blackmail Gulf rulers.

2. At least 13 killed in Israeli strikes on Gaza after Hamas agree disarmament deal

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: At least 13 people were killed in Israeli strikes on Gaza City, Khan Younis and Deir el-Balah, which Israel said targeted military operatives, including two Hamas commanders. The strikes came immediately after Hamas — and reportedly Palestinian Islamic Jihad — accepted the Board of Peace's disarmament framework announced by Trump. Board of Peace envoy Nickolay Mladenov signalled the strikes are obstructing disarmament and medical access, Turkey accused Netanyahu of sabotaging mediation, and exiled Fatah figure Mohammad Dahlan posted then deleted a claim that Jared Kushner had secured an Israeli halt to attacks.

Background: Trump's Gaza roadmap sequences a verified disarmament of Hamas and other armed groups under a Board of Peace mechanism, followed by an IDF withdrawal in Phase II.

Why it matters: Whether the strikes continue determines if the US-brokered ceasefire architecture and the promised Israeli withdrawal survive past the disarmament step.

Different views: Al Jazeera casts it as Israel openly "defying Trump's peace plan" while escalating deadly attacks and pushing hardline demands; the BBC reports it flatly as Israel striking "military operatives"; the Jerusalem Post frames the same events as Washington and Jerusalem balancing IDF operational freedom against US diplomacy, and amplifies voices — including an Israeli-backed Gazan militia figure — arguing Hamas will never actually disarm; Turkey blames Netanyahu personally for regional destabilization.

3. Iran denies Trump's claim that new talks will begin on Monday

BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream

What happened: Iran publicly denied Trump's assertion that US-Iran negotiations would begin on Monday, with a foreign ministry spokesperson saying there are no plans to send or receive any delegation. Tehran said it is instead in talks with Oman about creating a temporary route through the Strait of Hormuz to let commercial shipping resume. Separately, six Saudi-flagged supertankers changed course in the Gulf of Aden amid Houthi threats.

Background: Trump had cited an imminent deal on denuclearization and Hormuz as his reason for calling off strikes, and Rubio described Iran as "eager" for agreements after US military pressure.

Why it matters: Iran's denial undercuts the stated basis for the US strike pause, leaving the ceasefire resting on a premise one side rejects — and channels the only live diplomacy through Muscat rather than Washington.

Different views: The BBC and Guardian report the denial as a straightforward contradiction of Trump and emphasize the Oman shipping channel as the real negotiation; Al Jazeera treats Trump's announcement as analysts' fodder for doubt about any durable peace; the Jerusalem Post relays Iranian denials while framing them as regime bluster and pairs them with Israeli sources saying Israel is readying for renewed US strikes.
