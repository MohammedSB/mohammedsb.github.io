## Top Stories

### 1. [UK announces sanctions on West Bank settlements prompting furious Israeli response](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c4g7zen0vveo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband announced an import ban on goods from Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank and sanctions on firms that finance or facilitate settlement construction, accusing settlers backed by Netanyahu's government of ethnic cleansing. Israel retaliated within hours, ordering the British consulate in East Jerusalem closed within 30 days and barring several MPs from entry. The UK says 12 countries, including France and Canada, back restrictions, while Australia declined to join the trade ban.

**Background:** Britain recognised a Palestinian state last year and has condemned settlements for decades without imposing economic costs. The move comes months before Israel's October 27 Knesset elections and amid settler violence in the West Bank.

**Why it matters:** It is the sharpest rupture in UK-Israel relations in decades and the first time a G7 state has attached trade penalties to the occupation, setting a template other Western governments are now weighing.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera foregrounds the ban's loopholes and low trade volumes and treats Palestinian and campaigner reactions as the benchmark for whether it goes far enough. The Guardian treats Miliband's 'ethnic cleansing' language as the story and editorialises in favour, giving the prime minister an op-ed slot. The BBC centres the diplomatic cost, reporting from both Jerusalem and West Bank date farms and giving equal weight to Israel's 'big mistake' response. The Jerusalem Post reports the measures straight but leads Israeli reaction with the Palestinian Authority's calls for an arms embargo, framing the ban as a first step toward wider isolation.


### 2. [US says Iranian tankers ‘destroyed’ after missile attacks on American warship](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/08/iran-underwater-drone-strait-of-hormuz)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The US military said it destroyed several Iranian tankers in the Gulf of Oman after Iran fired ballistic missiles at an American warship. Iran's Revolutionary Guard confirmed strikes on a US base in Jordan, two US vessels and eight tankers, and separately claimed to have captured a US underwater drone in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude passed $100 a barrel for the first time since July.

**Background:** The US-Israeli war on Iran has settled into a blockade and tit-for-tat pattern, with Washington and London layering new sanctions on Iranian airlines and oil to force the Strait of Hormuz open.

**Why it matters:** The exchange pulls Jordan into the line of fire, pushes oil to levels that threaten global inflation, and shows neither side has achieved its war aims despite months of strikes and sanctions.

**Different views:** The Guardian and Al Jazeera lead with the tanker strikes and the oil price, presenting the escalation as mutual and quoting both US and Iranian claims, with Al Jazeera's opinion pages arguing the war must end in compromise. The Jerusalem Post treats US statements as authoritative, foregrounds Iran's economic weakness and Rouhani's dissent, and frames Jordan as a frontline state under Iranian attack. On the underwater drone, Al Jazeera presents Tehran's capture claim and the Pentagon's malfunction explanation as competing accounts, while the Jerusalem Post reports Iran's claim with the Pentagon rebuttal as the closing word.


### 3. [Saudi Arabia vows to respond after Houthis attack cities and energy facilities](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cp849n2nz01o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Yemen's Houthis struck four Saudi cities and energy facilities overnight, injuring 73 people and setting Aramco installations ablaze, in the movement's first major attack on the kingdom since the Iran war began. Saudi Arabia and the coalition vowed to retaliate, and Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces reported strikes on Houthi positions in Marib.

**Background:** Riyadh had largely stayed out of the US-Iran fighting under a fragile truce with the Houthis dating to 2022. The attacks come as anti-Houthi forces push on Yemen's frontlines with Saudi backing.

**Why it matters:** It opens a new front that could draw Saudi Arabia formally into the regional war and threatens Gulf energy exports at a moment when oil is already above $100.

**Different views:** The BBC and Guardian describe the strikes as an Iran-backed group's significant expansion of the war and centre Saudi casualty figures and the pledge to retaliate. The Jerusalem Post runs the same facts but pairs them with analysis casting the Houthis as Iran's 'last man standing' and the attacks as a sign of proxy weakness. Al Jazeera avoids the 'Iran-backed' shorthand in its explainer and maps the actors on all sides, including the Saudi-backed Yemeni government offensive.
