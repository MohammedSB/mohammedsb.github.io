## Top Stories

### 1. [Trump abandons June MoU terms, complicating diplomatic efforts to reopen Strait of Hormuz - WSJ](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-906879)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** The Wall Street Journal reports that Trump has walked away from the terms of the June memorandum of understanding with Iran, which critics had attacked as too soft on Tehran, complicating negotiations over reopening the Strait of Hormuz. This comes as CENTCOM says roughly 50,000 US troops are sustaining a blockade of Iran while escorting tankers through the strait, and as Iranian officials insist Washington must first commit to ending the Gaza and Lebanon conflicts before Hormuz normalizes.

**Background:** The US-Israeli war on Iran began on 28 February 2026 with large-scale strikes that killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei on day one; Iran retaliated across the Gulf and the battle for Hormuz turned the war into a global economic event.

**Why it matters:** Hormuz carried 14-15 million barrels of oil a day before the war, so the terms on which it reopens set both the price of the war's end and the balance of leverage between Washington and a weakened Tehran.

**Different views:** Jerusalem Post coverage frames abandoning the MoU as correcting a deal too lenient on Iran and presents CENTCOM as having already 'cleared' the strait, while Al Jazeera splits between arguing US goals remain unfulfilled despite victory claims and conceding that sustained pressure is weakening Tehran; Iranian officials quoted in the Israeli press recast the strait as leverage contingent on Gaza and Lebanon.


### 2. [Board of Peace's Gaza envoy criticises Israeli strikes and Hamas actions](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cew92l07kwzo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Nickolay Mladenov, the Board of Peace's envoy for Gaza, publicly criticised both Israeli strikes and Hamas's actions, warning that a collapse of the ceasefire agreed last October would be 'a point of no return' for the region. His intervention lands alongside reports and footage of continued Israeli drone fire on civilians in Gaza.

**Background:** The October ceasefire ended the most intense phase of the Gaza war and created an internationally backed governance and reconstruction framework, but enforcement disputes and repeated strikes have strained it.

**Why it matters:** The envoy tasked with holding the deal together is signalling it may not hold, which would reopen the central conflict at a moment when the region is already absorbing the Iran war.

**Different views:** The BBC presents Mladenov's even-handed criticism of both sides as the story; Al Jazeera foregrounds Palestinian civilian harm — children sheltering from drone fire, lifeguards without equipment — as evidence Israel is the party breaking the truce; the Jerusalem Post instead emphasises Hamas embedding in civilian institutions, running the UNRWA-teacher disbarment case as its Gaza lead.


### 3. [SDF dissolution earns global praise, but questions remain over Syria's future - analysis](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-906803)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** The agreement dissolving the Syrian Democratic Forces has drawn broad international praise while leaving major questions about how its fighters are absorbed and what political appointments and Turkey-related decisions follow. In parallel, Damascus has introduced Kurdish into Syrian schools for the first time under Decree No. 13 of 2026, and analysts are asking whether the Kurdish women who fought ISIS will be given roles in the new security forces.

**Background:** The SDF, the Kurdish-led force that was the West's main partner against ISIS in Syria, has been negotiating integration into the post-Assad state under heavy Turkish pressure since the transition.

**Why it matters:** How Syria absorbs the SDF determines whether the country consolidates or fragments again, and it directly shapes Turkey's posture and the future of the anti-ISIS security architecture.

**Different views:** Israeli coverage pairs praise for the dissolution deal with scepticism about minority protections and the fate of Kurdish women fighters, treating the Kurdish-language decree as a test of Damascus's sincerity rather than a settled win.
