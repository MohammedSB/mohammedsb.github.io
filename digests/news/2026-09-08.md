## Top Stories

### 1. [UK accuses Israeli ‘terrorists’ of ethnic cleansing in West Bank with government backing](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/08/uk-accuses-israeli-terrorists-ethnic-cleansing-west-bank-government-backing)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband announced a ban on imports from Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, coordinated with France and Canada, and told parliament that Israeli 'terrorists' are carrying out ethnic cleansing with tacit government backing. Twelve further countries reportedly endorsed restrictions on settlement trade, while the US and Israel criticised the move ahead of the announcement.

**Background:** The ban follows Miliband's call last week for a 'comprehensive reset' of UK policy on the West Bank, amid rising settler violence, demolitions and killings of Palestinians documented by the BBC and others.

**Why it matters:** It is the strongest UK condemnation of Israel on record and marks a coordinated Western shift from statements to trade measures, raising pressure on the EU and others to follow.

**Different views:** The Guardian foregrounds the 'ethnic cleansing' and 'terrorists' language and pairs it with UN rapporteur Albanese saying the ban must go further than a 'press release'; Al Jazeera frames it plainly as sanctions on 'illegal' settlements; the Jerusalem Post stresses the multi-country coordination and US and Israeli criticism, and, in the Albanese-related piece, notes the US sanctions imposed on her.


### 2. [Houthis strike Saudi Arabian cities in significant expansion of Middle East war](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/08/houthi-strike-saudi-arabia-oil-facilities-middle-east-war-iran)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Yemen's Houthis struck four Saudi cities overnight, wounding 73 people and setting oil installations ablaze, including claimed hits on Aramco facilities. Saudi Arabia and the coalition vowed firm retaliation as Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces pledged to advance on Sanaa.

**Background:** The Houthis are acting in support of Iran during the ongoing US-Israeli war on Tehran, and the strikes reverse a de facto Saudi-Houthi truce that had held since 2022.

**Why it matters:** It opens a new front that directly threatens Gulf energy infrastructure and the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, widening the war beyond Iran and the Levant.

**Different views:** The Guardian and Al Jazeera lead with the 'significant expansion' of the war and Saudi casualties, with Al Jazeera also platforming a Saudi analyst who insists the conflict will not be prolonged; the Jerusalem Post foregrounds the coalition's 'terrorist militia' language, the ground push against the Houthis, and the strategic risk to Red Sea shipping as a lever to relieve pressure on Iran.


### 3. [Qatar says reopening Strait of Hormuz ‘unconditionally’ is a priority](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/8/qatar-says-reopening-strait-of-hormuz-unconditionally-is-a-priority?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Qatar said unconditionally reopening the Strait of Hormuz is a diplomatic priority as shipping traffic dipped and Iran warned Gulf energy infrastructure is vulnerable. The UAE said its exports would not be 'held hostage' and Gulf states signalled the US alliance alone is not enough, while reports emerged of a covert four-month US Navy SEAL mine-clearing mission in the strait.

**Background:** The strait has been largely avoided by commercial shipping since the US and Israel attacked Iran in February, and Washington is pressing partners such as South Korea to back its naval blockade.

**Why it matters:** Gulf states are publicly diverging from Washington on how to end the war, and Hormuz remains the single biggest chokepoint for global energy prices.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera frames the issue through Qatari mediation and BRICS as the venue for another ceasefire; the Jerusalem Post foregrounds Iranian threats, US military operations and Gulf frustration with the US, treating Iran's warnings as the driver of the shipping slowdown; neither UK outlet covers the strait directly.
