1. Iran says it downed US drones over Hormuz as bid to end war remains stalled

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Iran says it shot down US drones over the Strait of Hormuz and repeated its conditions for reopening the waterway, while efforts to end the war remain stalled. Washington put sanctions on four Iranians it says acted as 'oil trustees' and is pressing Singapore and Malaysia over ship-to-ship transfers of Iranian oil. At the same time, Foreign Minister Araghchi says Iran still wants an agreement that would ensure there is no further war (JPost [35]).

Background: The war between the US, Israel and Iran has turned Hormuz, one of the world's busiest oil shipping lanes, into a battlefield. Tankers have been hit by missiles and mines, and oil slicks are visible from space.

Why it matters: Diplomacy is stuck, the US and Israel are reportedly weighing new joint strikes, and Democrats are moving to limit war funding before they take control of Congress. The next few weeks could bring either a settlement or a wider war, with global energy supplies at stake.

Different views: Al Jazeera calls it the 'US-Israeli war on Iran', leads with Iran's claims and conditions, and adds environmental damage. The Jerusalem Post focuses on US and Israeli planning for more strikes and an expanded regional coalition, quoting Israeli officials and former commanders, one of whom predicts a joint strike 'within two weeks', and it runs heroic B-2 pilot accounts. The Guardian frames Hormuz mainly as an environmental disaster.

2. Saudi-led forces strike Yemen port as Houthis warn airlines off the kingdom

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Saudi-led forces struck the Yemeni port of Hodeidah after telling civilians to leave the area. This came a day after the Houthis hit Riyadh's tallest skyscraper. The Houthis then told airlines to avoid Saudi airspace, and Saudi Arabia says it intercepted three ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh, plus more missiles and drones over its south.

Background: The Saudi-led coalition has fought the Houthis in Yemen since 2015, but direct cross-border attacks had largely stopped in recent years. This week's exchanges are a sharp escalation.

Why it matters: Renewed Saudi-Houthi fighting opens another front alongside the US-Israel-Iran war. It threatens Saudi civilian aviation and its capital, and it worsens one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post covers the escalation as a run of military exchanges, relying mostly on Saudi statements about interceptions and mentioning the Houthis' Al-Masirah TV reports of Saudi airstrikes on Sanaa only in passing. The BBC tells the conflict through civilians trapped in Taiz under a Houthi siege. None of the Al Jazeera items in this batch cover the Saudi-Houthi escalation.

3. Iraqi forces to deploy in Kataib Hezbollah stronghold under deal to ease militia tensions

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Iraqi security forces will deploy into a stronghold of the Kataib Hezbollah militia under a deal meant to ease tensions. Before the deal, Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi had ordered forces to prepare to move against the group, which officials accuse of running an oil smuggling network.

Background: Kataib Hezbollah is one of Iraq's most powerful Iran-aligned armed groups and has long operated outside full state control.

Why it matters: This is a key test of whether the Iraqi state can rein in Iran-aligned militias. It comes as the US is cracking down on Iranian oil networks and the region is at war, so a breakdown could pull Iraq further into the conflict.
