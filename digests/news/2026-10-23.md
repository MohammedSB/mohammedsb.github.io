### 1. [Iran threatens to strike oil and AI facilities across Gulf if US and Israel renew attacks](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/22/iran-threatens-seven-gulf-countries-us-israel-renew-attacks)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Iran's supreme national security council secretary Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran would strike oil facilities, undersea cables, datacentres and AI facilities across up to seven Gulf states if the US and Israel attack again, declaring 'zero red lines'. The threat coincides with a report that Washington and Israel are planning a short, roughly three-day strike campaign on Iran, including nuclear sites, after the US midterms, and Brent crude has climbed above $115 a barrel, its highest since May.

**Background:** US-Israeli strikes on Iran in February opened a war whose front lines have since shifted, with the IRGC claiming hits on vessels in the northern Gulf and ships striking mines near Hormuz.

**Why it matters:** A renewed round of strikes with Iran explicitly targeting Gulf energy and digital infrastructure would turn a bilateral confrontation into a region-wide economic war with direct consequences for global oil and data flows.

**Different views:** The Guardian foregrounds Iran's threats and the oil-price shock as evidence of escalation risk, treating Iranian officials' statements and market data as the authoritative account; the Jerusalem Post foregrounds the US-Israeli strike plan and reports Trump's claim that Iran is fabricating tanker attacks to inflate oil prices before the midterms, treating the Iranian claims as suspect; Al Jazeera frames the whole period analytically as a war whose strategic landscape has shifted since February rather than reporting the day's threats directly.


### 2. [Netanyahu orders ‘very extensive’ operation in West Bank after two Israeli soldiers killed](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/23/netanyahu-orders-very-extensive-operation-west-bank-two-israeli-soldiers-killed)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Two Israeli soldiers were killed by two Palestinian gunmen near a checkpoint outside Hebron, and both attackers were shot dead. Netanyahu responded by ordering the army to prepare a 'very extensive' operation in the occupied West Bank, four days before Israel's general election.

**Background:** Polls suggest Netanyahu's pro-settler coalition could lose to more critical opponents, and the vote is being described as one of the most pivotal in Israel's history; settler attacks on Palestinian olive harvesters have accelerated in the run-up.

**Why it matters:** A major West Bank operation launched days before the election could reshape the campaign's security framing and the turnout calculations of both the right and the Arab parties that may hold the balance of power.

**Different views:** The BBC reports the attack straightforwardly as a gun battle with the election as context, and the Guardian leads with Netanyahu's order for an extensive operation, implicitly framing it as a pre-election escalation; the Jerusalem Post's West Bank coverage the same day centres on settler violence against Palestinian farmers and a wounded Washington Post journalist, plus an arson attack on a Jewish holy site near Nablus, giving a picture of two-way violence rather than a single security incident.


### 3. [Some US diplomats told to leave Saudi Arabia amid Houthi attacks](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/22/us-diplomats-told-to-leave-saudi-arabia-amid-houthi-attacks)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The US State Department ordered some diplomats and families of US government employees to leave Saudi Arabia as Houthi attacks hit near-daily, killing at least three people at a Dammam airport and an oilfield. Similar withdrawals from the UAE, Jordan, Qatar and Bahrain were reported, and Saudi Aramco told staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters.

**Background:** The Houthis have been striking airports and energy sites across the kingdom, with Saudi Arabia conducting counter-strikes in Yemen that a defence official says are supported by Israeli intelligence.

**Why it matters:** Drawing down US diplomatic presence across five Gulf and Arab states signals Washington expects a wider war, and the attacks on Aramco's home base directly threaten the world's largest oil exporter's core operations.

**Different views:** The Guardian links the withdrawals to concern about a restart of the US war on Iran after the midterms and treats the diplomatic drawdown as the central story; the BBC reports the airport deaths as the latest in a Houthi campaign without the Iran-war framing; the Jerusalem Post adds a defence-official account of Israeli intelligence helping Saudi Arabia against the Houthis, foregrounding emerging Israeli-Saudi security cooperation that the UK outlets do not mention.
