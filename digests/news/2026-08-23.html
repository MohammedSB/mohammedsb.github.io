1. Iran denounces new US sanctions as Washington targets Tehran’s oil trade with China

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: Washington announced a new round of sanctions aimed at Iran's oil exports to China, part of what Trump has called "the most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country." Iran's armed forces chief of staff, MG Ali Abdollahi, warned the response would be "crushing, punishing and devastating," while Tehran separately threatened to treat any state that helps enforce the US measures as an enemy. Iranian President Pezeshkian publicly conceded the population faces "many problems," even as officials such as Mohsen Rezaee claimed Iran had earned billions by selling roughly 70 million barrels of oil around the blockade.

Background: The sanctions campaign follows the US-Iran war and is now focused on choking Tehran's remaining oil lifeline, principally Chinese buyers and shadow-fleet tankers in the Gulf. Iran has meanwhile been leveraging the Strait of Hormuz, including selectively permitting Iraqi tankers through.

Why it matters: Whether the blockade breaks Iran's finances or merely raises regional risk will shape oil markets, Gulf shipping security, and Tehran's calculations about renewed military escalation ahead of US midterms.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post foregrounds Iranian threats and regime claims of sanctions-busting as evidence of bad faith and vulnerability; the Guardian frames it as Iranian civilians bearing the cost of a Trump policy he is politically "stuck" in; Al Jazeera reframes it as an American "economic war" and asks how Iran can legitimately retaliate against complicit third countries.

2. Syria expects to resume negotiations with Israel, urges to seize ‘historic opportunity’ for deal

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani said in Damascus that Syria expects to resume negotiations with Israel and urged Israel to seize a "historic opportunity" for a deal, while stressing that Damascus's immediate priority is halting Israeli strikes. The comments came days after Israel bombed the Abu al-Duhur airbase — a strike Israeli sources said was aimed at preventing Turkey from installing a radar and troops there. US envoy Tom Barrack said the strike brought Israel and Turkey within moments of direct confrontation.

Background: Since Assad's fall, Syria's new government has pursued Western and regional reintegration while Israel has kept striking Syrian military sites and pressed for a demilitarised south. Turkey's expanding military footprint in Syria has become the main friction point with Israel.

Why it matters: A Syria-Israel security arrangement would be the region's biggest diplomatic shift since the Abraham Accords, but the Turkish presence risks turning Syria into a direct Israel-Turkey flashpoint.

Different views: Al Jazeera presents Shaibani as the constructive party with the "door open" to diplomacy, implicitly placing the onus on Israel; the Jerusalem Post pairs the same interview with a security rationale for the airbase strike (blocking Turkish radar) and internal Israeli criticism of Barrack's Syria and Golan positions, casting the bombing as legitimate deterrence rather than an obstacle to talks.

3. Hamas support plunges among Palestinians as Barghouti emerges as top Abbas successor - poll

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: New polling presented by Khalil Shikaki shows confidence in Hamas falling sharply among Palestinians alongside continued distrust of the Palestinian Authority, rising support for a negotiated settlement, and imprisoned Fatah figure Marwan Barghouti emerging as the leading potential successor to Mahmoud Abbas. In parallel, Hamas has opened talks with its long-time Fatah rival Mohammed Dahlan ahead of legislative elections set for 28 November 2026 — the first in two decades.

Background: The last Palestinian legislative election was in 2006, won by Hamas and followed by its 2007 takeover of Gaza and the split with the PA in Ramallah. Barghouti has led every credible leadership poll for years while serving multiple life sentences in Israeli prison.

Why it matters: A real vote would reset Palestinian leadership and the legitimacy question at the centre of any postwar Gaza arrangement or statehood push — and would force Israel, the PA and Arab mediators to take positions on Barghouti's release.

Different views: Israeli coverage emphasises Hamas's collapsing support and pragmatic factional manoeuvring with Dahlan, treating the numbers as vindication of pressure on Hamas; the same data read from a pro-Palestinian frame stresses that support for negotiations and for Barghouti reflects demand for unified, credible representation rather than endorsement of Israeli policy.
