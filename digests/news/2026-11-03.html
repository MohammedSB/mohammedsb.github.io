1. US hits Qeshm Island, Pezeshkian signals more diplomacy: Iran war latest

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

The strikes occurred on the eve of US midterm elections, with Vice President JD Vance simultaneously expressing optimism about a potential deal.

Background: The US and Iran have been in an active conflict, with Qatar serving as a key mediator. Both sides have been reviewing proposals to bridge differences, but significant gaps remain. Oil prices had already dropped over 6% on hopes of a diplomatic breakthrough before the strikes.

Why it matters: The simultaneous pursuit of military action and diplomacy creates a highly volatile and unpredictable situation, with the risk that either track could derail the other. The timing — on the eve of US midterms and amid active Qatari mediation — underscores how entangled domestic US politics are with Middle East war and peace decisions.

2. Israel has ‘historic’ opportunity to change direction, says leader of Arab coalition

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Following Israeli general elections, the Joint List — Israel's main Palestinian Arab political coalition — won a record number of Knesset seats and now holds the balance of power. Its leader, Yousef Jabareen, declared the country faces a 'historic opportunity to change direction' and expressed readiness to join an opposition bloc to oust Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

Background: Israeli politics has been deeply polarised over Netanyahu's leadership, the ongoing conflict with Iran and Gaza policy, and growing international pressure over settlement expansion. A separate poll found a majority of Israelis believe settlements policy should change to avoid further sanctions.

Why it matters: A record showing for the Joint List and its potential role as kingmaker would be unprecedented in Israeli political history and could fundamentally reshape Israeli governance, foreign policy, and the Palestinian issue. It also signals significant domestic Israeli discontent with current government direction.

Different views: The Guardian is the only outlet among the four to cover this story prominently, foregrounding the Palestinian Arab coalition's perspective and Jabareen's statements directly. 3. Trump’s troubles with Iran could open the door to China

The Guardian · UK

What happened: China has been conducting a flurry of high-level diplomatic meetings with Gulf leaders, seeking to deepen its influence in the Middle East as the US-Iran conflict creates uncertainty about American reliability as a regional partner. The diplomatic push comes ahead of an anticipated Trump-Xi meeting at the APEC conference.

Background: China brokered the landmark 2023 Saudi-Iran rapprochement and has steadily expanded its economic ties across the Gulf. The ongoing US-Iran war has disrupted energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint for roughly 20% of global oil supplies, giving Beijing additional leverage as Gulf states seek alternative partnerships.

Why it matters: A sustained Chinese diplomatic offensive in the Gulf could accelerate a long-term shift in regional alignment away from exclusive US security dependence, with significant consequences for global energy markets, US strategic posture, and the future architecture of Middle East diplomacy.
