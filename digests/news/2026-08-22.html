1. Over 750 US servicemen have been wounded in Iran war, Pentagon numbers reveal - report

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: Pentagon figures reported this week put US losses in the ongoing US-Iran war at 18 servicemen killed and 756 wounded, with the casualty count still climbing despite ceasefire diplomacy. The disclosure lands alongside Washington's announcement of what it calls its toughest-ever sanctions package on Iran, and Iranian statements that the war should end while Tehran holds a 'position of power'.

Background: The direct US-Iran conflict has drawn American forces and regional bases into sustained exchanges, with partial ceasefire efforts so far failing to halt attrition. Trump has paired military pressure with escalating economic warfare rather than a negotiated settlement.

Why it matters: A four-figure American casualty toll turns the war into a domestic political liability ahead of US midterms, and it shapes whether Washington escalates, freezes, or bargains — with consequences for Gulf shipping, oil prices, and every Iranian-aligned actor from Iraq to Lebanon.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post frames the numbers within a narrative of sanctions 'buying time' and Iranian weakness, the Guardian casts Trump as trapped in a quagmire of his own making that Tehran is happy to exploit before elections, while Al Jazeera relays Iran's charge that the sanctions are 'full-scale classic colonialism' violating other states' sovereignty.

2. International contention continues as eight more countries condemn Israel's E1 settlement plan

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: Eight more countries condemned Israel's E1 settlement plan, widening an international protest campaign against construction that would sever the northern and southern West Bank from each other and from East Jerusalem. A US State Department spokesperson told Israel's N12 that Washington 'does not support annexation', a notable public caveat from Israel's closest ally.

Background: E1 sits between Jerusalem and Ma'ale Adumim and has been frozen for years precisely because it is seen as terminal for a contiguous Palestinian state. Its revival comes amid a broader government-backed settlement push, including the re-establishment of previously closed outposts.

Why it matters: E1 is the clearest test of whether Western condemnation carries any cost for Israel, and the US 'does not support annexation' line signals the outer limit of American tolerance as more capitals move toward recognition of Palestinian statehood.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post presents this as 'international contention' — external pressure on a sovereign planning decision, foregrounding the US caveat as reassurance rather than rebuke; the BBC reports it as Israel defying growing protest with Palestinians nearby left fearful; the Guardian and former Western diplomats describe the same construction as ethnic cleansing and the 'erasure' of Palestine.

3. Israel 'baited' Turkey with Abu al-Duhur airbase 'pre-election' strike, US Envoy Tom Barrack says

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: US envoy Tom Barrack said Israel deliberately 'baited' Turkey with a pre-election strike on the Abu al-Duhur airbase in Syria, prompting Ankara to consider scrambling its own fighter jets. For several moments, Barrack said, two key US allies came close to a direct confrontation neither wants.

Background: Since Assad's fall in December 2024, Turkey has expanded its military footprint in Syria while Israel strikes to prevent hostile capabilities near its border, leaving no deconfliction mechanism between them of the kind Netanyahu built with Putin.

Why it matters: An Israeli-Turkish air incident would pit a NATO member against Israel and force Washington to choose between allies, making Syria — not Gaza or Iran — the likeliest venue for an unplanned regional escalation.

Different views: Israeli coverage treats the episode as a strategic 'Turkey test' of Netanyahu's deconfliction playbook and questions Erdogan's intentions, while Damascus signals its own hedging by weighing a Mecca joint defence pact and publicly rejecting closer ties with Iran.
