1. Fresh US-Iran talks are ‘last chance’ for Tehran to avoid war escalating, Trump says

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Trump announced that fresh US-Iran negotiations would begin within a day or two, calling them Tehran's "last chance" to reach a deal on reopening the Strait of Hormuz before US strikes escalate further, and warning of "total surrender" or "decapitation." Iran's foreign ministry flatly denied that any talks with Washington are underway or planned, saying its negotiators remain in the country and that contacts run only through Oman. US Treasury Secretary Bessent nonetheless claimed a Hormuz deal could be reached "by tomorrow," while Iranian projectiles struck a US base in Kuwait and a cargo vessel off Oman.

Background: Months of US airstrikes have followed the Iran war and Tehran's effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz, with repeated ceasefire and mediation attempts — including a new Pakistani push — collapsing amid deep mutual mistrust.

Why it matters: The gap between Trump's claims of imminent diplomacy and Iran's denials leaves the world's most important oil chokepoint closed and raises the odds of a further American escalation with no agreed off-ramp.

Different views: The Guardian frames Trump as bluffing and outmatched by a regime that refuses to give him negotiating ground, while the Jerusalem Post foregrounds his "before decapitation" ultimatum, Iranian claims of dead US soldiers, and Israeli assessments that Tehran sees strategic advantage; Al Jazeera catalogues Trump's repeated past "last chance" threats as a pattern of empty deadlines, and the BBC presents the dispute mainly as a factual contradiction between Washington and Tehran.

2. Palestine weekly: Israel kills dozens in Gaza after Hamas disarmament deal

Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian

What happened: Israeli strikes on Gaza intensified in the days after Trump announced that his Board of Peace had secured an agreement for the complete disarmament of Hamas and all other armed groups, killing dozens. Hamas spokesman Ghazi Hamad refused to commit to disarmament on Piers Morgan, saying the group accepted the deal out of necessity but would not change its goals. Board of Peace chief met Netanyahu to request an end to the strikes, while exiled official Mohammad Dahlan posted then deleted a claim that Kushner had secured an Israeli halt to attacks.

Background: The US-brokered agreement was billed as a breakthrough toward ending the Gaza war, with an international force and phased IDF withdrawal conditioned on decommissioning weapons and military infrastructure.

Why it matters: Both pillars of the deal — Hamas disarmament and a halt to Israeli operations — are being publicly disowned within days, putting the entire US-brokered framework at risk of collapse.

Different views: Al Jazeera leads with the death toll and the gap between the "breakthrough" billing and continued killing; the Jerusalem Post centres Hamas's refusal to disarm as proof of bad faith and reports bereaved and hostage families demanding an emigration option for Gazans before any international force enters; Qatar, Egypt and Turkey are quoted accusing Israel — and Netanyahu personally — of sabotaging the peace track.

3. Jerusalem’s most important religious site facing ‘imminent’ threat of Israeli takeover, says Jordan

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Jordan says the most important religious site in Jerusalem faces an "imminent" threat of Israeli takeover and has convened an emergency meeting of foreign ministers from across the Arab and Islamic world. Amman warned that such a move could trigger a religious conflict extending well beyond the current wars.

Background: Jordan holds the Hashemite custodianship over the Jerusalem holy site under longstanding arrangements, and any change to the status quo there has historically been the single most reliable trigger for region-wide escalation.

Why it matters: A status-quo change at the compound would inflame the conflict along religious rather than territorial lines and could break Israel's peace treaty with Jordan and its normalisation track with Gulf states.

Different views: In this cycle the story is carried by the Guardian on Jordan's terms as an imminent threat, with no Israeli government response or denial represented in Israeli coverage — an asymmetry worth noting as the story develops.
