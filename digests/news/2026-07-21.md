## Top Stories

### 1. [US launches 10th round of strikes as Iran says it is in ‘full-scale war’](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/20/us-strikes-iran-strait-of-hormuz-oil-prices)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US military launched its 10th consecutive night of strikes on Iran to force the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, prompting Iran's president to declare the country is in a 'full-scale war.' Iran retaliated by striking US allies and shipping, while the Pentagon acknowledged nearly 100 US service members injured over two weeks and Trump vowed retaliation over US soldier deaths.

**Background:** The conflict has escalated over successive nights of US bombing, tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, and Iranian missile/drone reprisals against US bases in Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait and Jordan's Aqaba airport.

**Why it matters:** A sustained US-Iran shooting war threatens global energy supplies via Hormuz, risks drawing in Gulf states and NATO bases in Turkey, and marks a dramatic regional escalation with diplomatic off-ramps still uncertain.

**Different views:** The Guardian and BBC foreground US casualties, oil-price spikes and 'de-escalation talks continuing,' while Jerusalem Post amplifies IRGC damage claims and Israeli-analyst framing of Iran playing 'good cop/bad cop'; Al Jazeera stresses US insistence it will keep attacking unless Trump decides otherwise.


### 2. [Hamas names Khalil al-Hayya as new overall leader](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cx25nzkyj50o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Hamas named its Qatar-based chief negotiator Khalil al-Hayya as its new overall leader, the first appointment to the top post since Yahya Sinwar was killed by Israeli forces in Gaza in 2024. Al-Hayya won by a single vote and must reconcile rival factions.

**Background:** The leadership had been vacant amid the war in Gaza and repeated Israeli killings of senior Hamas figures; al-Hayya is a veteran wartime negotiator based abroad.

**Why it matters:** The choice shapes Hamas's negotiating posture, its independence from Iran, and the trajectory of any ceasefire, with leadership sitting outside Gaza to preserve influence.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera frames the pick as a movement 'divided over Iran' that al-Hayya must bridge while preserving independence; Jerusalem Post analysis casts the abroad-based leadership as a way for Hamas to 'claim it handed over control and still maintain power behind the scenes.'


### 3. [Lebanese army says troops deploying in 'pilot zone' after Israeli withdrawal](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cy8mynlmn55o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** The Lebanese army announced it is deploying troops into a 'pilot zone' in southern Lebanon under a US-brokered agreement that should see Israeli forces withdraw from a small area occupied during the conflict with Hezbollah. The move coincides with President Joseph Aoun's visit to Washington, where he is meeting Trump.

**Background:** A 'framework agreement' announced on 26 June stipulated Israeli withdrawal; Aoun is seeking US backing to end fighting and verify Hezbollah disarmament.

**Why it matters:** It is a tangible test of whether the US-mediated framework can produce an actual Israeli withdrawal and restore Lebanese state control over the south.

**Different views:** BBC presents it as a straightforward US-brokered withdrawal pilot; the Guardian emphasizes Lebanese accusations that Israelis fired near its troops; Jerusalem Post frames the deployment primarily as verifying 'Hezbollah-free zones' and confirming disarmament.
