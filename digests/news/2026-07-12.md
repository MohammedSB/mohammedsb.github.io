## Top Stories

### 1. [US and Iran exchange strikes as Tehran declares strait of Hormuz closed again](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/12/us-and-iran-exchange-strikes-as-tehran-again-says-strait-of-hormuz-is-closed)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** The US launched a third round of aerial strikes on Iran after Iran struck a Cyprus-flagged container ship in the Strait of Hormuz, and Tehran declared the strait closed again, reversing an agreement signed last month. Iran also fired missiles and drones at US-linked sites across the Gulf, with attacks reported in Qatar, UAE, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman and Jordan, injuring several people from falling shrapnel.

**Background:** The exchange follows roughly a week of escalating hostilities and comes after an MoU signed last month was meant to restore maritime traffic and pave the way for a broader peace deal.

**Why it matters:** Closing the Strait of Hormuz threatens a critical global energy chokepoint and risks dragging Gulf states and the US into a wider regional war.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera frames the events as Iranian retaliation for US bombing ('Tehran hits back'), the Jerusalem Post frames Iran as the aggressor deliberately 'spreading the conflict as wide as possible,' while the Guardian and BBC take a more neutral tit-for-tat framing centered on the ship attack and the failed peace deal.


### 2. [Former Emir of Qatar Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani dies aged 74](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/former-emir-of-qatar-sheikh-hamad-bin-khalifa-al-thani-dies-at-74?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar · pro-Palestinian*

**What happened:** Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, the former Emir of Qatar who ruled from 1995 to 2013 before handing power to his son Sheikh Tamim, died aged 74. Qatar entered mourning and global leaders offered condolences praising his legacy.

**Background:** Sheikh Hamad transformed the small gas-rich Gulf state into a wealthy, diplomatically influential player, founding Al Jazeera and positioning Qatar as a key regional mediator.

**Why it matters:** His death marks the passing of the architect of Qatar's modern soft-power role at a moment when Doha's mediation is again central to Gulf and Iran-related diplomacy.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera (which he founded) offers largely celebratory coverage of his legacy and soft power, while the Jerusalem Post's analysis stresses more pointedly that he 'reshaped regional conflicts' and made Doha indispensable in ways that cut against the grain of the region.


### 3. [US begins process to remove Syria from terrorism list, signaling major shift in relations](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-902149)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** The US began the formal process of removing Syria from its state sponsors of terrorism list, signaling a major shift in bilateral relations. The notification reportedly included a commitment to remove obstacles blocking Syria's reconstruction.

**Background:** The move follows the post-Assad transition in Syria and comes alongside broader Western re-engagement, including Macron's Damascus visit and Russian and French economic interest in Syrian ports and investment.

**Why it matters:** Delisting would unlock reconstruction financing and international investment, potentially reshaping Syria's economy and its alignment between Western, Russian and Gulf interests.
