## Top Stories

### 1. [Iran says US strike killed four at wedding in 'war crime' as US denies targeting civilians](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cj06q4ynpmjo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** A US strike reportedly hit a private home in the southern Iranian coastal city of Sirik during a wedding celebration, killing at least four people — including a young child, with Iranian media reporting two children among the dead — and injuring 68. Iran called it a war crime; the US denied targeting civilians. Tehran responded by launching missiles and drones at US bases across the region, including a reported strike on a US base in Kuwait.

**Background:** The exchange follows US strikes on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, which shattered a month-long uneasy truce between Washington and Tehran.

**Why it matters:** The collapse of the truce and direct Iranian attacks on US bases in Gulf states risk pulling the entire region — and host governments like Kuwait, Iraq and Saudi Arabia — into a wider US-Iran war.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and the Guardian foreground Iranian casualty claims and the 'war crime' accusation, with the Guardian noting Iranian state media revised the toll and BBC Verify treating the footage cautiously alongside the US denial; the Jerusalem Post inverts the frame, leading on Iranian missile attacks on US bases and casting the strikes as IRGC attempts to push the US out of the region before a renewed war with Israel.


### 2. [Israel ‘seeking Trump’s support for plan to expel Palestinians from Gaza’](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/02/israel-seeking-trumps-support-for-plan-to-expel-palestinians-from-gaza)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Israeli Defence Minister Israel Katz said Israel is seeking Donald Trump's backing to approve plans to expel Palestinians from the Gaza Strip, arguing there is 'no real solution for Gaza in the end without this migration.' He suggested US support could tip the regional balance in favour of forced resettlement, a step widely condemned as a potential war crime.

**Background:** Forced transfer of an occupied population is prohibited under international humanitarian law; the proposal revives 'voluntary migration' schemes floated repeatedly since the start of the Gaza war.

**Why it matters:** Explicit ministerial advocacy for mass expulsion, contingent on US approval, would reshape the post-ceasefire diplomatic landscape and puts Washington's position at the centre of a potential mass displacement.

**Different views:** The Guardian frames the plan flatly as a proposed war crime and pairs it with calls for UK trade bans and BDS-style pressure; Israeli coverage largely sidesteps the expulsion framing, focusing instead on Hamas's failure to disarm and on IDF strikes killing Hamas commanders, while Al Jazeera situates it within a broader narrative of Israeli impunity and stalled EU action.


### 3. [Iranian attack on Saudi tanker killed two Filipino sailors, Saudi Arabia says](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cx2zxgg4mx9o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK · mainstream*

**What happened:** Saudi Arabia said an Iranian attack on the tanker Sidr in the Strait of Hormuz killed two Filipino sailors, and condemned the targeting of civilian shipping. The IRGC separately said two oil tankers were disabled after hitting sea mines in the strait, and the US has reportedly begun a 'tanker for tanker' policy, striking Iranian vessels in retaliation rather than merely interdicting them under CENTCOM's blockade.

**Background:** The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of global oil supply; a blockade and mining campaign there has been a central feature of the current US-Iran confrontation.

**Why it matters:** Civilian seafarer deaths and mined shipping lanes internationalise the conflict, drag Gulf states off the fence, and threaten global energy supply in a way that ground strikes alone do not.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post and BBC attribute the tanker attack to Iran and emphasise Saudi condemnation and US retaliation; Al Jazeera instead frames Gulf insecurity structurally, arguing the 'no-war, no-peace' status quo with Iran chiefly benefits the US energy sector and Israel.
