1. US military strikes cargo ship from Bandar Abbas in Gulf of Oman, says it tried to breach blockade

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: US Central Command says it struck the Palau-flagged cargo ship Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman after it left Iran's Bandar Abbas port and ignored repeated warnings, which CENTCOM says was an attempt to breach the US blockade. The strike disabled the ship's propulsion, and no crew were injured. Around the same time, senior IRGC officials vowed to break the blockade and said Iran's ports remain 'open'. The IRGC also said a tanker hit a naval mine while trying to transit the Strait of Hormuz outside the authorised lanes.

Background: The US has imposed a naval blockade on Iranian shipping during the wider US–Iran war. The fight over the Strait of Hormuz has pushed fuel prices up worldwide, and Iranian strikes on US positions in the Gulf, including a base in Bahrain, have continued despite talks between Washington and Tehran.

Why it matters: A direct US strike on a ship running the blockade, a mine hit in the strait and IRGC promises to break through all point to a growing risk of naval clashes in one of the world's most important energy routes. The economic damage is already spreading, including airline failures.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post builds its account on official statements from both sides. It gives CENTCOM's version of the strike, IRGC claims that ports are open and the blockade is being broken, and the IRGC's explanation that the mined tanker strayed outside authorised lanes. Al Jazeera's related coverage leaves the military events aside and covers the crisis through its economic effects, calling it the 'Iran war' and reporting that Norse Atlantic has shut down because of fuel costs tied to the Strait of Hormuz crisis.

2. Saudis intercept Houthi cruise missile fired from Yemen in latest attack from Iran-backed group

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Saudi Arabia intercepted a Houthi cruise missile fired from Yemen. A day earlier, a projectile hit Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery, killing a Nepalese worker and wounding several others. Separately, an armed group boarded and seized a cargo ship off Balhaf, Yemen, according to UKMTO.

Background: The Houthis are attacking Saudi territory again as part of the wider regional conflict involving Iran. Meanwhile, fighting continues inside Yemen between government forces and the Houthis.

Why it matters: Strikes on Saudi energy sites and shipping off Yemen are pulling the kingdom further into the regional war. They also add to the danger in the waterways around the Arabian Peninsula while Hormuz is already contested.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post focuses on the attacks themselves: the interception, the deadly refinery strike and the casualties. It consistently calls the Houthis 'Iran-backed', which places the attacks inside the conflict with Tehran. The BBC reports from Riyadh instead, about how the attacks are testing the kingdom's image of security. It says markets and restaurants stay busy and treats residents' trust in the government's ability to protect them as the main story.

3. Momentous decision looms after judges call for Netanyahu charges to be dropped

The Guardian · UK

What happened: The judges in Benjamin Netanyahu's corruption trial have asked prosecutors to end the proceedings, which is a highly unusual step. The attorney general now has to decide whether to drop the charges of bribery, fraud and breach of trust.

Background: Netanyahu's corruption trial has run for years and has overshadowed Israeli politics. Al Jazeera reports at the same time that coalition talks between Gadi Eisenkot and Netanyahu have stalled over a rotation deal.

Why it matters: Whatever the attorney general decides could reshape Israel's political landscape and coalition talks. It could also set a precedent for how Israeli courts deal with cases against sitting leaders.
