1. Saudi Arabia further suspends flights after latest deadly Houthi attack on airport

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Saudi Arabia has suspended all flights to and from King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh until next Saturday after a new round of Houthi missile attacks. The earlier strike on 10 October killed 13 people and wounded more than 300 (the Jerusalem Post puts the toll at 14 across several attacks). Saudi ministers have promised a 'serious response', and the Houthis say 'the worst is yet to come'. They have also declared Saudi Aramco oil facilities legitimate targets.

Background: The strikes come as the Riyadh-backed Yemeni government pushes a counteroffensive on the ground, including advances in Marib, while Saudi Arabia runs an air campaign against the Iran-backed Houthis.

Why it matters: This is a major escalation that has exposed gaps in Saudi air defences and pulled in other players: Pakistan has pledged military backing, the US FAA has issued warnings, and Iran has offered to mediate. It raises the risk of a wider Gulf war and of attacks on energy infrastructure.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post calls the Houthis a 'terror group' and stresses their Iranian backing, their threats against Aramco and the US, and the regional alliances forming around Riyadh. The BBC and the Guardian call them an 'armed group', 'rebels' or a 'paramilitary group' and focus on why Saudi defences are failing and on the Yemeni government counteroffensive. Al Jazeera's coverage in this batch has no item on the airport strike itself. It covers the ground war instead, including Houthi shelling of homes and hospitals in Taiz, government advances in Marib and families of the missing, plus Iran's threat to Gulf data centres.

2. Israeli forces raid Palestinian NGOs in occupied West Bank

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Israeli forces raided several Palestinian NGO offices in Ramallah in the occupied West Bank. The Palestinian news agency Wafa reported snipers posted outside the entrances to the Palestine Medical Complex.

Background: Ramallah is the administrative seat of the Palestinian Authority. Israeli military operations in the West Bank and pressure on Palestinian civil-society groups have been a recurring source of international criticism.

Why it matters: Raids on NGOs and deployments near a major hospital in the PA's seat of government suggest pressure on Palestinian civil society is intensifying. They are likely to strain Israel's relations with European and other donors who fund these groups.

Different views: In this batch, only Al Jazeera reports the raids, with a correspondent in Ramallah, and it relies on Palestinian sources (Wafa) for details. No Israeli military explanation appears in the coverage provided, and neither the Jerusalem Post, the BBC nor the Guardian carries the story here.

3. NAZA: Netanyahu orders identification and prosecution of film’s interviewees

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has ordered the attorney general and the head of the Shin Bet security service to identify the soldiers and officers interviewed in NAZA, an investigative documentary produced by the Guardian. He wants them prosecuted for causing 'harm to Israel'.

Background: The documentary includes testimony from Israeli military personnel about conduct during the Gaza war, which has already produced genocide allegations, including from a UN commission linked to Nobel Peace Prize winner Navi Pillay.

Why it matters: A head of government directing the security services to unmask and prosecute military whistleblowers raises serious press-freedom and accountability concerns. It could also discourage future testimony about wartime conduct.

Different views: This report comes from the Guardian, which produced the film, so the outlet is covering a story it is part of. The Jerusalem Post does not cover the order in this batch. Its Gaza-accountability coverage here instead focuses on Hamas praising Navi Pillay over the genocide allegations.
