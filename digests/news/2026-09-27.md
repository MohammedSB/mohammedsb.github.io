### 1. [Trump rejects Iran’s seven-day peace deal to reopen strait of Hormuz](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/26/trump-dismiss-iran-seven-day-peace-deal-hormuz)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** President Trump said he rejected an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and resume nuclear talks. In return, the US would have lifted its naval blockade of Iranian ports. Reports say he expects renewed strikes on Iran after the US midterm elections. Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi said Tehran was still waiting for mediators to pass on a formal US response.

**Background:** The US–Iran conflict is in about its sixth month. A US naval blockade of Iran's southern ports and disruption in the Strait of Hormuz have choked off Iran's oil exports and pushed it to reroute trade through the Caspian Sea.

**Why it matters:** Rejecting the offer shuts a possible route out of a war that affects global energy supply through Hormuz. It also points to more fighting later in the year rather than a negotiated pause.

**Different views:** The Guardian leads with Trump's rejection and the report that he expects strikes after the midterms. The Jerusalem Post quotes Trump saying Iran 'has no money coming in', and its analysis explains the stall as Washington wanting more to show for six months of conflict. The Post also carries Iranian officials' statements (Araghchi still awaiting a formal reply, Pezeshkian saying Tehran no longer trusts talks) alongside an IRGC claim to have seized a US underwater drone. Al Jazeera covers the story mainly through the blockade's economic effect on Iran and its move to Caspian trade routes.


### 2. [Saudi coalition says it intercepts Houthi missiles, drones targeting Khamis Mushait, Riyadh](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-909759)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli*

**What happened:** The Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted Houthi missiles and drones aimed at Khamis Mushait and Riyadh. The attack came hours after military chiefs from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey met in Riyadh to review threats to the kingdom. Schools in Riyadh switched to remote learning without warning, and Yemeni government forces say they widened air and ground attacks on the Houthis in Taiz.

**Background:** Saudi–Houthi fighting has flared again after a period of relative calm. Riyadh has turned to defence partners such as Pakistan and Turkey for its security.

**Why it matters:** Houthi strikes reaching the Saudi capital, together with a renewed government offensive inside Yemen, suggest the Yemen war is escalating again. That would add another active front to a region already shaken by the US–Iran war.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post relies on the Saudi coalition's account of the interceptions. It also covers disruption inside Riyadh, and one of its analyses argues the attacks expose the limits of Saudi diplomacy and create an opening for Israeli intelligence and defence cooperation. Al Jazeera looks at it from inside Yemen: it reports the Yemeni government's claims about operations in Taiz and Djibouti's appeal for help with Yemeni refugees, putting the humanitarian cost in front rather than the Gulf security angle. The Guardian has no coverage in this set.


### 3. [‘No role for UNRWA in Gaza’: Does the Board of Peace toe Israeli lines?](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/27/no-role-for-unrwa-in-gaza-does-the-board-of-peace-toe-israeli-lines?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** The Board of Peace overseeing Gaza has excluded UNRWA, the UN agency for Palestinian refugees, from any role in the territory. Al Jazeera's piece asks whether the decision shows the body adopting Israel's positions.

**Background:** Israel has long pushed to sideline UNRWA, accusing some of its staff of links to Hamas. The agency has been central to aid delivery and services for Gaza's refugee population for decades.

**Why it matters:** Gaza is facing a massive reconstruction job, including an estimated 61 million tonnes of rubble and up to 10,000 bodies still buried. Excluding its biggest aid agency changes who delivers relief and services there, and it raises questions about how neutral the post-war governance body is.

**Different views:** Among these sources, only Al Jazeera covers the decision. It frames the exclusion as lining up with repeated Israeli demands. Neither the Guardian nor the Jerusalem Post reports it here, though the Guardian's Gaza coverage centres on the UN clearance effort and civilian daily life.
