### 1. [Lightning Houthi advance in Yemen may bring dangerous new dimension to Iran war](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3v4zgzr1kxo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Houthi forces seized control of Yemen's Red Sea coast in a rapid offensive, including the strategic island of Perim in the Bab al-Mandab strait, pushing out the Saudi-backed government. Yemeni government forces responded with air strikes near Mocha, while Iran publicly celebrated the advance as a 'divine triumph.' Reports indicate Trump declined a Saudi request for direct US strikes on the Houthis.

**Background:** The offensive comes amid an ongoing US-Israel war on Iran and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which has made Saudi Arabia dependent on Red Sea export routes that now run past Houthi-held territory.

**Why it matters:** Iran-aligned control over both Bab al-Mandab and Hormuz would put the two chokepoints for Gulf oil exports under pressure simultaneously, and the fighting has already killed or wounded over 1,000 people and displaced tens of thousands.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera's coverage foregrounds the Houthi and Iranian celebrations and the civilian toll while noting Iranian officials have not spoken of closing Bab al-Mandab; the BBC frames it as a 'dangerous new dimension to the Iran war' and the Houthis' most impressive military coup in years; the Guardian frames the Houthis as 'Iran's allies' seizing a route Saudi Arabia relies on and leads its humanitarian piece with nearly 50,000 displaced; the Jerusalem Post focuses on Washington's refusal to strike the Houthis at Saudi request.


### 2. [Saudi Arabia shuts critical oil pipeline after drone attack: What it means](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/12/saudi-arabia-shuts-critical-oil-pipeline-after-drone-attack-what-happened?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Saudi Arabia shut its East-West oil pipeline after a drone attack that Riyadh says was launched from Iraqi territory near the Iranian border. Iraq dismissed a military commander, opened investigations, and closed the Shalamcheh border crossing with Iran, while Trump said Iran was probably 'behind' the attack.

**Background:** Riyadh had sharply increased use of the East-West pipeline to Yanbu on the Red Sea to bypass the closure of the Strait of Hormuz during the US-Israel war with Iran.

**Why it matters:** With Hormuz shut and the Red Sea route now threatened by Houthi gains, the pipeline was Saudi Arabia's main remaining export lifeline, and the attack from Iraqi soil risks drawing Baghdad deeper into the regional conflict.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and the Guardian foreground the economic mechanics and Saudi reliance on the pipeline as a Hormuz bypass, with Al Jazeera separately reporting Trump's attribution to Iran as his claim; the BBC and Jerusalem Post foreground Iraqi accountability, leading with the dismissed commander and border closure, and the Jerusalem Post adds the Turkey-Saudi-Pakistan mutual defense pact as relevant context.


### 3. [Israel-Lebanon talks postponed as explosions rock southern Lebanon](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/12/israel-lebanon-talks-postponed-as-explosions-rock-southern-lebanon?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** Israel said it destroyed a major underground Hezbollah base in southern Lebanon in a blast so large it registered as a magnitude 4.1 earthquake. Planned Israel-Lebanon talks were postponed, with President Aoun saying there would be 'no new negotiations with Israel at the moment,' even as the US State Department said an upcoming round would move from Rome to Washington.

**Background:** A first round of direct Israel-Lebanon talks was held in Rome on August 4, an unusual step given the two countries have no diplomatic relations and Israel continues to strike Hezbollah targets in Lebanon.

**Why it matters:** The strike and the postponement suggest the fragile diplomatic track is faltering under military pressure, raising the risk of renewed wider escalation on Israel's northern front while the Iran war continues.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera leads with the postponement and Aoun's refusal to negotiate, treating the explosions as the cause; the BBC leads with Israel's account of destroying a Hezbollah base and the seismic scale of the blast; the Jerusalem Post treats the talks as still on track, citing the State Department's venue change to Washington and a source describing the negotiations as 'positive,' without mentioning Aoun's statement.
