## Top Stories

### 1. [Iran says it targeted US military radar in Bahrain in retaliatory strike](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/13/iran-us-radar-bahrain-sirik-hormuz)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps says it targeted and destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, in retaliation for a US airstrike on a communications tower in Sirik overlooking the Strait of Hormuz. US CENTCOM confirmed that Iran targeted the radar. It is described as Iran's first direct attack on US military installations in months.

**Background:** The strike comes amid the ongoing US-Iran war and a US blockade, with the Strait of Hormuz an active flashpoint where Iran has reported repeated mine strikes on tankers.

**Why it matters:** A direct Iranian strike on a US installation in a Gulf host state breaks a months-long pause in such attacks and risks a new escalation cycle that could draw in Gulf states hosting US forces.

**Different views:** The Guardian leads with the IRGC's own account, including its claim that the radar was destroyed, and presents the strike as a response to a US airstrike on Sirik. The Jerusalem Post leads with CENTCOM's confirmation that the radar was targeted and attributes the triggering US strike to the Guards' description ('what they described as an air attack') rather than stating it as fact.


### 2. [UN says 25 seafarers killed in, around Hormuz since US-Israel war on Iran](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/14/un-says-25-seafarers-killed-in-around-hormuz-since-february?traffic_source=rss)
*Al Jazeera English · Qatar*

**What happened:** UN official Khaled Khiari said 25 seafarers have been killed in and around the Strait of Hormuz since the start of the war on Iran, warning the situation 'cannot become the new normal'. Iran reported three more vessels hitting mines, and Iranian media separately reported tankers, including a liquefied gas tanker, struck by explosions in the waterway.

**Background:** Hormuz is a chokepoint for global oil and gas shipping; Iran has claimed a string of mine strikes on tankers in recent weeks while the US maintains a blockade.

**Why it matters:** The mounting toll on civilian crews and the threat to commercial passage carry consequences for global energy supply and for any effort to de-escalate the war.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera foregrounds the UN's death toll for seafarers and calls the conflict the 'US-Israel war on Iran'. The Jerusalem Post foregrounds the individual tanker incidents, stresses that Iran's mine-strike claims are not independently verified and are a means of pressuring the US over its blockade, and relays the IRGC's line that the vessels took an 'unauthorized' route and ignored warnings.


### 3. [UN watchdog 'increasingly concerned' over uninspected Iran nuclear site](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cm75261e3pdko?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** The UN nuclear watchdog, the IAEA, said it is 'increasingly concerned' about a uranium enrichment plant at Isfahan that Iran announced more than a year ago and that inspectors have never been able to visit. The agency said it does not know the plant's exact location, its safeguards status, or whether it contains nuclear material.

**Background:** Iran's nuclear programme is a central issue in its confrontation with the US and Israel, and the IAEA's ability to verify it depends on inspector access.

**Why it matters:** An enrichment site that inspectors cannot locate or verify leaves a gap in international oversight during an active war, which could sharpen the dispute over Iran's nuclear programme.

**Different views:** Both the BBC and the Jerusalem Post treat the IAEA as the authoritative source and report the same core facts. The BBC headline uses the agency's measured wording ('increasingly concerned'), while the Jerusalem Post headline says the IAEA 'raises alarm' and quotes the agency's admission that it does not know the plant's location or contents.
