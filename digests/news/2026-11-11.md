### 1. [Trump says more US attacks on Iran 'highly unlikely'](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cq5yny4nl2d3o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** President Trump said further US attacks on Iran are 'highly unlikely', describing a 'status quo' while remaining 'locked and loaded' if the situation changes. In parallel, Secretary of State Rubio told reporters after G7 meetings in Paris that major combat operations are unnecessary given the US economic pressure campaign, and Qatar's prime minister told G7 ministers that Doha had secured a two-week window to prevent a US attack, with key differences between Washington and Tehran still unresolved.

**Background:** The US-Iran war began in late February and has since shifted towards an economic campaign led by the US Treasury, including pressure on Iraq to close border crossings with Iran. Shipping in the Gulf has been hit repeatedly, with the International Maritime Organization confirming 130 incidents and at least 25 seafarers killed.

**Why it matters:** Whether the pause hardens into a negotiated deal or collapses back into strikes determines the security of the Gulf, global shipping and energy flows. The signals remain mixed: Iran is unveiling an underground 'missile city' and warning of retaliation beyond the region, and Treasury Secretary Bessent has reportedly been moved to a military base over an Iranian threat.

**Different views:** The BBC leads with Trump's own words about strikes being unlikely, while the Jerusalem Post spreads the story across several pieces that foreground Rubio's insistence that economic pressure is not a stalemate, Qatar's mediating role, IRGC missile displays and a former Mossad official's assessment that a deal is more likely. On the Bessent threat, Al Jazeera's summary puts 'Iranian threats' in quotation marks and highlights his $12.5m mansion, the Guardian stresses his role as the driving force behind the effort to cripple Iran's economy, and the Jerusalem Post foregrounds US officials' view that Iran's ability to mount large attacks abroad is limited.


### 2. [Israeli opposition hurries to seize control as new Knesset is sworn in](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/10/israeli-opposition-hurries-to-seize-control-as-new-knesset-is-sworn-in)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Israel's new Knesset was sworn in and opposition lawmakers immediately used their majority to move to take control of the legislature from Benjamin Netanyahu and his allies, including requesting a vote for a new speaker. Former military chief Gadi Eisenkot holds the first mandate to try to form a government, with Netanyahu next in line if he fails.

**Background:** The recent election left the anti-Netanyahu bloc with a parliamentary majority but too divided to agree easily on a coalition. The speaker controls the legislative agenda, which makes the post a key lever during coalition talks.

**Why it matters:** This is a serious chance to end Netanyahu's tenure, with direct consequences for Israeli policy on Gaza, Lebanon and Iran. A failure by Eisenkot could return the initiative to Netanyahu or prolong the political deadlock.

**Different views:** The Guardian frames the moment as the opposition 'seizing control' and focuses on the speakership as a route to ousting Netanyahu, while stressing the opposition's divisions. The BBC presents it more procedurally, as leaders competing to form a government with Eisenkot getting the first chance. No Jerusalem Post article on the swearing-in appears in this set.


### 3. [Thousands without shelter face lethal conditions in storm-hit Gaza as they wait for 150,000 tents](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2026/nov/11/thousands-without-shelter-lethal-cold-storm-gaza-israel-tents-displaced-people-flood)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Deadly weekend storms left tens of thousands of displaced Palestinians in waterlogged and damaged tents, with at least four children and a woman reported killed. Aid agencies say 150,000 shelters are being held outside Gaza awaiting Israeli approval, and the Trump-created Board of Peace issued rare criticism, calling Israel's aid response 'insufficient' while also blaming Hamas for civilian suffering.

**Background:** More than 940,000 displaced people are living in flood-prone areas after the war destroyed much of Gaza's housing and drainage infrastructure. UNICEF says Israel is blocking repairs to facilities such as the Sheikh Radwan stormwater basin, which is contaminated with sewage and at risk of overflowing.

**Why it matters:** The floods are a test of whether the Board of Peace and the wider Trump peace plan can deliver basic relief, and the board's criticism of Israel is unusual. With winter only beginning, the risk of disease and further deaths is high.

**Different views:** The Guardian and Al Jazeera foreground aid agencies' accounts, the shelters awaiting Israeli approval and the collapse of infrastructure, and Al Jazeera's summary does not mention Hamas. The BBC centres the Board of Peace statement and notes that it faults both Israel and Hamas. The Jerusalem Post reports UNICEF's accusation that Israel is blocking repairs, but its analysis piece frames the crisis around Hamas still ruling Gaza and the peace plan stalling.
