1. Iran denies dropping preconditions amid ‘very productive’ three-hour UN talks in New York

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi and Trump's envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held three hours of surprise talks in New York, with Qatar mediating, on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly. Trump called the talks 'very productive' and said more could follow. Brent crude fell below $100 a barrel for the first time in two weeks. Tehran denied that it had dropped its earlier preconditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and it set out conditions for ending the war that include lifting sanctions.

Background: These are the first US-Iran talks since a ceasefire collapsed in June and a memorandum of understanding failed in July. Since then the two sides have exchanged fire on and off. The talks came right after Trump threatened to 'annihilate' Iran and President Pezeshkian told the UN that Iran would never 'bend the knee'.

Why it matters: This is the first real diplomatic opening in the US-Iran war in months. Its outcome affects the Strait of Hormuz, global oil prices and regional security, from the Gulf to Iraq's Kurdistan region.

Different views: The Guardian leads with Iran's denial that it gave up preconditions, the oil-price drop and Qatar's role as mediator. It also presents Pezeshkian as calling the US the real 'bully' while offering dialogue. The BBC frames the talks and the speech mainly as a response to Trump's 'annihilation' threat and highlights Iran's refusal to 'bend the knee'. The Jerusalem Post runs a cautiously hopeful news report alongside an expert who argues the IRGC gains from 'false hopes' of negotiation, and it gives prominence to Rubio's reassurances to Saudi Arabia.

2. Trump's Board of Peace unveils $2.45bn Gaza recovery plan

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: Trump's Board of Peace announced a $2.45bn Gaza recovery plan at a board meeting on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly. Board chief Nickolay Mladenov urged countries to back it. According to Axios, the plan has 66 projects covering temporary housing, rubble removal, hospital repairs and deployment of an International Stabilization Force. Hamas said it was 'ready' to move forward with the Board's ceasefire roadmap.

Background: The Board of Peace is the US-led body set up to oversee Gaza's post-war transition. The war is approaching its fourth year, and UN agencies report worsening humanitarian conditions, including a surge in violence against women and in forced marriages.

Why it matters: The plan is the most concrete international framework so far for Gaza's reconstruction and security. Whether it succeeds depends on donor money, a stabilisation force being deployed and both Hamas and Israel accepting the roadmap.

Different views: The BBC reports mainly the headline figure and the setting of the announcement. The Jerusalem Post gives more detail on the plan's security part, the stabilisation force, and calls Hamas a 'terror group' while reporting the Hamas spokesperson's claim that Israel is escalating and that the Board cannot restrain it. The Guardian does not cover the plan in this set. Instead it runs first-person accounts of daily life in Gaza and promotes a documentary about Israel's 'mass killing of civilians', putting the focus on Palestinians' experience rather than diplomatic plans.

3. Sharaa: October 7 cannot dictate Israel's Syria policy, talks reached 90% agreement

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli

What happened: Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa revealed that Syria and Israel have been in direct talks for nearly a year and have reached '90% agreement'. He said the 7 October attacks cannot be allowed to dictate Israel's policy toward Syria. He called on Israel to change its security approach and insisted on a return to the 1974 disengagement lines.

Background: Since the fall of Assad's government, Israeli forces have operated beyond the 1974 disengagement line in southern Syria. Neighbouring states have also raised alarm: Jordan's King Abdullah warned at the UN about Israel's actions in Syria.

Why it matters: Syria's leader has publicly confirmed advanced direct talks with Israel. That points to a possible security agreement, one that would redraw a front line frozen for decades and shift regional alignments.
