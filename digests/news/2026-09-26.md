### 1. [Trump reportedly rejects Iran’s seven-day peace deal to reopen strait of Hormuz](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/26/trump-dismiss-iran-seven-day-peace-deal-hormuz)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Iran offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days and restart nuclear talks. In return it wants the US to lift its naval blockade of Iranian ports. Reports say Donald Trump has rejected the offer and expects to resume strikes after the US midterm elections, although Tehran says it has not yet received an official US reply.

**Background:** The proposal follows the US-Israel war with Iran and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz. Foreign Minister Araghchi says it builds on a US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding from June and would only go ahead if certain conditions are met.

**Why it matters:** About a fifth of the world's oil normally passes through Hormuz. Rejecting the plan points to renewed US-Iran fighting after November, with knock-on effects for Lebanon, the Gulf and energy markets.

**Different views:** The Guardian foregrounds Trump's reported expectation of renewed strikes and the hard concessions the deal would demand, including reining in Israel in Lebanon. Al Jazeera frames it around analysts forecasting post-midterm attacks and, elsewhere, gives Pezeshkian room to say Iran no longer trusts talks and that Qatar and Pakistan are carrying its messages. The Jerusalem Post also reports the rejection, but gives weight to a US official calling the mediated talks 'positive, constructive'. It pairs this with an Iranian official ruling out nuclear concessions, which presents Tehran as the side refusing to compromise.


### 2. [Saudi Arabia intercepts wave of Houthi missiles as oil climbs to one-week high](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/25/saudi-arabia-intercepts-houthi-missiles-oil-prices-climbs)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Saudi Arabia says it intercepted a wave of Houthi ballistic missiles and drones aimed at Taif, the Yanbu area on the Red Sea, Khamis Mushait and Riyadh. Oil prices rose to a one-week high. Hours earlier, military chiefs from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan met in Riyadh to review threats to the kingdom.

**Background:** The Iran-backed Houthis have been attacking Yemen's Saudi-backed government and firing into Saudi territory. Yanbu is the main route Saudi oil can use to avoid the Gulf.

**Why it matters:** Strikes on Saudi Arabia's alternative export route, while Hormuz is disrupted, raise the risk of a wider supply shock. They are also pushing Riyadh to firm up a Saudi-Turkish-Pakistani security bloc outside its reliance on the US.

**Different views:** The Guardian leads on oil markets and how exposed Yanbu is. Al Jazeera describes the Riyadh meeting as a 'Mecca defence alliance' of Muslim powers backing Saudi Arabia. The Jerusalem Post runs the same meeting next to a Washington Post report that Riyadh has not ruled out nuclear weapons. It adds an analysis arguing the attacks show the limits of Saudi diplomacy and create an opening for Israeli intelligence and defence cooperation.


### 3. [Netanyahu defends Israeli military action as delegates walk out before UN speech](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3y0z7kxlegdo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Benjamin Netanyahu defended Israel's military campaigns at the UN General Assembly. Delegates walked out en masse as he began, and he called them 'moral cowards'. Outside, large protests led to more than 100 arrests, and he travelled and spoke without being detained despite an ICC arrest warrant.

**Background:** The International Criminal Court has an arrest warrant out for Netanyahu over the Gaza war, which is now in its third year. Washington has reportedly drawn up sanctions against the court.

**Why it matters:** The walkout and protests show how isolated Israel is diplomatically. The fact that he was not arrested shows how little power the ICC has to enforce warrants when a major power objects.

**Different views:** The BBC centres Netanyahu's own defence and quotes his words directly, pairing this with a neutral legal explainer on why the warrant wasn't enforced. The Guardian calls the speech 'inflammatory' and leads with the walkout, arrests and celebrity protesters. Al Jazeera, in related coverage, points out that Israeli politicians facing ICC warrants can still stand in elections while Arab parties are barred. The Jerusalem Post articles in this set don't cover the speech itself.
