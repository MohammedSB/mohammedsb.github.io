**Background:** The US-Israeli strikes on Iran have unsettled the broader Middle East, prompting regional powers to recalibrate their security postures. Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan all have complex relationships with both the US-Israeli coalition and Iran.

**Why it matters:** A formal defense coordination mechanism among Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan would represent a significant new security architecture in the Islamic world, with implications for how the Iran conflict could expand or be contained. It also reflects the limits of US influence over regional allies' independent security calculations.
