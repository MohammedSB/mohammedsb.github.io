### 1. [Yemen’s anti-Houthi forces launch inquest into sudden military collapse](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/18/yemen-anti-houthi-forces-inquest-military-collapse)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** Yemen's anti-Houthi forces have opened a bitter inquest after a much-publicised offensive on Sana'a collapsed into a rout, with more than 15 brigades defeated; some politicians allege that commanders were paid billions to abandon positions. The Houthi advance toward the Red Sea strait has triggered a Saudi-hosted multinational naval coalition meeting, an EU Aspides alert-level rise, air-defence alerts over Riyadh, a suspension of Saudi crude deliveries to Europe after an attack on the East-West pipeline, and UN reports of 112,000 newly displaced Yemenis.

**Background:** The internationally recognised anti-Houthi coalition had billed the Sana'a push as decisive after years of stalemate since the 2022 truce, while the Houthis have used their position astride Bab al-Mandeb to threaten global shipping since 2023.

**Why it matters:** Houthi consolidation near the strait gives Iran's closest remaining regional ally leverage over roughly a tenth of world trade and over Saudi energy exports, and it is already pulling Gulf states, the EU and possibly Pakistan into a widening confrontation.

**Different views:** The Guardian foregrounds internal failure — corruption, bribery and broken coordination inside the anti-Houthi camp — treating Yemeni politicians and officials as its sources, while the Jerusalem Post frames the same advance as an externally directed Iranian project coordinated with Iraqi militias, leaning on Israeli security assessments; Al Jazeera centres the Saudi-led naval response and shipping protection rather than either the rout or Iranian command, and the humanitarian toll appears mainly through UN figures rather than as a consequence attributed to any party.


### 2. [Why US plan to sell F-35 warplanes to Saudi Arabia is controversial](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c3x2zrn01pxko?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
*BBC Middle East · UK*

**What happened:** Washington advanced a possible $24.3 billion sale of 48 F-35 fighter jets to Saudi Arabia, moving ahead despite Israeli officials' efforts to make the sale conditional on Saudi normalisation with Israel. The decision ends years of Saudi lobbying for the aircraft and comes as Riyadh faces escalating Houthi attacks.

**Background:** US law requires preserving Israel's qualitative military edge in the region, which has long blocked F-35 sales to Arab states; a similar UAE deal stalled after the Abraham Accords.

**Why it matters:** Selling the most advanced US fighter to Riyadh without a normalisation quid pro quo signals that Washington is willing to arm Gulf partners on security grounds alone, weakening Israel's leverage over the shape of any future Saudi-Israeli deal.

**Different views:** The BBC frames the sale as inherently controversial and explains the qualitative-edge doctrine and Saudi lobbying history for a general audience; the Jerusalem Post frames it as a setback delivered over Israeli objections, foregrounding that Israeli officials wanted the jets tied to normalisation and treating Israeli security concerns as the central stake, with less attention to Saudi threat perceptions from Yemen.


### 3. [From ovations in Venice to death threats in Israel: the extreme responses to NAZA documentary](https://www.theguardian.com/film/2026/sep/19/ovations-venice-death-threats-israel-extreme-responses-to-naza-documentary)
*The Guardian · UK*

**What happened:** The Gaza documentary NAZA, which drew a 25-minute standing ovation at the Venice film festival, has provoked death threats against its directors in Israel and a backlash across much of the political establishment, centred on soldiers' testimony about conduct in Gaza. The furore is unfolding five weeks before Israel's 27 October election.

**Background:** Israel goes to the polls on 27 October in a campaign widely described as the most consequential in its history, with the conduct of the Gaza war and accountability for it a central dividing line.

**Why it matters:** The reaction shows how contested the record of the war has become inside Israel itself, and the dispute is being absorbed into an election that will shape Israeli policy toward Gaza, the West Bank and any regional normalisation track.

**Different views:** The Guardian centres the film-makers as targets of an intimidation campaign, moving between the Venice acclaim and the threats they now face, and treats the soldiers' testimony as credible; Al Jazeera frames the same episode around why long-standing allegations about Gaza are only now igniting Israeli fury, foregrounding the political establishment's rejection of the testimony as the story rather than the film's reception abroad. Neither presents the Israeli government's own account of the incidents described as settled.
