1. Two container ships sink near strait of Hormuz amid surge in Iranian attacks

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Missiles struck two container ships near the Strait of Hormuz and both sank. One was the Singapore-flagged GFS Pride, and its 21 crew were evacuated safely. Video shows containers and debris floating around the strait, and separately CENTCOM says US forces destroyed an Iranian-affiliated dhow in the Gulf of Oman after removing its crew.

Background: The US-Iran war began on February 28. Washington reinstated its blockade of Iranian ports in July and launched 'Operation Economic Outcast' in August, and Trump has reportedly ruled out new strikes on Iran before the US midterms.

Why it matters: Attacks on shipping are rising faster than at any point since the war began, and the sinkings threaten one of the world's most important energy chokepoints. That could deepen the global energy shock and push the US-Iran stalemate back into open fighting.

Different views: The Guardian blames Iran directly and reads the surge as an attempt to close the waterway before the midterms. The BBC and Jerusalem Post say only 'projectile', citing Singapore's maritime authority and unnamed sources, though the Post places the sinking in its tally of losses since the US-Iran war began. Al Jazeera's video refers only to 'recent attacks on vessels' and does not say who carried them out.

2. Houthi missile hits Aramco building after Saudi-led coalition strike on Yemen fuel depot

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: A Houthi missile hit Saudi Aramco's headquarters complex in Dhahran. This came after a Saudi-led coalition strike on a fuel depot in Yemen. The Houthis have promised more attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities, while their ground advance on Taiz has cut off supplies to the city and residents are stockpiling food.

Background: Fighting in Yemen has escalated in recent weeks. The UN says more than 200,000 people have been displaced, and aid agencies say hospitals are overwhelmed and aid has been cut to 800,000 people.

Why it matters: Direct strikes on Saudi energy and transport infrastructure have reopened the Saudi-Houthi front and exposed gaps in Saudi air defences. The fallout is already wider: an EU-Gulf summit was postponed, the Asian Cup could be delayed, and the Saudi investment forum known as 'Davos in the Desert' is letting executives attend remotely.

Different views: The BBC presents the Aramco strike as retaliation for a coalition attack and quotes the Saudi energy ministry's description of a 'limited fire'. The Jerusalem Post reports 'extensive damage', leads with the Houthis' threats of more attacks and asks whether Israeli technology could help defend Saudi Arabia. Al Jazeera frames it as a Houthi effort to raise the cost of the war for Riyadh, and the Guardian focuses on hungry civilians in besieged Taiz rather than the strikes on Saudi targets.

3. Israel blocks construction of key Gaza base created by Trump peace board

The Guardian · UK

What happened: Israel is not allowing work to start on a base in Gaza designed to house 150 international troops for Trump's UN-authorised Board of Peace. Sources say contractors and trucks have been kept out since the Board issued its first construction contract in August.

Background: The Board of Peace was set up in 2025 under a UN mandate, with Trump as chair, to oversee Gaza's stabilisation and reconstruction. Its plan links rebuilding to Hamas disarmament.

Why it matters: It is a rare open dispute between Israel and a Trump-led body. It also stalls the international security presence that the reconstruction-for-disarmament plan depends on.

Different views: The Guardian broke the story as an exclusive and makes Israel's obstruction the main point. The Jerusalem Post reports it second-hand ('report') and runs it alongside German support for Gaza rebuilding. A separate Post piece blames slow progress mainly on the 'enormous gap' between Hamas's behaviour and US hopes for disarmament, citing Israeli security officials.
