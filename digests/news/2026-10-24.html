1. Iran says won’t allow US warships to get close as Trump ponders new attacks

Al Jazeera English · Qatar

What happened: Iran said it will not allow US warships to approach its waters as Trump weighs renewed strikes, while Tehran threatened retaliation against Gulf refineries, datacentres and undersea internet cables. US intelligence warned that Iranian plots against American personnel in the region have reached advanced stages, prompting staffing adjustments at US diplomatic posts, and Brent crude passed $117 a barrel.

Background: The US and Israel fought a war with Iran earlier this year, and a fragile pause has held since. Foreign ministers from eight European and Middle Eastern states met in Istanbul to urge against a fresh round of attacks.

Why it matters: A second US-Israeli campaign against Iran would widen the conflict to Gulf infrastructure and shipping, with immediate consequences for oil prices and the safety of US personnel across the region. The Istanbul grouping marks the first organised diplomatic bloc pushing back on Washington and Jerusalem.

Different views: Al Jazeera foregrounds the view from Tehran and Iranian resolve not to back down, while the BBC and Jerusalem Post lead with the US intelligence warning about plots against Americans, treating the US memo as the authoritative account; the Post also carries Trump's claim that Iran is faking tanker hits to raise oil prices before the midterms. The Guardian centres the Istanbul anti-war summit and the oil price spike, framing the crisis through its economic and diplomatic fallout rather than the threat picture.

2. Israeli forces begin demolishing Palestinian buildings in Hebron after two Israeli soldiers shot dead

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: After two Palestinian gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers at a checkpoint outside Hebron on Friday, Israeli forces began a days-long operation with raids and demolitions in the city and neighbouring Halhul. Netanyahu said 'we will destroy this place', and the Hebron municipality said about 35 hectares had been levelled and hundreds of families driven from their homes.

Background: Israeli operations and settler violence in the occupied West Bank have intensified since the Gaza war, with demolitions and displacement in Hebron already a longstanding flashpoint. The operation comes days before Israel's general election on Tuesday.

Why it matters: The scale of the demolitions and the explicit language from Netanyahu and ministers signal punitive displacement as policy rather than a targeted security response. It also plays directly into the election, where Netanyahu is campaigning on toughness.

Different views: The Guardian describes the operation as 'collective punishment' and 'razing a neighbourhood', relying on Hebron municipality figures for the scale of destruction and leading with the displacement of families. The BBC leads with the killing of the two soldiers and presents the demolitions as a response to that attack, while Al Jazeera frames the piece around why the operation is happening and foregrounds Netanyahu's 'destroy this place' quote. The Jerusalem Post does not cover the demolitions in this set.

3. Seven shot dead by gunmen at church funeral in Jordan, officials say

BBC Middle East · UK

What happened: Gunmen opened fire on a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in Jordan's Karak governorate, killing at least seven people and injuring more than thirty. Authorities called it a terrorist act by extremists, a manhunt is under way, and King Abdullah ordered a firm response.

Background: Attacks on Jordan's Christian minority are extremely rare, and the kingdom has repeatedly foiled plots by Islamist militants in recent years. Jordan is a key US ally and has absorbed heavy regional pressure since the Gaza war.

Why it matters: A mass-casualty attack on a church inside Jordan tests the stability of a state that has largely insulated itself from regional violence. If a known group is behind it, the implications for Jordan's security posture and its relationship with Washington are significant.

Different views: Death tolls differ, with the Guardian reporting eight dead and the BBC, Al Jazeera and Jerusalem Post reporting seven. The Guardian foregrounds the targeting of the Christian minority, the Jerusalem Post emphasises the US embassy's security warning and the history of foiled Islamist plots, and Al Jazeera centres the king's response while stressing that authorities say it is too early to link the attackers to any known group.
