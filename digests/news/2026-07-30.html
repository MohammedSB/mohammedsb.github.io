1. US hits dozens of targets in Iran overnight as peace efforts under threat

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: The US military launched large-scale strikes on dozens of targets inside Iran overnight, described as roughly twice the size of previous bombardments, hitting sites including Qeshm Island, Bushehr, Kish Island, and Ahwaz. The strikes were in retaliation for an Iranian attack on US forces in Jordan, which the IRGC claimed killed US soldiers and destroyed F-35s. Iran launched retaliatory missile barrages, with Jordan intercepting five missiles targeting its territory.

Background: The US has been engaged in an escalating military confrontation with Iran, including a naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. The latest cycle of strikes and counter-strikes represents a significant intensification, with Saudi Arabia publicly joining US forces for the first time in strikes on Iran-backed militias in Iraq.

Why it matters: This marks one of the most serious direct US-Iran military exchanges in decades, threatening to drag multiple regional actors — Jordan, Saudi Arabia, Iraq — into open conflict and putting diplomatic peace efforts at severe risk. The Strait of Hormuz blockade and drone attacks on tankers at Egyptian ports signal potential global energy supply disruptions.

Different views: Al Jazeera and the Guardian emphasize humanitarian risks, the threat to peace negotiations, and regional destabilization, framing US strikes as escalatory. The Jerusalem Post and BBC focus on Iranian aggression as the trigger, highlight Saudi Arabia's milestone decision to join strikes, and present the military campaign as a necessary deterrent against IRGC-directed attacks.

2. US-Saudi strikes in Iraq push region into ‘uncharted territory’, analysts warn

The Guardian · UK · left-leaning

What happened: Saudi Arabia publicly joined US forces in striking Iran-backed Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF) militia sites in Iraq, following a series of drone and missile attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure. Analysts described the move as pushing the region into 'uncharted territory,' marking the first time Riyadh has openly partnered with Washington in offensive strikes against Iranian proxies. Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi condemned the strikes as a violation of Iraqi sovereignty.

Background: Saudi Arabia has long faced Houthi and Iranian proxy attacks but historically avoided publicly aligning with US military operations against Iran-linked groups. The kingdom is simultaneously trying to build a coalition to protect Red Sea shipping from Houthi attacks while navigating its own domestic economic vulnerabilities tied to oil infrastructure.

Why it matters: Saudi Arabia's overt military partnership with the US against Iranian proxies fundamentally reshapes the regional alignment, potentially foreclosing Saudi de-escalation options with Iran and drawing Riyadh deeper into a war it had sought to manage carefully. It also signals a broader Sunni Arab bloc consolidating against Iran.

Different views: The Guardian and Al Jazeera stress analyst warnings of an 'unpredictable' widening war and frame Saudi involvement as dangerous overreach. The Jerusalem Post presents the US-Saudi cooperation as a historic and necessary milestone in countering Iranian aggression, describing it as a strategic achievement.

3. Negotiators working on final roadmap for Gaza’s future, Hamas disarmament, source tells ‘Post’

The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning

What happened: Negotiators are working on a final roadmap for Gaza's future that includes Hamas disarmament and a framework for eliminating all tunnels, weapons depots, and production facilities across the Gaza Strip, according to a source cited by the Jerusalem Post. Separately, Al Jazeera reported that Hamas officials met with Qatari, Egyptian, and Turkish mediators in Cairo to discuss a truce, even as Israeli strikes continued killing civilians including children.

Background: Gaza ceasefire negotiations have been ongoing for months with Qatar, Egypt, and the US as primary mediators. Any framework requiring Hamas disarmament represents a significant escalation of Israeli and US demands beyond previous ceasefire proposals, which had focused on hostage-for-prisoner exchanges and temporary pauses in fighting.

Why it matters: A disarmament-focused framework would be a major departure from prior negotiations and could either represent a breakthrough pathway to ending the conflict or a maximalist Israeli demand that collapses talks entirely. The simultaneous continuation of deadly strikes underscores the fragility of any diplomatic progress.

Different views: The Jerusalem Post frames the disarmament roadmap as a serious and constructive diplomatic development. Al Jazeera foregrounds the ongoing Israeli strikes killing children and civilians during the same period, implicitly questioning Israeli good faith in negotiations, while emphasizing the role of Arab and Turkish mediators.
