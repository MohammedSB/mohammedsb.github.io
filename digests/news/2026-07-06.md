### 1. [Millions join funeral procession for Iran’s supreme leader Ali Khamenei](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/06/ali-khamenei-funeral-procession-iran-supreme-leader)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Millions of mourners filled Tehran for a week-long funeral procession for Iran's supreme leader Ali Khamenei, who was killed in US-Israeli airstrikes on 28 February. Crowds carried coffins from Revolution Square to Azadi Square amid chants of 'death to America' and open calls for the killing of Donald Trump, while Iran vowed revenge and said it would pursue legal action against Israel and the US.

**Background:** Khamenei was assassinated during the US-Israel war on Iran; his son Mojtaba is the presumed successor but has remained absent from public view, and Iran's leadership is visibly in transition.

**Why it matters:** The death of the man who ruled Iran for decades marks a generational rupture at the top of the Islamic Republic, with major implications for Iran's revenge posture, regional proxies, and any future confrontation with Israel and the US.

**Different views:** Al Jazeera and Guardian foreground the sheer scale of grief and the 'turnaround' for a regime recently facing protests; the Jerusalem Post frames the funeral as engineered 'theater of mourning' and highlights 'kill Trump' chants, the reappointed 'Judge of Death,' and Iran plotting to reclaim regional influence, while the BBC emphasizes how the new regime differs from the old.


### 2. [Hamas official resigns in first step to dissolve Gaza governing body, hand authority to NCAG](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-901534)
*The Jerusalem Post · Israeli · right-leaning*

**What happened:** A Hamas official resigned in what is described as a first step to dissolve Gaza's governing body and hand administrative authority to a technocratic committee (NCAG) under the postwar 'Board of Peace.' The decision also ends UNRWA's role in Gaza, which Palestinian officials denounced as politically motivated, while disarming Hamas remains the central unresolved hurdle to the Trump plan.

**Background:** Hamas has governed Gaza since 2007; the postwar Trump framework envisions a technocratic administration replacing it, but Hamas's refusal to disarm and questions about enforcement dominate the transition.

**Why it matters:** This is the first concrete move toward postwar governance in Gaza and could determine whether the ceasefire holds and whether a Hamas-free administration is viable.

**Different views:** The Jerusalem Post presents the handover and end of UNRWA positively while stressing Hamas's disarmament refusal and alleged bribery of medical evacuation lists; Palestinian officials (via the Post) call ending UNRWA an Israeli right-wing effort to 'destroy the Palestinian cause'; the Post itself notes some officials may exaggerate the Hamas threat to justify reinvasion.


### 3. [Iran seeks to tighten control over strait of Hormuz alongside Khamenei funeral](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/05/iran-control-strait-of-hormuz-ali-khamenei-funeral)
*The Guardian · UK · left-leaning*

**What happened:** Iran moved to tighten control over the Strait of Hormuz during the Khamenei funeral, with IRGC warnings forcing some ships to turn back. Iranian officials said they will charge ships 'service fees' for transit — which Tehran insists are not tolls — despite Trump's denial that such charges exist.

**Background:** Roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes through the strait; control of the waterway is being cast as central to Khamenei's legacy and Iran's leverage after the war.

**Why it matters:** Any Iranian move to restrict or tax passage through Hormuz threatens global energy supplies and could trigger a fresh military confrontation with the US and Gulf states.

**Different views:** The Guardian frames it as a diplomatic-military contest tied to Khamenei's legacy and Qur'anic messaging to Gulf delegations; the Jerusalem Post frames it around the dispute with Trump over whether the fees are effectively a toll.
